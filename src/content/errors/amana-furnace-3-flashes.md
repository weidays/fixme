## What this code means

Three flashes on your Amana furnace control board means **pressure switch stuck open** — the inducer motor is running, but the pressure switch did not close to confirm proper draft.

The pressure switch is a safety device. Before the burners light, the board starts the inducer (draft) motor and waits for the switch to sense adequate airflow through the flue. If the switch stays **open**, the board assumes the furnace cannot vent safely and refuses to ignite. This protects you from combustion gases spilling into the home.

On most Amana integrated furnace control boards this fault **auto-retries** — if the switch closes on the next attempt, heating resumes and the code clears from active status. If the condition persists across retries, the board holds and continues flashing until the problem is corrected. Legends vary slightly by board revision, so confirm the 3-flash meaning on the wiring label inside your furnace's lower door.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked flue or intake vent** — debris, ice, insects, or a bird's nest in the exhaust or intake pipe (often the outdoor termination) prevents proper draft.
2. **Condensate backup** — on 90%+ condensing furnaces, a clogged condensate trap, drain line, or full pump backs up water and blocks the pressure switch from closing.
3. **Disconnected, cracked, or plugged pressure switch hose/tubing** — the small rubber tube between the inducer and switch is loose, kinked, or full of condensate.
4. **Weak or failing inducer motor** — the motor spins but no longer moves enough air to create the vacuum the switch needs.
5. **Faulty pressure switch** — the switch itself is stuck open or out of calibration and won't close under normal draft.
6. **Blocked inducer housing or drain port** — sediment or condensate in the inducer assembly reduces airflow.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do — no cabinet interior work:

- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to Heat and the setpoint is above room temperature; replace batteries if applicable.
- **Reset the furnace once.** Flip the furnace switch or breaker off, wait 30 seconds, then back on — do this **only once**. Repeated resets are not safe.
- **Inspect visible outdoor vents.** Look at the exhaust and intake pipe terminations outside. Clear any leaves, snow, ice, insects, or nests you can reach safely.
- **Check the condensate drain.** If your furnace is high-efficiency, look for standing water or an overflowing condensate pump. Clear a visibly clogged drain line or empty a full pump reservoir.
- **Check the air filter** as general maintenance. Replace it if it's dirty, and match the size printed on the edge of the existing filter. Be aware that a dirty filter is *not* a cause of this code — restricted return or supply airflow trips the high limit (4 flashes on this family), not the draft pressure switch, so a new filter will not clear a 3-flash fault.

If the code returns after these checks, stop and call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A pro will verify the failure in order so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Read the flash history** on the board and confirm the 3-flash code.
2. **Meter the pressure switch** while the inducer runs, checking that it closes at the rated pressure stamped on the switch.
3. **Measure inducer draft** with a manometer at the pressure tap to confirm the motor is producing spec vacuum.
4. **Inspect the hose and ports** for cracks, kinks, or condensate blockage.
5. **Check the flue and intake** internally for obstruction or excessive length — and find and correct any blockage *before* condemning the pressure switch or the inducer, since a restricted vent makes a healthy switch and a healthy motor both look bad.
6. **Check the condensate system** — trap, drain, and inducer drain port — on condensing models.
7. **Test inducer amp draw** to judge motor health before condemning it.

They should isolate whether the fault is a blockage (cheap), a switch, a hose, or the inducer motor (most expensive) — not just replace parts blindly.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inducer runs, no ignition, 3 flashes | Pressure switch stuck open | One reset; check vents & condensate | Meter switch, verify draft |
| Water pooling near furnace, 3 flashes | Condensate backup blocking draft | Clear visible drain / empty pump | Clear trap, inducer drain port |
| Code after storm / snow | Blocked outdoor vent termination | Clear visible obstruction outside | Inspect full flue run |
| Inducer sounds weak or noisy | Failing inducer motor | None — call pro | Test amp draw, replace inducer |
| Whistling / loose tube near switch | Cracked or plugged hose | None — call pro | Replace/clear pressure tubing |
| Intermittent fault, then works | Marginal switch or partial blockage | Note when it happens | Confirm draft under load |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; varies by region and access):

- **DIY vent/condensate clearing:** $0
- **Air filter:** $10 – $40
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Pressure switch hose/tubing:** $80 – $180
- **Condensate trap/line service:** $100 – $250
- **Inducer motor/assembly replacement:** $400 – $1,200 — condensing furnaces and cramped-access installs commonly land in the $700 – $1,000 range with an OEM part
- **Diagnostic service call:** $90 – $180 (often credited toward the repair)

If your furnace is under warranty, the pressure switch and inducer may be covered parts — you'd typically pay labor only.
