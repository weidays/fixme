## What this code means

"Not responding to remote" isn't a diagnostic code. It's a symptom: the indoor unit doesn't beep, change its display or change modes when you press the remote.

The fault can sit in one of three places:

- **The remote:** it isn't transmitting.
- **The signal path:** the infrared receiver can't see the signal.
- **The indoor unit:** the receiver, display board or main control board isn't processing the signal, or the unit has no power.

This symptom doesn't auto-reset like a lockout code. If the cause is a temporary control glitch, one power cycle at the breaker often clears it. If the unit is also flashing lights, see the blinking light article instead. A flashing light means the board has logged a fault and may be refusing commands until it's repaired.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Weak or dead remote batteries.** This is by far the most common cause. The remote's LCD may still show a display even when the batteries are too weak to transmit reliably.
2. **Remote problems.** The remote may be in the wrong setting, locked, or set to the wrong mode or temperature units. Some American Standard and OEM remotes have a child-lock or "lock" function, and some have a reset pinhole. The details vary by remote model.
3. **Blocked line of sight or infrared interference.** Furniture, curtains or a dirty receiver window can block the signal. Direct sunlight or some LED and fluorescent lighting can also wash out infrared signals.
4. **Control glitch after a power surge or outage.** The indoor board can freeze. One breaker power cycle often clears it.
5. **No power to the indoor unit.** A tripped breaker or an off disconnect means the unit can't respond at all. Most units power the indoor head from the outdoor unit's circuit.
6. **Damaged remote.** A drop or liquid spill can break the remote. A failed transmitter LED or keypad needs a replacement remote matched to your model.
7. **Failed infrared receiver or display board** in the indoor unit.
8. **Main indoor control board fault or loose communication wiring.** Loose connectors or board failure are less common. They often come with blinking fault codes.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the batteries.** Use fresh alkaline batteries, not rechargeables, and check the orientation.
- **Test the remote.** Use the phone-camera test described in the FAQ.
- **Check the remote's settings.** Look for a lock icon or the wrong mode. If your remote has a reset function, use the method in the owner's manual.
- **Check the signal path.** Stand within about 20 ft, point directly at the indoor unit's display or receiver window, and remove any obstructions. Draw the blinds if strong sunlight hits the unit. Wipe the receiver window gently with a dry cloth.
- **Check the breaker and disconnect.** Confirm the breaker is on and the outdoor disconnect is in place. If a breaker has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and see the breaker keeps tripping article.
- **Reset the unit once.** Switch the mini-split's breaker off, wait about 5 minutes, then turn it back on. Try the remote again. Do this only once.
- **Try the manual button.** Lift the front panel, the plastic face cover only, and press the manual or emergency button. Then close and seat the panel firmly.
  - If the unit runs from the button, the board and power are likely fine, and the fault is the remote or the receiver.
  - If nothing happens, the problem is power or the board.
- **Check for drain backup.** Make sure the filter is clean and the condensate drain isn't backed up. Some units display a fault and limit operation when the drain backs up.

Don't open the electrical cover or test wiring yourself. Indoor heads carry line voltage.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech should be able to explain each of these steps:

- Confirm the remote transmits, with a camera or IR tester, or by using a known-good shop remote for your series.
- Check for stored fault codes or a blinking display, and look them up in the service manual for your model.
- Verify the supply voltage at the outdoor unit and the voltage reaching the indoor head across the interconnect terminals.
- Inspect the receiver or display board harness and its connection to the main board, then reseat it.
- Test the receiver or display board, and replace it if the unit runs manually but never registers IR commands.
- Evaluate the main indoor board if it doesn't respond to the manual button even though power is good.
- Check for surge damage if the problem started after a storm.

Be cautious if a quote jumps straight to replacing the main board or outdoor components. That's a red flag when the manual button works normally.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remote screen dim or blank | Dead batteries | Replace batteries | None |
| Remote screen fine, no camera flicker | Faulty remote | Reset per manual; buy a model-matched remote | Supply and verify an OEM remote |
| Camera flicker, unit doesn't beep | Blocked or failed IR receiver | Clear line of sight, wipe window, block sunlight | Test and replace the receiver or display board |
| Unit works only by manual button | Receiver or display board | Use manual mode for now | Replace the receiver or display board |
| No display, no manual response | No power or main board | Check breaker and disconnect, one power cycle | Verify voltage, diagnose the board |
| Lights blinking, ignores remote | Stored fault | Note the blink pattern | Diagnose per the service manual |
| Stopped after a storm or outage | Board lockup or surge damage | One breaker power cycle | Test boards, recommend surge protection |

These are typical US ranges. Parts availability varies by series.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
