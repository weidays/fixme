## What this code means

A frozen evaporator coil is not a stored fault code on most Amana air conditioners. It is a condition where ice builds up on the indoor coil, which sits above or beside your furnace or air handler. It can also show up as frost on the large copper suction line running to the outdoor unit.

The coil is designed to run just above freezing. When it gets too little warm air across it, or runs at too low a refrigerant pressure, its surface drops below 32°F. Moisture from your home's air then freezes onto it. The ice blocks airflow, which makes the coil colder still, so the problem snowballs.

**What you'll typically notice:**
- Weak airflow or warm air from the vents
- Visible frost on the refrigerant lines
- Water around the indoor unit as the ice melts

**Codes and reset behavior:**
- Standard single-stage and two-stage Amana condensers usually show nothing.
- Communicating or inverter systems (for example AVXC models) may log low-pressure or airflow-related faults. What appears depends on the specific model and control board.
- Because there is usually no code, there is nothing to reset. The coil must thaw, and the cause must be corrected, or it will freeze again.

This page focuses on icing. For general warm-air problems without ice, see Amana AC Not Cooling.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter.** This is the most common cause by far. Restricted airflow lets the coil drop below freezing.
2. **Closed or blocked supply vents or return grilles.** Furniture, rugs, or closing too many registers reduces airflow across the coil.
3. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Low charge means low suction pressure and a coil below freezing. Leaks are often found in the evaporator coil itself.
4. **Dirty evaporator coil.** Dust and debris matted on the coil fins restrict airflow, especially in systems that have run without a filter.
5. **Blower problems.** Possible issues include:
   - A failing blower motor or blower capacitor (on PSC motors)
   - A dirty blower wheel
   - A fan speed set too low for cooling on the furnace or air handler board
6. **Running the AC in cool outdoor weather or with a very low setpoint.** Operating below roughly 60–65°F outdoors, or for long hours at very low thermostat settings, can promote icing.
7. **Metering device or refrigerant restriction.** A malfunctioning TXV, or a restriction in the line set or filter drier, starves the coil and causes icing.
8. **Thermostat or control issue keeping the compressor running continuously.** Examples include a stuck relay or contactor, or a thermostat that never satisfies.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Stop cooling and thaw the coil.** Set the thermostat to OFF and the fan to ON.
   - Allow 2–6 hours for the ice to melt fully.
   - Never chip ice off the coil or use a heat gun or hair dryer. Coil fins and tubing puncture easily.
2. **Replace the air filter.** Check the filter slot at the furnace or air handler and any return-grille filters. If it's gray or bowed, replace it with the same size, which is printed on the frame.
   - Avoid very high-MERV filters unless your system was designed for them.
3. **Open every supply register.** Clear return grilles of furniture, curtains, and rugs.
4. **Check thermostat settings and batteries.**
   - Confirm the mode is COOL and the fan is set to AUTO or ON as intended.
   - Avoid setpoints below about 70°F for long periods.
   - Replace thermostat batteries if the display is dim or blank.
5. **Watch the condensate drain while thawing.**
   - Make sure the melt water drains through the condensate line rather than overflowing the pan.
   - If the line is clogged, you can clear the accessible end. Use a wet/dry vacuum at the outdoor drain outlet, or pour a cup of plain water or diluted vinegar into the access tee if one is provided.
   - Many systems have a float or safety switch that shuts cooling off when the pan fills.
6. **Restart and observe.** After the coil is fully thawed and dry, return to COOL mode.
   - Check the vents for cold, strong airflow within 15 minutes.
   - Recheck the line and coil area after a few hours.
7. **Seat the panels.** Make sure the furnace or air handler access panels are fully seated, since a loose blower door can stop the system or leak air.

If the coil re-freezes after a clean filter and open vents, stop running cooling and call a licensed HVAC technician. Do not add refrigerant yourself, open the indoor cabinet, or repeatedly run the system frozen.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Use this to sanity-check what you're told and quoted.

1. **Confirm the coil is fully thawed.** Readings taken on an iced coil are meaningless, so a good tech will thaw it before measuring.
2. **Measure airflow.**
   - Check total external static pressure.
   - Verify blower speed tap or ECM settings against the Amana/Goodman spec, typically around 350–400 CFM per ton of cooling.
   - Inspect the blower wheel and test the motor and run capacitor.
3. **Inspect the evaporator coil.** The tech looks for dirt matting, using a light or by pulling the coil access panel, and cleans the coil if needed.
4. **Check the refrigerant charge with gauges or digital probes.**
   - Measure suction and liquid pressures, superheat, and subcooling.
   - Compare the readings to the charging chart on the condenser data plate. Most Amana units with a TXV are charged by subcooling.
5. **Hunt for leaks if the charge is low.** Methods include an electronic detector, bubble solution, UV dye, or nitrogen pressure testing. Evaporator coils and Schrader valves are common leak points.
   - **Red flag:** a tech who "tops off" refrigerant without looking for a leak is setting you up for a repeat freeze.
6. **Evaluate the metering device and line set.** A temperature drop across a component points to a restriction. Abnormal superheat with normal charge suggests a TXV problem.
7. **Check the controls.**
   - Verify the thermostat call ends properly.
   - Check that the outdoor contactor isn't welded closed.
   - Read fault history on communicating models.
8. **Check warranty status.** Many Amana units carry long parts warranties, and some include unit replacement coverage. Registration status matters, so have your model and serial numbers ready.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on coil, filter visibly dirty | Clogged air filter | Thaw with fan ON, replace filter | Only if it re-freezes |
| Weak airflow, several rooms with closed vents | Restricted supply/return | Open registers, clear return grilles | Static pressure test if still weak |
| Coil freezes repeatedly despite clean filter | Low refrigerant charge from a leak | Turn cooling off, thaw, call a pro | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge to spec |
| Ice plus musty smell, filter often missing | Dirty evaporator coil | Thaw, install correct filter | Clean coil (chemical or removal) |
| Air barely moves even after thaw | Blower motor, capacitor, or wheel issue | Check filter and breaker only | Test motor and capacitor, clean wheel, correct fan speed |
| Freezing only on cool nights or at very low setpoints | Low load operation | Raise setpoint, avoid cooling below ~60°F outdoors | Check charge and airflow if it persists |
| Frost on the outdoor suction line, normal airflow | Restriction or TXV fault | Turn cooling off, call a pro | Diagnose superheat and subcooling, replace TXV or drier |
| Outdoor unit never shuts off | Stuck contactor or thermostat fault | Check thermostat settings and batteries | Replace contactor, check control wiring |
| Water on floor during thaw | Overflowing pan or clogged drain | Clear the accessible condensate line | Clear the trap, replace the float switch or pan |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor, before warranty:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Air filter (DIY) | $5–$40 |
| Service call / diagnostic | $80–$200 |
| Evaporator coil cleaning | $100–$400 (more if the coil must be pulled) |
| Blower capacitor | $120–$300 |
| Blower motor (PSC) | $350–$700 |
| Blower motor (ECM/variable speed) | $600–$1,500 |
| Leak search | $150–$500 |
| Minor leak repair (Schrader core, brazed joint) plus recharge | $200–$800 |
| Refrigerant recharge (R-410A or R-454B) | $150–$600+ depending on amount and refrigerant price |
| TXV replacement | $400–$1,200 |
| Filter drier replacement | $200–$500 |
| Contactor replacement | $120–$300 |
| Evaporator coil replacement | $1,200–$3,000+ |

**Notes on coil replacement:**
- Amana's parts warranty may cover the coil itself, leaving you to pay labor and refrigerant.
- Older R-22 systems may make replacing the whole system the better value.
