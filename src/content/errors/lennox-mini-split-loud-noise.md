## What this code means

"Loud noise" is not a Lennox error code. It is a symptom. Lennox mini-splits do not show a code just because the unit is noisy. The protection logic only steps in if a related fault occurs, such as a fan motor speed fault or compressor overcurrent. There is therefore nothing to reset; the noise continues until its cause is fixed.

This page covers the ductless system specifically: the indoor wall head (or cassette) and the inverter outdoor unit. Furnace and central-AC noises are covered on separate pages.

Before you call anyone, separate normal sounds from problem sounds.

**Normal sounds:**
- Soft gurgling or hissing from refrigerant flow.
- Faint clicks from panels expanding or the expansion valve adjusting.
- A rising and falling hum as the inverter compressor changes speed.
- A whoosh or hiss during defrost in heat mode.

**Problem sounds:**
- Grinding, scraping or screeching.
- Rattling or buzzing that does not stop.
- Loud banging or clanking at startup.
- A constant loud hiss.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty indoor filters or debris on the blower wheel.** Clogged filters make the indoor head whistle or roar. Dust buildup on the cross-flow blower wheel unbalances it and causes a wobble or thumping.
2. **Loose panels, louvers or mounting.** Vibration from these makes rattling and buzzing. Common sources include:
   - a loose indoor front cover or louver motor;
   - an outdoor unit not level on its pad or bracket;
   - missing anti-vibration pads;
   - refrigerant lines touching the wall or the cabinet.
3. **Debris in the outdoor unit.** Leaves, twigs or ice touching the outdoor fan blade make clicking, scraping or slapping sounds.
4. **Failing fan motor bearings, indoor or outdoor.** Worn bearings cause grinding, squealing or a growling noise that gets louder with fan speed.
5. **Damaged or cracked fan blade or blower wheel.** This causes a rhythmic thump or wobble, often after ice or debris has hit the blade.
6. **Refrigerant issues.** A constant loud hiss, or a gurgling that comes with poor performance, can mean low charge, a leak or a restriction.
7. **Compressor problems.** Loud banging, clanking or knocking from the outdoor unit can come from worn internal parts, loose compressor mounts, or liquid slugging. Some of this noise is normal during defrost changeover. Persistent noise is not.
8. **Electrical buzzing.** A loud buzz from the outdoor unit can come from a failing contactor, reactor or inverter board component. The parts involved vary by model.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Clean the indoor filters.** Turn the unit off with the remote, lift the front panel, then slide out and rinse the mesh filters. Let them dry fully before you reinstall them.
- **Confirm the remote settings.** Very high or "Turbo" fan modes are loud by design. Try Auto or a lower fan speed, and replace the remote batteries if the remote seems unresponsive.
- **Check the outdoor unit from outside.** Look without touching the cabinet internals:
  - Clear leaves, grass clippings and snow from around the unit, keeping about 2 feet of clearance.
  - Look for anything touching the fan guard.
  - Check whether the unit looks tilted or has shifted on its pad or bracket.
- **Make sure exterior panels are seated.** If the indoor front cover is loose, press it closed until it latches. Seat the outdoor unit's service cover if it is visibly ajar, but do not open it.
- **Check the condensate drain.** If you hear gurgling together with dripping, make sure the drain line exit outside is not blocked or kinked.
- **Rule out normal defrost.** In heat mode, a hiss and steam lasting under about 10 minutes is a normal defrost cycle.
- **Reset the power once if needed.** If a grinding noise is accompanied by a blinking light, turn the system off, cycle the breaker or disconnect once, and note whether the noise returns. If it does, leave the unit off and call a technician.

Stop and shut the unit off if you hear grinding, screeching or metal-on-metal sounds. Running it can destroy a motor or compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Locate the noise.** They will confirm whether it comes from the indoor head, the outdoor unit or the line set, and whether it changes with fan speed or compressor speed. Running the fan only versus cooling helps them separate fan noise from compressor noise.
- **Inspect the indoor blower wheel.** They will check it for dirt buildup, cracks and wobble, and spin the motor by hand to feel for bearing drag.
- **Inspect the outdoor fan.** They will check the blade for damage, the motor shaft for play, and the bearings for noise.
- **Check mounting and vibration.** This includes:
  - the outdoor unit's level;
  - the vibration isolators;
  - the wall bracket fasteners;
  - the compressor mounting bolts;
  - line-set contact points.
- **Measure refrigerant pressures and temperatures.** They will compare the readings with Lennox charging data to look for undercharge, overcharge or a restriction, and leak-test if the charge is low.
- **Read fault history and operating data.** Where the model supports it, they will check the board's fault history and compressor operating data, and look at inverter and fan motor feedback.
- **Inspect electrical components.** They will check for buzzing contactors, reactors or failing board parts.

A good quote names the specific component and explains how it was confirmed. Be cautious of a compressor or full-system replacement recommended without pressure readings or a clear noise source.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whistling or roaring from the indoor head | Clogged filters | Wash the filters and reinstall them dry | Clean the coil and blower wheel if dirt has built up |
| Rattling or buzzing from the indoor head | Loose front panel or louver | Press the front cover fully closed | Secure the panel, louver motor or mounting plate |
| Thumping or wobbling from the indoor head | Dirty or damaged blower wheel | Lower the fan speed and schedule service | Clean or replace the blower wheel |
| Clicking or slapping from the outdoor unit | Debris or ice touching the fan | Clear debris from outside the guard | Inspect the blade and replace it if damaged |
| Grinding or squealing that changes with fan speed | Worn fan motor bearings | Shut the unit off | Replace the fan motor |
| Vibration humming through the wall | Unlevel unit, worn pads or lines touching the structure | Check the unit's level visually and report it | Re-level, add isolators, re-secure lines |
| Constant loud hiss with poor performance | Refrigerant leak or restriction | Shut the unit off and call | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Banging or knocking from the compressor | Compressor mounts, slugging or internal wear | Shut the unit off | Diagnose the charge and mounts, or replace the compressor |
| Loud electrical buzz from the outdoor unit | Contactor, reactor or board component | None; keep clear | Electrical testing and part replacement |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges. Prices vary by region, series and warranty status, and Lennox parts warranty may cover the part but not the labor.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter cleaning | $0 DIY |
| Service call and diagnosis | $100 – $250 |
| Tighten panels, re-level the unit, add vibration pads | $100 – $300 |
| Indoor coil and blower wheel deep cleaning | $200 – $500 per head |
| Indoor blower wheel replacement | $200 – $500 |
| Indoor fan motor replacement | $300 – $700 |
| Outdoor fan blade replacement | $150 – $400 |
| Outdoor fan motor replacement | $400 – $900 |
| Leak repair and refrigerant recharge | $400 – $1,500 |
| Outdoor inverter board or electrical component | $500 – $1,500 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500 – $2,500+ |

If the system is out of warranty and needs a compressor replacement, replacing the outdoor unit or the whole system is often the better value.
