## What this code means

"Runs constantly but not enough heat" is not a flash-code number on Carrier furnaces — it is a symptom, not a fault stored by the control board. The furnace calls for heat, the burners light, and the blower runs, but the thermostat never reaches its setpoint, so the cycle never ends.

On a modulating or two-stage Carrier (Infinity and many Performance models), the furnace may correctly run at a low fire stage for long stretches by design in mild weather — that is efficient behavior, not a fault. The problem is genuine only when the house **loses ground** or never climbs to the setpoint even in cold weather.

Because this is a performance symptom rather than a lockout, there is no code to clear and nothing auto-resets. You diagnose it by finding what is limiting either airflow or heat output.

## Safety first

If a **CO alarm is sounding**, or you smell **gas, burning, or hot metal**:

1. Get everyone — including pets — out of the house and into fresh air immediately.
2. Call **911 or the fire department** from outside or from a neighbor's home.
3. Do not go back inside, do not open panels, and do not try to find or fix the source yourself.
4. Only after the fire department says the house is safe should you schedule an HVAC technician to inspect the furnace. Do not run it again until they have.

Everything else on this page assumes none of those conditions are present.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged or wrong air filter** — the number-one cause. Restricted airflow means warm air barely moves, so rooms never heat and the furnace never satisfies the thermostat.
2. **Thermostat set too high or miscalibrated** — an unreachable setpoint, wrong Heat/Auto mode, or a failing thermostat sensor keeps the call for heat active indefinitely.
3. **Closed, blocked, or too few open registers/returns** — furniture over vents, closed dampers, or a starved return choke the whole system.
4. **Undersized furnace or extreme cold** — on the coldest days an undersized or aging unit simply can't keep up. Common after additions or in poorly insulated homes.
5. **Duct leakage or disconnected ductwork** — heated air dumping into an attic or crawlspace before it reaches rooms.
6. **Furnace stuck in low fire (two-stage/modulating)** — a control, sensor, or staging fault prevents the unit from ramping to high fire when it should.
7. **Partially failed gas valve or restricted gas supply** — reduced burner output means the furnace makes less heat than rated.
8. **Dirty burners or a failing inducer/flame issue** — burners cutting out early or firing weakly reduce heat delivered per cycle.
9. **Cracked or failed heat exchanger** — not really a cause of weak heat, but a serious safety finding a technician may uncover while diagnosing this symptom. It usually announces itself as limit trips, short cycling, flame movement or roll-out when the blower starts, or CO concerns rather than as a gradual loss of heat output. If it is found, the furnace should be shut down until it is repaired or replaced.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do:

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray or you can't see light through it, swap it. This alone fixes most "runs but no heat" complaints. Filter sizes vary by cabinet — read the size printed on the edge of the filter you pull out, or on the filter-rack door, before buying a replacement.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's in **Heat** mode, the setpoint is above room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto** (not On, which pushes cool air between cycles). Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Walk the house and open vents.** Make sure supply registers are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs, and that return grilles aren't covered.
- **Look at the outdoor intake/exhaust (high-efficiency models).** Clear snow, leaves, or nests from the PVC pipes outside.
- **Check the breaker/switch.** Confirm the furnace breaker is on and the service switch by the unit hasn't been bumped off. If the furnace has locked out, you can try **one** reset — if it locks out again, stop and call a technician.
- **Check the condensate line (high-efficiency 59-series models).** A blocked drain trips the pressure/condensate switch, which normally shuts the burners down entirely rather than producing weak heat — so it is an unlikely explanation for a furnace that runs and runs with poor heat. Still worth a look: if you see standing water at the furnace or in the drain pan, that's a sign to call a pro.

If the furnace still won't reach setpoint after a clean filter and open vents, stop here — the rest is technician work.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech won't just quote a part. Expect them to:

- **Measure temperature rise** across the furnace (return vs. supply air) and compare it to the rating-plate spec. Low rise points to a gas/burner problem; high rise points to airflow restriction.
- **Check static pressure** to confirm whether ductwork or filtration is choking airflow.
- **Verify gas pressure** at the valve (inlet and manifold) against Carrier's spec.
- **Confirm staging** on two-stage/modulating units — is it commanding high fire and actually reaching it?
- **Inspect burners, inducer, and flame** for weak or short cycling combustion.
- **Perform a heat exchanger inspection** (visual and often camera/combustion analysis) to rule out cracks — especially if there have been limit trips, flame disturbance when the blower starts, or any CO reading.
- **Do a heat-load sanity check** if the equipment may simply be undersized for the home.

Ask to see the temperature-rise and static-pressure readings — they tell you whether the problem is airflow or heat output, and keep a quote honest.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow from registers, filter gray | Clogged air filter | Replace filter | Check static pressure, blower speed |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor unless noted):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 installed
- **Duct sealing/repair:** $350–$1,500 depending on access and extent
- **Gas valve replacement:** $400–$900 installed
- **Burner cleaning/service:** $150–$400
- **Inducer motor:** $400–$1,200 installed, toward the top of that range (or above it) on Infinity and variable-speed inducer assemblies
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900 for a standard blower; ECM and variable-speed blower jobs on Carrier equipment commonly run **$700–$1,800 installed**
- **Two-stage/modulating staging or control board:** $400–$1,200, with Infinity boards at the upper end
- **Heat exchanger replacement:** $2,000–$3,500+ once labor and refractory/gasket parts are included on 58/59-series cabinets (often makes replacement worth considering on older units)
- **Full furnace replacement (undersized/aged out):** $4,000–$9,000+

Diagnostic/service call fees ($90–$180) usually apply and may be credited toward the repair.
