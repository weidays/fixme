## What this code means

Six flashes on an American Standard integrated furnace control indicates **115 VAC reversed polarity or poor grounding**. The control board expects line voltage (hot) and neutral to be on the correct terminals, and it expects a solid earth ground. If it detects that hot and neutral are swapped, or that grounding is missing or high-resistance, it flashes this code and typically refuses to start the ignition sequence.

This matters because the board relies on the neutral and ground as a reference for **flame rectification** — the tiny DC signal it reads through the flame sensor. Without correct polarity and a good ground, the board cannot reliably sense flame, so it treats the condition as unsafe and locks out.

On most boards this code **clears automatically** once the wiring is corrected and power is cycled at the disconnect or breaker. It will continue flashing and block heat until the underlying electrical fault is repaired. Because the fix lives in house wiring or a junction box, this is **pro-level electrical work**, not a DIY reset.

Legends vary by board revision. Always confirm six flashes against the diagnostic legend printed on the blower-door label or the wiring diagram before assuming polarity/ground is the cause.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Reversed line and neutral wiring** at the furnace junction box, disconnect switch, or the receptacle feeding the furnace — hot and neutral landed on the wrong terminals.
2. **Missing, loose, or high-resistance ground** — the equipment ground wire is disconnected, corroded, or not bonded, so the board can't reference ground.
3. **Miswired outlet or disconnect** (common after a recent electrical change, panel work, or DIY receptacle swap).
4. **Ungrounded outlet or two-prong circuit** that was adapted for the furnace without a proper ground path.
5. **Backfed or floating neutral** upstream — less common, but a poor neutral connection at the panel can trip this code intermittently.
6. **Failed or misreading control board** — only after correct polarity and ground are verified with a meter and the code still appears.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform:

- **Confirm the breaker is on.** Check the furnace circuit at the main panel; reset it once if tripped.
- **Check the furnace switch.** The wall switch that looks like a light switch near the furnace should be ON.
- **Check the thermostat.** Set it to Heat and a temperature a few degrees above room temp; replace thermostat batteries if the display is blank.
- **Seat the blower door.** Make sure the front panel is fully closed — the door switch must be engaged for the furnace to run.
- **Note any recent electrical work.** If an outlet, switch, or panel was changed recently, mention it to your technician — that's a strong clue for reversed polarity.

Do **not** open the control compartment, test wiring, or attempt to rewire the outlet or junction box. Line-voltage polarity and grounding work is for a licensed technician or electrician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

1. Read the flash code and confirm it against the board's legend.
2. Use a multimeter to verify **line voltage between hot and neutral**, then hot-to-ground and neutral-to-ground, confirming correct polarity.
3. Check the **equipment ground** for continuity and low resistance back to the panel.
4. Inspect the furnace **junction box, disconnect, and receptacle** for reversed or loose conductors.
5. If wiring at the furnace is correct, trace back to the **outlet or panel**, identifying a floating neutral or an ungrounded circuit — often calling in an electrician for house-side corrections.
6. Correct the wiring, cycle power, and confirm the code clears and the furnace completes a normal ignition cycle.

If polarity and ground both test correct and the code persists, the technician will suspect a **faulty control board**.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 flashes, no ignition | Reversed hot/neutral wiring | Confirm breaker and switch are on | Test polarity, correct wiring at box/outlet |
| Code appeared after electrical work | Miswired outlet or disconnect | Note the recent work for the tech | Rewire receptacle or disconnect correctly |
| Intermittent 6 flashes | Loose or high-resistance ground | Seat blower door; leave rest | Tighten/repair ground bonding |
| Furnace on two-prong/adapted circuit | Ungrounded circuit | None | Add proper ground path or new circuit |
| Polarity and ground test good, still 6 flashes | Failed control board | None | Verify readings, replace board |

## Repair costs

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90 – $180 typical.
- **Rewiring a reversed receptacle or disconnect:** $120 – $250 depending on access.
- **Repairing or adding a proper ground / bonding:** $150 – $350, more if a new grounded circuit is needed.
- **Electrician for panel-side floating neutral:** $200 – $450+ depending on scope.
- **Control board replacement (only if board is confirmed bad):** $350 – $650 installed.

Many 6-flash calls resolve at the low end — a single miswired outlet corrected in one visit. The high end applies when house wiring or a ground path must be rebuilt.
