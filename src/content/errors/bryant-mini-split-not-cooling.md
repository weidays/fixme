## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a single fault code. It covers a Bryant ductless mini-split that is running but not cooling the room, cooling only weakly, or not starting the outdoor unit at all.

Mini-splits behave differently from the central AC described in our ducted Bryant AC not-cooling page:

- **The indoor unit has its own washable filter and evaporator coil.** These clog quickly.
- **The outdoor unit uses an inverter-driven, variable-speed compressor.** It ramps its speed up and down instead of simply turning on and off.
- **The indoor and outdoor units communicate over a control wire.** A communication fault can stop cooling even when both units have power.

**What the unit does on its own:**

- Many Bryant ductless models protect themselves by delaying the compressor restart for about 3 minutes, so a short wait is normal.
- They can also cut back output for coil freeze protection or high-temperature protection.
- Some protection modes clear automatically once conditions return to normal.
- Hard faults usually stay active and show a blinking LED or display code until the fault is fixed and power is cycled.

Exact behavior depends on the model, so check your indoor unit's display for a code before calling.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Wrong mode or setpoint on the remote.** The unit is in fan, dry, heat or auto mode, or the setpoint is above room temperature. A remote with weak batteries may not send the settings at all.
2. **Dirty indoor filter.** Restricted airflow causes weak cooling and can freeze the coil.
3. **Dirty indoor coil or blower wheel.** These are common in units that have gone years without professional cleaning. Airflow and capacity drop.
4. **Outdoor unit blocked or dirty.** Debris, vegetation or a clogged condenser coil triggers high-pressure or high-temperature protection.
5. **Power problem at the outdoor unit.** A tripped breaker or a switched-off disconnect means the indoor fan runs but no cooling happens.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** Flare connections on the line set are a well-known leak point on ductless systems. Symptoms include weak cooling, ice on the lines, and low-pressure or protection codes.
7. **Failed sensor or communication fault.** This includes a bad thermistor or a problem with the indoor-outdoor communication wiring. It usually shows a code.
8. **Electrical component failure in the outdoor unit.** Examples are the fan motor, inverter or control board, or the expansion valve.
9. **Compressor failure.** This is the least common cause and the most expensive one.
10. **Unit undersized or room heat load too high.** This shows up as weak cooling during heat waves and is a design issue, not a defect.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the remote.** Confirm it is set to cool mode (snowflake icon) with a setpoint a few degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or the unit doesn't beep when you press buttons.
- **Wait out the restart delay.** After any change, give the unit 5–10 minutes to start the compressor.
- **Clean the indoor filters.** Lift the front panel as your owner's manual shows and slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry completely, and reinstall them. Do this monthly in cooling season. Don't go any deeper into the unit than the filter.
- **Check the breaker and disconnect.** Look for a tripped breaker and make sure the outdoor disconnect is on. You can reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a pro.
- **Do one power reset.** Follow the reset steps in the FAQ above, and only do it once.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Keep about 2 feet of clearance and remove leaves and debris from the outside of the unit. Don't open it and don't pressure-wash the coil.
- **Check the airflow path.** Make sure curtains or furniture aren't blocking the indoor unit and that the louvers move.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Water dripping from the indoor unit points to a blocked drain or a frozen coil. Make sure the drain hose outlet outside isn't kinked or buried.
- **Thaw a frozen coil.** If you see ice on the indoor coil or lines, switch to fan-only or turn the unit off for a few hours to thaw. Then clean the filter. If the ice comes back, call a technician.
- **Note any error code.** Write down any blinking LED pattern or display code to give your technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

1. **Read the fault codes.** The tech reads the indoor display or LED code and checks the outdoor board's diagnostic LEDs or display, then looks up the meaning in the model's service manual.
2. **Measure cooling performance.** This includes the temperature difference between return and supply air at the indoor unit and an inspection of the indoor coil and blower wheel.
3. **Check power and communication.** The tech verifies voltage at the outdoor unit and checks the communication signal between the indoor and outdoor units.
4. **Test sensors and motors.** Thermistor resistance is compared against the manual's chart, and the fan motors are checked.
5. **Measure refrigerant condition.** Gauges or probes measure pressures, superheat and subcooling. Inverter units are best tested in the manufacturer's test or forced-cooling mode.
6. **Leak-test if the charge is low.** This uses an electronic detector, bubble solution or a nitrogen pressure test, with special attention to the flare joints.
7. **Repair or replace components.** This includes coil and blower cleaning, the outdoor fan motor, the expansion valve coil, the inverter board or the compressor.

**Red flag:** A tech who wants to "top off" refrigerant without finding and fixing the leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan blows but air isn't cold | Wrong mode, outdoor unit off, or restart delay | Set cool mode, check breaker/disconnect, wait 10 min | Diagnose outdoor power, board or communication fault |
| Weak airflow, ice on coil | Dirty filter or coil | Clean filter, thaw in fan mode | Deep-clean coil and blower wheel; check charge |
| Outdoor unit runs, still weak cooling | Low refrigerant / leak | None beyond filter check | Leak search, repair, evacuate, weigh in charge |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Fan motor or board failure | Clear debris from outside of unit | Test and replace motor or board |
| Blinking LED or error code | Sensor, communication or protection fault | Note the code, do one power reset | Look up code, test the indicated component |
| Water dripping from indoor unit | Blocked drain or frozen coil | Check drain outlet, clean filter | Clear drain line or pan, check for low charge |
| Cools fine except on very hot days | Undersized unit or high heat load | Close blinds, reduce indoor heat sources | Load calculation, recommend added capacity |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Electrical short or compressor fault | Leave breaker off, do not keep resetting | Electrical diagnosis, compressor testing |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, parts plus labor. Prices vary by region and warranty status.

| Item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter cleaning, settings or batteries (DIY) | $0–$10 |
| Service call / diagnostic | $100–$250 |
| Professional deep clean of indoor coil and blower | $150–$400 per head |
| Condensate drain clearing | $100–$250 |
| Thermistor (sensor) replacement | $150–$350 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $300–$700 |
| Leak repair plus recharge | $300–$1,500+, depending on leak location and refrigerant |
| Expansion valve or coil | $300–$800 |
| Indoor or outdoor control/inverter board | $400–$1,200 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500–$3,500; replacing the unit may make more sense out of warranty |

Bryant parts warranties often cover components such as boards and compressors if the unit was registered, but labor usually isn't covered. Ask your dealer to check your warranty status.
