## What this code means

A flashing **Heat On** indicator on a Honeywell programmable thermostat means the **compressor protection delay** is active. Honeywell's manuals describe this the same way across many models: the system will wait about 5 minutes before restarting, so the compressor is not damaged.

The thermostat is calling for heat but is holding the call until the timer runs out. The delay **resets on its own**. Once it finishes, the indicator turns solid and the equipment starts. You do not need to press anything or cycle power.

This delay protects a compressor. That means you will mostly see Heat On flash on **heat pump** systems, where the compressor makes the heat. On a gas or electric furnace set up as conventional heat, Heat On typically does not flash.

Our separate page, Honeywell Thermostat Cool On Flashing, covers the same feature in cooling mode. This page focuses on what is specific to heating:

- heat pump heat mode
- emergency heat
- a thermostat set up as the wrong system type

Some newer models show the delay differently. T-series, T9/T10 and Lyric thermostats display "Wait" or "Waiting for Equipment" instead of a flashing icon.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal compressor protection after the heat pump just shut off.** This is the cause Honeywell documents. If the heat pump stopped within the last few minutes and the thermostat calls for heat again, it holds the call for about 5 minutes.
2. **Power was just restored.** The delay starts after a power outage, a breaker reset, or a battery change. The thermostat can't tell how long the compressor has been off, so it runs the full delay to be safe.
3. **Recent setting or mode changes.** Each of these can start a new call that has to wait out the timer:
   - switching from Cool or Off to Heat
   - changing the setpoint
   - a program period starting
4. **Thermostat set up as a heat pump when the system is a furnace.** If you see the delay on every heating cycle and you have a gas or conventional electric furnace, the installer setup (system type) may be wrong. The setup menu varies by model.
5. **Low batteries or unstable power causing repeated restarts.** On battery-powered models, weak batteries can make the thermostat reset. Each reset can restart the delay, so the flashing seems to go on far longer than 5 minutes. Models powered only by the C-wire can do the same thing if power is interrupted.

If Heat On has stopped flashing (it's solid) but no warm air comes out, the delay is over and the problem is somewhere else. See Honeywell Thermostat Heat Not Working.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Wait 5–10 minutes.** Leave the thermostat alone while you wait. Changing modes or the setpoint can restart the timer.
- **Confirm the mode and setpoint.** The system should be set to **Heat**, not Auto or Em Heat, unless that is what you intend. The setpoint should be a few degrees above the room temperature.
- **Replace the batteries** if your model uses them. Use fresh alkaline AA or AAA cells, then wait the full delay once.
- **Check the breakers** for the indoor unit and the outdoor heat pump. You can reset a tripped breaker **one time**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.
- **Check the furnace or air handler service switch.** It often looks like a light switch near the unit and should be ON.
- **Check the air filter** and replace it if it's dirty. A clogged filter can cause short cycling, which brings back the delay more often.
- **Open the supply registers and return grilles** and make sure nothing is blocking them.
- **Watch whether it only happens after an outage or mode change.** If so, this is normal behavior and needs no repair.
- **Look at the installer setup only to note the system type.** Write down what it says, for example "heat pump" or "conventional," before calling for service. Do not change settings you don't understand.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will:

- **Time the delay.** They confirm it clears after about 5 minutes, which shows the thermostat is working as designed.
- **Check the installer setup.** They confirm the system type matches your equipment (heat pump vs. conventional) and check the reversing valve (O/B) setting and the number of heating stages.
- **Inspect the wiring** at the thermostat and the equipment control board. They look for loose conductors or a missing C-wire that could cause power loss and restarts.
- **Measure 24 V control voltage** with a multimeter to confirm the power is stable.
- **Look for short cycling** of the heat pump if the delay shows up constantly. Possible causes include airflow restrictions, an oversized system, or poor thermostat placement.
- **Replace the thermostat** only if it keeps rebooting or misbehaves when power and wiring check out.

A quote for major heat pump repairs based only on "Heat On flashing" is a red flag. The flashing icon itself does not point to a failed part.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flashes for about 5 min, then turns solid and heat starts | Normal compressor protection | Wait. Don't change settings | None needed |
| Flashes after a power outage or battery change | Delay restarts at power-up | Wait 5–10 min | None needed |
| Flashes every cycle on a gas furnace | System type set to heat pump | Note the setup setting and call | Correct the installer setup |
| Flashing never stops, or the screen flickers or resets | Weak batteries or unstable power | Replace batteries. Check breaker and service switch | Check C-wire and 24 V supply, repair wiring |
| Delay comes back very often | Short cycling | Replace filter, open vents | Diagnose airflow, sizing or placement |
| Solid Heat On but no warm air | Equipment problem, not the delay | Check filter and breaker once | Diagnose the heat pump or furnace |
