## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code — it's the symptom York homeowners describe most often. Your air conditioner may run, blow air, and appear to work, but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

York air conditioners cool by circulating refrigerant between the outdoor condenser and the indoor evaporator coil. If any part of that loop — airflow, refrigerant charge, the compressor, or the controls — is compromised, you lose cooling capacity.

Because York builds several platforms (single-stage Latitude, two-stage LX, and communicating Affinity systems), the *way* a fault shows up varies. Communicating Affinity units may flash an LED code or display a message at the thermostat; basic single-stage units give you no code at all — just warm air. Check your outdoor unit's control board for a blinking LED and note the pattern before calling anyone.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil solid.
2. **Thermostat set wrong or dead batteries** — set to "fan/on" instead of "cool," or a blank display from dead batteries.
3. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the single most common reason a York blows lukewarm air; always caused by a leak, never normal loss.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a cheap part; when it fails the compressor or condenser fan won't start, so no cooling.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — a coil caked with dirt, grass, and cottonwood can't reject heat, so the unit runs but barely cools.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
7. **Condenser fan motor failure** — if the outdoor fan won't spin, the compressor overheats and cuts out.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown contactor** — the outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive; the unit hums or trips but produces no cooling.
10. **Control board or communication fault (Affinity models)** — a failed board or lost communication between components.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system or bypass any safety switch.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of poor cooling and frozen coils.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **AUTO**. Replace the batteries if the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated trips signal an electrical fault.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for a spinning fan on top. Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris away from the coil fins (power off first). Gently rinse the outside of the coil with a garden hose from top to bottom.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate:** If your indoor unit has a float switch and the drain is clogged, the system may shut down. Check for a full drain pan or standing water and clear a visible clog.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** but leave the fan on **ON** to thaw it (can take a few hours). Then replace the filter. If it refreezes, you need a tech.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the process helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is ~15-20°F).
2. **Check airflow** — inspects filter, blower speed, and coil for ice or restriction.
3. **Electrical checks** — tests the capacitor with a meter, checks the contactor, and confirms voltage to the condenser and compressor.
4. **Refrigerant pressures** — connects gauges to read suction and liquid pressures and calculates superheat/subcooling. Low charge points to a leak.
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, uses electronic detector or dye to find the leak. **A reputable tech will not just "top off" refrigerant without finding the leak.**
6. **Compressor test** — checks amp draw and windings if the compressor won't start or trips.
7. **Board/comms diagnosis** — on Affinity communicating systems, reads fault history and checks the data bus between components.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check blower & duct static |
| Blank thermostat | Dead batteries | Replace batteries | Replace thermostat/wiring |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker/contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, find fault |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed capacitor or fan motor | None (electrical) | Test & replace capacitor/motor |
| Lukewarm air, unit runs constantly | Low refrigerant (leak) | None (sealed system) | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Ice on lines/coil | Low airflow or low charge | Thaw with fan on, new filter | Diagnose charge/airflow |
| Unit hums then trips | Failed compressor or capacitor | Shut off, don't reset | Test compressor, quote replacement |
| Dirty outdoor coil, poor cooling | Clogged condenser | Rinse coil (power off) | Professional coil cleaning |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor), varying by region and unit age:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Professional coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,600 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type; R-410A vs. older R-22 raises cost sharply)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often worth comparing against full system replacement on older units)
- **Control/communication board (Affinity):** $400–$900

If your York is 12+ years old and facing a compressor or coil replacement, ask for a repair-vs-replace comparison before committing.
