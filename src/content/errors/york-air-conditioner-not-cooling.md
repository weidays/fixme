## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on York equipment — it's a symptom. Most residential York air conditioners (LX, Affinity, Latitude series) don't display alphanumeric error codes at the thermostat the way a furnace board flashes LEDs. Instead, "not cooling" describes any situation where the system runs but the house never reaches the setpoint, or the air from the vents isn't cold.

The underlying problem falls into one of a few buckets: airflow restriction, a refrigerant charge issue, an electrical component failure, or a control/thermostat problem. On newer variable-speed and communicating York systems, the indoor or outdoor control board may log a fault that a technician can read with a service tool — but that's not visible to you as a homeowner.

Because refrigerant, electrical, and compressor work are involved, this is rated **pro severity**: you can safely check a handful of things, but the actual repair usually needs a licensed HVAC technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the evaporator coil, and kills cooling capacity. The single most common cause.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, cottonwood, and dust block heat rejection so the system can't cool effectively.
3. **Thermostat set wrong or dead batteries** — mode set to "fan" instead of "cool," or a dead battery leaving the AC off.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure that stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting; often causes humming or a warm-but-running outdoor unit.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling and can cause coil icing. Always a leak — refrigerant is not "used up."
6. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit may be off while the indoor blower still runs.
7. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit fails to pull in.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — the result of any of the airflow or refrigerant problems above.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome, usually the end result of long-neglected problems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything feels beyond this list.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A dirty filter causes more no-cool calls than anything else.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it has them.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler/furnace and the outdoor unit. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed registers aren't choking airflow. Return vents especially must be unblocked.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear grass, leaves, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance on all sides. You can gently rinse the coil fins from the outside with a garden hose (power off first).
- **Condensate:** Check for standing water near the indoor unit or a full drain pan — a clogged drain can trip a safety switch and stop cooling on some systems.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or coil, **turn the system to OFF and the fan to ON** to let it thaw, then call a technician. Running a frozen system damages the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York tech should work through a logical sequence — this helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply and return air temperature (a healthy split is about 16–22°F).
2. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower operation, and coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Measure electrical values** — test the capacitor's microfarad rating, check the contactor, and confirm proper voltage at the outdoor unit.
4. **Read control faults** — on communicating/variable-speed York systems, pull any logged codes from the board with a service tool.
5. **Attach refrigerant gauges** — check pressures and superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct.
6. **Leak search** — if refrigerant is low, they must find the leak (electronic detector or dye) rather than just topping it off.
7. **Inspect the compressor** — check windings and amp draw if a compressor failure is suspected.

Be wary of any tech who adds refrigerant without checking for a leak, or who quotes a compressor replacement without measuring electrical components first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and duct |
| Outdoor unit hums but fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — pro repair | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on refrigerant lines or coil | Low refrigerant or airflow restriction | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Indoor blower runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker, contactor, or fuse | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, fuse, wiring |
| House never reaches setpoint on hot days | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Clean/rinse outdoor coil | Verify charge and coil condition |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries or control fault | Replace batteries | Test control wiring and board |
| Loud running, then shutdown, warm air | Failing compressor | None — pro repair | Test windings, amp draw |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Actual price varies by region, unit age, and refrigerant type (older R-22 systems cost far more to service).

- **Air filter:** $10 – $40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75 – $200 professionally
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600 – $1,600 (higher for R-22 systems)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900 – $2,000+
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300 – $2,200+ (often the point where replacing the whole unit makes more sense)

If your York system is older than 12–15 years and facing a compressor or coil, get a quote on full replacement before repairing.

## Related codes

- **York AC frozen coil / iced lines** — an airflow or refrigerant symptom that overlaps heavily with no-cool.
- **York outdoor fan not running** — often a capacitor or motor issue causing poor or no cooling.
- **York thermostat not responding** — control-side cause of a no-cool complaint.
- **York AC tripping breaker** — electrical fault that can present as intermittent no-cool. Do not repeatedly reset.
