## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific fault code — most York air conditioners don't display an alphanumeric error for it the way a furnace flashes codes. Your outdoor unit or air handler either runs without lowering the temperature, or won't run at all while the thermostat calls for cooling.

Because York builds several platforms (LX, Affinity, Latitude) with different control boards and single- or two-stage compressors, the internal diagnostics vary. Some communicating Affinity systems store fault data readable at the thermostat or control board; most standard models give you no code at all. Where your specific board reports something, treat this page as the general framework and confirm against your unit's wiring diagram and installer manual.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the most common cause. Restricted airflow drops capacity and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so nothing cools.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common electrical failure; the compressor or fan hums but won't start.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling and often freezes the coil. Requires a licensed tech.
5. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — caked with grass, dust or debris, it can't reject heat.
6. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a wiring/setting fault.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler has lost power.
8. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the compressor doesn't close.
9. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive cause; unit runs but produces little or no cooling.
10. **Clogged condensate drain / float switch trip** — a full drain can shut the system down on some setups.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for a homeowner. Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting off.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the temperature is set below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and check it against a light. If it's gray and clogged, replace it — this alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler or outdoor unit. Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings and debris away from the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system OFF (fan can stay ON) and let it fully thaw for a few hours before restarting.
- **Condensate drain:** If there's standing water at the indoor unit, the drain may be clogged; a wet/dry vac on the outdoor drain line can clear a simple clog.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York tech follows a logical sequence — knowing it helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirms the complaint** and checks thermostat settings and the call for cooling.
2. **Verifies airflow** — inspects filter, blower operation and coil condition.
3. **Measures electrical** — checks incoming voltage, tests the capacitor with a meter, and confirms the contactor pulls in.
4. **Reads system pressures** — connects gauges to measure suction and liquid pressures, checking for low refrigerant (a sign of a leak).
5. **Checks superheat/subcooling** to judge charge and system health accurately.
6. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — using electronic detectors or dye rather than just topping off.
7. **Tests the compressor** — amp draw and windings if cooling is weak but the unit runs.

Be cautious of any tech who adds refrigerant without finding the leak, or quotes a compressor without measuring electricals first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air handler runs, air isn't cold | Dirty filter / frozen coil | Replace filter; thaw coil with system off | Check airflow, charge, coil |
| Ice on refrigerant lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Thaw fully; replace filter | Find leak, recharge, verify charge |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None (capacitor holds charge) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Nothing runs on cooling call | Tripped breaker / dead thermostat | Reset breaker once; new batteries | Diagnose power, contactor, board |
| Weak cooling, high energy bills | Dirty condenser or low charge | Rinse condenser exterior gently | Measure pressures, clean coil |
| Water pooling indoors | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain with wet/dry vac | Clear line, check float switch |
| Runs constantly, little cooling | Failing compressor / low charge | None | Amp-test compressor, evaluate replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Regional rates and system size affect totals.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries or replacement:** $0–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Blower motor:** $400–$700
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ — often worth comparing against a full system replacement on older units

Note: R-22 systems cost far more to recharge than newer R-410A units, which can push repair-vs-replace decisions.
