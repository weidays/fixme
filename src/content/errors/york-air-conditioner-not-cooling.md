## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific fault code — it's a symptom. Your York air conditioner may be running (the fan spins, the thermostat calls for cooling) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the set temperature. On communicating York systems (like some Affinity models), the control board or thermostat may display an actual fault or status message; on standard contactor-based units there's no code at all, just warm air.

Because "not cooling" covers everything from a $15 filter to a failed compressor, the goal here is to help you narrow it down and sanity-check any repair quote you're given.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops efficiency, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat issue** — wrong mode (heat vs. cool), dead batteries, or a bad setting.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser or air handler has lost power.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system can't absorb heat; often shows warm air plus ice on the lines.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or fan motor won't start.
6. **Bad contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the condenser sticks or burns out.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat exchange.
8. **Condenser fan motor failure** — the outdoor unit overheats and shuts down.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive cause; the heart of the system won't run.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do not go beyond them.

- **Air filter:** Pull it out and check it against a light. If it's gray or clogged, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the temperature is set below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel. York AC systems usually have two circuits — one for the indoor air handler and one for the outdoor condenser. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain:** If your air handler has a float switch and the drain is clogged, it may shut the system off. Look for standing water in the drain pan and clear the visible drain line if you can safely reach it.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or coil, turn the system to **OFF** (fan can stay ON) and let it thaw for a few hours before calling. Running a frozen system can damage the compressor.

If the filter, thermostat, and breaker are all fine and it still won't cool, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York technician will work through a logical sequence. Knowing it helps you spot a rushed or padded quote:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** at the thermostat and check line voltage at the condenser.
2. **Measure capacitor microfarads** with a meter — a weak capacitor reads below its rating.
3. **Inspect the contactor** for pitting or burned contacts.
4. **Check refrigerant pressures** with gauges (high and low side) to determine if the charge is correct.
5. **Measure superheat and subcooling** to confirm a leak or a metering/airflow problem.
6. **Test the compressor and fan motor** for proper amp draw and windings.
7. **Inspect the coils** (indoor and outdoor) for dirt, ice, and airflow restriction.

Ask the tech to show you the readings. A quote for a compressor or refrigerant leak should always come with pressure and electrical measurements, not just a guess.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, filter is gray | Clogged filter | Replace the filter | — |
| No air at all, thermostat blank | Dead thermostat batteries / power | Replace batteries, check breaker | Wiring or control board check |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor fan runs | Failed capacitor or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test & replace capacitor/contactor |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find leak, recharge, restore airflow |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Fan motor or capacitor | Confirm power is on | Replace fan motor/capacitor |
| Running constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant / undersized charge | Check filter and vents | Leak search and recharge |
| Humming then shutting off | Compressor or hard-start failure | Reset once, then stop | Compressor / hard-start diagnosis |
| Water on floor, system off | Clogged condensate drain / float switch | Clear visible drain line | Clear line, verify float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10 – $40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run capacitor:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300 – $650
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75 – $250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $200 – $1,500 (depends on leak location)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $650 – $2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200 – $2,200+ (often worth comparing to a full system replacement)

A service call/diagnostic fee of **$75 – $200** is typical and is often applied toward the repair. If your York unit is more than 12–15 years old and needs a compressor or coil, ask the tech to compare repair cost against replacement.

## Related codes

- **York AC frozen coil / iced lines** — often the same root causes as "not cooling."
- **York AC not turning on** — no power, breaker, or control-board issues.
- **York AC short cycling** — turning on and off rapidly without cooling.
- **York communicating fault codes** — on Affinity/communicating systems, check the thermostat or control board for a specific status message that may pinpoint the fault.
