## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on most York units — it's the symptom you notice when something in the cooling cycle has failed. Your York air conditioner may run, blow air, or hum, but the air coming from your vents is warm, lukewarm, or not much cooler than room temperature.

York central air conditioners rely on a full sequence working together: the thermostat calls for cooling, the outdoor condenser and compressor start, refrigerant moves heat outside, and the indoor blower pushes cooled air through your ducts. If any link in that chain breaks — airflow, electrical, or refrigerant — you get warm air.

Because York builds many series (LX, Affinity, Latitude, and older platforms), the exact indicator varies. Some newer models with communicating controls will store a fault; most basic single-stage units give you nothing but warm air and a bill for a service call. This page covers the general causes and what a pro should check.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — a clogged filter starves airflow, freezes the indoor coil, and kills cooling. The single most common cause.
2. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil packed with grass, dust, or cottonwood can't reject heat, so cooling drops off.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit loses power while the indoor blower keeps running, giving you warm air.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a bulged or dead capacitor stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced charge means less heat is moved. This is a repair, not a "top-off."
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; ice blocks the coil and cooling stops entirely.
7. **Faulty contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit fails to close.
8. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a wiring fault means the unit never gets a proper cooling call.
9. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; the heart of the system won't pump refrigerant.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass safety switches, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting off.

- **Air filter** — pull it. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. Run the system 30 minutes and recheck.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. If it's battery-powered, install fresh batteries.
- **Breaker** — check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker labeled AC, condenser, or air handler. Flip it fully OFF, then back ON — **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents** — make sure furniture, rugs, or closed registers aren't blocking airflow in rooms.
- **Condensate line** — a clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Check for standing water near the indoor unit; a wet-dry vacuum on the drain line outlet can clear a simple clog.
- **Outdoor unit** — clear grass clippings, leaves, and debris away from the condenser fins. Do not spray or disassemble it.
- **Ice** — if you see ice on the refrigerant lines or coil, turn the system to **OFF** but leave the fan **ON** to thaw it, then replace the filter. Persistent icing needs a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified York tech should follow a logical path so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the thermostat call** and confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor unit.
2. **Check airflow** — filter, blower operation, and evaporator coil condition.
3. **Test electrical components** — capacitor microfarad reading, contactor operation, and voltage at the compressor.
4. **Measure refrigerant pressures** with gauges (suction and liquid) and check superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct.
5. **Inspect for leaks** if the charge is low, using an electronic detector or dye — a proper leak search, not a "recharge and hope."
6. **Test the compressor** for windings and amp draw if it won't start.

Be wary of any tech who wants to "just add refrigerant" without finding a leak. A sealed system doesn't consume refrigerant — if it's low, there's a leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, indoor fan runs | Dirty filter or frozen coil | Replace filter, thaw coil | Correct airflow, check charge |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor fan runs | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, test power |
| Humming outdoor unit, fan won't spin | Failed capacitor | None — call a pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on lines/coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Find cause, repair leak |
| Weak cooling, high energy bills | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris, gently rinse | Deep coil cleaning, charge check |
| No cooling at all, thermostat blank | Dead batteries or thermostat fault | Replace batteries | Diagnose/replace thermostat |
| Loud, then shuts off; won't restart | Failing compressor | Do not keep resetting | Compressor test/replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Actual prices vary by region, unit age, and refrigerant type (older R-22 units cost far more to service).

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ (higher for R-22 systems)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,800+ — often the point where replacing the whole unit makes more sense on older systems.
- **New thermostat:** $150–$400 installed

If your York unit is 12–15+ years old and facing a compressor or major leak repair, ask your contractor for a replacement quote to compare.

## Related codes

- **York AC not turning on** — no power, blown fuse, or control board issues.
- **York AC frozen coil / icing** — airflow and refrigerant charge problems.
- **York AC short cycling** — thermostat, capacitor, or oversized/overcharged system.
- **York AC blowing warm air on heat pump models** — reversing valve or defrost control faults.
- **York outdoor fan not running** — capacitor or fan motor failure.
