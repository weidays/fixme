## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's a symptom. Your York air conditioner is powered and often running, but the air coming from your vents is warm, lukewarm, or barely cool. On variable-speed and communicating York systems (like the Affinity YXV or YXT series), the control board or thermostat may log a related fault such as a low-pressure lockout, high head pressure, or a compressor fault. On older single-stage York units (LX, YCG package units), there's no diagnostic display — you're working from symptoms alone.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, this page ranks the likely causes and shows you which checks are safe to do yourself versus what needs a licensed HVAC technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection, so the unit runs but can't cool.
3. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a miswired/failed thermostat.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit is dead while the indoor blower still runs.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the single most common "runs but warm" cause; requires a pro to find and repair.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums but won't start.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; blocks cooling entirely.
8. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor unit gets no signal to energize.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening a sealed panel, touching refrigerant lines, or working near a charged capacitor.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 preventable cause.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (disconnect) breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated tripping signals a real fault.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around and on top of the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain:** If your indoor unit has a full drain pan or a tripped float switch, clear the drain line. A blocked drain can shut cooling down on many systems.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant line or indoor coil, turn the thermostat to **OFF** but run the **FAN** for a few hours to thaw it, then replace the filter before restarting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech will work systematically — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply and return air temperature (a healthy split should be a 16–22°F drop).
2. **Check airflow** — inspect the filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Inspect electrical** — test the contactor, capacitor, and disconnect for proper voltage and continuity.
4. **Read the board/thermostat** — on communicating York units, pull stored fault codes for pressure or compressor faults.
5. **Attach gauges** — measure suction and head pressure, then calculate superheat/subcooling to judge refrigerant charge.
6. **Leak search** — if the charge is low, use electronic detectors or dye to locate the leak rather than just topping off.
7. **Test the compressor** — check amp draw and windings if pressures and electrical point to compressor failure.

A good tech finds and fixes the *root cause*. Be wary of anyone who wants to just "add a pound of Freon" without searching for the leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air handler runs, vents blow warm | Low refrigerant / bad compressor | Check filter & outdoor coil | Leak search, recharge, or compressor test |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor runs | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, diagnose electrical |
| Outdoor fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — pro only | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on coil or refrigerant line | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, run fan to thaw, swap filter | Find airflow or refrigerant cause |
| Weak airflow, some cooling | Dirty filter or blocked vents | Replace filter, open vents | Clean coil, check blower motor |
| Water pooling, unit shuts off | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain line | Clear line, test float switch |
| Warm air, dirty outdoor unit | Blocked condenser coil | Rinse debris off exterior | Deep coil cleaning, pressure check |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Your quote varies by region, unit size, and refrigerant type (R-410A vs older R-22 is far pricier).

- **Filter replacement:** $10–$50 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$15 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0–$150
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location and refrigerant)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000+
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often near the cost of a new condenser)

If the compressor has failed on an older unit, ask about replacing the whole condenser or system rather than the compressor alone — it's frequently the better value.

## Related codes

- **York low-pressure lockout** — often the same underlying low-refrigerant cause
- **York high head pressure / high-pressure fault** — dirty condenser or overcharge
- **York frozen evaporator coil** — airflow or charge problem
- **York AC not turning on** — no power, contactor, or thermostat signal
- **York AC short cycling** — related capacity and control faults
