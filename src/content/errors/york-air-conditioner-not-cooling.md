## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single York fault code — it's a symptom. Your York air conditioner may be running (fan spinning, thermostat calling for cool) but the air coming from the vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches setpoint. On communicating York systems (Affinity/Acclimate with a color touchscreen thermostat), you may see an actual fault or status code on the display; on older non-communicating units, there's no code at all — just warm air.

Because York uses several different control boards across its LX, YXV, YCG and YCE lineups, the exact way a "no cool" condition is reported **varies by model**. Some boards flash an LED error at the outdoor unit; communicating systems show text alerts. When behavior differs, we say so below rather than guess your specific board.

The root causes fall into a few buckets: airflow problems, refrigerant issues, electrical component failures, and control faults. Most homeowners can safely rule out the easy stuff, but refrigerant and compressor work is professional territory.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, causes weak or warm air and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, dust and cottonwood block heat rejection; the unit runs but can't cool.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs. "auto"), or a miswired/failed stat.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit isn't getting power.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor and/or fan motor can't start; you may hear humming.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, ice on the lineset, longer run times.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; blows warm and drips water.
9. **Failing compressor** — hums, trips the breaker, or won't start (often after a capacitor fails).
10. **Control board or sensor fault** — less common; diagnosed by a tech reading the board.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" complaints.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to **Cool**, fan to **Auto**, and lower the setpoint 3–5°F below room temp. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check the panel for a tripped AC breaker (both indoor air handler and outdoor condenser circuits). Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop — call a pro.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers are open and unblocked and return grilles aren't covered by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for a filthy coil, leaves, or tall grass choking the fins. Turn the unit off at the disconnect and gently rinse the coil with a garden hose (no pressure washer).
- **Condensate drain:** If the indoor unit's drain pan is full or overflowing, a clogged drain may have tripped a safety float switch. Clear standing water if you can reach it.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting down.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work in a logical order — this is what a fair quote looks like:

1. **Confirm the call for cooling** at the thermostat and check 24V control voltage reaching the outdoor unit.
2. **Check power and electrical components** — line voltage, contactor, and capacitor (with a meter/capacitance tester).
3. **Inspect airflow** — filter, blower operation, evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
4. **Read the control board** — on communicating York systems, pull stored fault codes; on others, read LED flash patterns.
5. **Connect refrigerant gauges** and take temperature readings (superheat/subcooling) to judge the charge and find leaks.
6. **Test the compressor** — windings, amp draw, and start behavior.

Ask to see the readings. A tech who jumps straight to "you need refrigerant" without checking for a leak, or "you need a new compressor" without testing the capacitor first, is cutting corners.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm airflow | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Check blower & coil if unresolved |
| Outdoor fan runs, air not cold | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse coil with hose | Deep coil clean, check charge |
| Nothing turns on | Tripped breaker / blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Find cause of trip, replace fuse |
| Humming, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Test & replace capacitor |
| Ice on lineset/coil | Low refrigerant or low airflow | Turn off, replace filter, thaw | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Long runtimes, high bills | Low charge / undersized | Check filter & coil | Superheat/subcooling test |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Failing compressor / short | Stop resetting | Compressor & electrical test |
| Warm air, no display response | Thermostat / board fault | Replace batteries | Read board codes, test 24V |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Blower motor:** $400–$700
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often makes replacing the whole condenser worth considering on older units)
- **Control board:** $400–$900

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older R-22 is far pricier), and whether your system is still under York's parts warranty — check your registration, as many York units carry a 10-year parts warranty on the original owner.

## Related codes

- **York AC not turning on** — no power, tripped breaker, or failed contactor.
- **York AC frozen coil / icing** — low airflow or low refrigerant.
- **York AC short cycling** — capacitor, thermostat, or refrigerant issues.
- **York communicating system fault codes** — displayed on Affinity/Acclimate touchscreen thermostats; codes are model-specific, so match yours to your control board's service documentation.
