## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on most York air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your York unit may run, blow air, and show no error at all, yet the air coming from the vents is warm or only mildly cool. On some York models with communicating controls or a diagnostic board, you may see fault indications, but many standard LX and Latitude units give you no code, only the lack of cold air.

This page covers the full range of reasons a York AC stops cooling properly, from simple airflow blocks you can fix yourself to sealed-system problems that legally require a licensed HVAC technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode/setting, dead batteries, or a failed thermostat not calling for cooling.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, dust, and debris insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — if the outdoor unit has no power, only the indoor blower runs, giving warm air.
5. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; the coil ices over and cooling stops.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — a common failure that stops the compressor or fan motor from starting.
7. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit can pit or stick, cutting power to the compressor.
8. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system undercharges and can't absorb heat. Requires leak repair, not just a top-off.
9. **Failing or seized compressor** — the most expensive cause; the "heart" of the system stops pumping.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, remove electrical panels on the outdoor unit, or bypass any safety switch.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the fan to **AUTO**, and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel. Both the indoor air handler/furnace circuit and the outdoor AC circuit should be ON. If a breaker is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated resets can be dangerous.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs. Closing too many rooms starves the system of airflow.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the outside of the coil fins with a garden hose (no pressure washer) with the unit powered off at the breaker.
- **Condensate:** If cooling stopped and you see water near the indoor unit, a full drain pan or a tripped float switch may have shut the system off. Emptying a full pan is fine; clearing a clogged drain line is a pro job on many setups.
- **Ice:** If you see frost or ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, **turn the system off** and let it thaw (this can take several hours) before troubleshooting further. Never chip at ice.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed York-familiar technician will typically:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** — confirm the thermostat is actually sending 24V to the outdoor unit.
2. **Check power and controls** — test voltage at the contactor, inspect the capacitor with a meter, and look for a tripped high-pressure or float switch.
3. **Measure airflow** — inspect the filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or icing.
4. **Attach refrigerant gauges** — read suction and head pressures and compare to York's charging chart for your model and outdoor temperature.
5. **Check superheat/subcooling** — this tells them whether the charge is correct, low (leak), or overcharged.
6. **Leak search** — if refrigerant is low, they'll use electronic sniffers or dye to find the leak rather than just adding gas.
7. **Test the compressor** — check amp draw and windings if pressures suggest the compressor isn't pumping.

A good quote should identify a specific failed component, not just "it needs freon." Low refrigerant always means a leak somewhere.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, bad contactor or capacitor | Reset breaker once; check thermostat mode | Test/replace contactor or capacitor |
| Weak airflow, mild cooling | Dirty filter or blower issue | Replace filter | Clean blower, check motor |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, let thaw, replace filter | Diagnose charge/airflow, repair leak |
| Outdoor fan spins, no cold air | Failed capacitor or compressor | None — call a pro | Test capacitor, compressor amp draw |
| Cools then quits, water near unit | Clogged condensate drain / float switch | Empty full pan | Clear drain line, test float switch |
| Runs constantly, never reaches setpoint | Low refrigerant, dirty condenser | Rinse outdoor coil, replace filter | Leak search, recharge, coil cleaning |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Weak capacitor or seizing compressor | None — call a pro | Replace capacitor or compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Fan motor (condenser):** $400–$700
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500+
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to full-system replacement on older units)

If your York system is over 12–15 years old and needs a compressor or coil, ask your technician whether replacement makes more financial sense than repair.

- **York AC short cycling** — turns on and off rapidly without cooling.
- **York AC outdoor fan not spinning** — capacitor or motor fault.
- **York thermostat not calling for cool** — control and wiring issues upstream of the AC.
