## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific York fault code — it is the symptom you notice: the air conditioner runs, but your home stays warm or the air from the vents isn't cold. Some York models with an outdoor or indoor control board flash diagnostic LEDs for specific faults (high-pressure trip, low-pressure lockout, contactor issues). Many older or basic York units have no fault display at all, so you diagnose by observation.

Because the exact indicator LEDs and lockout behavior **vary by model and board revision**, check the label inside your unit's access panel and the wiring diagram for your specific serial number rather than assuming a code meaning.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), or a miswired/failed stat.
3. **Failed run capacitor** — a swollen or dead capacitor stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting; the unit hums or the fan won't spin.
4. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil caked with dirt, grass or cottonwood can't reject heat, killing cooling capacity.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak or no cooling and often a frozen coil or hissing. Requires a licensed tech.
6. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so the condenser never energizes.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit gets no power at all.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from airflow or refrigerant issues; ice blocks cooling entirely.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive; the compressor won't start or has failed internally.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do — no panels that expose live wiring or refrigerant lines:

- **Filter:** Pull the air filter. If it's gray or clogged, replace it and give the system an hour to recover.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to COOL and several degrees below room temperature. Swap in fresh batteries. Make sure the fan is on AUTO, not ON (ON blows warm air between cooling cycles).
- **Breaker:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker on both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) circuits. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Open all supply and return vents. Closing too many can freeze the coil.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for grass clippings, leaves or debris smothering the outdoor coil. Gently rinse the fins with a garden hose from outside only, power off — never poke inside or remove panels.
- **Condensate:** If you have a float switch and a full drain pan, a clogged condensate line can shut the system down. Clear a visible clog and use drain tablets.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw fully before running again.

If none of these restore cooling, it's a pro job.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. Confirm the thermostat is calling for cooling and 24V is reaching the equipment.
2. Read any York board fault LEDs (on models that have them) and check the sequence of operation.
3. Measure voltage at the contactor and test the run/start capacitor with a meter.
4. Check that the compressor and condenser fan energize and draw normal amperage.
5. Attach gauges to read refrigerant pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling to spot a leak or overcharge.
6. Inspect the coils and TXV/metering device, and look for airflow restrictions.
7. Leak-search with an electronic detector or dye if refrigerant is low.

Sanity check: a good quote names the *specific* failed part (capacitor, contactor, leak location) and shows readings — not just "needs a recharge."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan runs, air not cold | Dirty filter / low refrigerant | Replace filter, wait 1 hr | Check charge, find leak |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor |
| Unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Test & replace capacitor |
| Ice on lines/coil | Airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, new filter | Diagnose charge/airflow |
| Warm air between cycles | Fan set to ON | Set fan to AUTO | N/A |
| Weak airflow, poor cooling | Dirty coil / blower issue | Rinse outdoor coil, hose off | Clean coils, check blower |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Short / failing compressor | Stop resetting | Electrical & compressor test |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$300
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location
- **TXV / metering device:** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$3,000+ (often worth weighing against a new system if the unit is aged or out of warranty)

York compressors and coils may still be under a parts warranty if the system was registered — have your serial number ready and ask.
