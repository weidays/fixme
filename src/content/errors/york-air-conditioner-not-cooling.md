## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom rather than a specific York fault code — your air conditioner is powered on and may even be running, but it isn't producing cold air or maintaining the thermostat setpoint. On many York units the indoor blower and outdoor fan can run while the compressor is not, which still delivers airflow but no cooling.

Some newer York systems with communicating controls (Affinity, Acclimate) will display fault text or blink codes at the control board or thermostat. Older LX and Latitude units generally give no code at all — you simply notice warm air. Because the display and diagnostic behavior **varies significantly by model year and control type**, use the physical symptoms below rather than expecting one universal code.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow can cause the coil to freeze, cutting cooling entirely.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setpoint, or a wiring/calibration fault.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — a coil clogged with grass, dust, or debris can't reject heat, so the unit runs but barely cools.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common electrical failure. The fan may spin but the compressor won't start (often with a humming sound).
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak or no cooling and often a frozen indoor coil. This is a repair, never a simple "top-off."
7. **Faulty contactor** — the relay that sends power to the compressor and fan is pitted or stuck.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — result of low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause, usually diagnosed after everything else is ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. If none resolve the issue, call a pro.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL, fan on AUTO, and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the leading cause of no cooling.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor AC breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris away from the condenser. Gently rinse the outside fins with a garden hose (power off first). Confirm the fan is spinning when the unit calls for cooling.
- **Frozen coil / condensate:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or refrigerant lines, turn the system to OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw fully — often several hours — before restarting. Check that the condensate drain isn't clogged or overflowing.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work through a logical sequence so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** at the thermostat and confirm 24V control voltage reaching the outdoor unit.
2. **Check line voltage and the contactor** — measuring for power in and out, and inspecting for pitted contacts.
3. **Test the capacitor** with a meter against its rated microfarad value.
4. **Inspect airflow and coils** — filter, blower operation, and both coils for dirt or ice.
5. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and measure superheat/subcooling to determine whether the charge is correct. Low readings point to a leak, which should be located (dye or electronic detector) — not just refilled.
6. **Test the compressor** for proper amp draw, windings, and start behavior if all else checks out.

A trustworthy quote names the failed part and the reason. Be cautious of any tech who wants to "add a pound of Freon" without looking for a leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, outdoor fan running | Low refrigerant or bad compressor | Check filter and coil | Gauge system, find leak, test compressor |
| Indoor coil/lines iced over | Low airflow or low charge | Set to OFF/fan, thaw, replace filter | Diagnose charge/airflow, repair leak |
| Outdoor unit humming, not starting | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Nothing runs outside | Tripped breaker, bad contactor/fuse | Reset breaker once | Replace fuse/contactor, find fault |
| Weak cooling, hot outdoor unit | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse fins with hose | Deep coil cleaning, verify charge |
| Thermostat blank | Dead batteries or wiring | Replace batteries | Trace 24V/control fault |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor; regional and unit variations apply:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Low-voltage fuse:** $75–$150
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often worth comparing against a new system if the unit is older or out of warranty)

Ask whether your York unit is still under its parts warranty — many carry a 10-year limited parts warranty if registered, which can significantly reduce compressor or coil costs (you'd still pay labor).

## Related codes

- **York AC freezing up / iced coil** — closely tied to airflow and refrigerant issues covered above.
- **York AC short cycling** — turns on and off rapidly; often capacitor, thermostat, or charge related.
- **York AC not turning on** — no power or control-signal issues.
- **York communicating fault / blink codes** — on Affinity and Acclimate boards; consult your model's specific service literature since indicators vary.
