## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific fault code — it's the symptom York homeowners report when the system runs but the house won't reach the setpoint, or when the air from the vents isn't cold. Some York units with communicating controls (Affinity series) may display a numeric fault on the thermostat or control board, but most standard LX and Latitude units give no code at all — you just feel warm air.

Because there's no single code, the cause could be anything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor. This page walks you through ruling out the cheap, safe stuff first, then explains what a technician should check so you can sanity-check any quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection.
3. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan won't start; you may hear humming or clicking.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — weak or warm air, ice on the copper lines, longer run times.
5. **Faulty contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or burns out.
6. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a wiring/calibration fault.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit gets no power.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — usually a downstream effect of low airflow or low refrigerant.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — unit runs hot and shuts down on high pressure.
10. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; the unit won't cool at all despite power.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Everything else involves electrical, refrigerant, or pressure hazards.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the #1 cause and costs a few dollars.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered — a fading thermostat can lose the cooling signal.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel and the outdoor disconnect. If a breaker is tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated resets can be dangerous.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for tall grass, leaves, or debris packed against the condenser fins. Clear away anything within about two feet.
- **Condensate:** Check for water backing up around the indoor unit. A full or clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Cleaning tablets and clearing the drain line are homeowner-safe.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system OFF (leave the fan running) and let it thaw fully before restarting. Don't chip at the ice.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York technician follows a logical sequence — knowing it helps you spot a padded quote:

1. **Confirms the complaint** and checks thermostat settings and the temperature split across the indoor coil (should be roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Inspects airflow** — filter, blower, and coil condition.
3. **Checks voltage and amperage** at the contactor and measures the run capacitor with a meter (a weak capacitor reads below its rated microfarads).
4. **Tests the contactor and fan motor** for pull-in and proper operation.
5. **Connects refrigerant gauges** (certified only) to read pressures and superheat/subcooling — this reveals a low charge, a leak, or a metering-device issue.
6. **Performs a leak search** with an electronic detector or dye if the charge is low.
7. **Checks the compressor** windings and amp draw if all else passes.

Ask for the measured readings — capacitor microfarads, temperature split, and pressures. A legitimate diagnosis produces numbers, not guesses.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Clean coil/blower if filter isn't it |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Failed run capacitor | None (electrical) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Humming outdoor unit, won't start | Bad capacitor or contactor | None | Replace capacitor/contactor |
| Ice on copper lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find/seal leak, recharge |
| Outdoor unit totally dead | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Long run times, mild cooling | Dirty condenser or low charge | Clear debris around unit | Clean coil, check charge |
| Water pooling indoors, no cooling | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear drain, use tablets | Clear line, verify float switch |
| Loud buzz then shutdown | Failing compressor | Shut off | Test compressor, quote replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Prices vary by region and unit age.

- **Air filter:** $5–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$700
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type — R-410A vs older units)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800–$3,000+ (often worth comparing against full system replacement on older units)

If your York unit is out of warranty and over 12–15 years old, weigh a major repair against a new system, especially given the phase-out of older refrigerants.

## Related codes

- **York compressor short-cycling** — related low-charge or capacitor issues.
- **York outdoor fan not spinning** — capacitor or fan motor overlap.
- **York frozen evaporator coil** — a downstream symptom of this problem.
- **York thermostat not calling for cooling** — control-signal faults that mimic "not cooling."
- **York high-pressure lockout** — often triggered by a dirty condenser or failed fan motor.
