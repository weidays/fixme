## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific York fault code — it's a symptom. Your York air conditioner may be running, but the air coming from your vents is warm, weak, or barely cool. Some newer York communicating systems (like certain Affinity models) display fault codes on the thermostat or control board; older single-stage LX and Latitude units often give no code at all and simply underperform.

Because York equipment varies significantly by series and board revision, the exact diagnostic path depends on your model. This page covers the most common mechanical and electrical causes behind poor or no cooling across York split systems.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a miscalibrated or failing thermostat.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser has lost power while the indoor blower still runs.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; the unit blows warm air until it thaws.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure that stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling capacity; requires a licensed tech to find and repair.
7. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor unit won't energize.
8. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, cutting efficiency.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces little or no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here — do not open sealed refrigerant lines, the compressor, or electrical panels beyond the breaker.

- **Air filter:** Locate and inspect it. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" complaints.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the temperature is below room temp. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Try lowering the setpoint 3–4°F to trigger a call for cooling.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker labeled AC, condenser, or air handler. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not repeatedly reset it.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply vents and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Outdoor unit:** Check that the condenser (outside) is running and not buried in leaves, grass clippings, or debris. Clear at least 2 feet of space around it.
- **Frozen coil / lines:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the AC **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** to thaw it — this can take a few hours. Replace the filter before restarting.
- **Condensate:** A full drain pan or clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Check for standing water near the indoor unit.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. Confirm the thermostat is calling for cooling and verify low-voltage signal at the equipment.
2. Check indoor airflow and inspect the evaporator coil and filter.
3. Measure voltage and amperage at the contactor, capacitor, and compressor.
4. Test the run/start capacitor with a meter (a common, cheap failure).
5. Attach gauges to measure refrigerant pressures and check superheat/subcooling — this reveals low charge, restrictions, or a bad compressor.
6. If low on refrigerant, perform a leak search (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen) rather than just topping off.
7. On communicating York systems, read stored fault codes from the board or thermostat.

If a quote jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search, or "replace the compressor" without testing the capacitor first, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect coil, blower |
| Indoor fan runs, no cool air | Outdoor unit off | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, capacitor |
| Ice on lines/coil | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, thaw, new filter | Check charge, find leak |
| Outdoor fan spins, no cooling | Failed run capacitor | None | Test & replace capacitor |
| Humming outdoor, no start | Bad capacitor/contactor | None | Replace part |
| Gradual loss of cooling | Refrigerant leak | None | Leak search & repair |
| Runs constantly, barely cools | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris outside | Professional coil cleaning |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Electrical/compressor fault | Stop resetting | Diagnose short/compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair:** $600–$1,600 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type)
- **Refrigerant recharge (with valid repair):** $200–$700+ depending on quantity and R-410A vs. R-22
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to a new system on older units)
- **New condenser or full system:** $4,000–$8,000+ if repair isn't cost-effective

Note: R-22 systems (older York units) are far more expensive to service due to refrigerant scarcity. Replacement is often the smarter option.
