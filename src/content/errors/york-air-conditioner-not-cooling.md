## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific York fault code — most residential York air conditioners don't display alphanumeric error codes at the outdoor unit. It means the system is running (or trying to run) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

On York systems with a **communicating control board** (some Affinity models), a fault may be logged and readable through the thermostat or a service tool. On standard single-stage York units, there is no display — you're diagnosing by behavior, temperature, and airflow.

Because "not cooling" can range from a $5 filter to a failed compressor, this page helps you separate the safe DIY checks from the work that legitimately needs a licensed HVAC technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling, and can freeze the coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (set to "heat" or "fan on"), or a miswired/failing stat.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked with dirt, grass, or debris, so heat can't be rejected.
5. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks cooling entirely.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — compressor or fan motor hums but won't start.
7. **Low refrigerant / leak** — the system slowly loses cooling capacity; almost always a leak, not "used up."
8. **Failing contactor** — the outdoor unit's electrical relay pits or sticks and won't energize.
9. **Failed compressor or fan motor** — the most expensive outcome, usually last on the list.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here and call a pro for anything electrical, refrigerant, or sealed-system related.

- **Air filter** — Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the #1 cause of weak or no cooling.
- **Thermostat** — Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker** — Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Flip it fully **off**, then back **on** once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting it.
- **Vents and registers** — Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit** — Confirm the fan is spinning and the coil isn't buried in leaves, grass clippings, or cottonwood. Gently clear debris with the power off.
- **Condensate** — Check for standing water or a full drain pan. A tripped float switch (from a clogged drain) can shut cooling down. Clearing a visible clog is safe; wiring is not.
- **Ice** — If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and the fan to **On** for a few hours to thaw before calling. Never chip at ice.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York tech works from cheap to expensive and should explain each step:

1. **Verify airflow** — filter, blower operation, and static pressure across the coil.
2. **Confirm power and controls** — voltage at the disconnect, contactor pull-in, thermostat call for cooling.
3. **Check the capacitor** — a meter reading against the microfarad rating stamped on the cap.
4. **Read refrigerant pressures** — gauges on the suction and liquid lines, plus superheat/subcooling to judge charge and airflow.
5. **Inspect the coils** — condenser and evaporator for dirt or freezing.
6. **Leak search** — if charge is low, a dye or electronic detector to find the actual leak (adding refrigerant without finding the leak is a red flag).
7. **Compressor test** — resistance and amp draw if all else checks out.

Ask for the measured readings (capacitor µF, pressures, superheat). A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "new compressor" without these numbers deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warmish air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check blower & duct static |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, find cause |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call a pro | Test & replace capacitor |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw with fan | Diagnose airflow/charge |
| Gradual loss of cooling over weeks | Refrigerant leak | None — call a pro | Leak search & repair, recharge |
| Warm air, outdoor coil filthy | Dirty condenser | Rinse gently, power off | Deep coil cleaning |
| No cooling, water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear visible clog | Clear drain, test float |
| Nothing cools, big amp draw | Failed compressor | None — call a pro | Compressor replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **New thermostat:** $100–$350 installed
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate pump or drain clearing:** $75–$300
- **Coil cleaning (condenser or evaporator):** $150–$450
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,600 depending on leak location
- **Condenser fan motor:** $400–$700
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (on an older unit, weigh this against full replacement)

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs older R-22 systems), and unit age. Get a written diagnosis with measured readings before authorizing major work.

## Related codes

- **York AC not turning on** — no power, breaker, or thermostat fault.
- **York AC frozen coil / iced lines** — airflow or refrigerant restriction.
- **York AC short cycling** — capacitor, overcharge, or oversized system.
- **York outdoor fan not spinning** — capacitor or fan motor failure.
- **York condensate float switch tripped** — clogged drain shutting down cooling.
