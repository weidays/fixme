## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code on most York units — it's the symptom you're seeing: the system is powered and may be running, but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house isn't reaching setpoint.

York air conditioners span a wide range of designs. Older and mid-range **Latitude** and **LX** series units use standard 24-volt controls with few onboard diagnostics. Higher-end **Affinity** communicating systems can display fault codes on the thermostat or indoor board. Because of this, "not cooling" can point to anything from a $15 filter to a failed compressor. This page walks you through ruling out the cheap causes first, then explains what a technician should check so you can sanity-check any quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause of weak or no cooling. Restricted airflow can also freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a wiring/calibration fault telling the system to do the wrong thing.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit or the whole system may have lost power.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common outdoor failure; the fan or compressor won't start, or hums and shuts off.
5. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — a coil packed with dirt, grass, or cottonwood can't reject heat, so the house won't cool.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks the coil and stops cold air.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces or eliminates cooling capacity. This is a repair, not a top-off.
8. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor relay that powers the compressor sticks or fails to close.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor or blower motor** — no airflow over a coil means no usable cooling.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause, usually a last-resort diagnosis after everything else is ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the checks any homeowner can safely do:

- **Filter:** Pull the air filter. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the fan is on **AUTO**, and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler/furnace and the outdoor unit. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop — that's an electrical fault for a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Visually check that the outdoor condenser isn't buried in leaves, grass clippings, or debris. Clear obstructions around it, but leave the panels closed.
- **Condensate:** If your system shut off and there's a full drain pan or a tripped float switch, a clogged condensate drain may be the cause. You can clear a visible clog and empty the pan.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn cooling **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** to let it thaw (can take several hours), then call a tech.

Do **not** open sealed electrical panels, add refrigerant, bypass safety switches, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent York tech should work methodically — here's the typical flow so you can follow along on the invoice:

1. **Verify the complaint** and check thermostat settings and call for cooling.
2. **Measure temperature split** — the difference between return and supply air. A healthy system is usually around 15–20°F; a low split points to airflow or refrigerant issues.
3. **Inspect airflow** — filter, blower operation, and coil cleanliness.
4. **Check electrical** — line and low voltage, the contactor, and the capacitor (a capacitor reading well below its rating explains a no-start).
5. **Read any fault codes** — on Affinity communicating systems, the board or thermostat may store codes that narrow the cause.
6. **Attach gauges** — measure refrigerant pressures and superheat/subcooling to confirm whether the charge is correct or the system is low (indicating a leak).
7. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — with electronic detectors or dye.
8. **Test motors and compressor** for amperage draw and windings.

A good quote names the specific failed part and the test result behind it. Be cautious of a "just add refrigerant" quote with no leak search — that's a temporary fix on a leaking system.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter / blocked vents | Replace filter, open vents | Inspect blower, clean coils |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off cooling, run fan to thaw | Find airflow or leak cause |
| Outdoor fan spins, no cold air | Low refrigerant / bad compressor | None | Gauge pressures, leak search |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor runs | Tripped breaker / bad contactor or capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test capacitor, contactor, wiring |
| Humming outdoor unit, no start | Failed capacitor | None | Test and replace capacitor |
| System short-cycles | Dirty coil, thermostat, or control fault | Clean debris around unit | Diagnose controls / charge |
| Nothing runs at all | Power loss / blown 24V fuse | Check breaker and thermostat batteries | Trace low-voltage fault |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor included unless noted:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$450
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $200–$1,500+ depending on leak location
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,800+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement)

Prices vary by region, unit size, refrigerant type, and whether your unit is still under a York parts warranty — many York systems carry a 10-year parts warranty if registered, which can significantly cut part costs.

## Related codes

- **York AC freezing up / iced coil** — airflow and refrigerant-driven; overlaps heavily with "not cooling."
- **York AC not turning on** — no power, contactor, or low-voltage faults.
- **York Affinity communicating fault codes** — check the thermostat display for stored codes on communicating systems.
- **York AC blowing warm air** — a subset of "not cooling," usually refrigerant or compressor related.
- **York AC short cycling** — often points to airflow, thermostat, or charge problems.
