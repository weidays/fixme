## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's a symptom. Your York air conditioner may be running (the outdoor fan spins, air blows from the vents) but the air isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On some York systems with communicating controls or a modern thermostat, you may also see a generic fault or "system error" message.

Because York uses different control boards across its LX, Affinity, and Latitude lines — and pairs with York, Coleman, and Luxaire air handlers — there is no single universal error display. The underlying cause is almost always one of a short list of issues affecting airflow, the electrical components, or the refrigerant charge.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow freezes the indoor coil, which stops cooling.
2. **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil** — leaves, grass, and dirt insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a bulging or dead capacitor stops the compressor or fan motor from starting.
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak** — causes weak or no cooling and often ice on the lines. Requires a pro.
5. **Frozen indoor evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; you'll see ice and warm air.
6. **Failed contactor or relay** — the outdoor unit won't energize at all.
7. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a wiring/setting fault.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit gets no power.
9. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit hums but won't cool.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything feels beyond your comfort level.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and check it against light. If it's gray or clogged, replace it — this alone fixes many "not cooling" complaints.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker labeled AC, condenser, or air handler. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around and on top of the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain:** If you see standing water at the indoor unit, check for a clogged drain line and clear any obvious blockage; a full pan can trip a safety float switch.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF and set the fan to ON to let it thaw. Do **not** run cooling on a frozen coil.

Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, remove electrical panels on the condenser, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Confirm the thermostat call and 24V control signal reaching the outdoor unit.
- Measure line voltage and test the **capacitor** and **contactor** with a meter.
- Check the compressor and fan motor for correct amperage draw.
- Attach gauges to read **suction and liquid pressures** and calculate superheat/subcooling to judge refrigerant charge.
- Inspect the coils and measure the temperature split across the indoor coil (usually a healthy 16–22°F drop).
- If charge is low, perform a **leak search** (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test) before adding refrigerant.

Sanity check: if a quote says "just add refrigerant" without any mention of finding the leak, ask why — topping off a leaking system is a temporary fix, not a repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower, ductwork |
| Outdoor unit runs, no cold air | Low refrigerant / leak | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor fan won't start, humming | Bad capacitor | None (capacitors hold charge) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, thaw with fan on | Diagnose airflow vs. charge |
| Nothing runs outside | Tripped breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, wiring, board |
| Water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain, add tablets | Clear line, check float switch |
| Unit hums but won't cool | Failing compressor | None | Test and replace compressor |
| Thermostat blank | Dead batteries / power | Replace batteries | Check wiring and transformer |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Regional pricing varies widely.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $0 DIY – $200 professional
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,800 (often worth comparing to a new system on older units)

Diagnostic/service-call fees typically run $75–$200 and are sometimes credited toward the repair.

## Related codes

- **York "system error" / communicating fault** — generic control-board fault on Affinity communicating systems.
- **Frozen coil / low airflow lockout** — related airflow-driven no-cool condition.
- **Compressor high-pressure or low-pressure lockout** — safety trips that present as no cooling.
- **Thermostat "cool on" but no response** — 24V control or wiring faults.

Because York's displays and lockout behavior vary by board and thermostat, match any on-screen message to your specific model's installation manual before acting.
