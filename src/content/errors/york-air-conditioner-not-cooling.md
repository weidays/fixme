## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on York air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your system may be running, the indoor blower may be moving air, but the air coming from the vents is warm or only mildly cool, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

York central AC systems (LX, Affinity, Latitude, and similar split systems) don't display alphanumeric error codes on the unit itself the way furnaces do. Diagnosis relies on symptoms, temperature readings, electrical measurements, and refrigerant pressures. Because there's no code to read, the cause could be anything from a $20 filter to a failed compressor — which is why this is rated a **pro** problem once the simple checks are ruled out.

If your York system is paired with a communicating thermostat, that thermostat may display its own alerts or fault messages — check its screen and manual, as behavior varies by model.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops efficiency, and can freeze the coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
3. **Low refrigerant due to a leak** — the system can't remove heat; often accompanied by ice or hissing.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor won't start or runs weakly.
5. **Dirty condenser coil (outdoor unit)** — traps heat outside so the system can't reject it.
6. **Bad contactor** — worn or pitted contacts prevent the outdoor unit from energizing.
7. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a wiring/calibration fault.
8. **Tripped breaker to the outdoor unit** — the condenser gets no power.
9. **Failing compressor** — the heart of the system; expensive and requires a pro.
10. **Blower motor or control board fault** — varies by model; reduces or stops indoor airflow.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. If none of them fix it, stop and call a licensed technician.

- **Check the air filter.** Pull it out and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the single most common cause of weak cooling.
- **Verify the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the fan is on **AUTO**, and the setpoint is below room temperature. If it's battery-powered and blank or dim, replace the batteries.
- **Look at the breakers.** Central AC usually has two breakers — one for the indoor air handler and one for the outdoor condenser. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Inspect your vents.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture, rugs, or closed doors.
- **Look for ice.** If you see ice on the outdoor refrigerant lines or a frozen indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** to thaw it (this can take several hours). Do not run cooling on a frozen coil.
- **Check the condensate drain.** A clogged drain or full pan can trip a float switch and stop cooling. If you can see standing water, that's a sign to call a pro.

**Do not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting down.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York technician follows a logical sequence — knowing it helps you sanity-check the quote:

1. **Confirms the complaint** and checks thermostat settings and call for cooling.
2. **Measures temperature split** — the difference between return and supply air (typically 15–20°F on a healthy system). A low split points to refrigerant or airflow problems.
3. **Inspects airflow** — filter, coil, and blower operation.
4. **Checks electrical components** — capacitor microfarad rating, contactor, and voltage at the outdoor unit with a multimeter.
5. **Reads refrigerant pressures** with gauges and compares to charging charts for your model. This reveals undercharge (leak), overcharge, or a restriction.
6. **Performs a leak search** if refrigerant is low — with electronic detectors or dye.
7. **Tests the compressor** for proper amp draw, windings, and starting.

Expect a diagnostic fee of **$80–$150**, often credited toward the repair. Be wary of any tech who wants to "just add refrigerant" without finding the leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty air filter | Replace filter | Check blower and ductwork |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Find airflow restriction or leak |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Failed capacitor or compressor | None — call pro | Test/replace capacitor or compressor |
| Outdoor unit completely dead | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Blank thermostat | Dead batteries | Replace batteries | Check thermostat wiring/board |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant leak | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor coil caked in dirt | Dirty condenser | Gently rinse fins with water | Chemical coil cleaning |
| Water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Check drain pan | Clear drain line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor). Prices vary by region and model.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,500+ (often near the cost of a new condenser)
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900
- **Diagnostic fee:** $80–$150 (frequently credited toward repair)

If your York system is 12+ years old and facing a compressor or coil failure, ask about full-system replacement — repair costs may not be worth it.
