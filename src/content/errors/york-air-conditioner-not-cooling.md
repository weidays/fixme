## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom York homeowners search for most. Your air conditioner may be running (outdoor fan spinning, indoor blower moving air) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

Most York central air conditioners and heat pump condensers don't have a digital display on the outdoor unit, so there's usually no error number to read. The diagnosis comes from symptoms, temperature readings and electrical measurements. Some communicating York systems (Affinity with a compatible thermostat) *will* show status or fault messages at the thermostat — check yours if you have one.

Because "not cooling" can mean anything from a $10 filter to a $1,500 compressor, this page ranks the likely causes and tells you which you can safely check yourself.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops output and can freeze the coil.
2. **Dirty condenser coil / blocked outdoor unit** — the unit can't reject heat, so cooling drops off, worst on hot days.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — ice blocks airflow; often caused by #1, low refrigerant, or a weak blower.
4. **Low refrigerant / leak** — undercharged systems can't absorb heat. Requires a pro; leaks must be found and sealed.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a common failure that stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting.
6. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
7. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a miswired/failing thermostat.
8. **Failing compressor or contactor** — the contactor (relay) or the compressor itself has failed. Most expensive category.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If it's gray or you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel. York AC uses **two** breakers — one for the indoor air handler/furnace and a separate 240V breaker for the outdoor condenser. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trigger a safety float switch that stops cooling. If your drain line ends outside, check that water is flowing and the line isn't backed up. Condensate tablets help prevent algae buildup.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings and debris from the fins and keep at least 2 feet of clearance. Do **not** open the unit or clean deep inside it — that's pro territory.

If the coil is iced over, turn the system to **OFF** (or fan-only) and let it thaw fully before running cooling again. Never chip at the ice.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed HVAC tech will typically:

1. Confirm the complaint and check thermostat settings and airflow.
2. Measure the **temperature split** — the difference between return and supply air (should be roughly 14–20°F on a healthy system).
3. Inspect and clean the condenser and evaporator coils if dirty.
4. Test the **run capacitor** with a meter for correct microfarad rating.
5. Check the **contactor** for pitting and proper pull-in.
6. Connect gauges to read refrigerant pressures and calculate **superheat/subcooling** — this tells them if the charge is correct or if there's a leak.
7. If undercharged, perform a **leak search** (electronic detector or dye) rather than just topping off.
8. Test compressor windings and amp draw if a compressor fault is suspected.

Sanity check: a quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search, or "replace the compressor" without capacitor and electrical testing, deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warmish air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check blower & duct static |
| Ice on refrigerant line/coil | Low refrigerant or airflow restriction | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find leak, recharge, check blower |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Bad capacitor or motor | None – call pro | Test/replace capacitor or fan motor |
| Indoor blower runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, wiring, capacitor |
| Cooling drops off on hot afternoons | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris around unit | Deep-clean coil, check charge |
| System short-cycles, hums | Failing compressor/hard start | None – call pro | Test compressor, install hard-start kit |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged condensate/float switch | Clear visible drain line | Clear drain, reset float switch |
| No cooling, blank thermostat | Dead batteries or power loss | Replace batteries | Test thermostat wiring & transformer |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$50 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$200
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Run/dual capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,200 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type)
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ installed
- **Full condenser/system replacement:** $4,000–$8,000+

Note: R-22 systems (older York units) cost far more to recharge because R-22 is phased out and expensive. If your unit uses R-22 and has a significant leak, replacement is often the smarter investment.
