## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on York equipment — it's the symptom homeowners describe when the system runs but the house won't reach the setpoint, or the air from the vents isn't cold. Most York air conditioners don't display a plain-language error; instead, the outdoor control board (on newer Affinity and variable-speed units) may flash an LED fault pattern, while older single-stage units give no code at all.

Because "not cooling" covers everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, the goal below is to narrow it down. Behavior varies by model: a variable-speed Affinity system may throttle down or lock out with a board fault, while a basic Latitude single-stage unit simply runs without producing cold air.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the indoor coil, and kills cooling. The single most common cause.
2. **Thermostat set wrong or dead batteries** — set to "fan on" instead of "cool," wrong temperature, or a blank/dead thermostat.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit has no power, so the compressor and fan don't run.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or condenser fan hums but won't start.
5. **Frozen indoor evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines.
6. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked with dirt/grass, the unit can't reject heat and cooling drops off.
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so nothing energizes.
8. **Low refrigerant / leak** — warm air and long run times; requires a licensed tech to diagnose and repair.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — fan doesn't spin, unit overheats and shuts down on a high-pressure trip.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; unit runs but produces no cooling, or won't start at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Air filter** — Pull it and hold it to the light. If it's gray or you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat** — Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breaker** — Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler and outdoor unit. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers** — Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain** — If there's a full drain pan or a float switch that shut the system off, clear the visible drain line and empty standing water. Drain tablets help prevent clogs.
- **Outdoor unit** — Look for obvious debris, tall grass, or leaves blocking the condenser. Make sure the outdoor disconnect near the unit is switched on.

If the indoor coil or refrigerant lines are covered in ice, turn the system to **Off** (leave the fan running to thaw) and call a technician — running a frozen system risks compressor damage.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence, and knowing it helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirms the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F).
2. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and coil for ice or blockage.
3. **Verifies power** — tests voltage at the disconnect, contactor, and control board; reads any York fault LED codes.
4. **Tests the capacitor** — with a meter, against its rated microfarads. A weak cap is cheap and common.
5. **Checks the contactor and fan motor** — for pitting, stuck contacts, or a seized motor.
6. **Reads refrigerant pressures** — connects gauges to check for low charge or a leak (this is where a pro is legally and technically required).
7. **Evaluates the compressor** — amp draw and windings if everything upstream checks out.

Expect a diagnostic fee of roughly **$75–$200**, often credited toward the repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm vents | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower/coil |
| Ice on refrigerant lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn to Off, run fan to thaw | Find/fix leak, recharge |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, test board |
| Fan/compressor hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor fan won't spin | Bad fan motor or capacitor | None | Replace fan motor/capacitor |
| Long run times, house never cools | Low refrigerant or dirty condenser | Rinse debris off outdoor coil | Check charge, clean coil |
| System shuts off, water near air handler | Clogged condensate drain/float switch | Clear visible drain, empty pan | Clear line, test float switch |
| Runs constantly, no cold air at all | Failed compressor | None | Diagnose/replace compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Breaker reset:** $0 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 pro
- **Run/fan capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$300
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against replacing the whole outdoor unit)

Prices vary by region, unit age, and whether your system is still under York's parts warranty — check your registration, as it may cover the part while you pay labor.

## Related codes

- **York outdoor board LED fault flashes** — patterns vary by model; consult the label inside the control panel access door.
- **AC freezing up / iced coil** — low airflow or low refrigerant.
- **AC blowing warm air** — overlapping causes; usually capacitor, refrigerant, or contactor.
- **Outdoor unit not turning on** — power, contactor, or capacitor related.
- **Condensate float switch tripped** — clogged drain shutting the system down.
