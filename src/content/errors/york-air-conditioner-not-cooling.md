## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's a symptom. Your York air conditioner may be running (fan spinning, outdoor unit humming) but blowing warm or room-temperature air, or it may not be starting at all. On York units with communicating control boards (Affinity S1/Hx series), you may also see a specific fault flashed on the thermostat or board LED. Older single-stage Latitude and LX units have no display, so you diagnose by symptom.

The root problem is almost always one of three things: **airflow is restricted**, **the refrigerant charge is wrong** (usually a leak), or an **electrical component has failed** (capacitor, contactor, or compressor). Some of these are safe homeowner checks; most require a licensed HVAC technician with gauges and refrigerant certification — which is why this page is rated **pro**.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling output, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — ice on the indoor coil blocks airflow entirely; caused by low airflow or low refrigerant.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection, so the system can't shed heat.
4. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), or a miswired/failing thermostat.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system runs but can't cool; requires leak repair and recharge by a pro.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan hums or won't start; a very common, moderate-cost failure.
7. **Bad contactor** — pitted or stuck contacts stop the outdoor unit from energizing.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; the unit runs but produces no cooling, or trips repeatedly.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the electrical panel of the condenser, touch refrigerant lines, or bypass any safety switch.

- **Air filter** — Pull it. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat** — Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the temperature is below room temperature. Set the fan to **Auto**, not On. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers** — Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers** — Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit** — Clear grass, leaves, and debris away from the condenser. Gently rinse the outside fins with a garden hose (power off) if they're caked with dirt.
- **Condensate drain / ice** — If you see ice on the indoor coil or refrigerant lines, **turn the system off and set the fan to On** to let it thaw fully (can take several hours) before restarting. If it re-ices, you have an airflow or refrigerant problem for a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Confirms the complaint** and reads any board/thermostat fault codes on communicating units.
2. **Checks airflow** — filter, blower operation, coil condition, and static pressure.
3. **Inspects the condenser** — coil cleanliness, fan operation, contactor, and capacitor readings (microfarads).
4. **Takes electrical readings** — voltage at the contactor, capacitor microfarad values, and compressor amp draw.
5. **Connects refrigerant gauges** — checks suction/liquid pressures and superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct. Low readings point to a leak.
6. **Performs a leak search** if refrigerant is low — with electronic detector or dye — because recharging without fixing the leak is a temporary and costly fix.
7. **Tests the compressor** for windings, start capability, and amperage if all else checks out.

Be wary of any tech who tops off refrigerant without searching for a leak, or condemns a compressor without showing you amp or resistance readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace the filter | Check blower & static pressure |
| Ice on coil or line set | Frozen evaporator | Turn off, thaw, run fan | Find low airflow or low charge |
| Outdoor unit dirty, poor cooling | Blocked condenser | Rinse fins, clear debris | Deep coil cleaning |
| No cool air, fan runs, thermostat off | Thermostat issue | Set to Cool/Auto, new batteries | Test thermostat wiring |
| Outdoor unit humming, not starting | Bad run capacitor | None — call a pro | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor & voltage |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant (leak) | None — call a pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Runs but no cooling / trips repeatedly | Failed compressor | Stop resetting, call a pro | Test & replace compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10 – $40 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75 – $250
- **Thermostat replacement:** $100 – $350
- **Run capacitor:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor:** $150 – $400
- **Low-voltage fuse / minor electrical:** $100 – $250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500 – $1,500 (varies with leak location and refrigerant type)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900 – $2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800 – $2,500+ (often worth weighing against full replacement on older units)

Note: R-22 units cost significantly more to recharge and are often better replaced than repaired.

## Related codes

- York communicating S1/Hx board **fault codes** (flashed at the control board or thermostat)
- **Low-pressure / high-pressure lockout** conditions on units with pressure switches
- **Frozen coil / airflow** faults
- **Compressor short-cycling** or hard-start faults

On non-communicating Latitude and LX units there are no numeric codes — diagnosis is by symptom and technician measurement, as described above.
