## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific York fault code — the air handler or blower may run, but the air coming out of the vents is warm or only slightly cool. On York systems this points to a break somewhere in the cooling chain: the thermostat call, the outdoor condenser and compressor, the refrigerant charge, or airflow across the coils.

Basic York contactor-and-capacitor units usually give you no on-screen code at all — you just feel warm air. Higher-end York Affinity communicating systems and inverter-driven models may show a fault or status code on the thermostat or control board that narrows things down. Because this behavior varies by model and board, the exact diagnostic path depends on which unit you have.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow can freeze the indoor coil, so warm air blows even though the system is "running."
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto," not set to "cool"), or a miswired/failing thermostat.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit loses power while the indoor blower keeps running.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure. The compressor or condenser fan can't start, so the outdoor unit hums or does nothing.
5. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit doesn't pull in, so the condenser never starts.
6. **Dirty condenser coil** — a coil caked in dirt, grass, or cottonwood can't reject heat, killing cooling capacity.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces or eliminates cooling and often freezes the coil. Requires a licensed pro.
8. **Condenser fan motor failure** — the compressor overheats and shuts down on its internal protector.
9. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
10. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive outcome, typically diagnosed last after everything else is ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do — no panels beyond the filter, no electrical parts, no refrigerant.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the AC. Reset it **once**. If it trips again immediately, stop and call a pro — repeated resets can damage the compressor.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for tall grass, leaves, or debris packed against the condenser fins. Clear the area and gently rinse the outside of the coil with a garden hose (power off) if it's visibly dirty.
- **Condensate:** Check for a full drain pan or a tripped float switch (some systems shut off cooling when the drain backs up). Clear standing water.
- **Ice:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and run the fan only to let it thaw before calling — running a frozen unit makes it worse.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York technician works from cheap and common toward expensive:

1. **Confirms the thermostat call** reaches the outdoor unit (24V at the contactor).
2. **Checks the contactor and capacitor** with a meter — verifies the contactor pulls in and tests capacitor microfarads against the rating on the label.
3. **Measures amp draw** on the compressor and condenser fan motor to spot a stalled or overloaded motor.
4. **Inspects airflow and coils** — filter, blower, and both coils for dirt or ice.
5. **Attaches refrigerant gauges** and takes temperature readings (superheat/subcooling) to judge the charge. Low charge means a **leak search**, not just a top-off.
6. **Reads any board/thermostat fault codes** on Affinity communicating or inverter models to confirm the diagnosis.
7. **Tests the compressor** electrically (windings, start capability) only after ruling out the above.

If a quote jumps straight to "you need a new compressor" or "just add refrigerant" without a leak search or component testing, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blower runs, warm air | Dirty filter / frozen coil | Replace filter, run fan to thaw | Check airflow, coil, charge |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor and 24V signal |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None (electrical) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Weak cooling, ice on lines | Low refrigerant leak | Turn off, thaw | Leak search and recharge |
| Compressor runs, fan doesn't spin | Condenser fan motor | Power off, clear debris | Replace fan motor / capacitor |
| Dirty outdoor coil, poor cooling | Blocked condenser | Rinse fins, clear debris | Deep coil cleaning |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Short or failing compressor | Stop resetting | Electrical + compressor test |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear drain, empty pan | Clear line, check switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10–$25 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** under $10 (DIY)
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$650
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location; evaporator coil replacement pushes the high end)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often the point where replacing the whole unit is worth pricing)

Prices vary by region, unit tonnage, and whether your York system is still under parts warranty — many York units carry a 10-year parts warranty if registered, which can cut costs sharply.
