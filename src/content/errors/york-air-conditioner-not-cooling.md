## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you're living with when your York air conditioner runs but the house never gets comfortable, or the air from the vents isn't cold. Depending on your model, the York control board may or may not flash a diagnostic LED code when something is wrong.

York split systems and packaged units cover a lot of ground here. Older units simply run until something stops them. Newer Affinity communicating systems can store fault codes and display them at a compatible thermostat, while LX and Latitude series units may use a blinking LED on the outdoor or indoor board. **Because behavior varies by model and control board, the flash pattern you see (if any) should be matched against the label inside your specific unit's access panel — not assumed.**

The bottom line: "not cooling" points to airflow problems, refrigerant problems, or electrical/component failures. This page helps you narrow it down and sanity-check any repair quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — the single most common cause. A clogged filter chokes airflow, which drops cooling capacity and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (Heat instead of Cool), or a bad thermostat that isn't calling for cooling.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler has lost power while the other keeps running.
4. **Dirty or blocked condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so cooling drops dramatically on hot days.
5. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from restricted airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the indoor coil or refrigerant line.
6. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common electrical failure that stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak or no cooling and often a frozen coil. Requires a certified tech to find and repair.
8. **Failed condenser fan motor or contactor** — the outdoor unit hums or does nothing while the indoor blower runs.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; the system runs but produces no cold air at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. If none of them fix it, stop and call a pro — don't keep resetting a unit that won't stay running.

- **Check and replace the air filter.** If it's gray or clogged, swap it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the temperature is set below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Check the breakers.** Look at your electrical panel for a tripped breaker for the AC or air handler. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Look at your vents and registers.** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Check the condensate drain.** A backed-up drain can trigger a safety float switch that shuts down cooling. If you see standing water in the drain pan, clear it and check for a clog.
- **Look for ice.** If you see frost or ice on the indoor coil or the copper line, turn the system to **Off** and the fan to **On** to let it thaw. Running a frozen unit makes things worse. Once thawed, if it re-freezes, you need a technician.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, remove the outdoor unit's electrical cover, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York technician will work through a logical sequence. Knowing it helps you spot a rushed or padded diagnosis:

1. **Confirm the complaint** — measure supply vs. return air temperature. A healthy system usually shows a 15-20°F drop; less means a problem.
2. **Check airflow** — inspect the filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Verify electrical** — test the capacitor, contactor, and voltage at the outdoor unit with a meter.
4. **Read any stored codes** — on Affinity/communicating systems, pull fault history from the control; on LED-code boards, count the flash pattern.
5. **Check refrigerant charge** — connect gauges and measure pressures and superheat/subcooling. Low readings point to a leak.
6. **Leak search** — if the charge is low, a proper repair means finding the leak (electronic detector or dye), not just adding refrigerant.
7. **Test the compressor** — measure amp draw and windings if the compressor is suspected.

Be cautious of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without measured pressures and an amp reading.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower & coil if unchanged |
| No cold air, wrong mode | Thermostat setting/battery | Set to Cool, replace batteries | Test/replace thermostat |
| Indoor runs, outdoor dead | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, wiring, capacitor |
| Ice on coil or line | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Clear visible clog | Clear drain, replace float switch |
| System runs, zero cooling | Compressor or refrigerant failure | None — call pro | Diagnose compressor/refrigerant |
| Weak cooling on hot days | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse gently with hose (power off) | Deep coil cleaning, charge check |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges. Your price varies by region, unit age, and refrigerant type (older R-22 systems cost far more to service).

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Float/safety switch replacement:** $75–$200
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Coil cleaning (deep):** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (higher for R-22 systems)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement on older units)

If your unit is more than 12-15 years old and facing a compressor or coil replacement, ask your technician to compare the repair cost against a new system.

## Related codes

- **York outdoor unit not running** — capacitor, contactor, or fan motor failures.
- **York AC short cycling** — turns on and off repeatedly without cooling properly.
- **York communicating system fault codes** — Affinity-series diagnostic codes shown at the thermostat.
- **York condensate float switch tripped** — drain backup shutting down cooling.

Match any blinking LED code against the diagram on your unit's access panel, since patterns differ across York model series.
