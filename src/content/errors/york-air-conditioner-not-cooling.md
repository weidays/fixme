## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific York fault code — it is the symptom homeowners report when the air conditioner runs but the home stays warm, or the system won't run at all. Modern York variable-speed units (YXV, YFE) with communicating controls may display an actual fault code on the thermostat or control board, while older single-stage units simply fail silently.

Because York uses several generations of control boards, **what you see depends on your model and thermostat**. If your system does show a numeric or lettered code, look that up specifically. If there's no code and the house just isn't cooling, this page walks through the most common causes in order.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a miswired/failing thermostat not calling for cooling.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser or air handler has lost power.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or fan motor hums but won't start.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so cooling capacity collapses.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines or coil.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling and eventually freezes the coil. Always indicates a leak.
8. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the compressor doesn't engage.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor or blower motor** — one side of the system won't move air.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome, often after long-term neglect.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening the sealed refrigerant system or the electrical panel beyond the breaker.

- **Filter:** Pull the air filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of poor cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the AC. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated trips mean a fault.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the outside of the coil fins with a garden hose (unit off) if they look dirty.
- **Condensate drain:** If the unit shut off and you have a full drain pan, the safety float switch may have tripped. Clear the drain line if you're comfortable, or call a pro.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the coil or refrigerant lines, turn the system to OFF and set the fan to ON to let it thaw. Don't run cooling until it's clear — and expect to need a pro for the underlying cause.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work systematically, not just "top off" refrigerant. Expect them to:

1. Verify the thermostat is calling for cooling and check control voltage (24V) at the board and contactor.
2. Inspect the air filter and measure airflow across the evaporator coil.
3. Test the run/start capacitor with a meter (microfarad reading vs. rated value).
4. Check the contactor for pitting and proper pull-in.
5. Measure compressor and fan motor amp draw against the nameplate.
6. Attach gauges to read suction and head pressures, then calculate **superheat and subcooling** to judge the refrigerant charge — this is how they confirm a leak versus a properly charged system.
7. If low on refrigerant, perform a **leak search** (electronic detector or dye) before adding anything.
8. Inspect the coils, blower, and drain for airflow and condensate issues.

A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without checking pressures or looking for a leak is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, house slowly warms | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check ductwork, blower speed |
| No air at all, fan silent | No power / tripped breaker | Reset breaker once | Diagnose electrical fault, board |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None (electrical) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw with fan | Find cause, check charge/leak |
| Runs constantly, warm air | Low refrigerant / dirty coil | Rinse outdoor coil | Leak search, recharge, coil clean |
| Blank thermostat | Dead batteries / no power | Replace batteries | Check thermostat wiring, transformer |
| Unit shut off, water in pan | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain line | Clear line, test float switch |
| Outdoor fan spins, no cooling | Failed compressor/contactor | None | Test contactor, compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $400–$650
- **Blower motor:** $450–$800
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 (more if the coil must be replaced)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500+
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often worth comparing to full unit replacement on older systems)

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older R-22), and whether your unit is under warranty. York registered systems may carry a 10-year parts warranty, which can dramatically cut part costs — labor is usually still charged.

## Related codes

- **Frozen evaporator coil** — often the visible result of low airflow or low refrigerant.
- **Condensate overflow / float switch trip** — a common shutdown that mimics "not cooling."
- **Communicating fault codes on YXV/YFE thermostats** — variable-speed York systems display specific codes; look up the exact code shown.
- **AC short cycling** — related airflow and electrical issues that reduce cooling.
- **Outdoor unit not turning on** — overlaps with capacitor, contactor, and power faults above.
