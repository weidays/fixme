## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific York fault code. Your York air conditioner may be powered on, the thermostat may be calling for cooling, and the air handler may be blowing — but the air coming out of your vents is warm or barely cool.

York units don't display a universal alphanumeric error for this condition the way a furnace flashes a fault LED. Instead, the cause has to be traced through the system. Some York communicating systems (with a compatible thermostat) will surface specific fault text or a blinking control-board LED, but **what those codes mean varies by model and control board** — always check the wiring diagram and service label inside your specific unit.

The bottom line: something in the chain from thermostat → indoor blower → refrigerant circuit → outdoor condenser has failed or is restricted. This page helps you rule out the easy stuff before paying for a diagnostic.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow drops cooling capacity and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (heat vs. cool), or a setpoint that isn't actually calling for cooling.
3. **Tripped breaker to the outdoor unit** — the blower runs off indoor power, so you get airflow with no cooling.
4. **Frozen indoor evaporator coil** — from a dirty filter, closed vents, or low refrigerant. Ice blocks heat transfer.
5. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, cottonwood, and dirt insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — a common failure that stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting.
7. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit can pit, stick, or fail to close.
8. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces or eliminates cooling; requires leak repair, not a "top off."
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit may hum, trip the breaker, or do nothing.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything looks damaged or smells like burning.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler and the outdoor unit. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs. Restricted airflow can freeze the coil.
- **Outdoor unit:** Confirm the fan is spinning and the coil isn't buried in leaves, grass, or debris. Gently clear debris with the power off at the disconnect.
- **Condensate:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch that shuts the system down. Look for standing water near the indoor unit or a full drain pan.
- **Frozen coil / lines:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, switch the system to **FAN ONLY** for a few hours to thaw it, then replace the filter and try again.

Do not open sealed refrigerant lines, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the workflow helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Check airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Confirm the call for cooling** — checks 24V control signal at the thermostat and board.
4. **Electrical test at the condenser** — measures voltage at the contactor, tests the capacitor with a meter, and inspects the contactor for pitting.
5. **Compressor and fan check** — verifies both motors start and draw normal amperage.
6. **Refrigerant charge** — connects gauges to read pressures and superheat/subcooling. Low readings prompt a **leak search** (electronic detector or dye), not just a recharge.
7. **Compressor diagnosis** — if pressures and electricals point to a dead compressor, they'll test windings for open, shorted, or grounded conditions.

A legitimate quote should name the failed part and, for refrigerant issues, describe finding the leak — not just "add Freon."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and duct |
| Warm air, blower runs | Tripped outdoor breaker | Reset breaker once | Find cause of trip |
| Ice on coil/lines | Low airflow or low charge | Fan-only to thaw, new filter | Check charge, find leak |
| Outdoor fan won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit silent | Bad contactor or capacitor | Check breaker | Test 24V, replace part |
| Cools poorly on hot days | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris, rinse coil | Deep-clean coil |
| Some cooling, slow, hissing | Refrigerant leak | None — call pro | Leak search and repair |
| Humming, breaker trips | Failed compressor | Shut off, call pro | Test windings, replace |
| Wrong mode / dead stat | Thermostat issue | Set to COOL, new batteries | Replace/rewire stat |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Prices vary by region, unit age, and refrigerant type (older R-22 systems cost far more to service).

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $100–$400 installed
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$300 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 (higher for R-22 or coil replacement)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement on older units)
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80–$200 (frequently credited toward the repair)

If your York unit is 12–15+ years old and facing a compressor or coil replacement, ask for a replacement quote to compare — repairing an aging system can be poor value.

## Related codes

- **York AC frozen coil / iced lines** — restricted airflow or low charge.
- **York outdoor fan not spinning** — capacitor or fan motor failure.
- **York AC short cycling** — thermostat, low charge, or control issue.
- **York AC blowing warm air on heat pump models** — reversing valve or defrost board faults (behavior varies by model).
- **Communicating system fault text on compatible thermostats** — meaning varies by board; consult the unit's service label.
