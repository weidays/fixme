## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's a symptom. Your York air conditioner may be running (fan spinning, thermostat calling for cool) but the air coming from your vents is warm, weak, or barely cooler than room temperature. In some cases the outdoor unit won't start at all.

Higher-end York units like the **Affinity** and **YXV** series display fault codes on the control board or communicating thermostat, which can point to a specific issue (pressure faults, compressor lockout, sensor errors). Basic single-stage **LX** and **Champion** units have no display, so diagnosis relies on symptoms and gauge readings. Because refrigerant, electrical, and compressor problems are common causes here, this is rated a **pro** repair — but several safe, free checks come first.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setpoint, or a wiring/calibration fault.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
4. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — caked with dirt, grass, or cottonwood, so heat can't dump outside.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums but won't start.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — weak cooling, ice, or long run times. Requires a licensed tech.
8. **Contactor failure** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. Run the system 30 minutes.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Set the fan to **AUTO**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker (both indoor and outdoor circuits). Flip it fully OFF, then ON — **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Keep 2 feet of clearance. Don't spray water into the electrical panel.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** for 1–3 hours to thaw. Replace the filter before restarting.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trigger a float switch that stops cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan.

Do **not** open sealed refrigerant lines, bypass safety switches, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech will typically:

1. Verify the thermostat call and confirm 24V reaches the outdoor contactor.
2. Check the breaker, disconnect, and fuses for the outdoor unit.
3. Measure **capacitor** microfarads against the rating printed on it.
4. Read **refrigerant pressures** with gauges and check superheat/subcooling — this reveals low charge, a leak, or restriction.
5. Inspect the condenser and evaporator coils for dirt and airflow issues.
6. On Affinity/YXV units, pull stored **fault codes** from the board or communicating thermostat.
7. Test the compressor's windings and amp draw to confirm whether it's failing.
8. If a leak is suspected, perform a **leak search** (dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen pressure test).

A quote that jumps straight to "you need a new compressor" without pressure readings or an electrical test is worth a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, dirty filter | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, run 30 min | Check coil for damage |
| Ice on lines/coil | Low airflow or low charge | Thaw with fan ON, new filter | Find leak, recharge, correct airflow |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit dead | Breaker, capacitor, or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test capacitor/contactor, replace |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None | Replace capacitor |
| Weak cooling, long run times | Low refrigerant leak | None | Leak search + EPA-certified recharge |
| Thermostat blank or wrong mode | Dead batteries/settings | Replace batteries, set COOL | Check thermostat wiring |
| Water around indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged condensate/float switch | Clear standing water, add tablets | Clear drain line, test float switch |
| Runs constantly, no cold air at all | Failing compressor | None | Compressor test/replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor). Regional rates and system size vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$300
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $650–$1,600
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,800 (often near the cost of a new condenser — get a system-age comparison)

If your York unit is out of warranty and 12+ years old, weigh a major repair against replacement. Registered York equipment often carries a 10-year parts limited warranty — check your model's coverage before paying for parts.
