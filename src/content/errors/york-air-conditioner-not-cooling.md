## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific York fault code. Your York air conditioner is running (or trying to run) but the air coming from the vents isn't cold, or the house won't reach the thermostat setpoint. On many York systems there is no single error displayed — the outdoor unit either doesn't start, the indoor blower moves warm air, or the system short-cycles.

Some York communicating systems (Affinity with a communicating thermostat or control board) **will** log fault codes for pressure, lockout, or sensor issues. Conventional York units with a standard thermostat give you no readout at all, so the diagnosis relies on symptoms and instrument readings. Because behavior varies by model and board, treat the causes below as ranked probabilities rather than a guaranteed answer.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow can freeze the evaporator coil and stop cooling entirely.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer and you get warm or no airflow.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), or a misconfigured/communicating thermostat that isn't calling for cooling.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common electrical failure. The compressor or condenser fan hums, won't spin, and cooling stops.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit gets no power, indoor blower may still run warm air.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced capacity, long run times, frozen coil. Requires leak repair and recharge by a pro.
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so the compressor never energizes.
8. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is clogged with dirt/grass, so it can't reject heat. High head pressure, weak cooling.
9. **Condensate overflow / float switch open** — a full drain pan trips the safety switch and stops cooling to prevent water damage.
10. **Failed compressor or control board** — least common but most expensive; the compressor won't start or the board won't command a cooling cycle.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. If none of them fix it, stop and call a pro.

- **Air filter:** Pull it. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to **Cool**, fan to **Auto**, and drop the setpoint 3–5°F below room temperature. Replace batteries if it uses them. Confirm it isn't on a schedule or "hold."
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel and the outdoor disconnect for a tripped breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, do not keep resetting — call a technician.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure vents aren't closed or blocked by furniture, and returns aren't covered.
- **Outdoor unit:** Confirm the condenser fan is spinning and the coil isn't buried in leaves, grass clippings, or lint. Gently clear debris with the power off.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** but leave the **fan On** for a few hours to thaw before running cool again.
- **Condensate drain:** Check for a full drain pan or standing water near the indoor unit — a tripped float switch will stop cooling.

Do **not** open sealed refrigerant lines, bypass any safety switch, or add refrigerant yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York technician will:

1. Confirm the thermostat is calling for cooling and verify 24V at the control board and contactor.
2. Check indoor airflow — filter, blower operation, and evaporator coil condition.
3. Measure capacitor microfarads against the rating printed on it and inspect the contactor.
4. Attach gauges to read suction and head pressures and take a **superheat/subcooling** measurement to judge refrigerant charge.
5. Read temperature split across the evaporator (return vs supply air) — a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F.
6. Inspect the condenser coil and check compressor amp draw and start behavior.
7. On communicating York systems, pull stored fault codes from the board or thermostat to confirm pressure or lockout events.

Sanity check: if a tech says "you're low on refrigerant" but never mentions finding the **leak**, ask where it's leaking — recharging without repair means you'll be paying again soon.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air from vents, filter dirty | Airflow restriction | Replace filter, open vents | Verify blower and coil airflow |
| Ice on lines/coil | Frozen evaporator | Turn to fan-only to thaw | Find low airflow or low charge |
| Outdoor unit silent | Breaker/fuse/contactor | Reset breaker once | Test 24V, contactor, blown fuse |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Runs constantly, weak cooling | Low refrigerant leak | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Weak cooling, hot outside | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris, rinse gently | Deep coil clean, pressure check |
| Cooling stops, water near unit | Condensate float tripped | Clear drain, empty pan | Clear line, replace switch/pump |
| No response, board silent | Control board / compressor | None — call pro | Board/compressor diagnosis |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$10 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Low-voltage fuse / minor wiring:** $100–$250
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location and R-410A amount)
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900
- **Control board:** $400–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to a new system if out of warranty)

Prices vary by region, unit size, and whether your York is still under its parts warranty — register/warranty coverage can drop your out-of-pocket to labor only.
