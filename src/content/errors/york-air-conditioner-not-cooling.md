## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a stored fault code. On most York central air conditioners, it means the system isn't removing enough heat from your home.

Some York units have diagnostics:
- **Communicating and variable-speed models** (some Affinity and LX-series) may show a fault on the outdoor control board LED or a compatible thermostat. Use that code to narrow the cause.
- **Basic single-stage condensers** have no display. Diagnosis comes from observation and technician testing.

This page covers York **air conditioners** only. If you have a York heat pump, see the separate heat-pump not-cooling page. Heat pumps have a reversing valve and defrost controls that a straight AC doesn't.

**Restart behavior:**
- Most York condensers have an anti-short-cycle delay of about 5 minutes after power loss or a thermostat change.
- Models with high- or low-pressure switches may lock out after repeated trips within one call for cooling. Behavior varies by board, so check your installation manual.
- A single power cycle at the breaker or disconnect clears a lockout. If the unit locks out again, stop and call a technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow.** This reduces airflow across the indoor coil and can cause the coil to freeze. It is the most common and cheapest cause.
2. **Thermostat setting or power issue.** Common examples:
   - Mode not set to Cool
   - Fan set to On instead of Auto
   - Dead batteries
   - Setpoint above room temperature
3. **Failed run capacitor.** The outdoor fan or compressor hums but won't start, or the fan spins slowly. This is a very common York outdoor-unit failure in hot weather.
4. **Failed or pitted contactor.** The outdoor unit doesn't energize even though the thermostat is calling for cooling. Insects or ants in the contactor are a frequent culprit.
5. **Tripped breaker, outdoor disconnect off, or blown disconnect fuse.** The indoor blower runs but the condenser is silent.
6. **Condensate float or safety switch tripped.** A clogged drain line raises water in the pan, and the switch cuts the cooling call to prevent a leak.
7. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil.** Cottonwood, grass clippings or dryer lint block heat rejection. This raises head pressure and can trip the high-pressure switch on equipped models.
8. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** Signs include ice on the suction line, long run times and poor temperature split. Evaporator coils and service valves are common leak points.
9. **Frozen indoor coil.** This usually results from causes 1, 7 or 8, or from a weak indoor blower.
10. **Indoor blower or outdoor fan motor failure.** No airflow inside, or a condenser fan that won't spin while the compressor runs.
11. **Control board or low-voltage wiring fault.** Examples include a blown 3A or 5A fuse on the furnace or air-handler board, chewed thermostat wire, or a failed outdoor board on communicating models.
12. **Compressor failure.** Signs include a locked rotor, an open internal overload, or failed windings. This is less common, and the system should be tested before it's condemned.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Thermostat.**
   - Set mode to **Cool**, fan to **Auto**, and the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature.
   - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
   - Wait 5–10 minutes for the anti-short-cycle delay.
2. **Air filter.**
   - Pull the filter. If you can't see light through it, replace it with the same size.
   - If you see ice on the indoor unit or refrigerant lines, turn cooling **off** and set the fan to **On** for 2–4 hours to thaw before restarting.
3. **Breakers and switches.**
   - Check the AC breaker and the furnace or air-handler breaker in the panel.
   - Check the service switch near the indoor unit (it looks like a light switch).
   - Check the outdoor disconnect.
   - Reset a tripped breaker **once only**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a pro.
4. **One reset of a locked-out unit.** Turn the AC off at the thermostat. Switch off the breaker for 5 minutes, then restore it. Do this only once.
5. **Vents and registers.**
   - Open the supply registers.
   - Clear furniture or rugs away from return grilles.
   - Outside, clear plants and debris to at least 2 feet around the condenser.
   - You can gently rinse the outside of the condenser fins with a garden hose (not a pressure washer) with power off at the disconnect.
6. **Condensate.**
   - Check the drain pan and the float switch area for standing water.
   - Make sure the drain line exit outside is flowing.
   - You can clear the line from the access tee with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor end, or pour 1 cup of distilled vinegar into the access port.
7. **Panels.** Make sure the blower door on the furnace or air handler is fully seated. A loose door opens the safety switch and stops the blower.

Stop at that point. Do not open the outdoor unit's electrical panel. It holds capacitor and line-voltage hazards even with the power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically work in this order:

1. **Confirm the call for cooling.**
   - Verify 24V on the Y and C terminals at the condenser.
   - Check the low-voltage fuse on the indoor board.
   - Read any fault code on the outdoor board or thermostat (communicating and variable-speed models).
2. **Check high voltage.**
   - Measure line voltage at the disconnect and the contactor.
   - Inspect the contactor for pitting or debris.
3. **Test the capacitor.** Measure microfarads against the label rating. A reading more than about 6% below rating usually means replacement.
4. **Check the motors.**
   - Check the amp draw of the compressor and condenser fan against nameplate RLA and FLA.
   - Check the compressor windings for open or grounded circuits.
5. **Check airflow.**
   - Measure static pressure.
   - Check the blower speed setting.
   - Inspect the evaporator coil for dirt or ice.
6. **Check refrigerant.**
   - Connect gauges or digital probes.
   - Measure superheat (fixed-orifice systems) or subcooling (TXV systems, which most modern York units use).
   - Compare readings to the charging chart on the unit's panel.
7. **Find leaks.** If the charge is low, the tech should run an electronic leak search, bubble test, or nitrogen pressure test. Recharging alone is not a repair.
8. **Check safeties.** Confirm the high- and low-pressure switches and the condensate float switch are operating normally.

**Red flags on a quote:**
- Condemning the compressor without amp readings or winding tests
- Adding refrigerant with no leak search
- Replacing the control board without checking 24V signals first

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat blank or not calling | Dead batteries, tripped breaker, blown board fuse | Replace batteries, check breakers | Test 24V transformer and board fuse, trace wiring |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or disconnect, failed contactor, low-voltage fault | Check breaker and disconnect, reset once | Test contactor, voltage at Y/C, replace contactor |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan doesn't spin | Failed run capacitor or fan motor | Turn system off to protect compressor | Test and replace capacitor or fan motor |
| Outdoor unit runs, air not cold | Low refrigerant, dirty coil, compressor not running | Replace filter, hose off condenser fins | Gauge readings, leak search, compressor amp test |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Dirty filter, low airflow, low refrigerant | Turn cooling off, fan On to thaw, replace filter | Check charge and blower, find leak |
| Unit stops and water in drain pan | Clogged condensate line tripped float switch | Clear drain line with wet/dry vac or vinegar | Clean pan, check pump and float switch |
| Runs nonstop, house barely cools | Dirty condenser coil, low charge, undersized system, leaky ducts | Clear debris around condenser, close blinds | Clean coils, check charge, duct inspection |
| Starts then shuts off repeatedly | High-pressure trip, weak capacitor, overheating compressor | One power reset only | Test pressures, safeties, capacitor, compressor |
| Fault code on outdoor board or thermostat | Model-specific fault (communicating or variable-speed models) | Note the code, reset once | Diagnose per York service manual for the model |
