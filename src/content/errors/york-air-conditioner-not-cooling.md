## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code on most York air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your York unit may be running, blowing air, and showing no error, yet the air coming from the vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

Depending on your model, York equipment handles faults differently:

- **Basic single-stage York units (LX, Latitude series)** have no display. There's no code — you diagnose by observation and measurement.
- **Communicating York systems (Affinity, YXV variable-capacity)** can store fault codes on the indoor board or communicating thermostat. If a specific code is present, look it up instead of treating this as a generic "not cooling" issue.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, this page focuses on ruling out the easy causes, then explains what a technician should check so you can sanity-check a quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the indoor coil, and kills cooling output. The #1 cause.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — trapped heat can't escape, so the system can't cool efficiently.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the compressor or outdoor fan won't start; you may hear a hum or clicking.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — warm air, ice on the copper line, or hissing. Always a leak, never "used up."
5. **Failed contactor** — the electrical switch that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
6. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; ice blocks cooling entirely.
8. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a wiring/communication fault.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything is beyond them.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A blocked filter is the single most common cause.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's a battery-powered model and the display is dim or blank.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor and outdoor breakers. Reset one **once** if tripped. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting it.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed registers aren't blocking airflow.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance. Do **not** remove panels or bend the fins.
- **Condensate drain:** If the indoor unit is near a full drain pan or you see water, a clogged drain line can trigger a float switch that stops cooling. Clear standing water and add drain tablets.
- **Frozen coil / iced line:** If you see ice on the outdoor copper lines, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** for a few hours to thaw before calling. Running a frozen system can damage the compressor.

If cooling doesn't return after a fresh filter, correct thermostat settings, and restored power, the remaining causes require a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work in this order — use it to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Confirm the complaint** and check thermostat operation and settings.
2. **Verify power** at the outdoor disconnect and measure incoming voltage.
3. **Check the capacitor** with a meter against its rated microfarad value.
4. **Inspect the contactor** for pitting, chatter, or a stuck state.
5. **Measure temperature split** (return vs. supply air) — around 15–20°F is normal.
6. **Take refrigerant readings** with gauges: pressures, superheat, and subcooling. Low readings point to a leak, which they should locate before adding refrigerant.
7. **Inspect coils** (indoor and outdoor) for dirt and ice.
8. **Test the compressor** windings and amperage if all else checks out.
9. **On communicating models,** read stored fault codes from the board or thermostat.

Be wary of a quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search, or "replace the compressor" without ruling out the capacitor and contactor first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Verify airflow, thaw coil |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cooling | Failed capacitor | None (electrical) | Test & replace capacitor |
| Ice on copper lines | Low charge or low airflow | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find leak, recharge |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Humming, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None | Replace capacitor |
| Warm air, hissing sound | Refrigerant leak | None (EPA regulated) | Locate & repair leak, recharge |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Failed compressor or low charge | None | Test compressor, measure charge |
| Blank thermostat | Dead batteries or power loss | Replace batteries | Check control wiring / transformer |
| Water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain, add tablets | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and amount
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,800+ (often near the cost of a new condenser)
- **Full outdoor unit replacement:** $3,500–$7,000+

Diagnostic/service call fees typically run $80–$180 and are often applied toward the repair.

## Related codes

- **York AC not turning on** — no power, blown fuse, or control board fault.
- **York AC freezing up / frozen coil** — low airflow or low refrigerant charge.
- **York communicating fault codes (Affinity / YXV)** — check the board or thermostat for a specific stored code.
- **York AC short cycling** — turns on and off rapidly; often thermostat, capacitor, or charge related.
