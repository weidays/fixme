## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on most York air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your York condenser and indoor air handler may be running, but the air coming from the vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

York equipment ranges from basic single-stage units with no diagnostics to communicating **Affinity** systems with control boards that flash fault codes or display messages at the thermostat. If your system has a communicating board, note any code or LED flash pattern before troubleshooting — it narrows the problem considerably. On older or entry-level units, you'll be diagnosing by symptom alone.

The root cause almost always falls into one of three buckets: **airflow** (filter, coil, blower), **refrigerant** (low charge from a leak), or **electrical** (capacitor, contactor, compressor).

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow can freeze the evaporator coil, which stops cooling entirely.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant. Ice on the copper lines near the air handler is a giveaway.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked with dirt, grass, or debris, so the unit can't reject heat.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — cooling weakens over weeks or months; often paired with icing and hissing.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the fan or compressor hums but won't start, or trips the breaker.
6. **Faulty contactor** — the outdoor unit won't energize at all.
7. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a wiring/C-wire issue.
8. **Failed compressor** — the outdoor fan runs but no cooling; the most expensive outcome.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should attempt:

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" complaints.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Breakers** — check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers** — make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain** — a clogged drain can trigger a safety float switch that shuts the system down. Look for standing water near the indoor unit.
- **Ice check** — look at the copper lines and indoor coil. If you see ice, turn the system to **OFF** but leave the fan on **ON** to help it thaw, then call a technician.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass the float switch, or repeatedly reset a breaker that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good York tech will follow a logical sequence you can sanity-check against the quote:

1. **Verify airflow** — inspect filter, blower, and return ducting.
2. **Check the thermostat and control signal** — confirm the call for cooling reaches the outdoor unit; on Affinity systems they'll read board fault codes.
3. **Measure electrical** — test the capacitor with a meter, check the contactor, and inspect wiring for burnt terminals.
4. **Take refrigerant readings** — connect gauges and measure superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct. Low charge means they'll perform a **leak search** (dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen).
5. **Inspect coils** — check the condenser and evaporator for dirt and icing.
6. **Test the compressor** — measure amp draw and windings if the outdoor fan runs but there's no cooling.

Be wary of any tech who wants to "add a couple pounds of refrigerant" without finding the leak — that's a temporary patch, not a repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm airflow, dirty filter | Clogged filter restricting airflow | Replace the filter | Inspect blower and ducting |
| Ice on indoor coil or lines | Frozen evaporator | Turn AC off, fan on to thaw | Find airflow or refrigerant cause |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — electrical | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit fully dead | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Cooling weakens over weeks, hissing | Refrigerant leak | None | Leak search, seal, recharge |
| Water pooling near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Clear visible clog / check float | Clear drain line, test switch |
| Fan runs outside, no cooling at all | Failed compressor | None | Compressor test / replacement |
| Thermostat blank | Dead batteries or C-wire fault | Replace batteries | Diagnose wiring/power |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $800–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$1,800+ (often makes replacing the whole condenser worth considering)

If your unit is over 12–15 years old and needs a compressor or coil, get a quote for full replacement too — repairing an aging R-22 system rarely pays off.
