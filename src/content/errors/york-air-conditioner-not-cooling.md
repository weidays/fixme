## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on most York air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your York condenser and indoor coil work together to move heat out of your home, and any break in that chain (airflow, electrical, or refrigerant) shows up as warm air from the vents.

Some newer York communicating systems (Affinity with the S1-TTSCC/HX controls) may display a numeric fault or flash a diagnostic LED on the control board. Older LX and Latitude units give you no code at all — just poor cooling. Because behavior varies by model and thermostat, treat the list below as a ranked checklist rather than a single answer.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — the #1 cause. Restricted airflow across the indoor coil drops capacity and can freeze the coil solid.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (HEAT vs COOL), or a schedule holding a high setpoint.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit (condenser) has its own breaker or disconnect that may be off.
4. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is caked with dirt, grass, or cottonwood, so it can't reject heat.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a common $15–$40 part; when it fails the compressor or fan motor won't start (you may hear humming).
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks all cooling.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — never "normal." Requires a licensed tech to find and seal the leak, then recharge.
8. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or burns out.
9. **Bad condenser fan motor** — the outdoor fan won't spin, the unit overheats and shuts down.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome, usually on older systems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Stop if anything is hot, smoking, or smells burnt.

- **Filter:** Pull the indoor filter. If you can't see light through it, replace it and let the system run.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to COOL, drop the setpoint 5°F below room temperature, and confirm the fan is set to AUTO. Replace the batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker for both the air handler and the outdoor unit. Flip it fully OFF, then ON — **once only**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Confirm the pull-out disconnect box near the condenser is fully seated.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit clearance:** Clear weeds, leaves, and debris at least 2 feet around the condenser so it can breathe. Do not spray water into electrical components.
- **Condensate:** If cooling stopped and you see water near the indoor unit, a full condensate pan or clogged drain may have tripped a safety float switch. Clear standing water and check the drain line.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw for several hours before restarting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheap to expensive so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verifies the complaint** — measures supply and return air temperature; a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F.
2. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Tests electrical** — measures voltage at the contactor, tests the run capacitor with a meter, and inspects the fan and compressor windings.
4. **Connects refrigerant gauges** — reads suction and head pressures against the target for your York model and outdoor temperature (superheat/subcooling).
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, uses electronic detector or dye to find the leak *before* adding refrigerant.
6. **Reads any board diagnostics** — on communicating Affinity systems, retrieves stored fault history.

Be wary of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search, or "replace the compressor" without capacitor and electrical testing first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, filter is gray/clogged | Dirty filter restricting airflow | Replace filter, restart | Check coil for resulting ice |
| Nothing runs at all | Tripped breaker / dead thermostat | Reset breaker once, replace batteries | Test transformer, wiring, contactor |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit dead | Failed capacitor or contactor | None — call a pro | Test and replace capacitor/contactor |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Weak run capacitor | None — call a pro | Replace capacitor, test compressor |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Check charge, find leak |
| Cools weakly on hot days only | Dirty condenser coil / low charge | Clear debris, rinse fins gently | Coil cleaning, refrigerant check |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed fan motor | None — call a pro | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant or failed compressor | None — call a pro | Leak repair or compressor quote |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Regional labor rates vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$300
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$1,800
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$1,800+ (often makes replacing an old unit worth considering)

If your York system is 12+ years old and facing a compressor or coil, ask your tech to compare repair cost against a new, higher-efficiency system.

- **York AC short cycling** — overheating, dirty coil, or oversized system
