## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a single fault code on York equipment — it's a symptom. Depending on your system, the thermostat may show the AC running with no cold air, the outdoor unit may not start, or a communicating York thermostat (on Affinity systems) may display a specific fault or lockout number.

Because York uses several control platforms across the LX, Latitude, and Affinity lines, the exact on-board diagnostics vary. Older single-stage units may only flash an LED at the outdoor control board, while newer communicating systems report faults to the thermostat. **If you see a numeric fault code, note it exactly** — that narrows the diagnosis considerably.

At its core, "not cooling" means the refrigeration cycle isn't moving heat out of your home effectively. That can come from airflow problems, electrical faults, or refrigerant issues.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (not set to COOL), or a bad setting.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — traps heat and cripples cooling capacity.
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — very common; the compressor or fan hums but won't start.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, ice on lines, warm supply air.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — often downstream of low airflow or low refrigerant.
8. **Failed condenser fan motor** — unit runs but overheats and locks out.
9. **Failed contactor or control board** — no signal reaching the compressor.
10. **Failed compressor** — least common but most expensive; unit hums or trips on start.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should perform. Stop if anything feels unsafe.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the #1 cause of weak cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated tripping means a real fault.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit airflow:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance. Do not spray water into an energized unit.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan and clear the drain line if you're comfortable doing so.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw fully before running again. Persistent icing points to a pro problem.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a locked-out unit.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the workflow helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** and read any stored fault codes at the board or communicating thermostat.
2. **Check airflow** — filter, blower operation, and coil condition.
3. **Measure electrical values** — incoming voltage, capacitor microfarads, contactor operation, and control voltage.
4. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and take temperature readings to calculate superheat and subcooling. This tells them if the charge is correct.
5. **Leak-search** if refrigerant is low — a proper repair means finding and fixing the leak, not just topping off.
6. **Test the compressor** windings and amperage draw if start problems are suspected.

A trustworthy tech won't just "add a pound of Freon" without explaining where it went. Low charge always means a leak somewhere.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and ducts |
| No cold air, blower runs | Wrong thermostat mode/dead batteries | Set to COOL, replace batteries | Test thermostat wiring |
| Outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker / blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Find cause of trip, replace fuse |
| Compressor/fan hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Diagnose charge / find leak |
| Warm air, hissing, low cooling | Refrigerant leak | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed fan motor | None — call pro | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Unit trips out repeatedly | Compressor or electrical fault | Do NOT keep resetting | Full diagnostic, compressor test |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $100–$400
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$600
- **Coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against a new system if the unit is old)

If your York system is more than 12–15 years old and uses R-22 refrigerant, a major repair like a compressor or coil leak may make full replacement the smarter investment. Ask your tech to price both.

## Related codes

- **York AC frozen coil / iced lines** — usually low airflow or low refrigerant.
- **York AC not turning on** — often breaker, contactor, or control board.
- **York outdoor fan not running** — capacitor or fan motor.
- **York AC short cycling** — thermostat, capacitor, or refrigerant issues.
- **Communicating Affinity thermostat fault codes** — specific numeric codes vary by board; note the exact number for your technician.
