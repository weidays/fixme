## What this code means

"Loud noise" is not a numbered fault code on most Amana air conditioners. It's a symptom: the outdoor condenser or indoor air handler is making a sound that is new, louder than usual, or different in character. Single-stage and two-stage Amana units usually have no board diagnostics for noise. This includes series such as the ASX13/14/16 and ASXC16/18, as well as older Amana split systems. Inverter models like the AVXC20 can store a related fault if a motor or compressor is misbehaving. The fault may show on the outdoor control board's display, and reset behavior varies by board.

The type of sound is your best clue:

- **Buzzing or humming without the fan spinning** usually points to an electrical part: the capacitor or contactor.
- **Grinding or squealing** usually points to the fan motor bearings.
- **Rattling** usually means loose panels, debris, or a loose fan blade.
- **Banging, clanking or loud knocking** can mean internal compressor trouble or a loose compressor mount.
- **Screaming or very high-pitched whining** that continues while the unit runs can mean a compressor under very high pressure. Shut the system off. A different sound is a brief loud noise right at shutdown. Scroll compressors can make this sound as they momentarily run in reverse, and it is normally harmless if it is short. A sustained scream during operation is the warning sign.
- **Hissing or bubbling** can indicate a refrigerant issue.

Severity is **pro**. Beyond the basic checks below, every fix involves electrical components, motors or refrigerant inside the cabinet.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

Amana does not publish a ranked troubleshooting order for noise. This order reflects how often each cause turns up in field service on residential split systems.

1. **Debris or loose exterior panels.** Sticks, leaves or toys in the condenser fan, or a loose access panel or screw, cause rattling and ticking.
2. **Failing outdoor fan motor or bearings.** This causes squealing, grinding, or a growl that worsens as the unit runs. A bent or loose fan blade causes rhythmic thumping.
3. **Weak or failed run capacitor.** This causes loud buzzing or humming while a motor struggles to start. It often happens alongside the fan not spinning; see the "Outdoor fan not spinning" page.
4. **Contactor chatter or a pitted contactor.** This causes buzzing or rapid clicking at the outdoor unit. Low control voltage or a worn contactor are the usual culprits.
5. **Indoor blower problems.** A loose blower wheel, worn motor bearings, or debris in the wheel cause rattling, squealing or thumping at the air handler or furnace.
6. **Loose or worn compressor mounts, or refrigerant lines vibrating.** These cause rattling or banging at startup and shutdown.
7. **Compressor internal failure or a hard-start condition.** This causes loud clanking, knocking, or repeated loud humming followed by a click as the overload trips; see "Compressor not running."
8. **High head pressure or refrigerant issues.** A restriction, overcharge or non-condensables can cause a sustained high-pitched scream while the unit runs. This is different from the brief reverse-rotation sound some scroll compressors make at shutdown. Hissing or gurgling can indicate a leak or a restriction.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the system off at the thermostat** if you hear grinding, screaming, banging or metal scraping. Don't keep running it.
- **Check the thermostat.** Make sure it's set to Cool with a normal setpoint. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank, because erratic calls can cause rapid on/off clicking.
- **Replace the air filter.** A clogged filter makes the indoor blower strain and whistle, and it can make return grilles roar.
- **Check visible vents and registers.** Open closed supply registers and make sure nothing blocks the return grilles. Too many closed vents cause whistling and loud airflow.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from outside.** First turn the power OFF at the thermostat and the outdoor disconnect. Then clear leaves, grass clippings and objects from around the unit and off the top grille by hand. Keep 2 feet of clearance. Don't reach into the fan or remove the top grille.
- **Seat the exterior panels.** If an outer access panel is visibly loose and rattling, press it back into place and snug its exterior screws by hand. Don't open panels.
- **Check the breaker.** If the breaker tripped after a loud noise, you may reset it ONCE. If it trips again, or the noise returns, leave it off and call a pro. See "Breaker keeps tripping."
- **Check the condensate line.** Gurgling at the indoor unit can come from a partially clogged drain. Make sure the line drains freely at its outlet.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent technician should:

1. **Listen and locate.** They'll confirm whether the noise comes from the condenser, the air handler, or the line set, and when it happens: at startup, during the run, or at shutdown.
2. **Inspect the fan.** They'll check the condenser fan for blade damage, set-screw tightness and bearing play, and measure the motor amp draw against the nameplate.
3. **Test the capacitor.** Using a meter, they'll compare the run capacitor's microfarads to its rating (typically within ±6%).
4. **Inspect the contactor.** They'll look for pitting and chatter and measure the 24V control voltage at the coil.
5. **Check the compressor.** They'll measure amp draw (RLA/LRA), winding resistance, and mounting grommets. If it's hard-starting, they may recommend a hard-start kit where appropriate.
6. **Check refrigerant pressures.** They'll take superheat and subcooling readings to rule out overcharge, a restriction, or non-condensables that cause high-pressure screaming.
7. **Check the indoor blower.** They'll inspect the wheel, motor bearings and mounts in the air handler or furnace.
8. **Check fault history.** On communicating or inverter Amana models, they'll read the outdoor board's fault history.

A quote that jumps straight to "replace the compressor" without amp readings, capacitor tests and pressure readings deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rattling outdoors | Debris or loose exterior panel | Power off, clear debris, seat panel | Tighten internal hardware and fan blade |
| Squealing or grinding outdoors | Fan motor bearings failing | Turn system off | Replace condenser fan motor and capacitor |
| Loud buzz, fan not turning | Weak run capacitor or seized motor | Turn off, don't keep restarting | Test and replace capacitor and/or motor |
| Rapid clicking or buzzing at condenser | Contactor chatter or low control voltage | Replace thermostat batteries | Replace contactor, diagnose 24V circuit |
| Banging or clanking at startup or shutdown | Loose compressor mounts or internal compressor damage | Turn system off | Inspect mounts, test compressor, replace if failed |
| Brief loud sound only at shutdown | Scroll compressor momentary reverse rotation | Note how long it lasts; usually harmless if short | Evaluate if it is prolonged or getting worse |
| Sustained high-pitched scream while running | Very high refrigerant pressure | Shut off immediately | Check pressures, clear restriction, correct charge |
| Hissing or bubbling | Refrigerant leak or restriction | Turn off, note location | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Whistling at vents or return | Dirty filter or closed registers | Replace filter, open vents | Evaluate duct sizing and static pressure |
| Thumping or rattling indoors | Blower wheel loose or dirty, motor bearings | Replace filter | Clean or secure wheel, replace blower motor |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, parts and labor:

| Repair | Cost range |
|---|---|
| Debris removal, tightening panels (DIY) | $0 |
| Service call and diagnosis | $75 – $200 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $120 – $350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Condenser fan blade | $150 – $400 |
| Condenser fan motor (PSC, single- and two-stage units) | $300 – $700 |
| Condenser fan motor (ECM / inverter, e.g. AVXC20) | $700 – $1,500+ |
| Indoor blower motor (PSC / ECM) | $400 – $900 / $700 – $1,800 |
| Hard-start kit | $150 – $400 |
| Compressor mount and grommet repair | $150 – $400 |
| Refrigerant leak repair and recharge | $300 – $1,500+ |
| Compressor replacement, part under warranty (labor and refrigerant only) | $1,000 – $2,500 |
| Compressor replacement, out of warranty | $1,500 – $3,500+, with R-454B and inverter compressors such as the AVXC20's at the higher end |

Costs vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs. R-454B on newer units), and whether the parts are under Amana's warranty.
