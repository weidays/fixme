## What this code means

"Ice on indoor coil" is a visible symptom, not a numbered fault code. Frost or ice has formed on the evaporator coil inside the wall-mounted head, ceiling cassette, or ducted air handler of your York mini-split. You may see:

- White frost behind the louvers or filters
- Weak or no airflow
- Water dripping when the ice melts

The indoor coil freezes when its surface drops below 32°F. That happens when too little warm air crosses the coil, or when the refrigerant side runs too cold.

**How York units respond varies by series and indoor-unit type.** Many York inverter mini-splits have indoor coil temperature sensing and anti-freeze protection. When the coil gets too cold, the unit slows or pauses the compressor and resumes on its own once the coil warms up. Some models also show a protection code or blink pattern while this happens. That self-resetting protection can hide a developing problem until visible ice appears.

If your unit is showing a code, see the York Mini-Split Blinking Light Error page and check the code against your model's manual.

This page covers the mini-split indoor head. For central split systems with a furnace or air handler, see York AC Frozen Evaporator Coil.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty indoor filters.** Mini-split mesh filters clog quickly with dust and pet hair. This is the most common cause by far.
2. **Dirty indoor coil or blower wheel.** The barrel-style blower wheel in wall-mount heads collects buildup, sometimes with mold or biofilm. This cuts airflow even when the filters are clean.
3. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Flare connections at the indoor head and line set are a well-known leak point on ductless systems. Low charge drops coil pressure and temperature, so ice often starts at one section of the coil.
4. **Indoor fan motor or fan control fault.** If the blower runs slowly or stops while the compressor keeps running, the coil freezes quickly.
5. **Faulty indoor coil temperature sensor (thermistor).** If the sensor reads wrong, anti-freeze protection doesn't kick in when it should.
6. **Operating conditions outside design range.** Examples include running cooling or dry mode for long periods at the lowest setpoint and lowest fan speed when it's cool outdoors. Behavior depends on the model's low-ambient capability.
7. **Expansion valve (EEV) or restriction problems.** A failing electronic expansion valve, a kinked line set, or a restriction underfeeds the coil. Less common, but documented.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Stop the freeze.** Turn the unit off, or set it to fan-only on high, and let the ice melt completely. Put a towel under the indoor unit. Do not chip, scrape, or use a heat gun on the ice.
2. **Clean the filters.** Lift the front panel and slide out the mesh filters. Vacuum them, or rinse with lukewarm water. Let them dry completely and reinstall. Clean them every 2 to 4 weeks during heavy use.
3. **Check the remote settings.** Use Cool mode, auto or medium fan, and a reasonable setpoint (about 72-78°F). Avoid long runs at the lowest setpoint on low fan. Replace the remote batteries if the display is faint or unresponsive.
4. **Clear the airflow path.** Move curtains, furniture, or shelves that block the air intake on top or the louvered outlet. Make sure the louvers aren't stuck closed.
5. **Check the condensate drain.** Watch for overflow while the ice melts. Make sure the visible drain line outside isn't kinked, crushed, or blocked at the outlet.
6. **Do one power reset if needed.** If the unit stopped with a code after thawing, reset it once at the breaker or disconnect. Restart in cool mode and watch for 30-60 minutes.
   - If frost returns, stop resetting and call a technician.
   - If the unit locks out again, stop resetting and call a technician.

**Do not do these yourself:**
- Remove the front cabinet or spray coil cleaner into the head.
- Touch the blower wheel or sensors.
- Tighten flare nuts.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified HVAC technician, EPA 608 certified for refrigerant work, will typically:

1. **Confirm airflow.** They inspect the filters, coil face, and blower wheel, and verify the fan reaches its commanded speeds.
2. **Check refrigerant performance.** They take readings at the outdoor unit's service port and compare suction pressure, superheat, and subcooling against York's charging data for that model.
3. **Leak-search if the charge is low.** This means an electronic leak detector, bubble solution, or nitrogen pressure test. They pay special attention to indoor flare joints. York and industry practice is to find and repair the leak, then evacuate and weigh in the charge. Simply "topping off" is not proper practice.
4. **Test the sensors.** They check the indoor coil and room thermistors against the manufacturer's resistance-versus-temperature table.
5. **Check the expansion valve.** They verify EEV operation and look for restrictions or a kinked line set.
6. **Read stored codes.** They review stored fault history on the indoor or outdoor board, where supported.

**Red flags in a quote:**
- Adding refrigerant without a leak search
- Recommending a full system replacement before checking airflow and charge

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frost across the whole coil, weak airflow | Clogged filters | Thaw, then clean the filters | Not needed if fixed |
| Ice returns with clean filters, musty smell | Dirty coil or blower wheel | Keep filters clean; schedule service | Deep-clean the coil and blower wheel (may require removal) |
| Ice on one section or near the lines, poor cooling | Low refrigerant from a leak | Shut off, thaw, call a pro | Leak search, flare or line repair, evacuate, weigh in charge |
| Fan slow or stopped while the outdoor unit runs | Indoor fan motor or control fault | Shut off, one reset, call if it repeats | Test and replace the fan motor or control board |
| Freezes despite normal airflow, no protection code | Faulty coil thermistor | Note conditions, call a pro | Test resistance, replace the sensor |
| Freezes on cool nights at a low setpoint | Out-of-range operation | Raise setpoint, use auto or higher fan | Check low-ambient settings for the model |
| Recurring ice, odd pressures | EEV or restriction | Call a pro | Diagnose and replace the EEV or clear the restriction |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges and vary by region and access.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter cleaning | $0 (DIY) |
| Service call and diagnosis | $100-$250 |
| Indoor coil and blower wheel deep cleaning | $150-$500 per head (higher if the wheel is removed) |
| Coil temperature sensor replacement | $150-$350 |
| Indoor fan motor replacement | $300-$700 |
| Indoor control board | $350-$900 |
| Flare leak repair plus evacuation and recharge | $400-$1,200 |
| Line set or coil leak repair, recharge | $1,000-$2,500+ |
| EEV replacement | $500-$1,200 |
| Indoor head replacement (if the coil is unrepairable) | $1,200-$2,500+ |

Warranty coverage may reduce parts costs. York parts warranties often require registration, so check your registration status before approving the work.
