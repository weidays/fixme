## What this code means

"Auxiliary heat on constantly" is not a stored fault code. It is a symptom: your thermostat shows "Aux Heat" (or "Backup Heat") for long stretches, even in mild weather. On a Bryant heat pump system, aux heat comes from one of two sources:
- electric heat strips in the fan coil, or
- the gas furnace in a dual-fuel setup.

The thermostat or communicating control calls for aux heat for one of two reasons:
- the heat pump can't raise the indoor temperature fast enough, or
- it was told to use aux heat by a setting or wiring connection.

So constant aux heat usually means one of these:
- **The heat pump is underperforming or not running.** The backup heat is covering for it.
- **A thermostat setting is calling for aux heat when it shouldn't.**
- **A heat strip relay or sequencer is stuck closed.** In that case the strips run whether the thermostat calls for them or not.

The system doesn't lock out, and nothing needs to be reset. The behavior clears once the cause is fixed. If the outdoor unit isn't heating at all, see "Bryant Heat Pump Not Heating" first. Evolution-communicating systems may also log specific outdoor-unit faults on the thermostat's service screen. Exact fault names vary by model and control.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat setup or use.** Common triggers include:
   - Emergency Heat was left on.
   - The setpoint is raised by 2-3°F or more at a time.
   - Setback/recovery programs bring the system back up from a lower temperature.
   - The equipment type or O/B reversing valve setting is wrong.
   - No aux-heat lockout (balance point) is set.
2. **Restricted airflow.** A dirty filter, closed registers or a blocked return reduce heating capacity, so the system leans on aux heat.
3. **Heat pump not running or underperforming.** The outdoor unit may have failed (compressor, contactor, capacitor or fan), or the refrigerant charge may be low from a leak. The strips then carry the whole load.
4. **Defrost problems.** Aux heat normally runs during defrost. If the unit defrosts too often or gets stuck in defrost, it looks like constant aux heat. See "Bryant Heat Pump Stuck in Defrost Mode" and "Bryant Heat Pump Frozen, Not Defrosting."
5. **Reversing valve stuck or mis-signaled.** The heat pump runs but produces little or no heat.
6. **Stuck heat strip relay or sequencer, or a wiring fault on the W/aux terminal.** The strips run even with no call for heat. Sometimes they run even with the thermostat off.
7. **Faulty outdoor temperature sensor or wrong balance-point settings** on communicating or dual-fuel systems. The control then thinks it is colder outside than it is and switches to backup heat.
8. **Undersized heat pump or a poorly insulated home.** In real cold snaps, constant aux heat can be expected behavior, not a fault.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat mode.** Make sure it is set to Heat, not Em Heat or Emergency Heat. Raise the setpoint by only 1-2°F at a time.
- **Thermostat batteries.** Replace them if the display is dim or you see a low-battery warning.
- **Thermostat settings.** In the installer menu, check:
  - the system type (heat pump, with or without aux),
  - an aux-heat lockout or balance-point temperature (often 35-40°F),
  - whether "smart recovery" is turned on.

  If you're not sure what a setting does, write down the original values before changing anything.
- **Air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty.
- **Vents.** Open all supply registers and clear any blocked return grilles.
- **Outdoor unit.** While the thermostat calls for heat, look and listen:
  - Is the fan spinning?
  - Is the compressor humming?
  - Is warm air coming from the indoor vents?

  Also clear snow, leaves and debris from around the unit. Don't open any panels.
- **Breakers.** Check the outdoor unit's breaker and outdoor disconnect, plus the indoor unit's breaker. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.
- **Condensate line.** On the indoor unit, make sure it isn't clogged or overflowing.
- **Strips running with the thermostat off.** If you hear or smell the strips heating with the thermostat set to Off, switch off the indoor unit's breaker and call a pro. This points to a stuck relay.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Verify the thermostat calls.** They check whether the W2/aux terminal is energized while only Y (and O/B) should be. On communicating systems, they review the call status on the service screens.
- **Check the outdoor unit.** Measure compressor and fan amps, and inspect the contactor and capacitor.
- **Measure the temperature split** across the indoor coil with the strips locked out. A healthy heat pump usually delivers a roughly 20-30°F rise in mild weather.
- **Check refrigerant charge** using pressures, superheat and subcooling. If it's low, leak-search before adding refrigerant.
- **Test the reversing valve** solenoid and its operation.
- **Check the heat strips.** Test the sequencers and relays for welded contacts, and check strip amperage with no call for heat.
- **Check defrost operation.** Verify the defrost board, coil sensor and outdoor air sensor readings against their temperature-resistance charts.
- **Confirm settings.** Check the aux lockout and balance-point settings, and the dual-fuel changeover settings.

A sound quote names the specific failed component. Be cautious of "add refrigerant" quotes that don't include a leak check.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aux shows after every setpoint change | Thermostat recovery or staging | Raise the setpoint 1-2°F at a time; review recovery settings | Configure aux lockout and staging |
| "Em Heat" shown on display | Emergency heat selected | Switch to normal Heat | None, unless the heat pump has failed |
| Outdoor unit silent during heat call | Outdoor unit failure or tripped breaker | Reset the breaker once | Diagnose the contactor, capacitor, compressor or control |
| Outdoor unit runs, air lukewarm, aux constant | Low charge or reversing valve problem | Replace the filter; open vents | Leak search and recharge; reversing valve diagnosis |
| Aux on during frequent defrosts | Defrost sensor or board fault | Clear snow and debris from the unit | Test the defrost board and sensors |
| Strips heat with thermostat off | Stuck sequencer or relay | Shut off the indoor breaker | Replace the relay or sequencer; check the wiring |
| Aux constant only below ~20°F | Normal capacity limit | Nothing to fix | Optional load or sizing review |
