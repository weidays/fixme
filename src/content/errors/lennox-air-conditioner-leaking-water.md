## What this code means

"Leaking water" is a symptom, not a fault code. Lennox outdoor condensers do not display an error for it.

In cooling mode, the indoor evaporator coil pulls moisture out of the air. A typical system produces several gallons of condensate a day. That water drips into a drain pan and flows out through a condensate line, or through a condensate pump.

A leak at the indoor unit almost always means water is not leaving that path. If water is pooling around the outdoor unit in summer, it is usually just the condensate line discharging there, which is normal.

Some installations include a float (safety) switch in the pan or drain line. If yours has one, it cuts cooling when water backs up. That can make the AC seem dead with no leak at all. The system restarts by itself once the water drains and the switch drops. There is no lockout to reset.

Whether you have a float switch depends on the installer and local code. It is not standard on every Lennox setup.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged condensate drain line.** Algae, sludge, dust or insects block the PVC line, and the pan overflows. This is by far the most common cause, especially after the first hot weeks of the season.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil melting.** A dirty filter, weak airflow or low refrigerant ices the coil. When it thaws, the water overwhelms the pan. This topic is covered separately in Lennox AC Frozen Evaporator Coil.
3. **Failed or unplugged condensate pump.** This applies to attic, basement or closet units without a gravity drain. The pump's float sticks, its motor fails or it loses power.
4. **Cracked, rusted or shifted drain pan.** Older metal pans rust through, and plastic pans can crack. A coil that is not level can tip water past the pan.
5. **Disconnected or poorly installed drain.** Causes include a separated fitting, a missing or dry trap, inadequate slope, or a secondary drain that was never connected. These show up most often in new installs.
6. **Air leaks or sweating ductwork.** Uninsulated ducts or a gap in the coil cabinet produce condensation outside the pan. This is more common in attics and humid climates.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Switch the thermostat to OFF** (fan to AUTO) to stop producing more condensate. Mop up water and protect the ceiling and floor below.
- **Check the air filter.** If it is clogged, replace it. A dirty filter is a leading cause of coil freezing and the resulting meltwater.
- **Look for ice.** If the refrigerant lines or coil area are frosted, leave the cooling off and run the fan only, so the ice thaws before you restart.
- **Clear the condensate line from the outside end.** Find where the PVC drain exits, usually near the outdoor unit or a floor drain. Hold a wet/dry vac tightly over the opening and run it for 1 to 2 minutes. If an access tee with a cap is accessible on the line near the indoor unit, pour in a cup of white vinegar or water and confirm it flows out.
- **Check the condensate pump, if you have one.** Make sure it is plugged in and the outlet has power. Also make sure its discharge tube is not kinked. Do not open the pump's housing or wiring.
- **Check the secondary drain.** Some systems have a second line that ends above a window or eave. Water dripping from it means the primary drain is blocked, so call for service.
- **Check the breaker and the indoor unit's service switch.** Confirm both are on. If the float switch has shut the system off, it will restart once the water drains. Do not bypass or remove the switch.

Do not remove the indoor unit's cabinet panels or touch the coil, pan, wiring or float-switch connections. That is technician work.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Inspect the primary pan, the overflow or auxiliary pan, and the float switch for standing water and corrosion.
- Flush the drain line with a pump, nitrogen or a drain snake, and confirm the trap and slope. Lennox and code guidance typically call for at least 1/4 inch of fall per foot and a trap on draw-through coils.
- Check that the coil is level and that the pan is intact and seated.
- Test the condensate pump's operation and its safety switch wiring.
- Measure airflow, temperature drop across the coil and refrigerant pressures if freezing is suspected.
- Inspect duct and plenum insulation and the cabinet seals for sweating.

A credible quote names the specific failure, such as "drain blocked at trap" or "pan rusted through." It should not just say "service AC."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water around indoor unit, AC still cooling | Clogged drain line | Shop-vac the outdoor end; vinegar through the access tee | Flush or snake line, repair trap |
| AC stopped and pan is full of water | Float switch tripped by blocked drain | Clear the drain line; let it restart on its own | Clear the drain, verify the switch |
| Ice on lines, then puddles | Frozen coil | Replace filter, run fan only to thaw | Airflow and refrigerant diagnosis |
| Pump reservoir full, water overflowing | Failed condensate pump | Confirm it is plugged in and has power | Replace pump |
| Leak continues with a clear drain | Cracked or rusted pan, coil not level | Turn cooling off, contain the water | Replace pan or level the coil |
| Water dripping from the secondary line by a window | Primary drain blocked | Turn cooling off, schedule service | Clear the primary drain |
| Drips from ducts or ceiling registers | Sweating ductwork | Keep supply registers open | Seal and insulate ducts |

## Repair costs

| Repair | Typical US cost |
|---|---|
| DIY drain clearing or filter change | $0–$40 |
| Professional drain line clearing | $75–$250 |
| Float or safety switch install or replacement | $100–$300 |
| Condensate pump replacement | $150–$450 |
| Drain line or trap re-pipe | $150–$500 |
| Secondary or overflow pan install | $150–$400 |
| Primary drain pan replacement | $300–$1,000+ (labor-heavy; coil may need removal) |
| Duct insulation or sealing | $200–$1,500 |
| Evaporator coil replacement (if the pan is integral and failed) | $1,200–$2,800+ |

Prices vary by region, unit location (attic jobs cost more) and warranty status. Lennox parts warranties typically require registration, and they cover parts, not labor.
