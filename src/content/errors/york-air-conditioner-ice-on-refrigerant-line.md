## What this code means

"Ice on refrigerant line" is a visible symptom, not a numbered York fault code. You will usually see frost or solid ice on the larger, insulated copper pipe (the suction line). That line runs from your indoor coil to the outdoor condensing unit, and the ice often shows up where the line enters the outdoor service valve.

The suction line is normally cool and sweating, around 40–50°F. Ice means the refrigerant inside it has dropped below 32°F. That happens when the system is not picking up enough heat at the indoor coil, or when the refrigerant pressure is too low.

Line ice often goes hand-in-hand with a frozen evaporator coil. This page focuses on what the line ice tells you and on the refrigerant-side causes. For coil icing driven by airflow, see the related Frozen evaporator coil guide.

Key behavior:
- There is nothing to "reset." Most York split ACs have no control board that flags this.
- Ice melts once cooling stops, but returns if the cause remains.
- On models with a low-pressure switch, the compressor may lock out or short cycle. Behavior varies by model.

**First step:** set the thermostat to OFF and the fan to ON. Let all ice melt fully before running cooling again.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Restricted airflow across the indoor coil.** A clogged filter, closed or blocked registers, or a dirty evaporator coil means too little warm air reaches the coil. Refrigerant pressure then drops and the suction line freezes. This is the most common cause and the first one to rule out.
2. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Common leak points on York and other split systems include:
   - evaporator coil leaks (formicary corrosion on copper/aluminum coils)
   - flare or brazed joints
   - Schrader valve cores at the service ports

   Ice often starts at the outdoor service valve and creeps back toward the house.
3. **Indoor blower problem.** A blower running at too low a speed, a failing blower motor or capacitor, or a wrong speed tap after a repair all reduce airflow. Ice shows on the coil and the suction line.
4. **Metering device fault.** A sticking or failed TXV, a TXV with a lost or loose sensing bulb, or a wrong-size piston can underfeed the coil. Some York models use a piston and others a TXV, so this varies by unit.
5. **Refrigerant restriction.** A clogged liquid-line filter drier or a kinked line set causes a pressure drop. Frost may appear at the restriction point itself rather than at the suction line.
6. **Running in cool outdoor temperatures.** Running the AC when it is below about 60–65°F outside, without a low-ambient kit, can drop pressures enough to freeze the line. This is common on cool nights in spring and fall.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thaw first.** Set the thermostat to OFF with the fan ON (or AUTO). Do not chip, scrape or heat the copper line.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it is gray, dusty or clogged. Use the size printed on the old filter's frame.
- **Open all supply registers and return grilles.** Move furniture, rugs and curtains off them. Do not close more than a couple of rooms.
- **Check the thermostat.**
  - Confirm it is not set extremely low (below about 68°F) for long run times.
  - Replace the batteries if the display is dim.
  - Make sure the fan is not stuck on a low setting by a smart-thermostat schedule.
- **Check the condensate line and drain pan.** When ice melts it produces a lot of water. Make sure the drain is flowing and the pan is not overflowing. If the pan has a float switch that tripped, the system may stay off until the water clears.
- **Make sure the indoor unit's panels are seated.** A loose blower door can disable the blower or cause air bypass.
- **Avoid cooling on cool days.** If it is under about 60°F outside, don't run cooling.
- **Test-run after thawing.** Run cooling and check the suction line after 30–60 minutes.
  - Cool and sweating is normal.
  - Frost returning means it is time for a technician.
  - If the system shuts off or trips a breaker, stop and call.

Do not open the indoor cabinet or outdoor unit, add refrigerant, or touch the service ports. Refrigerant work requires EPA Section 608 certification.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will:

1. **Confirm a full thaw.** Readings taken on a frozen coil are meaningless.
2. **Measure airflow.** Expect a temperature split across the coil (usually 16–22°F), a static pressure test, and a check of blower speed settings against York's specs for your tonnage.
3. **Inspect the evaporator coil.** They look for dirt, matting or bio-growth and clean it if needed.
4. **Connect gauges or digital probes.** They measure:
   - suction and liquid pressures
   - superheat (for piston systems) or subcooling (for TXV systems), compared to York's charging chart on the unit's data plate
5. **Leak search if the charge is low.** Methods include an electronic detector, bubble solution, UV dye, or a nitrogen standing-pressure test. You should be told where the leak is, not just "it needed a top-off."
6. **Check the metering device.** They confirm whether you have a piston or TXV and verify its size or bulb mounting. A TXV fault looks like high superheat with normal or high subcooling.
7. **Check for restrictions.** A temperature drop across the filter drier or a kinked line points to a restriction.
8. **Check blower components.** This includes the blower capacitor and motor amperage.

**Red flags in a quote:**
- Adding refrigerant with no leak search on a system that has been working for years
- Quoting a new system without stating measured pressures, superheat or subcooling

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on line, dirty filter, weak airflow at vents | Airflow restriction | Thaw, replace filter, open registers | Clean coil if ice returns |
| Ice returns within a day even with a new filter | Low refrigerant from leak | Thaw, stop cooling, call | Leak search, repair, evacuate, recharge by weight or chart |
| Ice on line, very weak or no indoor airflow | Blower motor, capacitor or speed setting | Check thermostat fan setting, seat blower door | Test capacitor and motor, set correct speed tap |
| Frost at outdoor service valve, poor cooling, long run times | Low charge or metering underfeed | Thaw, call | Measure superheat/subcooling, check TXV or piston |
| Frost at one spot on the small liquid line or drier | Restriction (filter drier, kink) | Turn off, call | Replace drier, repair line set |
| Ice only on cool nights | Low outdoor ambient operation | Don't cool below ~60°F outside | Install low-ambient kit if cooling is needed |
| Water on floor after thaw | Meltwater overwhelming drain | Clear condensate line, check pan | Clean drain, check float switch |
