## What this symptom means

"Won't turn on" is a symptom, not a stored fault code. It means a call for cooling produces no response at all. The indoor blower does not start, the outdoor unit stays silent, and often the thermostat screen is blank.

This page covers the "nothing happens" case. Other situations have their own guides:

- If the indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit doesn't, see Trane AC Fan Runs But No Cold Air.
- If only the compressor is silent while the outdoor fan spins, see Trane AC Compressor Not Running.

In almost every case, the problem is one of two things:

- **Lost power** to the indoor or outdoor unit.
- **Lost 24-volt control power.** This low-voltage signal comes from a transformer in the furnace or air handler and runs to the thermostat.

Behavior varies by model:

- **Conventional (24 V) Trane systems** simply sit idle until power or the control signal returns. There is no lockout to clear.
- **Communicating systems** (ComfortLink II, Link) display alerts on the thermostat. Some of these faults hold until power is cycled. Check your thermostat or outdoor unit manual for your specific model.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat settings or dead batteries.** The thermostat may be set to Heat or Off, the setpoint may be above room temperature, or the screen is blank because the batteries are dead.
2. **Tripped breaker or open service switch.** This could be the breaker for the indoor unit (furnace or air handler) or the outdoor condenser. The furnace's light-switch-style service switch is often turned off by accident.
3. **Condensate float or overflow switch tripped.** A clogged drain line raises water in the pan, and the switch cuts 24 V power. This is very common in attic air handlers.
4. **Blower door safety switch open.** If the furnace or air handler front panel is not seated, a door switch kills power to the control board.
5. **Blown 3- or 5-amp low-voltage fuse on the indoor board.** This is usually caused by a shorted thermostat wire, often in the outdoor run where it has been chewed or nicked.
6. **Failed 24 V transformer or indoor control board.**
7. **Broken thermostat wiring or a failed thermostat.**
8. **Outdoor-side failures, if only the condenser is dead.** These include a failed contactor, a high- or low-pressure switch open, or a communicating outdoor control fault. These overlap with the compressor and outdoor fan guides.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set it to Cool and lower the setpoint 3–5°F below room temperature. Replace the batteries if the screen is blank or dim. Then wait 5 minutes, because many thermostats and outdoor controls impose a delay of up to about 5 minutes before the system starts.
- **Breakers:** At the main panel, check both the AC/condenser breaker and the furnace or air handler breaker. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Switches:**
  - Make sure the service switch near the furnace or air handler is on.
  - At the outdoor disconnect box, confirm the lever or switch is in the ON position. Do not pull out the disconnect block or open the cover.
  - If the pull-out block looks damaged, or checking it would mean opening the cover, call a technician.
- **Exterior panels:** Confirm the furnace or air handler front panel is fully seated and latched. Do not open it.
- **Condensate:**
  - Look for water in the secondary drain pan or a tripped float switch on the drain line.
  - If you see water, clear the condensate line at its access port or outside drain end with a wet/dry vacuum.
  - Allow time for the switch to reset.
- **Filter and vents:** Replace a clogged filter and open the supply registers. These won't usually stop startup, but they prevent repeat problems.

If none of this works, stop here. Fuses, transformers, wiring and contactors involve live voltage inside the cabinet.

**Why this page is rated for a pro:** The most likely causes (thermostat, breaker, float switch, door panel) are homeowner checks, so start with the steps above. But once those checks fail, every remaining cause is inside the cabinet or the outdoor unit and needs a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

1. They confirm line voltage at the indoor unit and at the outdoor disconnect.
2. They measure 24 VAC across R and C at the indoor board. If there is none, they check the transformer's primary and secondary sides and the low-voltage fuse.
3. They check continuity through the door switch and the float or overflow switch.
4. If the fuse is blown, they isolate the thermostat and outdoor wires to find the short before replacing the fuse.
5. They jumper R to Y and G at the board to test whether the thermostat or its wiring is the fault.
6. At the condenser, they check for 24 V at the contactor coil, the contactor's condition, and the pressure switches.
7. On communicating systems, they read the stored alerts from the thermostat or control's diagnostic menu.

**Red flag:** A quote to replace the compressor or the entire system for a "won't turn on" problem, without documented voltage readings, deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat screen blank | Dead batteries, tripped breaker, float switch, blown fuse, failed transformer | Replace batteries; check breakers and service switch; check drain | Test 24 V, replace fuse or transformer, find the short |
| Screen on, nothing runs | Wrong mode/setpoint, delay timer, broken wire, failed thermostat | Set to Cool, lower setpoint, wait 5 min | Jumper test, repair wiring, replace thermostat |
| Water in pan or overflow line | Clogged condensate drain tripped float switch | Vacuum and clear the condensate line | Clean or flush the drain, replace the float switch |
| Worked until a filter change or service visit | Front panel not seated (door switch) | Re-seat and latch the exterior panel | Replace a faulty door switch |
| Fuse blows repeatedly | Shorted thermostat or outdoor control wire | None beyond one breaker reset | Trace and replace the damaged wire |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent (cross-reference: not covered on this page) | Contactor, pressure switch, outdoor breaker | Check the outdoor breaker once; confirm the disconnect is ON | See Trane AC Fan Runs But No Cold Air |
| Breaker trips immediately | Electrical short or compressor fault | Do not keep resetting | Full electrical diagnosis |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges, including labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Batteries, settings, breaker reset, drain clearing (DIY) | $0 – $20 |
| Service call and diagnosis | $90 – $200 |
| Condensate drain clearing (pro) | $100 – $250 |
| Float switch replacement | $120 – $250 |
| Low-voltage fuse plus finding the short | $100 – $300 |
| Thermostat wire repair or replacement | $150 – $400 |
| Door switch replacement | $120 – $250 |
| 24 V transformer replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Standard thermostat replacement | $150 – $400 |
| Communicating thermostat replacement | $300 – $700+ |
| Indoor control board replacement | $400 – $1,200 |
| Communicating outdoor control board replacement | $400 – $1,200+ |

Trane OEM boards cost more than universal boards. On communicating (ComfortLink II, Link) systems, OEM boards plus labor commonly land at the upper end of these ranges.
