## What this code means

An **ignition lockout** on a Goodman furnace means the control board asked the burners to light, waited through its trial-for-ignition sequence, and never proved a stable flame. Goodman controls make a fixed number of attempts — usually **three trials for ignition** — then shut off gas and go into lockout so the furnace stops dumping unburned fuel.

On many Goodman boards this fault shows as **1 red flash** ("ignition failure / lockout"). The exact indicator varies by board, so check the diagnostic label inside your furnace door.

**Reset behavior:** Goodman ignition lockout is usually a **soft lockout** — on many boards it **auto-resets after about an hour** and retries. That timing is not universal: single-stage, two-stage and modulating Goodman controls don't all use the same trial count or retry delay, and the diagnostic chart on your furnace door label is what governs your specific unit. You can also clear the lockout once immediately by cycling power at the furnace switch or breaker. If it locks out again, the problem is real and needs a technician.

This page covers the **general ignition-lockout condition**. If your board flashes a specific pattern, use the exact code page (see Related codes).

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or failed flame sensor** — burners light but the board can't prove flame, so it counts the trial as failed and eventually locks out. This is the most common real-world reason a Goodman furnace repeatedly fails to prove flame.
2. **Hot-surface igniter failure** — a weak igniter glows but never gets hot enough to light the burners reliably, or a cracked/open igniter doesn't glow at all and ignition never happens.
3. **Weak or misaligned burner ignition** — carbon buildup, dirty burners, or slightly wrong igniter/burner geometry cause unreliable lighting.
4. **Gas-supply problems** — low gas pressure, a partially closed manual valve, or a failing gas valve prevents a stable flame.
5. **Control board fault** — the board mis-times the ignition sequence or fails to open the gas valve at the right moment.
6. **Wiring/connection issues** — loose or corroded connections at the igniter, flame sensor, or gas valve.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Replace the **thermostat batteries** if it uses them.
- **Air filter:** A severely clogged filter can trip limits and interfere with normal operation. Replace it if it's dirty.
- **Breaker / furnace switch:** Cycle the furnace breaker or the wall switch **once** to clear the lockout and let it retry.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate line (high-efficiency models):** Check that the drain isn't clogged or overflowing.
- **Panels:** Confirm the front blower-access panel is fully seated — the door switch must be closed for the furnace to run.

**One reset only.** If the furnace locks out again after a single reset, stop and call a pro. Do not repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

- Read the stored fault/flash code and note how far the sequence gets (glow, gas, flame, or nothing).
- Measure the **hot-surface igniter resistance** and inspect it for cracks or hot spots.
- Check the **flame-sensor microamp signal** and clean or replace the sensor.
- Verify **inlet and manifold gas pressure** with a manometer and confirm the gas valve opens on command.
- Inspect burners for carbon, rust, or misalignment and check igniter positioning.
- Test the **control board** timing outputs to the igniter and gas valve, and check wiring/grounds.

This lets you sanity-check a quote: a real diagnosis measures igniter resistance, flame current, and gas pressure — not just a parts swap.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Igniter glows, no flame | Weak igniter or low gas pressure | Reset once; replace filter | Test/replace igniter; check gas pressure |
| Burners light then shut off | Dirty/failed flame sensor | Reset once | Clean or replace flame sensor; verify microamps |
| No glow at all | Cracked/open igniter or board | Reset once | Test igniter resistance; replace igniter or board |
| Locks out every cycle | Repeated failed ignition | Stop resetting; call pro | Full ignition diagnosis; gas pressure test |
| Auto-clears then fails about an hour later | Soft lockout retrying | None | Diagnose root ignition fault |
| Rough or delayed light-off | Dirty burners / misaligned igniter | Replace filter | Clean burners; reposition igniter |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges for 2026 (parts + labor, varies by region):

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $100 – $250
- **Hot-surface igniter replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Burner cleaning / adjustment:** $150 – $350
- **Gas valve replacement:** $400 – $900+
- **Integrated control board:** $400 – $1,200 (single-stage boards sit at the low end; two-stage and variable-speed boards, and high-cost metros, push toward the top)

A **filter or single reset** is $0 DIY. Most real ignition-lockout repairs land in the **$150–$400** range once a technician confirms the failed part; gas valve and control board replacements are the outliers that carry the page's upper costs.
