## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific blink code — it's the symptom you notice at the thermostat: the system runs (or tries to) but the house won't reach setpoint. On Trane equipment this can come from the indoor side (air handler/furnace blower, evaporator coil, filter), the outdoor side (condenser, compressor, capacitor, contactor), the refrigerant charge, or the controls tying them together.

Some Trane systems with communicating controls (XV/XL variable-speed units paired with a ComfortLink II thermostat) will display fault text or a numeric code on the thermostat when a component faults. Single-stage systems (many XR models) usually give you no display — just warm air. Behavior varies by model and by the board/thermostat installed, so treat any code text on your screen as a lead, not a diagnosis.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so you get little or no cool air.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, dust, and dryer lint insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, ice, or a hissing sound; requires leak repair, not a top-off.
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — outdoor fan or compressor won't start or hums and shuts off.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
7. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a wiring/config fault.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit or control board has no power.
9. **Clogged condensate drain** — a full drain pan float switch cuts cooling to prevent overflow.
10. **Failed compressor or blower motor** — the expensive, last-on-the-list culprit.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Filter:** Pull it. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it and run the fan.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL, fan on AUTO, and setpoint at least 3–4°F below room temp. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents & registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris; keep at least 2 feet of clearance around the condenser. Gently rinse visible dirt off the coil with a garden hose (no pressure washer).
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or coil, turn the system to OFF and the fan to ON for a few hours to thaw before running cooling again.
- **Condensate:** If cooling stopped and you find water near the indoor unit, the drain may be clogged and the float switch may have tripped. Clear a visible clog if you can safely reach it.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, remove electrical panels on the outdoor unit, or bypass any float or safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Trane tech should:

1. Verify thermostat call and confirm the indoor blower and outdoor unit are getting power (24V and line voltage).
2. Measure temperature split across the indoor coil (return vs. supply) — a healthy system is roughly 16–22°F.
3. Inspect the filter, coils, and blower for airflow restriction; check for ice.
4. Test the capacitor(s) and contactor with a meter, and check amp draw on the fan and compressor.
5. Connect gauges to measure suction/liquid pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling to judge the charge — and, if low, **find the leak** rather than just topping off.
6. On communicating XV/XL systems, read fault history from the ComfortLink thermostat or board.

Ask to see the temperature split and pressure readings. A quote to "add refrigerant" with no mention of a leak search is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak flow | Dirty filter / restricted airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Check blower, coil, ductwork |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw with fan ON | Find airflow issue or leak, recharge |
| Outdoor unit silent | Breaker, fuse, capacitor or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace capacitor, contactor, fuse |
| Outdoor fan hums, won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — leave panel closed | Replace capacitor |
| Dirty/clogged outdoor coil | Debris insulating condenser | Rinse gently with hose | Deep coil cleaning |
| Cooling stops, water near unit | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear visible drain clog | Clear drain, test float switch |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant or failing compressor | Nothing safe | Leak search, charge, or compressor test |
| Thermostat blank/unresponsive | Dead batteries or power loss | Replace batteries, check breaker | Check 24V, wiring, board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional and model variation is large):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Low-voltage fuse / minor control repair:** $100–$300
- **Condenser coil deep cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $200–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Blower or condenser fan motor:** $400–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to a new system on older units)
- **Full system replacement:** $5,000–$12,000+

If your Trane is under the standard 10-year registered parts warranty, major parts like the compressor may be covered — you'll still pay labor and refrigerant. Ask the tech to confirm warranty status by model and serial number.
