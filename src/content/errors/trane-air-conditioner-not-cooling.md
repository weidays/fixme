## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single blink code — it's a symptom. Your Trane air conditioner is powered and may even be running, but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On communicating Trane systems (ComfortLink II), the thermostat may show a fault or an alert message instead of a numbered code, while older single-stage units simply run without producing cool air.

Because the cause can range from a $15 filter to a failed compressor, this page walks you from the cheapest, homeowner-safe checks up to the professional diagnostics — so you can fix what's simple and sanity-check any quote for what isn't.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the #1 cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection, so the unit runs but can't cool.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), or a miswired/failing thermostat.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks cooling entirely.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — warm vents, longer runtimes, possible hissing or ice on the line set.
6. **Tripped breaker or outdoor disconnect** — outdoor unit dead, indoor fan may still blow warm air.
7. **Full condensate pan / tripped float switch** — a safety switch cuts cooling to prevent water damage.
8. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums but won't start.
9. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck open.
10. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs, draws power, but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Air filter:** Pull it out and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **AUTO** (not ON — "ON" blows room-temp air between cycles and can feel like it's not cooling). Replace batteries if it uses them.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Flip it fully OFF, then ON. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — that's an electrical fault.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** The pull-out box near the condenser can be dislodged. Make sure it's fully seated.
- **Vents and registers:** Confirm supply vents are open and unblocked, and return grilles aren't covered by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate:** If you see standing water at the indoor unit or a full drain pan, the float switch may have cut power. Clearing the drain line can help, but persistent clogs need service.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, remove electrical panels on the outdoor unit, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Trane tech should work through this in order — use it to sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F).
2. **Inspect airflow** — check filter, blower, and evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Check electrical** — test the capacitor, contactor, and voltage at the outdoor unit.
4. **Read the control board / thermostat** — on ComfortLink II systems, pull stored fault history and confirm proper communication between components.
5. **Gauge the refrigerant** — connect manifold gauges and measure superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct or the system is leaking.
6. **Leak search** — if low, use electronic detector or dye to find the leak *before* recharging.
7. **Compressor test** — check amp draw, windings, and starting components if cooling is absent despite a proper charge.

Be wary of anyone who "tops off refrigerant" without a leak search — that's a temporary fix that vents regulated refrigerant and hides the real fault.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm-ish air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Clean blower/coil if neglected |
| Indoor coil or line iced up | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw, replace filter | Find restriction or refrigerant leak |
| Outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker/disconnect | Reset breaker once, seat disconnect | Diagnose electrical fault, contactor |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None (electrical) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Warm vents, long runtimes | Low refrigerant / leak | None (EPA regulated) | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| System off, water present | Full pan / float switch | Clear drain, add tablets | Clear clog, replace pump/switch |
| Never reaches setpoint on hot days | Dirty condenser coil | Gently rinse fins with hose (power off) | Deep coil clean, verify charge |
| No response, blank thermostat | Dead batteries / wiring | Replace batteries | Check thermostat wiring & board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor; varies by region and model):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Condensate pump / float switch:** $150–$400
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 (more for communicating ComfortLink II models)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000+
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ — often the point where replacing the whole condenser or system makes more sense, especially out of warranty

Trane parts may be covered under the registered limited warranty (typically 10 years on parts if registered), but **labor is rarely included** — always ask what the warranty does and doesn't cover.
