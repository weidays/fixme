## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a blink code on the board — it's the symptom homeowners describe when a Trane air conditioner is powered on and running but the house won't reach the thermostat setpoint. Depending on your system, that can look like warm air from the vents, weak airflow, an outdoor unit that hums but won't start, or an indoor coil that has frozen into a block of ice.

Trane central systems (XR, XL and XV series condensers and heat pumps) don't usually flash a dedicated "not cooling" fault. Instead, some communicating systems with a **ComfortLink II thermostat** or a variable-speed indoor unit will display airflow, communication, or lockout messages that *cause* the no-cool condition. Single-stage XR-series units give you no display at all — you diagnose by symptom.

Because low cooling almost always traces back to refrigerant, electrical components, or a compressor, this is rated a **pro-level** problem. There are safe checks you can do first, but the actual repair usually needs a licensed HVAC tech.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return** — the number-one cause. Restricted airflow drops capacity and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode (fan "on" vs. cool), dead batteries, or a miswired/failed thermostat.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser and indoor blower are often on separate circuits; one can trip while the other runs.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common part failure. The compressor or fan motor hums but won't start, or the fan spins slowly.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil packed with grass, dust, or cottonwood can't reject heat, killing capacity.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines and near-zero airflow.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — undercharge from a slow leak in coils, joints, or the line set. Always paired with a leak that must be repaired.
8. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor relay that pulls in the compressor; pitted or stuck contacts stop the condenser.
9. **Condensate safety switch tripped** — a full drain pan or clogged line trips a float switch that shuts off cooling to prevent water damage.
10. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; the outdoor unit trips its breaker, buzzes and shuts down, or won't run at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner and can save a service call.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Do this first, every time.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **AUTO** (not ON). Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers in your panel. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers are open and unblocked, and the return grille isn't covered by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser and gently rinse the outer coil fins with a garden hose (power off). Keep at least two feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain:** If your unit has a float switch and there's water pooling near the indoor unit, a clogged drain may have shut cooling off. Clearing the drain line is DIY; a pan treatment tablet helps prevent regrowth.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** to thaw it (can take a few hours). If it re-freezes, you have an airflow or refrigerant problem for a pro.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass the float switch, or remove electrical panels on the condenser — capacitors hold a dangerous charge even with power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheap to expensive, and their quote should reflect actual findings:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (the "delta-T," typically 16–22°F on a healthy system).
2. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, coil, and blower operation before touching refrigerant.
3. **Electrical checks** — tests the capacitor with a meter, inspects the contactor, and confirms voltage at the condenser.
4. **Refrigerant readings** — attaches gauges to measure pressures and **superheat/subcooling** to confirm whether the charge is correct. Low readings point to a leak, not a "top-off."
5. **Leak search** — if undercharged, uses electronic detector or dye to find the leak. A reputable tech won't just add refrigerant without addressing the leak.
6. **Compressor test** — checks amp draw, windings, and start components if the compressor won't run.

If a tech proposes adding refrigerant without mentioning a leak search, ask why — repeated "top-offs" waste money and vent refrigerant.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak airflow | Dirty filter / blocked return | Replace filter, open vents | Clean blower and coil, check duct static |
| Ice on copper lines / coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Find airflow issue or leak, recharge |
| Outdoor fan runs, warm air inside | Blower not running / indoor breaker tripped | Reset indoor breaker once | Test blower motor and control board |
| Condenser hums but won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — pro repair | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit dead, indoor runs | Tripped condenser breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check for shorts |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged drain tripped float switch | Clear drain line | Clear/repair drain, verify switch |
| Dirty, hot outdoor coil | Blocked condenser coil | Rinse fins with hose (power off) | Chemical coil cleaning |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Failing compressor or short | Stop resetting — call pro | Diagnose compressor/wiring, replace |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Regional labor and system size affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $8–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 pro
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$300
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 (more if evaporator or condenser coil must be replaced)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth weighing against full-system replacement on older units)

If your Trane condenser or coil is still under Trane's registered limited warranty (typically 10 years on parts for registered systems), the compressor or coil part may be covered — you'd pay labor and refrigerant only. Check your registration date.
