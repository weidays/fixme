## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific Trane fault code — it's the symptom homeowners describe when the system runs but the house won't reach setpoint, the air feels warm, or the AC won't start at all. Trane air conditioners don't display a plain-text "not cooling" message; instead, control boards on newer communicating systems (XL and XV series) may log or flash diagnostic codes at the indoor board or thermostat.

This page covers the mechanical and electrical reasons a Trane split-system AC fails to cool. Because Trane spans single-stage (XR), two-stage (XL), and variable-speed communicating (XV) equipment, exact diagnostics vary. Communicating systems report faults through the ComfortLink II or a linked thermostat, while older units give you no code at all — you diagnose by symptom.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat setting or wiring issue** — set to "fan" instead of "cool," wrong setpoint, dead batteries, or a loose wire.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser has its own circuit; if it trips, the indoor fan may still blow warm air.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure that stops the compressor or condenser fan motor from starting.
5. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, cottonwood, and dirt block heat rejection, killing efficiency.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — caused by low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks cooling entirely.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces capacity and can freeze the coil; requires leak repair, not a top-off.
8. **Stuck or burned contactor** — the relay that powers the compressor may fail closed or open.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — the compressor overheats and shuts down on internal protection.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome, often the end result of ignored earlier problems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe. Do not open the electrical panel of the condenser, touch refrigerant lines, or handle the capacitor.

- **Air filter** — Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" complaints.
- **Thermostat** — Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **AUTO**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breaker** — Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers in your panel. If one is tripped, switch it fully OFF then ON once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents** — Make sure they're open and unblocked by furniture or rugs. Closed vents choke airflow.
- **Outdoor unit** — Look for obvious debris, leaves, or tall grass blocking the coil. You can gently rinse the outside of the coil with a garden hose (unit off) from top to bottom — never a pressure washer.
- **Condensate drain** — A full drain pan or clogged line can trip a safety float switch and shut down cooling. If you see standing water, a wet/dry vac on the outdoor drain line end can clear it.
- **Ice check** — If you see frost or ice on the refrigerant line or coil, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** to thaw it. Running a frozen unit causes damage. If it refreezes, you have a refrigerant or airflow problem — call a pro.

If cooling doesn't return after these, the remaining causes involve refrigerant, high-voltage components, or the compressor — all of which are professional territory.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech follows a repeatable sequence so you can sanity-check the visit:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Check airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Electrical checks** — tests the capacitor (microfarad reading), contactor, and voltage at the condenser with a meter.
4. **Reads control board codes** — on XL/XV communicating systems, pulls fault history from the board or ComfortLink thermostat.
5. **Gauges the refrigerant** — connects manifold gauges and measures superheat/subcooling to confirm proper charge; identifies overcharge, undercharge, or restriction.
6. **Leak detection** — if charge is low, uses electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen to find the leak before adding refrigerant.
7. **Compressor and motor tests** — checks amp draw, windings, and start performance.

A good tech won't just "add a pound of freon" without finding the leak. If a quote proposes a refrigerant top-off with no leak search, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indoor fan blows warm air | Outdoor breaker tripped, thermostat in fan-only, or dead capacitor | Check thermostat mode and breaker | Test capacitor, contactor, condenser power |
| Runs constantly, never reaches setpoint | Dirty filter/coil or low refrigerant | Replace filter, rinse outdoor coil | Measure charge, find and repair leak |
| Ice on refrigerant line or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw, replace filter | Diagnose airflow vs. charge, repair root cause |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor fan runs | Failed capacitor, contactor, or breaker | Reset breaker once | Replace capacitor/contactor, test compressor |
| Outdoor unit humming then shutting off | Failed start capacitor or seized compressor | None — call a pro | Test start components, assess compressor |
| Water around indoor unit, cooling stops | Clogged condensate drain, float switch tripped | Clear drain, vacuum line | Clear line, verify float switch, add tablets |
| Weak airflow from vents | Clogged filter or blower issue | Replace filter | Inspect blower motor and ductwork |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US pricing including parts and labor; your region and system size affect the total.

- **Air filter** — $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries or replacement** — $5 (batteries) to $150–$350 (smart thermostat installed).
- **Run/start capacitor** — $150–$400.
- **Contactor** — $150–$350.
- **Condenser coil cleaning** — $75–$250.
- **Condensate drain clearing** — $75–$250.
- **Condenser fan motor** — $300–$650.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge** — $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location; evaporator or condenser coil replacement pushes this higher.
- **Evaporator coil replacement** — $1,000–$2,500.
- **Compressor replacement** — $1,500–$2,800+; often worth comparing to full system replacement if the unit is 10+ years old or uses R-22 refrigerant.
