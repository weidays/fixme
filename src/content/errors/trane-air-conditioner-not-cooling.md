## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a blink code on a Trane control board — it's a symptom. Your Trane air conditioner may be running (indoor blower and/or outdoor unit humming) but the air coming from the vents is warm, lukewarm, or not moving. On some Trane systems paired with a communicating thermostat (like the ComfortLink II XL850/XL1050), you may instead see a fault message or a system indicator on the thermostat screen rather than a numeric code.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, this page walks you from the cheapest, most likely causes to the serious ones. Exact behavior varies by model — single-stage units (XR series), two-stage units (XL series), and variable-speed units (XV series) each report and respond differently.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so no cold air reaches you.
3. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode, dead batteries, bad programming, or a miswired/failing thermostat.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser may have lost power while the indoor blower still runs.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the single most common "runs but won't cool" cause on older systems.
6. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — dirt, grass, and cottonwood block heat rejection.
7. **Failed run capacitor** — a common, relatively cheap failure that stops the compressor or fan motor.
8. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor unit's electrical relay won't pull in, so the compressor never starts.
9. **Clogged condensate drain / float switch tripped** — a full-safety switch shuts cooling off to prevent overflow.
10. **Failed compressor or fan motor** — the most expensive outcome, usually preceded by buzzing, hard-starting, or breaker trips.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything is smoking, sparking, or smells burnt.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a huge share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on AUTO. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker (often two — one for the indoor air handler, one for the outdoor condenser). Reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the outside of the coil with a garden hose (unit off) if it's visibly dirty.
- **Ice check:** Look at the refrigerant lines and indoor coil. If you see ice, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** to thaw it (can take a few hours), then check the filter.
- **Condensate drain:** If your unit has a clear drain line or float switch and you see standing water, a clog may have tripped the safety. You can clear a visible clog, but don't bypass the switch.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the process helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply and return air temperature; a healthy split is typically 15–20°F.
2. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Confirms power and controls** — tests the thermostat call, contactor, capacitor, and low-voltage wiring at the condenser.
4. **Gauges the refrigerant** — connects manifold gauges to read pressures and superheat/subcooling. This tells them if the charge is low (leak) or if there's a metering/compressor issue.
5. **Leak search** — if low, uses electronic detector or dye to locate the leak before recharging.
6. **Electrical load test** — measures compressor and fan motor amp draw to spot a failing motor or seized compressor.
7. **Reads board/thermostat faults** — on communicating XL/XV systems, pulls stored fault data from the ComfortLink thermostat or board.

A good tech won't just "add a pound of Freon" without finding the leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blower runs, air is warm | Dirty filter / low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Clean coil, check blower speed |
| Ice on coil or line set | Frozen evaporator | Turn off, run fan to thaw, change filter | Diagnose airflow or low charge |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor runs | Tripped breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, test circuit |
| Outdoor unit buzzes, won't start | Failed capacitor or compressor | None — call pro | Test/replace capacitor or compressor |
| Cools weakly, runs constantly | Low refrigerant (leak) | None — call pro | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Warm air + hot outdoor coil | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse exterior with hose | Deep coil cleaning |
| Cooling stops, water present | Clogged drain / float switch | Clear visible clog | Clear drain, test safety switch |
| Thermostat blank/unresponsive | Dead batteries / wiring | Replace batteries | Diagnose wiring or replace stat |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Diagnosis fees ($90–$180) often apply toward the repair.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Coil cleaning (condenser or evaporator):** $100–$400
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs older R-22)
- **Fan motor replacement:** $300–$700
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,200+ (often worth weighing against a new system on older units)

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type, and whether your system is still under Trane's warranty — check your registration, as Trane compressors and coils often carry 10-year limited parts coverage.
