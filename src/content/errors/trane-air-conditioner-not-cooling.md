## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific fault code. Your Trane air conditioner may be running, blowing air, and drawing power — but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On systems with a diagnostic display or comfort control (such as Trane's variable-speed platforms), you may see a related fault or communication error; older single-stage systems often show nothing at all.

The core problem is always the same: heat isn't being removed from your home's air. That breaks down into three buckets — **airflow problems** (filter, coils, blower), **refrigerant problems** (leak, undercharge), or **electrical/control problems** (capacitor, contactor, thermostat, control board). Airflow issues are usually DIY. Refrigerant and most electrical faults are pro-level and are why this page is rated **pro severity**.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil. The single most common cause.
2. **Thermostat setting or wiring** — set to "fan" instead of "cool," wrong setpoint, dead batteries, or a failed thermostat.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection so the system can't cool effectively.
4. **Frozen indoor evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer and you get warm air.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — undercharged systems cool poorly, ice up, and eventually trip on high or low pressure. Requires a licensed tech.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums, struggles, or won't start; a very common electrical failure.
7. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so the condenser never energizes.
8. **Clogged condensate drain / float switch tripped** — a full drain pan trips the safety switch and shuts down cooling on many setups.
9. **Failed compressor or fan motor** — the most expensive outcome, often the endpoint of long-term neglect or electrical faults.
10. **Control board or communication fault** — more common on variable-speed and communicating Trane systems; behavior varies by model.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. If none of them fix it, call a licensed HVAC technician.

- **Check the air filter.** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Check the breakers.** Look at your electrical panel for the AC/air-handler breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Check your vents and registers.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Check the outdoor unit.** Clear leaves, grass, and debris away from the condenser (at least 2 feet of clearance). Gently rinse visible dirt off the outside of the coil with a garden hose — never a pressure washer.
- **Check the condensate drain.** If there's standing water near the indoor unit or the drain pan is full, a float switch may have shut the system down. Clearing a visible clog can restore cooling.
- **Look for ice.** If the indoor coil or the copper lines outside are frosted, **turn the system to Off and the fan to On** to thaw it. Running a frozen system makes things worse. If it re-freezes, you likely have a refrigerant or airflow problem for a pro.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheapest to most expensive cause, not straight to "you need a new system." Expect them to:

1. **Verify airflow** — inspect the filter, blower wheel, and both coils; measure temperature drop (split) across the evaporator, typically expecting around 15–20°F.
2. **Check the thermostat and control signal** — confirm the call for cooling reaches the equipment.
3. **Test electrical components** — measure the run capacitor against its rated microfarads, inspect the contactor, and check voltage/amperage at the compressor and fan.
4. **Connect refrigerant gauges** — read suction and liquid pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct. Low readings point to a leak.
5. **Perform a leak search** if refrigerant is low — using electronic detectors, dye, or nitrogen pressure testing to find where the leak is.
6. **Read fault/communication data** on communicating Trane systems via the comfort control or a service tool. Available codes vary by board and model.

A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search, or "replace the system" without testing the capacitor and charge, deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower, check duct static pressure |
| Fan runs, no cold air, no fault | Thermostat set wrong or failed | Set to Cool/Auto, replace batteries | Test t-stat wiring, replace thermostat |
| Outdoor unit runs, poor cooling | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse coil, clear debris | Deep coil cleaning, fin straightening |
| Warm air + ice on lines/coil | Frozen evaporator | Turn to Off, fan On to thaw | Diagnose low airflow or refrigerant |
| Poor cooling, ice, high bills | Low refrigerant / leak | None — call a pro | Leak search, repair, EPA-certified recharge |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit totally dead | Failed contactor | Check breaker once | Test and replace contactor |
| System off, water near indoor unit | Clogged drain / float switch | Clear visible clog, add tablets | Clear drain line, test float switch |
| Nothing runs, loud hum/silence | Failed compressor or motor | None | Diagnose and replace motor/compressor |
| Communicating system, cooling error | Board or comm fault | Reset once | Read data, replace board if needed |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, including parts and labor. Actual prices vary by region, unit age, and refrigerant type (older R-410A vs. newer refrigerants affect recharge cost).

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 — DIY.
- **Thermostat batteries or settings:** $0–$15 — DIY.
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$450 installed.
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250; float switch $100–$250.
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300.
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Refrigerant leak repair:** $300–$1,500+ depending on leak location; recharge adds $100–$600+ by refrigerant type and amount.
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ — often near the cost of a new outdoor unit, so replacement of the whole system may be discussed.

If your Trane system is under 10 years old, check whether parts are still covered under the manufacturer's limited warranty — you may only owe labor.
