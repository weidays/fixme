## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific error code on Trane air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your Trane unit may be running, blowing air, and showing no fault light, yet the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On systems with a communicating thermostat (ComfortLink II or Trane XL/XV controls), you may instead see a comfort-fault or "system communication" message rather than a numeric code.

Because Trane air conditioners themselves rarely display blink codes at the outdoor unit, most cooling problems are diagnosed by symptom and measurement, not by a stored code. Any diagnostic blink codes you see usually come from the indoor air handler or furnace control board, not the condenser.

This page covers a Trane central AC that is failing to cool a space that it previously cooled fine.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil. The #1 cause and always the first thing to check.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — caused by low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so warm air blows from vents.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, cottonwood, and dust block heat rejection, so the system runs constantly without cooling.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces capacity and causes long run times, warm air, and often coil icing. Requires a licensed tech.
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the outdoor fan or compressor hums but won't start, or the unit trips. A common, affordable failure.
6. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit doesn't close, so the condenser never runs.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
8. **Thermostat problem or dead batteries** — wrong mode, wrong programming, or a failed thermostat that isn't calling for cooling.
9. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces little or no cooling, or won't start at all.
10. **Blocked condensate drain triggering a float switch** — a full drain pan can shut cooling off on many indoor units.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

- **Filter:** Replace a dirty filter with a clean one of the correct size. This alone fixes many "not cooling" complaints.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if your thermostat uses them.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker for both the indoor air handler/furnace and the outdoor condenser. Reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor unit airflow:** Turn power off at the disconnect and clear grass, leaves, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the outside of the coil fins with a garden hose (low pressure, top to bottom) — never a pressure washer.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, set the system to **Off** (or fan-only) for 2–4 hours to thaw, then replace the filter and restart. Recurring ice means you need a technician.
- **Condensate drain:** Check for standing water in the drain pan. A clogged drain can trip a safety float and stop cooling. Clear a visible clog or drop in a drain tablet.

If cooling doesn't return after a clean filter, clear airflow, and a thawed coil, the remaining causes require a professional.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified HVAC tech will typically:

1. **Verify the thermostat call** and confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor unit's contactor.
2. **Measure electrical components** — check the capacitor's microfarad rating, test the contactor, and confirm the compressor and fan motor draw correct amps.
3. **Check airflow** across the indoor coil and inspect for a frozen or dirty coil.
4. **Connect refrigerant gauges** and measure superheat and subcooling to determine if the charge is correct — this is how they identify a leak versus a mechanical problem.
5. **Leak-search** with an electronic detector or dye if refrigerant is low.
6. **Test the compressor** windings and starting behavior if it won't run or produces no cooling.
7. On communicating **XL/XV systems**, read fault data through the ComfortLink II control rather than blink codes.

A good quote names the specific failed part and the measured reading behind it. Be cautious of a tech who only "adds a pound of refrigerant" without finding the leak — that's a temporary fix, not a repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check duct/coil restriction |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Fan-only to thaw, new filter | Measure charge, find leak |
| Outdoor unit runs constantly, no cooling | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse coil, clear debris | Deep coil clean, check charge |
| Long run times, gradual loss of cooling | Low refrigerant / leak | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor fan/compressor hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Indoor blower runs, outdoor unit dead | Contactor, breaker, or fuse | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor/fuse, find cause |
| No cooling at all, no response | Thermostat or control | Check mode, batteries | Test thermostat/control board |
| Cooling stops, water in pan | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain, add tablet | Clear line, test float switch |
| Runs but almost no cooling | Failing compressor | None — call pro | Test and replace compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Prices vary by region and unit size.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning (professional):** $150–$400
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Low-voltage fuse or wiring repair:** $100–$300
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$2,000+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A pricing varies)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement on older units)

If your Trane unit is under 10 years old, check whether the compressor or coil is still covered under Trane's parts warranty — you may only owe labor.
