## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific blink code on Trane equipment — it's the most common complaint homeowners report. Your Trane air conditioner may run but blow warm or room-temperature air, run constantly without lowering the temperature, or not turn on at all. Because Trane central ACs don't display a single "not cooling" fault, this page walks you through the real-world causes behind the symptom.

If your Trane furnace or air handler is flashing a diagnostic code (like Code 90), that points to a specific board fault — see Related codes. Otherwise, use the ranked list below to narrow things down.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil into a block of ice.
2. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode (heat vs cool), dead batteries, wrong programming, or loose wiring.
3. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — grass clippings, cottonwood, and dirt insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or fan hums, struggles, or won't start.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — warm air, ice on the copper lines, and long run times. Always a leak, never "normal loss."
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from airflow or refrigerant issues; blows warm until it thaws.
8. **Clogged condensate drain** — many systems have a float switch that shuts cooling off to prevent water damage.
9. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit doesn't pull in.
10. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; unit runs but produces no cooling, or trips repeatedly.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. If none of them fix it, call a licensed HVAC tech.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray or clogged, replace it. Run the system with a clean filter.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Set the fan to AUTO. Replace batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel and the outdoor disconnect. If a breaker is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance. Gently rinse visible dirt off the outside fins with a garden hose (power off).
- **Condensate drain:** If there's standing water in the drain pan, the float switch may have shut cooling off. Empty the pan and check that the drain line isn't clogged.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper refrigerant lines, turn the system to **OFF** and run the fan only for a few hours to thaw it, then replace the filter. If it re-freezes, you likely have a refrigerant or airflow issue needing a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech should work methodically. Here's what to expect so you can sanity-check the visit:

1. **Verify the complaint** — confirm airflow, thermostat call, and measure supply vs return temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Check power** — voltage at the disconnect, contactor, and control board.
3. **Test the capacitor** — with a meter, comparing microfarads against the rating printed on it.
4. **Inspect coils and airflow** — dirty coils, frozen coil, blower operation.
5. **Connect refrigerant gauges** — measure pressures and superheat/subcooling to judge charge. Low readings mean a leak search (dye, electronic detector, or bubble test).
6. **Check the compressor** — amp draw, windings, and whether it's starting.

Be cautious of any quote to "just add refrigerant" without a leak search — that's a temporary fix that vents refrigerant into the atmosphere.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, dirty filter | Clogged filter / airflow | Replace filter | Verify airflow, clean coils |
| Blower runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor, board |
| Hum, no start, warm air | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on coil or lines | Airflow or low refrigerant | Thaw with fan, new filter | Diagnose charge/airflow |
| Cooling stops, water in pan | Clogged condensate / float | Clear pan, flush drain | Clear line, test float switch |
| Warm air, long run times | Low refrigerant (leak) | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Runs constantly, weak cool | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse outdoor fins | Deep coil clean |
| Runs but no cooling at all | Compressor failure | None — call pro | Compressor diagnosis/replace |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Prices vary by region, brand of parts, and whether you're under warranty.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$10 (DIY)
- **Capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Coil cleaning (condenser or evaporator):** $150–$500
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$2,000 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $2,000–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against a new system on older units)

If your Trane AC is under the compressor or parts warranty, you may only pay labor — have your model and serial numbers ready when you call.
