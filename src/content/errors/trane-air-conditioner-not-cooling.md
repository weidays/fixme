## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific blink code — it's a symptom. Your Trane air conditioner is running (or trying to), but the air coming from your vents is warm, lukewarm, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On communicating variable-speed systems (like the XV18 and XV20i), the indoor control board or thermostat may log an actual fault code that points to the cause. On older single-stage units (XR13, XR14), there's usually no code at all — you diagnose by symptom.

The job of the system is to move heat from inside your home to outside. When it can't cool, that heat-transfer loop is broken somewhere: airflow, refrigerant charge, or an electrical component that drives the compressor or fans.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — Restricts airflow across the evaporator coil, which can freeze it into a block of ice and stop cooling entirely.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — Caused by low airflow or low refrigerant. A frozen coil blows warm air until it thaws.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — Grass clippings, dust, and debris block the coil, so the unit can't reject heat outside.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — A very common failure. The compressor or condenser fan won't start, or hums and shuts off.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — Undercharged systems lose cooling capacity. This is never a "top-off" fix; the leak must be found and repaired.
6. **Thermostat problem or wrong setting** — Dead batteries, a setting on "fan" instead of "cool," or a miswired/failing thermostat.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — Outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
8. **Contactor failure** — The relay that sends power to the compressor is pitted or stuck.
9. **Compressor failure** — The most expensive cause. The compressor won't pump refrigerant even with good power and capacitor.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening the sealed refrigerant system or the electrical cabinet on the outdoor unit.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker for both the indoor air handler and the outdoor condenser. Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed registers aren't choking off airflow.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF (leave the fan ON) and let it thaw for a few hours before running again.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris away from the condenser and gently rinse the fins with a garden hose (power off). Keep 2 feet of clearance around it.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Check that the drain line isn't backed up with water.

Do **not** repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping or locking out, and do not remove the outdoor electrical access panel.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech should work systematically — this is how you can sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower, and evaporator coil for ice or restriction.
3. **Confirm power and controls** — test voltage at the disconnect, contactor, and low-voltage signal from the thermostat.
4. **Test electrical components** — measure the capacitor's microfarad rating against its rated value; inspect the contactor.
5. **Read refrigerant pressures** — connect gauges and take superheat/subcooling readings to determine if the charge is correct or if there's a leak.
6. **Leak search** — if undercharged, use electronic detectors or dye to locate the leak rather than just adding refrigerant.
7. **Check the compressor** — test windings and amp draw if it won't start or trips out.

On communicating XV-series systems, the tech will also pull stored fault history from the thermostat or control board.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak airflow | Clogged filter / frozen coil | Replace filter; thaw coil with fan on | Verify airflow, check charge |
| Ice on copper lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find airflow restriction or leak |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed capacitor or fan motor | None — cabinet must stay closed | Test/replace capacitor or motor |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Bad capacitor or contactor | None | Replace capacitor/contactor |
| Indoor blower runs, no cold air | No power to outdoor unit | Reset breaker once | Test disconnect, contactor, wiring |
| House never reaches setpoint | Dirty condenser or low charge | Rinse condenser fins | Measure charge, clean coil |
| Water near indoor unit / no cooling | Clogged condensate drain, float switch tripped | Clear drain line | Clear drain, test float switch |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Short, failing compressor | Stop resetting | Diagnose electrical fault |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Coil thaw / airflow correction:** $0 DIY, or $90–$200 service call
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$600
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,800+ (often worth comparing against system replacement)

Prices vary by region, model, and whether your system is still under Trane's parts warranty (register within 60 days of install for the extended term).
