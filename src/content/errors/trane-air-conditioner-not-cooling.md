## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your Trane air conditioner runs but the house stays warm, or won't run at all. On single-stage Trane units (like the XR13/14/16), there is no on-board display, so you diagnose by symptom. On variable-speed communicating systems (XV18, XV20i paired with a Trane comfort control), the outdoor or indoor board may log a specific fault that your thermostat or a technician's tool can read.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a $10 filter to a failed compressor, this page walks you through the safe checks first, then what a technician should verify before quoting a repair.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (set to Fan/Heat instead of Cool), or a setpoint above room temp.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler has lost power.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — dust, grass clippings, or cottonwood block heat rejection.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system can't absorb heat; often shows as ice on the lines.
6. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums but won't start.
7. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor unit gets a call for cooling but never energizes.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from airflow or refrigerant issues; blows warm and then no air.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cold air.
10. **Clogged condensate line tripping a safety float switch** — shuts cooling off to prevent water damage.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything is wet, smells burnt, or you'd need to open a sealed panel.

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the #1 cause of weak cooling.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is at least 3–5°F below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker** — check the electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated tripping signals a real fault.
- **Return and supply vents** — make sure vents and returns aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Outdoor unit** — clear leaves, grass, and debris away from the condenser. With power off, you can gently rinse the outdoor fins from outside with a garden hose (low pressure, straight down).
- **Condensate drain** — if there's standing water near the indoor unit, the drain may be clogged and a float switch may have shut off cooling. Clear the drain line and empty the pan.

Do **not** open sealed refrigerant panels, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Trane tech should:

1. **Confirm the call for cooling** — verify 24V signal reaching the contactor at the outdoor unit.
2. **Read any stored fault codes** on communicating XV boards, or check indicator LEDs.
3. **Measure electrical values** — test the capacitor's microfarads against its rating and check the contactor for pitting.
4. **Check refrigerant charge by pressure and temperature** — measuring superheat/subcooling, not just "adding a can." Low readings prompt a leak search.
5. **Inspect the coils** — outdoor condenser and indoor evaporator for dirt or ice.
6. **Test the compressor** — for windings, amp draw, and whether it starts.

If a tech quotes a compressor or refrigerant recharge without showing you pressure readings or a leak-search result, ask for that data before approving the work.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan runs, air is warm | Low refrigerant / bad compressor | Check filter & coils | Leak search, recharge or compressor test |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor runs | Tripped breaker, contactor, or capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor & capacitor, replace |
| Weak airflow, some cooling | Dirty filter or frozen coil | Replace filter, let coil thaw | Diagnose airflow/refrigerant cause |
| Ice on refrigerant lines | Low charge or airflow restriction | Turn off, thaw, change filter | Find & fix leak, correct charge |
| Unit hums but won't start | Failed start/run capacitor | None — call pro | Replace capacitor |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged condensate drain, float switch tripped | Clear drain line | Clear drain, verify float switch |
| Nothing runs at all | Power loss, control board, thermostat | Check breaker & thermostat batteries | Test 24V control circuit & board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$10 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $0 DIY rinse – $150 professional
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,600 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,200+ (often worth weighing against a new system on older units)

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs. legacy R-22 is far pricier), and whether parts are under Trane's warranty. Always confirm your warranty status before paying for parts.
