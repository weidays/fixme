## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a blink code — it's a symptom. Your Trane air conditioner is running (or trying to), but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On most Trane systems this points to one of three problem areas: **airflow restriction**, **refrigerant loss**, or an **electrical fault** in the outdoor unit's starting components.

Trane's non-communicating units (XR, XL series) usually give no error display for this — you diagnose by measuring temperatures, pressures and amps. Communicating **ComfortLink II** systems (XV18, XV20i with a linked thermostat) may show a fault code or status message on the thermostat, so check that display first if you have one.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the #1 cause. Restricted airflow drops coil temperature until the evaporator ices over and cooling collapses.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — caused by low airflow or low refrigerant. The coil turns to a block of ice and no cold air moves.
3. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — Trane systems are sealed; if charge is low, there's a leak. Warm air, hissing, or ice on the copper lines are signs.
4. **Failed run/dual capacitor** — a very common electrical failure. The outdoor fan may spin but the compressor won't start, or nothing runs.
5. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit gets pitted or stuck, so the compressor never engages.
6. **Dirty condenser coil** — a caked outdoor coil can't shed heat, so the system runs but barely cools.
7. **Thermostat problem or settings** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a miswired/failing thermostat.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit gets no power.
9. **Failing compressor** — the worst case. It hums, trips its overload, or won't start even with a good capacitor.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop and call a pro if these don't restore cooling.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A dirty filter is the top cause of no cooling and coil freeze.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, fan on **AUTO**, and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor **disconnect/breaker**. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop — that's an electrical fault for a pro.
- **Vents & registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass and debris from the condenser fins so it can breathe. Keep 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain:** If cooling stopped and there's a full drain pan or a wet float switch, the safety switch may have shut the system down. Clear a clogged drain line if you can access it safely.
- **Frozen coil / ice on lines:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** for 1–2 hours to thaw it, then replace the filter. If it re-freezes, you likely have low refrigerant — call a pro.

Do **not** open sealed refrigerant lines, add refrigerant, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verify airflow** — inspect the filter, blower and evaporator coil for icing or dirt.
2. **Check thermostat call & 24V** — confirm the outdoor unit is getting a cooling signal.
3. **Test electrical starting components** — measure the capacitor's microfarads, inspect the contactor, and check the compressor and fan amp draw.
4. **Read refrigerant pressures** — connect gauges to measure superheat/subcooling. This reveals low charge, restrictions, or overcharge.
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, use electronic detectors or dye to find the leak (Trane won't just top it off legally).
6. **Inspect the condenser coil** and confirm the compressor is starting and pumping.
7. **On ComfortLink II units**, they'll pull status codes from the board/thermostat.

Expect a **diagnostic/service-call fee of $89–$175**, often credited toward the repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm airflow, dirty filter | Clogged filter, low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Inspect blower, coil, ductwork |
| Ice on indoor coil or copper lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw, change filter | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor fan spins, no cold air | Bad capacitor or compressor not starting | Check breaker only | Test capacitor & compressor amps |
| Nothing runs outside | Tripped breaker, bad contactor/fuse | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor/fuse, test control board |
| Runs constantly, never cools enough | Low refrigerant or dirty condenser | Rinse condenser, replace filter | Pressure test, leak search, recharge |
| System short-cycles / trips breaker | Failing compressor or electrical fault | Stop resetting | Amp draw, compressor & wiring inspection |
| Thermostat blank or wrong mode | Dead batteries, settings | Replace batteries, set to COOL | Replace/rewire thermostat |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor). Your area and unit size affect price.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / settings:** $0–$15 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Low-voltage fuse or wiring repair:** $75–$250
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500 (depends on leak location and R-410A cost)
- **TXV / metering device:** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,800+ (often worth weighing against replacing the unit)
- **Full system replacement:** $5,000–$12,000+ for older or repeatedly failing systems

If your Trane is under 10 years old, check whether the **compressor or parts warranty** still applies before authorizing a big repair.
