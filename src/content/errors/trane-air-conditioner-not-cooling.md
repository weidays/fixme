## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific blink code — it's the symptom homeowners search for when a Trane air conditioner is running (or trying to run) but the house won't reach the set temperature. On newer variable-speed Trane systems with **ComfortLink II** communicating controls, the thermostat or indoor board may show a specific fault or function code; on older single-stage XR and XL units, there's often no display at all, just warm air from the vents.

Because "not cooling" covers everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, this page walks you from the cheapest, safest checks up to the failures that require a licensed HVAC technician (and often refrigerant handling, which is regulated).

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow across the evaporator coil, cutting cooling and often freezing the coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure; the fan or compressor may hum but not start.
4. **Dirty condenser coil** (outdoor unit) — dust, grass and cottonwood block heat rejection so the system can't cool.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces capacity; you'll notice long run times and warm air. Requires a certified tech.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit doesn't pull in, so the condenser never runs.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — no power to the outdoor unit or control board.
8. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a wiring/communication fault.
9. **Clogged condensate drain / full float switch** — a safety float can shut down cooling to prevent overflow.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive failure; the outdoor unit may buzz, trip the breaker, or do nothing.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to **COOL**, the fan on **AUTO**, and the setpoint several degrees below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers** — check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor AC breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents** — make sure they're open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit** — clear leaves, grass and debris from the condenser fins; keep at least 2 feet of clearance. Gently rinse dirt off the outside with a garden hose (no pressure washer).
- **Condensate drain** — if you see a full drain pan or standing water, a float switch may have shut the system down. Clearing a clogged drain line can restore cooling.
- **Frozen coil / ice on the lines** — if you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** for a few hours to thaw, then replace the filter. If it re-freezes, you likely have low refrigerant — call a tech.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Trane technician follows a logical path, and knowing it helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** — checks the thermostat, confirms 24V is reaching the contactor.
2. **Reads any codes** — on ComfortLink II systems, pulls fault/status codes from the thermostat or board; on older units, works by measurement.
3. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, blower and coil for ice or restriction.
4. **Tests electrical components** — measures the capacitor's microfarads against its rating, checks the contactor and confirms the compressor and fan draw proper amps.
5. **Takes refrigerant readings** — connects gauges, checks superheat/subcooling and pressures to determine if the charge is correct or if there's a leak.
6. **Leak search** — if the charge is low, uses electronic detectors or dye to find the leak (topping off without repair is against EPA best practice).

Be cautious of any tech who recommends adding refrigerant without checking for a leak, or quotes a compressor without ruling out a $30 capacitor first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vents blow warm/room-temp air, unit runs | Dirty filter or low refrigerant | Replace filter; thaw coil | Check charge, find & repair leak |
| Ice on refrigerant lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn AC off, fan on, thaw, new filter | Diagnose airflow or refrigerant loss |
| Outdoor fan spins but no cold air | Failed compressor or capacitor | Confirm breaker on | Test capacitor/compressor, replace |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor blower runs | Contactor, capacitor or breaker | Reset breaker once | Test contactor and capacitor |
| Humming outdoor unit, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None (electrical) | Replace capacitor |
| Water near indoor unit, cooling stopped | Clogged condensate drain/float switch | Clear drain line, empty pan | Clear line, test float switch |
| No cooling, thermostat blank | Dead batteries or blown 24V fuse | Replace batteries | Replace low-voltage fuse, check wiring |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Compressor or electrical short | Do NOT keep resetting | Diagnose short/compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Regional pricing and system size vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Low-voltage (24V) fuse:** $75–$150
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$250
- **Condenser coil deep cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,600 (higher for R-410A and evaporator coil leaks)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,900+ (often makes replacing an older unit worth considering)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 (more for ComfortLink II communicating stats)

If your Trane is under 10 years old, check whether the compressor or coil is covered by Trane's limited parts warranty — you may owe only labor.
