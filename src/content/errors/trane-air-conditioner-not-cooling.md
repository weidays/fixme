## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific blink code — it's the symptom you'll notice before your Trane system ever flashes a fault. Your air conditioner is running (or trying to), but the air coming from your vents is warm, weak, or not getting the house to setpoint.

On Trane systems, cooling depends on a chain of parts working together: the thermostat calls for cooling, the indoor blower moves air across a cold evaporator coil, and the outdoor condenser (with its compressor and fan) rejects the heat outside using refrigerant. If any link in that chain breaks, you get warm air.

Some Trane equipment logs faults you can read. Variable-speed **XV-series** units and communicating systems (with a ComfortLink II thermostat) store fault history and often display plain-language alerts. Single-stage **XR** and older units typically give you far less — you diagnose by symptom, not by code. Because behavior varies this much by model and board, the checks below focus on what applies universally.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow drops cooling capacity and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, dust, and debris block heat rejection, so the system can't dump heat outside.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit may be dead while the indoor blower still runs, giving you room-temperature air.
4. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), or a miscalibrated/failing thermostat.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — you'll see weak cooling, ice on the copper lines, or long run times. This is a pro repair, not a top-up.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — a common failure that stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting; often shows as a humming outdoor unit that won't spin up.
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit; pitted or stuck contacts mean no compressor operation.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — caused by airflow restriction or low refrigerant; the coil turns to ice and blocks all airflow.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome. The unit runs but produces no cold; often the last suspect after everything else checks out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. If they don't fix it, stop and call a pro.

- **Set the thermostat correctly.** Confirm it's on **COOL**, set below room temperature, and the fan is on **AUTO** (not ON — "ON" runs the blower constantly and can feel like warm air).
- **Replace the thermostat batteries** if it's a battery-powered model, even if the screen still shows.
- **Check and replace the air filter.** A gray, matted filter is your most likely culprit. Swap it and run the system for 30 minutes.
- **Check the breaker.** Central AC usually has two breakers (indoor air handler and outdoor condenser). If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a pro — repeated resets can wreck the compressor.
- **Look at the outdoor unit.** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around it. Gently rinse the outer fins with a garden hose (water only, from the outside in). Give it a 2-foot clearance on all sides.
- **Check for ice.** If you see frost or ice on the copper lines or coil, turn the system to **OFF** but leave the fan on to help it thaw, then call a pro — icing means airflow or refrigerant problems.
- **Check the condensate drain.** A full drain pan or a triggered float switch can shut down cooling. If you see standing water, the drain line is likely clogged.
- **Confirm supply vents are open** and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch or float sensor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works the same chain you can't safely access. Use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verifies the call for cooling** at the thermostat and checks control voltage (24V) to the outdoor unit.
2. **Inspects airflow** — filter, blower operation, and evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Tests electrical components** at the condenser: capacitor microfarads, contactor operation, and compressor/fan amp draw.
4. **Connects refrigerant gauges** and reads suction/liquid pressures and superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct — and whether there's a leak.
5. **Leak-searches** if refrigerant is low, using electronic detectors or dye rather than just adding gas.
6. **Reads stored faults** on XV/communicating systems via the ComfortLink thermostat; on XR/single-stage units, they rely on measured readings.
7. **Tests the compressor** — windings, start ability, and amperage — before condemning it, since it's the priciest part.

A tech who wants to "just add a pound of freon" without finding a leak is cutting corners. So is one who condemns the compressor without amp readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter, run 30 min | Inspect blower & ductwork |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor fan runs | Tripped breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor, capacitor |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Replace capacitor, verify amps |
| Ice on copper lines or coil | Low refrigerant or airflow restriction | Turn to OFF, fan ON to thaw | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Long run times, house won't cool | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Rinse outdoor coil | Clean coil, check charge |
| Standing water / unit shuts off | Clogged condensate drain | Clear visible clog | Flush drain, test float switch |
| Runs constantly, no cold at all | Failed compressor | None — call pro | Amp-test compressor, quote replacement |
| Thermostat blank or wrong mode | Dead batteries / settings | Replace batteries, set COOL/AUTO | Replace/rewire thermostat |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included. Regional pricing and system size affect these.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$200
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $200–$1,500 depending on leak location (a leaking coil is far more than a fitting)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 (standard) or $400–$700 (communicating ComfortLink)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ — at this point, weigh it against a full system replacement, especially on older units.
