## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific Trane blink code — it's the symptom homeowners describe when the air handler runs but the house never reaches setpoint, or when warm air comes from the vents. Trane air conditioners don't usually flash a dedicated "no cooling" fault. Instead, the control board (in the indoor furnace or air handler) may show related codes, or the outdoor unit may simply sit idle.

Because this covers a wide range of Trane split systems — from single-stage **XR** units to two-stage **XL** and variable-speed **XV** models — the exact indicator behavior varies. Communicating systems (XV with a ComfortLink II thermostat) often display a plain-language alert, while older single-stage units give you no message at all.

The good news: several causes are safe, free DIY checks. The rest — refrigerant, compressor, and most electrical faults — are professional repairs.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the indoor coil, and kills cooling. The single most common cause.
2. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode (Fan vs Cool), dead batteries, or bad programming.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse to the outdoor unit** — the indoor fan runs but the compressor doesn't.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — blocked by grass, leaves, or dryer lint; the system can't reject heat.
5. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums but won't start. Common and relatively affordable.
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that sends power to the compressor doesn't close.
8. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — cooling gets progressively weaker over weeks or months.
9. **Failed compressor or fan motor** — the most expensive outcome, often after long-term neglect.
10. **Clogged condensate drain triggering a safety float switch** — shuts the system off to prevent water damage.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause and a free fix.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to both the indoor unit and the outdoor condenser. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Outdoor unit:** Turn off power, then clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Ensure at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain / float switch:** If your system shut off and you see standing water in the drain pan, a clogged drain may have tripped the float switch. Clear the visible pan and drain line access if you can reach it safely.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper lines, turn the system to **Fan Only** for a few hours to thaw it, then run cooling again. If it re-freezes, you likely have low refrigerant — call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the diagnostic path helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — confirms the setpoint, checks supply/return air temperatures, and measures the temperature split (typically 15–22°F across the coil).
2. **Check airflow** — inspects the filter, blower, and coil for restriction or icing.
3. **Electrical checks** — tests the capacitor with a meter, inspects the contactor for pitting, and verifies voltage at the outdoor unit.
4. **Refrigerant measurement** — connects gauges to read pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling. Low readings point to a leak.
5. **Leak search** — if refrigerant is low, uses an electronic detector or dye to locate the leak (a proper diagnosis, not just a "top-off").
6. **Compressor test** — measures amp draw and windings if the compressor won't start or run.
7. **Board/code read** — on ComfortLink II (XV) systems, pulls stored fault codes from the thermostat or board.

A trustworthy tech will show you the readings and explain what's out of spec before quoting a repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower & duct if persists |
| Indoor fan runs, no cold air | Breaker to outdoor unit tripped | Reset breaker once | Diagnose why it tripped |
| Ice on coil / copper lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Thaw on Fan Only | Find & repair leak, recharge |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — power off | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor runs | Bad contactor or breaker | Check breaker | Test & replace contactor |
| Outdoor coil caked in debris | Dirty condenser | Rinse gently, power off | Deep coil cleaning |
| System shut off, water in pan | Clogged drain / float switch | Clear visible drain | Clear line, treat drain |
| Cooling slowly getting worse | Refrigerant leak | None | Leak repair + recharge |
| Loud clicking, no cooling at all | Failed compressor | None | Compressor replacement / system quote |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; varies by region and model):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$10 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type — R-410A costs more than legacy blends)
- **Fan motor replacement:** $400–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often the point where replacing the system is worth pricing)

If your Trane is under 10 years old, check for a **10-year registered limited warranty** on parts — it can dramatically cut compressor and coil costs, though labor is usually not covered.
