## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on Trane equipment — it's a symptom. Your system may be running (fan spinning, air moving) but the air isn't cold, or it may cool weakly and never reach the thermostat setpoint. On communicating Trane systems with a ComfortLink II or XL824/XL850 thermostat, you may see an actual error or a status message; on standard 24V systems there's no display, just warm air.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, the causes below are ranked by how often they turn out to be the real problem. If your thermostat shows a specific numbered fault, look that up separately — this page covers the general no-cool symptom.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil into a block of ice.
2. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse at the outdoor unit** — the condenser has its own disconnect and breaker; if it's off, the indoor fan blows warm air.
3. **Dirty condenser coil (outdoor unit)** — grass clippings, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection so the system can't cool.
4. **Frozen indoor evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks the coil until it thaws.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common, inexpensive failure that stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — undercharge means weak or no cooling; requires leak repair, not just a "top-off."
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit burns or sticks.
8. **Thermostat problem or wrong setting** — dead batteries, set to "fan on" instead of "cool," or a wiring fault.
9. **Blower motor or control board failure** — no indoor airflow; on variable-speed and communicating systems, a comms fault can stop the blower.
10. **Compressor failure** — the worst case; the outdoor unit hums or trips but won't cool.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything is hot, smells burnt, or is iced over.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it has them.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the single most common cause.
- **Breakers:** Check both the **indoor air handler/furnace breaker** and the **separate outdoor condenser breaker**. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Near the condenser is a pull-out disconnect box — make sure it's fully seated/on.
- **Vents and returns:** Open closed supply vents and clear furniture or rugs off return grilles.
- **Condensate:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and shut cooling down. Check for standing water in the drain pan and clear a visible clog; drain tablets help prevent regrowth.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and the fan to **On** to thaw it (can take several hours), then replace the filter before restarting.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass the float or safety switches, or repeatedly reset a breaker that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheap-and-likely to expensive:

1. **Verifies the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Tests electrical** — confirms voltage at the disconnect, tests the **run capacitor** with a meter, and inspects the **contactor** for pitting.
4. **Gauges refrigerant** — connects manifold gauges to read pressures and superheat/subcooling; low readings point to a leak.
5. **Leak search** — if undercharged, uses electronic detector or dye to find the leak before recharging.
6. **Checks controls** — on communicating XV/ComfortLink systems, reads fault history and confirms indoor/outdoor units are talking.
7. **Tests the compressor** — measures amp draw and windings if it won't start or trips.

A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without measuring airflow, capacitor, and pressures deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan runs, air not cold | Dirty filter / dirty condenser | Replace filter, rinse outdoor coil | Verify airflow, check charge |
| Indoor coil or lines iced over | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Off + fan On to thaw, new filter | Find airflow restriction or leak |
| Outdoor unit silent, warm air | Tripped condenser breaker | Reset breaker once | Diagnose why it tripped |
| Outdoor fan spins, no cooling | Failed run capacitor | None (electrical) | Test/replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Capacitor, contactor, compressor | None | Test capacitor, contactor, compressor |
| Weak cooling all the time | Low refrigerant (leak) | None | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| No indoor airflow at all | Blower/board or comms fault | Check breaker, filter | Test blower, control board, wiring |
| Cooling stops, water near unit | Clogged condensate/float switch | Clear visible drain clog | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$15 (DIY).
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250.
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $75–$250.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type.
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500.
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900 (more for variable-speed ECM).
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,200+; on an older unit, often worth replacing the whole system.

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (older R-22 systems cost far more to recharge), and whether your system is still under Trane's parts warranty — check your registration.
