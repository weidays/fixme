## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific error code on your Trane — it's a symptom. Your air conditioner may be running, blowing air, and showing no fault light, yet the air coming from your vents is warm or only slightly cool. On communicating variable-speed models (the XV series with a linked thermostat), you may see a temperature-differential or comfort-related message, but single-stage XR and XL units give no on-board code for weak cooling.

Because Trane condensers don't have a blinking diagnostic LED for airflow or refrigerant problems the way the furnace control board does, you diagnose "not cooling" by symptom and measurement — not by a code. The root cause is almost always one of a handful of issues: restricted airflow, a frozen coil, a failed electrical component, or lost refrigerant charge.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow drops cooling capacity and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — ice on the coil blocks heat transfer. Caused by low airflow or low refrigerant.
3. **Failed dual run capacitor** — a swollen or dead capacitor stops the compressor and/or condenser fan. Very common and inexpensive.
4. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil caked with dirt, grass, or cottonwood can't reject heat.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced charge means poor cooling and often coil icing. Requires a licensed tech.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit; pitted or stuck contacts stop the condenser.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
8. **Thermostat fault or wiring** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a miswired call for cooling.
9. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive cause; unit hums, trips breaker, or won't start.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is well below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery model, and make sure the fan is on AUTO, not ON (ON alone blows air without cooling).
- **Air filter:** Pull and inspect it. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a huge share of weak-cooling complaints.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers in your panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure vents aren't closed, blocked by furniture, or covered. Confirm the return grille isn't obstructed.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for a fan spinning and coil that isn't buried in leaves, grass, or debris. Gently clear away debris (power off first).
- **Ice check:** Look at the copper lines and indoor coil. If you see ice, turn the system to OFF (fan can stay on AUTO) and let it thaw fully before running again.
- **Condensate:** A full drain pan or clogged drain can trigger a safety float switch that shuts down cooling. Check for standing water near the indoor unit.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, remove electrical panels on the condenser, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech works through this sequence — use it to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Airflow and filter check** — verifies the filter, blower operation, and return airflow first.
2. **Temperature split** — measures the difference between return and supply air; a healthy system typically shows a 16–22°F drop. A low split points to charge or airflow issues.
3. **Capacitor test** — reads microfarads on the dual run capacitor with a meter; a weak reading confirms replacement.
4. **Contactor and voltage check** — confirms 24V control signal and line voltage reaching the compressor and fan.
5. **Refrigerant gauges** — connects to the service ports to read pressures and superheat/subcooling. Abnormal readings indicate undercharge (leak) or restriction.
6. **Leak search** — if charge is low, a dye or electronic detector finds the leak before recharging (recharging without repair is a temporary fix and wastes refrigerant).
7. **Compressor check** — measures amp draw and windings if the unit won't start or trips the breaker.

A good tech should find the *cause* of a low charge, not just top it off.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, dirty filter | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, run 30 min | Check coil, verify airflow |
| Ice on lines/indoor coil | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, thaw fully | Test charge, find leak |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed capacitor or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test and replace capacitor/contactor |
| Unit hums, won't start | Bad capacitor or compressor | None | Meter capacitor, check compressor |
| Weak cooling, big house | Low refrigerant / undercharge | None | Gauge, leak search, recharge |
| No air at all, thermostat blank | Dead batteries or tripped breaker | Replace batteries, reset breaker | Diagnose control power/fuse |
| Water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear visible drain | Clear line, test float switch |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Short or failing compressor | Stop resetting | Electrical fault / compressor test |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$200
- **Dual run capacitor:** $150–$400 replaced
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 replaced
- **Low-voltage fuse:** $75–$150
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$2,000 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,900 (often makes older units candidates for full replacement)
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90–$180, usually credited toward the repair

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs. newer R-454B), and whether your Trane is still under its parts warranty — register your unit and check coverage before paying for parts.
