## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on most Trane air conditioners — it's the
symptom you notice: the system runs (or tries to) but the house won't reach the
thermostat setpoint. Some newer Trane systems with communicating controls (like the
ComfortLink II thermostat) will display alert text or a numeric fault, but standard
single-stage XR and XL units have no display and simply underperform.

Because "not cooling" covers a wide range of causes — from a $20 filter to a failed
compressor — this page walks you through what's safe to check yourself and what a
technician should confirm. Where the behavior depends on whether you have a
single-stage, two-stage, or variable-speed (XV) system, we'll say so.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter or blocked return** — the single most common cause. Restricted
   airflow drops cooling capacity and can ice the indoor coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, cottonwood, and dust blanket the coil so
   it can't reject heat. Very common in mid-summer.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a bulged or dead capacitor stops the compressor
   or condenser fan from starting. You may hear a hum or clicking.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — undercharged systems cool weakly, run constantly,
   and may ice up. This is a repair job, never a "top-off."
5. **Tripped breaker or failed contactor** — the outdoor unit gets no power, so only
   the indoor blower runs.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; you'll see ice on the
   copper lines or hear the blower moving no cold air.
7. **Thermostat problem or wiring fault** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a loose C-wire.
8. **Failed blower or condenser fan motor** — no air movement across a coil.
9. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; the unit hums but won't cool at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant
system, bypass any safety switch, or add refrigerant.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room
  temperature. Set the fan to **AUTO**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser)
  breakers. Flip a tripped breaker back **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by
  furniture, rugs, or closed doors.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris away from the condenser. Gently
  rinse the outer coil fins with a garden hose (power off) — never a pressure washer.
- **Condensate drain:** A full drain pan or clogged line can trip a float switch and
  shut down cooling. Empty the pan and clear the line if you can reach it.
- **Ice on the lines:** If you see ice on the copper lines or coil, turn the system to
  **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** for a few hours to thaw before calling. Running a
  frozen system can damage the compressor.

If none of these fixes it, the remaining causes involve refrigerant, electrical, or
sealed components — call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence, so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Confirm the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy
   split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Verify airflow** — checks filter, blower operation, and coil cleanliness.
3. **Electrical checks** — tests the capacitor with a meter, inspects the contactor,
   and confirms voltage at the condenser.
4. **Refrigerant readings** — connects gauges to measure pressures and calculates
   superheat/subcooling to judge the charge. Low readings point to a leak.
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, uses an electronic detector or dye to find the
   leak before recharging.
6. **Component isolation** — tests the compressor windings and condenser fan motor if
   the outdoor unit won't run.
7. **Controls** — on communicating XV/ComfortLink systems, reads stored fault codes at
   the thermostat or control board.

Ask for the measured temperature split and the refrigerant readings — a reputable tech
will share them.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air handler runs, weak/warm air | Dirty filter or coil | Replace filter, rinse outdoor coil | Deep coil clean, verify airflow |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor fan runs | Tripped breaker, contactor, or capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace capacitor or contactor |
| Loud hum, no start | Failed run/start capacitor | None | Replace capacitor |
| Runs constantly, never reaches setpoint | Low refrigerant (leak) | None | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Ice on copper lines/coil | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, thaw with fan ON | Find root cause, correct charge/airflow |
| No air from vents at all | Blower motor failure | Check filter & breaker | Replace blower motor/module |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Fan motor or capacitor | None | Replace fan motor or capacitor |
| Unit hums but nothing cools | Failed compressor | None | Compressor test / replacement quote |
| Thermostat blank or wrong mode | Dead batteries, mode, C-wire | Replace batteries, set to COOL | Repair thermostat wiring |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your region and system type affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $15–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,600 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type; R-410A vs. older R-22 changes cost dramatically)
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$650
- **Blower motor:** $450–$900 (more for variable-speed ECM modules)
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to full system replacement on older units)

If your Trane is under its 10-year registered limited warranty, major parts like the
compressor may be covered — but labor usually isn't. Ask the tech to check warranty
status by serial number.
