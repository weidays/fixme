## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single blink code — it's the symptom you notice when your Trane air conditioner runs but the air coming from the vents is warm, weak, or barely cool. Depending on your Trane system, the indoor control board or a communicating thermostat (like a Trane ComfortLink II XL850/XL1050) may or may not show a specific fault. On single-stage XR-series units, there's often no display at all, so you're diagnosing by behavior.

Cooling is a chain: the thermostat calls, the outdoor condenser and compressor start, refrigerant absorbs heat at the indoor evaporator coil, and the blower pushes cooled air through your ducts. Break any link — airflow, refrigerant charge, electrical, or controls — and the result feels the same: the house won't cool down. This page helps you narrow down which link failed before you pay for a service call.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — Restricted airflow is the #1 cause of weak cooling and can freeze the evaporator coil solid.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — Ice on the indoor coil blocks heat transfer. Caused by low airflow or low refrigerant. You'll get warm air even though the outdoor unit runs.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — A very common failure. The compressor or condenser fan hums or won't start. The outdoor unit may buzz then shut off.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — Trane systems don't "use up" refrigerant. Low charge means a leak, causing warm air and possible coil icing.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — The outdoor coil packed with grass, dust, or cottonwood can't reject heat, so cooling drops on hot days.
6. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — If the outdoor unit gets no power, the indoor blower runs and blows room-temperature air.
7. **Contactor failure** — The outdoor contactor doesn't pull in, so the compressor never starts.
8. **Thermostat problem or wrong settings** — Dead batteries, wrong mode, or a failed thermostat can stop the cooling call.
9. **Failed compressor** — The most expensive cause. The compressor won't start or runs but doesn't pump. Usually a last-resort diagnosis.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Stop if anything smells burnt, sparks, or trips a breaker twice.

- **Air filter** — Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a huge share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat** — Set it to COOL and drop the setpoint 3–5°F below room temperature. Confirm the fan is set to AUTO (not ON, which blows uncooled air between cycles). Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers** — Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers in your panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers** — Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit** — Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around and on the condenser coil. Gently rinse the outside fins with a garden hose (power off).
- **Check for ice** — If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF but set the fan to ON to help it thaw. Do not run cooling until it's fully melted.
- **Condensate drain** — A clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. If the drain pan is full, that's a sign to call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Trane tech works the cooling chain in order so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Confirms the call & controls** — Verifies the thermostat is calling for cool and the board is energizing outputs. On communicating XL/XV systems, they'll read fault history from the ComfortLink thermostat.
2. **Checks airflow** — Inspects the filter, blower operation, and evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Electrical checks at the condenser** — Tests the capacitor with a meter, checks the contactor, and measures voltage/amp draw on the compressor and fan motor.
4. **Refrigerant measurements** — Connects gauges and measures superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct. Low readings trigger a leak search (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test).
5. **Compressor testing** — If electrical and charge are good but the compressor won't pump, they'll test windings and startup behavior.

Ask any tech to *show* you the capacitor reading or refrigerant pressures. A refrigerant "top-off" without a leak search is a red flag — the charge will just leak out again.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Check blower & duct static |
| Ice on coil/lines, warm air | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw with fan ON | Find airflow issue or refrigerant leak |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Warm air, cooling drops on hot days | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse fins with hose (power off) | Deep chemical coil cleaning |
| Vents blow room-temp air, fan runs | No power to condenser | Check outdoor breaker | Diagnose electrical fault |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries / failed stat | Replace batteries | Replace thermostat |
| Outdoor runs but no cooling at all | Refrigerant leak or bad compressor | None — call pro | Leak repair or compressor replacement |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US pricing including parts and labor. Your area and system type affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$450
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,600 (higher if coil replacement is needed)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to a full system replacement on older units)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 (communicating ComfortLink models cost more)

If your Trane is under 10 years old, check warranty status — many Trane compressors and coils carry a 10-year registered parts warranty, which can cut costs to labor only.
