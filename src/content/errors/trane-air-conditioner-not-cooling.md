## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific flash code on Trane equipment — it's the symptom you notice when your air conditioner is powered on but the house won't reach the thermostat setpoint. Depending on your system, the indoor blower may run and blow air that's warm or only slightly cool, the outdoor condenser may not start, or the system may short-cycle.

Trane air conditioners generally do **not** show a numeric fault code on the AC itself for a cooling loss. Diagnostic blink codes on Trane systems live on the **furnace or air handler control board**, and Trane communicating systems (with a ComfortLink II or compatible thermostat) may display alerts on the wall unit. If you have a communicating thermostat showing a message, note the exact wording — it points a technician to the failed component faster.

Because low cooling can involve refrigerant, high-voltage components, and a compressor (the most expensive part in the system), this is rated **pro**. Several checks below are safe for homeowners; the electrical and refrigerant diagnosis is not.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling output, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (set to "fan" not "cool"), or a setpoint above room temp.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** to the outdoor condenser — the blower still runs indoors while the condenser sits dead.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — a coat of grass, dust, or cottonwood kills heat rejection and cooling capacity.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor and/or fan motor won't start or hums and trips.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the condenser is pitted or stuck, so the outdoor unit never energizes.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling and often a frozen coil. Requires leak repair, not just a "top-off."
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; blocks cooling entirely until thawed.
9. **Clogged condensate drain / float switch** — a safety float can shut cooling down when the drain backs up.
10. **Failed compressor or fan motor** — least common but most expensive; the unit won't cool at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or opening sealed equipment:

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray or clogged, replace it. Run the system 30 minutes and recheck cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, fan on **AUTO**, and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler and to the outdoor unit. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop — that's a fault a technician must diagnose.
- **Vents & registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for grass, leaves, or debris packed against the condenser coil. Turn the system off at the thermostat and gently clear obvious debris. Do not remove panels or bend fins aggressively.
- **Condensate:** If you see water pooling near the indoor unit or a full drain pan, the float switch may have cut cooling. Clear the visible drain line opening; don't disassemble the unit.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or coil, **turn the system to OFF (fan can stay on)** and let it thaw fully — running a frozen unit makes it worse and can damage the compressor.

Do **not** add refrigerant, open the sealed system, bypass the float switch, or repeatedly reset a breaker that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheap-and-likely to expensive:

1. **Confirms airflow** — checks the filter, blower operation, and coil for icing or dirt.
2. **Verifies thermostat call** — confirms 24V is reaching the outdoor unit on a cooling call.
3. **Tests electrical components** — measures the run capacitor with a meter and inspects the contactor for pitting or chatter.
4. **Checks the condenser** — verifies the fan and compressor start and draw normal amps; a compressor that hums and trips on overload points to a capacitor or the compressor itself.
5. **Reads refrigerant pressures** — connects gauges and measures superheat/subcooling against the setpoint. Abnormal readings indicate low charge (a leak) or a restriction.
6. **Leak search** — if the charge is low, a proper repair means finding the leak (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test), not just refilling.
7. **Inspects the coil, drain and float switch** — confirms a safety float hasn't opened.

Ask any tech quoting a refrigerant "recharge" whether they found and are repairing the **leak**. A recharge without leak repair will fail again within a season.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blower runs, air is warm | Dirty filter or outdoor breaker tripped | Replace filter; reset breaker once | Test condenser power, capacitor, contactor |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor blows | Tripped breaker, bad contactor, or capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor or capacitor |
| Outdoor fan runs, compressor hums | Failed run capacitor | None — call a pro | Replace capacitor, verify compressor amps |
| Weak cooling, warm-ish air | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Clear coil debris | Clean coil, check pressures, find leak |
| Ice on lines/coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, let thaw, change filter | Diagnose airflow or charge, repair leak |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged drain / float switch open | Clear visible drain opening | Clear drain, verify float switch |
| Nothing cools at all, unit dead | Failed compressor or major electrical | None — call a pro | Compressor/motor test and replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional and unit variation applies):

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90 – $180
- **Air filter:** $15 – $40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 – $10 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100 – $250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $75 – $250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500 – $1,500+ depending on leak location and amount
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300 – $650
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200 – $2,200+ (often worth weighing against a new system if the unit is older or out of warranty)

If your Trane is under its registered limited warranty, covered parts (including the compressor on many models) may be significantly cheaper — you typically still pay labor. Have your model and serial number ready.
