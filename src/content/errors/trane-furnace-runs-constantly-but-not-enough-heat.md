## What this code means

"Runs constantly but not enough heat" is not a flash-code — it's a performance symptom. Your Trane furnace is calling for heat and firing, but the home never reaches the thermostat setpoint, so the furnace never satisfies and shuts off.

On single-stage furnaces this usually means the unit is making heat but can't move enough of it into the home, or it's losing capacity. On two-stage and modulating Trane models (XC95, XV95, S9V2, etc.), a furnace **stuck in low/first stage** is a very common cause — it runs forever at reduced output and never ramps to high fire.

There is no lockout or stored fault with this symptom unless airflow gets bad enough to trip the high-limit, which would then show a limit-related flash code (see Related codes). The furnace keeps running because the thermostat keeps asking — it does not auto-reset or lock out on its own.

## Safety first: gas odor and carbon monoxide

- **If you smell gas** — rotten-egg or sulfur odor — do not operate the furnace, light switches, or your phone indoors. **Leave the building first, then call your gas utility's emergency line or 911 from outside.** Do not go back in until they tell you it's safe.
- **If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds**, get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911 from outside. Seek medical help for anyone with headache, dizziness, nausea or confusion.
- **A cracked or failed heat exchanger is a carbon monoxide risk.** If a cracked heat exchanger is suspected — weak heat with an odd smell or soot, or a CO alarm event — **stop using the furnace and leave it off until a qualified technician has inspected it.**
- Make sure you have **working CO alarms** on every level of the home, including near sleeping areas, and test them regularly.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged or wrong air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow means slow-moving, under-heated air and a furnace that never catches up.
2. **Thermostat setting or location issues** — setpoint too high for conditions, "auto" fan vs "on," cycle-rate settings, or a thermostat in a cold/drafty spot. (A *heat anticipator* only exists on older electromechanical thermostats; the digital and communicating thermostats paired with S-series and XC/XV furnaces don't have one.)
3. **Furnace stuck in low stage** (two-stage/modulating models) — a staging fault, wiring, or control-board logic keeps it in first stage so it never reaches full output.
4. **Dirty blower wheel or failing blower motor** — reduced CFM delivers less heat per minute; common on aging ECM and PSC motors.
5. **Closed, blocked, or leaky ductwork** — closed registers, crushed flex duct, or disconnected supply runs bleed off heated air before it reaches rooms.
6. **Partially blocked burners or low gas-manifold pressure** — reduced BTU output; the furnace runs but produces less heat than rated.
7. **Undersized furnace or extreme outdoor cold** — a correctly working furnace simply can't keep up with the load.
8. **Cracked or failed heat exchanger** — reduces effective heat transfer and is a carbon monoxide safety concern. Stop using the furnace and get a professional inspection.
9. **Dirty flame sensor** — a less likely fit for this symptom. When the sensor drops flame, the control closes the gas valve and retries, so it normally presents as **short cycling or cold air from the vents, not nonstop running**. Repeated retries do mean less total heat delivered, so it's worth checking once the airflow items are ruled out (technician diagnosis).

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the air filter.** Pull the old one and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, swap it. This alone fixes many "runs forever" complaints.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to **Heat**, the setpoint is above room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto** (not **On**). Replace the batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Check every supply and return register.** Open closed vents, move furniture and rugs off them, and make sure returns aren't blocked. Restricted airflow mimics a bad furnace.
- **Reset the breaker once.** Flip the furnace breaker (and the service switch on the furnace) off, wait 30 seconds, and back on. One reset only — repeated resets won't help here.
- **Check for obvious duct problems** you can see — a disconnected flex duct in the basement or a kinked run.
- **Clear the condensate line/drain** on high-efficiency models if it looks backed up, so a clog isn't causing nuisance shutdowns.

Everything inside the furnace cabinet — burners, blower, gas valve, control board, heat exchanger — is technician work. If none of the checks above restores heat, or if the furnace keeps running without satisfying, it's a pro job from here.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Measure **temperature rise** across the furnace (return vs supply) and compare to the rating-plate range — low rise points to airflow problems, high rise points to restriction.
- Check **static pressure** to confirm whether ducts, filter, or coil are restricting airflow.
- On two-stage/modulating units, watch the **control board and staging** to see whether the furnace ever commands high fire, and test the staging thermostat/wiring.
- Inspect and **clean the blower wheel**, check motor amp draw, and test ECM/PSC motor operation.
- Clean or test the **flame sensor** and watch the burners for a steady, even flame.
- Verify **gas manifold pressure** against the rating plate.
- Inspect the **heat exchanger** for cracks or blockage with a camera and combustion analyzer.
- Confirm the unit is **correctly sized** for the home via a load check if it's a chronic problem.

Sanity check: a quote should start with the cheap airflow items (filter, blower, ducts, staging) before jumping to a heat-exchanger replacement.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs nonstop, weak airflow | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Measure static pressure, clean blower |
| Never reaches setpoint, air feels mild | Stuck in low stage (2-stage/modulating) | None | Test staging, board, thermostat wiring |
| Some rooms cold, others fine | Closed/leaky ducts | Open vents, check visible duct | Seal/repair ducts, balance system |
| Slow warm air everywhere | Dirty/failing blower motor | None | Clean wheel, test/replace motor |
| Flame flickers, burner cycles on and off (cycling, not nonstop running) | Dirty flame sensor | None | Clean/replace flame sensor |
| Heats weakly even when clean | Low gas pressure / dirty burners | None | Check manifold pressure, clean burners |
| Keeps up in mild cold, fails in deep cold | Undersized unit / high load | Lower expectations, seal drafts | Load calc, discuss replacement |
| Weak heat + odd smell/soot (CO risk) | Cracked heat exchanger | **If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.** Otherwise stop using the furnace, leave it off until inspected, and check that CO alarms are working | Camera inspection, combustion analysis, replace exchanger |

## Repair costs

| Fix | Typical US cost |
|---|---|
| New air filter | $15 – $40 |
| Thermostat replacement | $80 – $300 installed for a basic programmable; $250 – $600 installed for a smart or Trane communicating thermostat |
| Blower wheel cleaning | $100 – $250 |
| Flame sensor clean/replace | $80 – $250 |
| Blower motor (PSC/ECM) | $450 – $1,800 installed; ECM/variable-speed motors on XV/XC and S9V2 units routinely $900 – $1,800 (motor plus module) |
| Gas valve / pressure correction | $200 – $600 |
| Control board (staging issue) | $400 – $1,200 installed; communicating/modulating boards usually land at the top of that range or above |
| Duct repair/sealing | $200 – $1,000+ |
| Heat exchanger replacement | $1,500 – $3,500+ even when the part is warranty-covered, because the job is labor-heavy; out of warranty it often costs more than a new furnace |

Diagnostic/service-call fees of $90–$180 typically apply and are often credited toward the repair.
