## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a numbered code. It means the condenser fan on top of your Rheem outdoor unit isn't turning while the system is calling for cooling.

That fan pulls air through the condenser coil to get rid of the heat removed from your house. Without it, the compressor overheats within minutes. You'll typically see:

- Warm air from the vents, or cooling that slowly fades
- A humming outdoor unit
- A compressor that cycles off on its thermal overload

Which parts are involved depends on the model:

- **Single-stage and two-stage condensers (most Classic and Classic Plus units):** a PSC fan motor powered through the contactor and started by a run capacitor. Often this is a dual capacitor shared with the compressor.
- **Variable-speed condensers (for example, Prestige RA20 with an EcoNet or communicating control):** an ECM fan motor commanded by the outdoor control board. These can log diagnostic codes on the board or the thermostat.

If you have a Rheem **heat pump**, see the separate heat pump outdoor fan page. Heat pumps add defrost-board behavior that doesn't apply to straight AC units.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor.** This is the most common cause on PSC-motor units. The fan may hum, start only when nudged, or spin slowly. A bulged or leaking dual capacitor can also affect the compressor.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor.** Seized bearings, burned windings, or a motor that trips its internal overload after running a few minutes. This is common on units 8–15 years old.
3. **Contactor problem.** Pitted or burned contacts, or one failed pole, so the fan and compressor get no power or partial power. Insects and debris in the contactor are a frequent culprit in warm climates.
4. **No 24-volt call or no line voltage.** Possible sources:
   - A tripped breaker or pulled disconnect
   - A thermostat not calling for cooling
   - An open safety in the low-voltage circuit (high- or low-pressure switch on equipped models)
   - A float switch that has cut power because of a clogged condensate line
5. **Obstructed or damaged fan blade.** Debris, sticks, or a loose blade binding on the shroud or coil guard.
6. **Wiring fault.** Rodent-chewed, burned, or loose terminals at the contactor, capacitor, or motor leads.
7. **Control board or ECM fault (variable-speed models only).** The board isn't commanding the fan, or the ECM module has failed. Diagnosis follows the model-specific Rheem service literature and board codes.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Turn cooling off at the thermostat now.** This protects the compressor while you check things.
2. **Check the thermostat.**
   - It should be set to Cool, with the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature.
   - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
3. **Check the breakers.** Look at the AC (condenser) breaker and the furnace/air handler breaker in your panel. If one has tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
4. **Confirm the outdoor disconnect is on.** This is the switch or pull-out block in the box on the wall next to the unit. Make sure it's fully seated. Don't open or work inside the box beyond that.
5. **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it's dirty. A badly clogged filter can freeze the indoor coil and lead to safety trips.
6. **Check the condensate drain.** Look at the drain line and pan at the indoor unit. If water is standing and the system has a float switch, the switch may have cut the cooling call. Clearing the drain line (for example, with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor end) can restore operation.
7. **Look at the outdoor unit without touching it.**
   - Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the unit, keeping about 2 feet of clearance.
   - Look through the top grille for sticks lodged in the blade.
   - Don't reach inside the unit.
8. **Make sure the access panels are fully seated.**
9. **Restart once.** After about 5 minutes off, set cooling again and watch the unit. If the fan still doesn't spin, or you hear humming, turn it off and call for service.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will usually work through these steps:

1. **Kill power and verify it's off.** Then check the capacitor with a meter, comparing the reading to its µF rating. A typical tolerance is ±6%.
2. **Inspect the contactor.** Check the contacts for pitting and debris. Measure voltage across them and confirm 24 V at the coil during a call.
3. **Check the motor.**
   - Spin the shaft by hand to find seized bearings.
   - Measure winding resistance and check for shorts to ground.
   - Note whether the motor is hot and tripped on its internal overload.
4. **Verify line voltage** at the disconnect and the unit.
5. **Check the low-voltage safeties** (pressure switches, float switch) in the control circuit.
6. **On variable-speed models,** read the board diagnostic codes and verify the fan command signal to the ECM, using Rheem's model-specific procedure.
7. **Check refrigerant pressures and compressor condition.** This matters if the unit ran hot without the fan.

When reviewing a quote, keep these points in mind:

- **A capacitor-only fix is the most common outcome.**
- **A motor replacement should include a new capacitor** and a matching motor, either OEM or a correct universal replacement with the same HP, RPM, rotation, and voltage.
- **Ask what measurements led to the diagnosis.**

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan still, unit humming | Weak or failed run capacitor | Turn cooling off; call for service | Test and replace capacitor |
| Fan spins only if nudged | Failed capacitor or weak motor | Don't nudge; shut off | Replace capacitor; test motor |
| Fan starts, then stops after minutes | Motor overheating on internal overload | Turn off; call | Replace fan motor and capacitor |
| Nothing outside runs at all | Breaker, disconnect, thermostat, float switch, contactor | Check thermostat, breaker (one reset), disconnect, condensate line | Test contactor, 24 V circuit, safeties |
| Compressor runs, fan doesn't | Fan capacitor section, motor, or wiring | Turn off immediately | Diagnose capacitor, motor, wiring |
| Grinding or blade hitting | Debris or loose blade | Clear visible debris around the unit with power off at the disconnect | Secure or replace blade; check motor shaft |
| Variable-speed unit shows fault code | Board or ECM fault | Note the code; power-cycle once | Follow Rheem board diagnostics; replace ECM or board |

## Repair costs

Typical US prices, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service call / diagnosis | $90–$200 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $120–$350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Condenser fan motor (PSC) with capacitor | $350–$750 |
| Fan blade replacement | $150–$350 |
| Wiring repair | $100–$300 |
| ECM fan motor (variable-speed models) | $600–$1,200+ |
| Outdoor control board | $400–$900 |

Some costs vary:

- **Parts warranty:** Rheem's standard parts warranty, typically 10 years with registration, may cover the part but not the labor.
- **Compressor damage:** if the compressor was damaged by running without airflow, costs rise sharply. This is a good reason to shut the system off promptly.
