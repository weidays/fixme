## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. On most Bryant split-system air conditioners, the outdoor (condenser) unit has no display. You notice the problem because the top fan is still while the unit hums, or because the house isn't cooling.

The outdoor fan pulls air across the condenser coil to release the heat removed from your home. When the fan stops:

- Pressure and temperature inside the system rise quickly.
- The compressor usually shuts off on its internal thermal overload.
- The compressor resets by itself once it cools, then may cycle on and off again.

This cycling is not a "lockout" you can clear. The fault persists until the failed part is repaired.

Behavior differs on Evolution variable-speed models. These use an ECM fan motor driven by an inverter or outdoor control board. That board may log a fan-related fault, which a technician reads at the board or on the communicating wall control. Whether a given fault clears automatically or requires a power cycle depends on the specific board. Check your model's service documentation instead of assuming.

If your indoor coil is icing or the house simply isn't cooling, the separate pages on those symptoms cover broader causes. This page covers only the stopped outdoor fan.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor (PSC-motor units).** This is the most common cause. The fan side of the dual capacitor weakens, especially after hot summers, and the motor can't start. A telltale sign is a fan that hums or spins slowly.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor.** Common causes are worn bearings, a burned winding, or an open internal thermal overload. Symptoms include a hot motor, a seized shaft, or a burnt smell.
3. **Contactor or control problem.**
   - On basic units, if the contactor is pitted or isn't pulling in, neither the compressor nor the fan runs. If one contactor pole has failed, the fan may stop while the compressor keeps running.
   - On two-stage and Evolution units, a control board or inverter fault, or a wiring fault, can stop the fan signal.
4. **Obstruction.** Debris, ice, a bent fan blade, or a loose blade on the motor shaft can stop the fan.
5. **Wiring problems.** Loose, burned, or rodent-chewed motor leads or terminals can cut power to the motor.
6. **No power to the outdoor unit.** A tripped breaker or a pulled or blown disconnect stops the whole unit, including the fan. A tripped breaker can also be the result of a shorted motor.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Turn the system off at the thermostat** to protect the compressor while you check things.
2. **Confirm the thermostat settings.** It should be set to Cool, with a setpoint a few degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
3. **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it's dirty. A dirty filter doesn't stop the outdoor fan, but it's worth fixing while you're troubleshooting.
4. **Check the breaker.** Look for a tripped AC breaker in your main panel and reset it **once**.
   - If it trips again, or the fan still won't spin, stop and call a technician.
   - Don't open the outdoor disconnect box or replace fuses yourself.
5. **Look at the outside of the unit without reaching in.** Clear away leaves, grass clippings, or debris piled against the coil or on the top grille. Never put hands or tools through the grille.
6. **Make sure the exterior service panel is seated and secured.** Leave it closed.
7. **Restart and watch.** Turn cooling back on and look at the unit for a minute.
   - If the compressor hums but the fan doesn't turn, shut the system off and schedule service.
   - If you smell burning, also shut the system off and schedule service.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirm power.** The tech checks line voltage at the disconnect and contactor, and confirms the 24V call from the thermostat.
- **Inspect the contactor.** They check that it pulls in, look for pitted contacts, and measure voltage across both poles.
- **Test the capacitor.** They measure microfarads on the FAN–C terminals and compare the reading to the rated value, usually within ±6%.
- **Test the fan motor.**
  - On PSC motors: winding resistance, a check for shorts to ground, amp draw against the nameplate rating, and a bearing spin test with power off.
  - On ECM motors (Evolution variable-speed): checks of the board or inverter output, fault history, and communication. They may also use a motor test tool.
- **Inspect wiring** for burned terminals or damaged leads.
- **Check the blade.** They confirm the blade is secure on the shaft and properly balanced.
- **Check the compressor.** After the repair, they verify operating pressures and temperatures and confirm the compressor wasn't damaged by overheating.

**What a reasonable quote looks like:** a quote for a capacitor-only fix should include a measured reading showing the capacitor was out of spec. Be wary of a motor and capacitor being replaced together without test results, unless the motor clearly failed (seized, shorted, or burned).

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan still, fan starts if nudged | Weak run capacitor | Turn system off and schedule service | Test and replace dual capacitor |
| Fan still, motor hot or burnt smell | Failed fan motor | Turn system off and call | Test windings and amps, replace motor (and usually capacitor) |
| Nothing runs outside, indoor blower runs | Tripped breaker, contactor, or control fault | Reset breaker once; check thermostat | Check disconnect, contactor, 24V control circuit |
| Fan stops after a few minutes, restarts later | Motor overheating on internal overload, or bad bearings | Clear debris around the unit; call | Amp test, bearing check, replace motor |
| Fan wobbles, scrapes or is stuck | Loose or bent blade, debris | Clear debris from outside only | Re-secure or replace blade |
| Evolution unit fan off, fault shown on wall control | ECM motor, board or inverter fault | Note the fault text; one power cycle at breaker | Read fault history, test ECM and board |
| Breaker trips when unit starts | Shorted motor or wiring | Do not keep resetting; call | Megger or ohm test, repair short |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service call and diagnosis | $75 – $200 |
| Dual run capacitor replacement | $120 – $350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150 – $400 |
| PSC condenser fan motor replacement (often includes capacitor) | $350 – $750 |
| ECM fan motor on variable-speed models | $600 – $1,200+ |
| Fan blade replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Wiring repair | $100 – $300 |
| Outdoor control board or inverter repair | $500 – $1,500+ |

Warranty coverage on parts can lower these costs significantly. Labor usually isn't covered unless you have an extended plan.

If the compressor was run hot for a long time, ask the technician to confirm it's healthy before you approve the repair.
