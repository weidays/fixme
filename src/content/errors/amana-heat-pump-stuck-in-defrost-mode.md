## What this code means

"Stuck in defrost mode" is not a numbered fault code. It describes what you see: the Amana heat pump enters defrost and does not leave it, or it keeps going back into defrost when the outdoor coil has no frost on it.

In a normal defrost, the reversing valve switches the system into cooling mode and the outdoor fan stops. Hot refrigerant then melts frost off the outdoor coil. The defrost control board ends the cycle when the defrost sensor reads warm enough. It also has a backup time limit that ends the cycle if the sensor never does. The exact limit depends on the board.

The defrost sensor comes in two types:

- **Older timed boards** use a bimetal defrost thermostat. It acts like a switch that opens or closes at set coil temperatures.
- **Newer demand-defrost boards** use a thermistor sensor. Its resistance changes with coil temperature.

Both are clamped to a tube on the outdoor coil. This page calls either one the "defrost sensor."

When the unit is stuck, you will usually notice some of these:

- The outdoor fan stays off for a long time.
- The unit keeps steaming or running loud.
- Cool or lukewarm air comes from the vents.
- Backup heat runs constantly, and your electric bills climb.

This page covers a unit that **won't leave** defrost. If your unit is iced over because it **never enters** defrost, see "Amana Heat Pump Frozen, Not Defrosting" instead.

**Does it reset itself?** On most Amana boards, a normal cycle ends on its own when the sensor or timer finishes it. A truly stuck condition usually comes back after a power cycle, because the cause is a failed part.

**Fault codes vary by model:**
- Some two-stage and communicating models show defrost-related fault codes on the board LED or on the ComfortBridge/ComfortNet thermostat.
- Basic single-stage units often have no fault display at all.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or misplaced defrost sensor.** This can be either the defrost thermostat or the thermistor. If the sensor reads cold when the coil is actually warm, or has slipped off its tube, the board never gets the signal to end defrost. Without a working sensor, defrost runs to its maximum time and may start again right away.
2. **Faulty defrost control board.** A failed relay or bad logic can hold the unit in defrost or keep the outdoor fan off. On some boards, a defrost test jumper left in place or set wrong after a past service call causes erratic defrost.
3. **Reversing valve stuck or slow to shift.** The board may end defrost, but if the valve sticks in the cooling position, the system keeps acting as if it is defrosting. You'll see cool air and a coil that stays warm or wet.
4. **Reversing valve solenoid coil or wiring fault.** A burned solenoid coil or a loose wire keeps the valve from shifting when the board tells it to.
5. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** With low charge, the coil may never get warm enough to satisfy the sensor. This leads to long cycles that end only on the timer, followed by frequent repeat defrosts.
6. **Outdoor fan or fan relay problem after defrost.** The cycle may have ended, but the fan doesn't restart, so the unit looks and acts stuck. For fan problems, see "Amana AC Outdoor Fan Not Spinning."

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Watch one full cycle.** Note the time when steaming starts and the outdoor fan stops. If the fan is back on and heating has resumed within about 15 minutes, defrost is probably working normally.
- **Thermostat settings and batteries.** Confirm the thermostat is set to HEAT with a setpoint above room temperature. Replace weak batteries. Check whether "AUX" or "EM HEAT" is lit constantly.
- **Air filter.** A clogged filter cuts airflow and makes defrost behave worse. Replace it if it looks dirty.
- **Supply vents and return grilles.** Make sure they are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit clearance.** From the outside only, clear snow, leaves or drifts away from the unit and the drain area. Don't chip ice off the coil or fins.
- **Breaker or disconnect reset, once.** Turn the thermostat off. Switch off the outdoor unit breaker or disconnect for 5 minutes, then restore power and set the thermostat back to HEAT. If the unit goes back into long or constant defrost, stop resetting.
- **Use emergency heat while you wait.** If you have electric backup heat, set the thermostat to EM HEAT to keep the house warm. Expect higher electric bills until the repair.
- **Exterior panels.** Make sure the access panels are fully seated. Don't open the cabinet.

Anything past these checks is technician work. That includes the defrost board, sensor, valve solenoid, wiring and refrigerant.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically:

- **Confirm the symptom.** They check whether the outdoor fan is off, the reversing valve is energized and backup heat is on. They also time the cycle.
- **Check the defrost board.** They read any LED or fault codes and check the test jumper and pin settings. They may force a defrost test cycle to see whether the board starts and ends defrost properly.
- **Test the defrost sensor.** On a thermistor, they check resistance against Amana's temperature chart. On a bimetal defrost thermostat, they check continuity. They also make sure the sensor is clamped tightly in the correct spot on the coil tube.
- **Check the reversing valve.** They confirm 24 V is reaching the solenoid during defrost and check the coil's resistance. They also measure line temperatures and pressures to tell whether the valve is shifting.
- **Check refrigerant charge.** They use gauges with superheat and subcooling readings. If the charge is low, they look for leaks.
- **Check the outdoor fan.** They test the fan motor, capacitor and fan relay output after defrost ends.

A credible quote will name the failed part and explain what test proved it. Be cautious if someone recommends replacing the reversing valve without first testing the solenoid voltage and the sensor.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defrost runs to its time limit, then restarts quickly | Bad or loose defrost sensor | Note cycle times and use EM HEAT | Test the sensor and replace or re-clamp it |
| Outdoor fan never restarts, unit steams for a long time | Defrost board relay failure | One power reset | Diagnose and replace the defrost board |
| Cool air for a long time, coil warm or wet | Reversing valve stuck in cooling position | Switch to EM HEAT | Test the solenoid and valve, then repair or replace the valve |
| Valve won't shift back after defrost | Burned solenoid coil or loose wiring | None | Replace the solenoid coil or repair the wiring |
| Long cycles plus weak heating overall | Low refrigerant from a leak | Replace the filter | Find the leak, repair it and recharge |
| Defrost ends but the fan stays off | Fan motor, capacitor or relay | Clear debris from the outside only | Test and replace the fan component |
| Erratic defrost after a recent service visit | Test jumper or pin setting left wrong | None | Correct the board settings |

## Repair costs

Typical US prices, parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic visit | $90–$200 |
| Defrost sensor or thermostat replacement | $150–$350 |
| Defrost control board replacement | $300–$700 |
| Reversing valve solenoid coil | $150–$400 |
| Reversing valve replacement (brazing, evacuation, recharge) | $900–$2,000+ |
| Refrigerant leak repair and recharge | $300–$1,500+ (depends on the leak and refrigerant type) |
| Outdoor fan capacitor | $120–$300 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350–$800 |
| DIY filter and batteries | $0–$40 |

The defrost board range applies to standard heat pump defrost boards. Communicating ComfortNet/ComfortBridge control boards can cost more, so expect a higher quote if you have one of those models.

Check your warranty before approving work. Many Amana units carry long parts warranties, often 10 years with registration, and some include a lifetime unit replacement limited warranty. Labor is usually not covered.
