## What this code means

"Runs constantly" is a symptom, not a Goodman fault code. Single-stage Goodman condensers have no diagnostic LED for it, and no lockout or auto-reset is involved. The system runs because either:

- the thermostat keeps calling for cooling, because the house never reaches setpoint, or
- something keeps the outdoor unit energized after the call ends.

**Check first whether the house is actually cooling.** The two cases have different causes:

- **House is cooling, but the AC barely or never cycles off.** This points to a capacity or airflow problem, or extreme weather.
- **Outdoor unit runs even with the thermostat off or satisfied.** This is an electrical fault. It is usually a stuck contactor or a thermostat or wiring problem. Shut the breaker off and call a pro.

If the system blows warm air or isn't cooling at all, see the related pages listed at the end instead.

**Note on two-stage and variable-speed units:** Goodman two-stage and inverter models (such as the GSXC/GSXV series) normally run long cycles at low capacity. Long run time alone is not a fault on these units.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter or restricted airflow.** This is the most common cause. Reduced airflow cuts cooling capacity, so the system can't reach setpoint.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil.** Cottonwood, grass clippings and dirt keep the unit from rejecting heat. Capacity drops sharply on hot days.
3. **Thermostat issues.** Possible problems include:
   - fan set to ON instead of AUTO (the blower runs nonstop, which can look like the AC running nonstop)
   - setpoint too low for conditions
   - thermostat in direct sun or near a heat source
   - miscalibration
   - weak batteries
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Some older Goodman evaporator coils have a documented history of leaks from formicary corrosion. A low charge reduces capacity and makes run times longer.
5. **Stuck or welded contactor in the outdoor unit.** Pitted contacts can weld closed. The compressor and outdoor fan then run with no thermostat call.
6. **Shorted thermostat wire.** A short between the R and Y conductors, for example a wire nicked by a lawn trimmer, keeps the contactor energized.
7. **Leaky or undersized ductwork.** Ducts in a hot attic that leak or lack insulation lose cooled air before it reaches the rooms.
8. **Undersized equipment, or a mismatched indoor/outdoor combination.** The system was never able to keep up on design-temperature days.
9. **Failing compressor, such as worn valves.** The unit runs and draws power but moves little heat. This is less common, and a technician diagnoses it after ruling out the causes above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat settings.** Confirm it's on COOL with the fan on AUTO, and that the setpoint is realistic. Most systems cool about 15–20 °F below outdoor temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blinking.
- **Thermostat-off test.** Set the thermostat to OFF and wait 5 minutes.
  - If the outdoor unit keeps running, switch off the AC breaker or the outdoor disconnect and call a technician.
  - Don't restore power until the unit has been checked.
- **Air filter.** Replace it if it's gray or clogged, or if it's more than 1–3 months old.
- **Vents and registers.** Open all supply registers. Make sure return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit, from outside.** Clear leaves, weeds and debris to about 2 feet around the unit. Don't open the panels or spray inside the cabinet.
- **Condensate line.** If the drain pan or safety switch area shows water backup, clear the line at the accessible cleanout, for example with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor drain end.
  - A tripped float or safety switch should normally shut the system off, not make it run constantly. A backed-up drain is worth clearing, but it is unlikely to be the reason your AC won't stop running.
- **Ice check.** Look for frost on the indoor refrigerant lines.
  - If you see frost, turn the system OFF and set the fan to ON to thaw it.
  - Then call for service. Frost points to an airflow or charge problem.
- **Panels.** Make sure the indoor unit's blower door and filter access panels are fully seated.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should:

1. **Confirm the complaint.** They compare supply and return temperatures. A temperature drop of about 16–22 °F across the indoor coil is typical.
2. **Check whether the outdoor unit de-energizes.** They test for 24V at the contactor coil with the thermostat satisfied.
   - Voltage present with no call means a thermostat or wiring fault.
   - No voltage, but the unit still runs, means a welded contactor.
3. **Measure static pressure and airflow.** This checks the filter, blower speed setting, evaporator coil cleanliness and duct restrictions.
4. **Inspect and clean the condenser coil.**
5. **Measure the refrigerant charge.** They check superheat and subcooling against the Goodman charging chart on the unit.
   - A low charge means they should leak-search with an electronic detector or bubbles. A recharge alone is not a fix.
6. **Check compressor amperage** against the rated load amps. This helps spot a weak compressor.
7. **Inspect the ductwork, and verify the matched system.** They confirm the indoor and outdoor model combination is AHRI-matched.

Be cautious if a quote says "just needs freon" with no leak search or superheat and subcooling readings. Be equally cautious if anyone recommends replacing the compressor before checking airflow and charge.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs nonstop, house slowly cools, filter is dirty | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, open registers | Check static pressure and blower speed, clean the evaporator coil |
| Runs nonstop on hot afternoons, fine at night | Dirty condenser, extreme heat, or undersizing | Clear debris around the outdoor unit | Clean the coil chemically, do a load calculation |
| Outdoor unit runs with thermostat OFF | Welded contactor or shorted thermostat wire | Turn off breaker or disconnect, call a pro | Replace contactor, repair the low-voltage wire |
| Indoor blower runs nonstop, outdoor unit cycles | Fan set to ON | Set fan to AUTO | Check the fan relay or control board if AUTO doesn't fix it |
| Runs nonstop, frost on lines, weak cooling | Low refrigerant or airflow issue | Turn system off to thaw, check filter | Leak search, repair, recharge to the charging chart |
| Runs nonstop, thermostat reads wrong temperature | Poorly located or faulty thermostat | Replace batteries, compare against a separate thermometer | Relocate or replace thermostat, check wiring |
| Some rooms hot, unit never stops | Duct leaks or poor insulation | Open closed registers | Seal ducts, add insulation, balance the system |
| Normal airflow and charge, still can't keep up | Weak compressor or undersized system | None | Check compressor amps, consider equipment sizing |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges, including parts and labor.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Air filter (DIY) | $10–$40 |
| Service call or diagnostic | $80–$200 |
| Condenser coil cleaning | $100–$250 |
| Evaporator coil cleaning | $150–$450 |
| Contactor replacement | $120–$300 |
| Thermostat wire repair | $100–$250 |
| Thermostat replacement | $150–$450 installed |
| Leak search and repair, plus recharge (R-410A on older units or R-32 on 2025+ units) | $400–$1,500+ |
| Evaporator coil replacement | $1,200–$2,800 |
| Duct sealing and insulation | $500–$2,500 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500–$3,500 |
| Full system replacement (if undersized or failing) | $5,000–$12,000+ |

**Refrigerant note:** Check the nameplate on your outdoor unit to see which refrigerant it uses before comparing quotes.

**Leak repair vs. coil replacement:** The $400–$1,500+ range covers finding and fixing an accessible leak and recharging the system. If the leak is in the evaporator coil and the coil must be replaced, expect the evaporator coil replacement cost of $1,200–$2,800 instead.

**Warranty note:** Goodman parts warranties are typically 10 years with timely registration, and some lines carry a lifetime compressor warranty. These may cover the coil or compressor, but labor is usually extra.
