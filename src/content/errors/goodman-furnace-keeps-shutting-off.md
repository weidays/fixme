## What this code means

"Keeps shutting off" is not a single Goodman error code — it's a behavior. Your furnace starts a heating cycle, runs for a while (seconds, a minute, or several minutes), then stops before satisfying the thermostat, and repeats. On Goodman integrated control boards, the specific reason is almost always stored as a **diagnostic flash code** on the LED visible through the lower door sight glass.

The pattern of *when* it shuts off tells you a lot:

- **Shuts off within seconds of the burners lighting** → flame not being proved (flame-sensing problem).
- **Shuts off after several minutes, often with the blower still running** → overheating, tripping the high-limit switch.
- **Shuts off and won't relight until power is cycled** → the board has gone into **lockout** after a set number of failed trials. Most Goodman integrated controls lock out after 3 ignition trials, though the exact behavior varies by board revision.

Once locked out, the board typically **holds in lockout until power is cycled**, or auto-resets after about one hour. Read the flash code before doing anything else.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, overheats the heat exchanger, trips the high-limit switch (often shows as 4 flashes). The single most common cause and the easiest to rule out.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — the furnace lights but the board can't detect flame, so it shuts the gas valve within seconds and retries, eventually locking out. Lockout after failed flame proving shows as 1 flash on most boards; a weak-but-present flame signal shows as 7 flashes on many boards — confirm against the label inside your blower door.
3. **Restricted airflow beyond the filter** — closed or blocked registers, undersized/collapsed return ductwork, or a dirty blower wheel causing high-limit trips.
4. **Pressure switch / flue or condensate blockage** — a plugged condensate drain, blocked flue or intake, or failing inducer keeps the pressure switch from proving draft (2 or 3 flashes).
5. **Failing high-limit switch** — the switch itself trips prematurely even with adequate airflow.
6. **Weak or faulty igniter / ignition control** — inconsistent lighting causes repeated trials and lockout.
7. **Faulty control board or gas valve** — the board mis-times the cycle or the valve closes intermittently. Least common, and diagnosed last.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for a homeowner. Stop at anything requiring the cabinet to be opened.

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Goodman furnaces ship in several cabinet widths, so read the size printed on the frame of the old filter and buy that size — don't guess. This alone fixes a large share of shut-off complaints.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to HEAT and the setpoint is above room temperature. If it's battery-powered, install fresh batteries.
- **Supply and return vents** — make sure registers throughout the house are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs, and that return grilles are clear. Restricted airflow causes overheating trips.
- **Condensate drain** (high-efficiency models) — if you see water pooling near the furnace or a full drain pan, a clogged condensate line can trip the pressure switch. Clearing a visible external clog is fine; internal work is not.
- **Breaker and furnace switch** — confirm the breaker isn't tripped and the service switch (looks like a light switch near the furnace) is on.
- **One reset only** — if the furnace is locked out, cycle the breaker off and on **once**. If it lights and runs fine, great. If it shuts off again, note the flash code and call a pro — do not keep resetting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will work in roughly this order, and you can sanity-check a quote against it:

1. **Read the flash code** and count the LED pattern through the door window before touching anything.
2. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower wheel, and static pressure across the system to confirm or rule out overheating trips.
3. **Test flame sensing** — measure the microamp flame signal; clean or replace the flame sensor if the signal is weak.
4. **Verify the pressure switch and draft** — check inducer operation, flue and intake for blockage, and the condensate trap.
5. **Test the high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and correct trip behavior.
6. **Check igniter resistance and ignition sequence** with a meter.
7. **Test the control board and gas valve** outputs last, since they're the most expensive and least likely.

If a shop quotes a board or gas valve without first checking the filter, airflow, and flame sensor, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs several minutes, blower keeps running after burners quit | High-limit trip from dirty filter/airflow | Replace filter, open all vents | Test limit switch, check blower wheel & static pressure |
| Lights then shuts off in a few seconds, retries | Dirty flame sensor / weak flame signal | None inside cabinet | Clean or replace flame sensor, measure microamps |
| Won't relight until breaker is cycled | Board in lockout after failed trials | One reset only; read flash code | Diagnose underlying ignition or safety fault |
| Water near furnace, shuts off intermittently | Clogged condensate line tripping pressure switch | Clear visible external drain clog | Clear trap, test pressure switch, check inducer |
| Inducer runs but burners never fire, then shuts down | Pressure switch open / flue blockage | Check outside intake/exhaust for blockage | Test switch, inspect flue, replace inducer if failed |
| Random short cycles, no clear pattern | Failing control board or gas valve | None | Meter board outputs and gas valve, replace if faulty |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter** — $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries** — $5–$10 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor clean/replace** — $80–$250.
- **High-limit switch** — $150–$350.
- **Pressure switch** — $150–$350.
- **Condensate drain clearing** — $100–$250.
- **Inducer motor** — $400–$1,200. Two-stage and condensing models sit at the upper end, where the inducer assembly alone often exceeds $300.
- **Hot surface igniter** — $150–$350.
- **Gas valve** — $300–$800, with the upper end common on condensing two-stage models.
- **Integrated control board** — $400–$1,200. Two-stage and modulating/variable-speed boards (GMVC, GCVC) cost more than single-stage boards.
- **Diagnostic/service call** — $90–$180, often credited toward the repair.
