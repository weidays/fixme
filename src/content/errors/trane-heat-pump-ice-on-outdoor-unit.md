## What this code means

"Ice on outdoor unit" is a symptom, not a numbered Trane fault code. Most Trane heat pumps do not show any alert for it. The key question is whether you are seeing **normal frost** or **problem ice**.

**Normal frost**
- In heating mode below about 40°F, the outdoor coil runs colder than the air and collects frost.
- Trane defrost boards reverse the system for a few minutes to melt it.
- Time/temperature boards are typically set to defrost every 30, 60 or 90 minutes of run time. Demand-defrost models decide based on sensor readings.
- During defrost you may see steam and hear a whoosh. Both are normal.

**Problem ice**
- A solid block of clear ice.
- Ice that covers the top, fan or cabinet.
- Ice that builds over several hours without clearing.
- Ice on the unit or lines in summer cooling mode.

If the coil stays encased and the defrost cycle never seems to run, see the related page "Frozen and not defrosting." This page focuses on telling the difference and on the other causes.

There is no reset for this condition. It clears only when the cause is fixed and the ice melts. On communicating models, any logged thermostat alerts behave according to that model's documentation.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal frost in cold, humid weather.** Light white frost that clears periodically is expected. No repair is needed.
2. **Water dripping onto the unit from above.** A leaking gutter, roof runoff or downspout can drip on the top grille and freeze into heavy ice. The defrost cycle cannot clear ice that keeps getting refed from above.
3. **Poor drainage or snow buildup at the base.** Defrost meltwater has to drain away. If the unit sits low, its pad has settled, or snow and leaves pile against it, the water refreezes around the bottom of the coil and grows upward.
4. **Restricted airflow.**
   - Outdoors: snow drifts, shrubs or debris blocking the coil.
   - Indoors: a clogged filter or closed registers.
   - In cooling mode, poor indoor airflow can drop refrigerant pressure enough to ice the lines.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Low charge makes the coil run colder than designed. It is a common cause of uneven icing in winter and of iced lines in summer. Recharging without finding the leak is only a temporary fix.
6. **Defrost control or sensor fault.** This includes a failed defrost board, a coil temperature sensor or thermostat that has come loose or failed, or (on demand-defrost models) a failed outdoor air sensor. These cause the ice to build without ever clearing. Details are on the "Frozen and not defrosting" page.
7. **Reversing valve or outdoor fan problems.** A valve that will not shift, or an outdoor fan relay that does not behave correctly during defrost, can leave ice in place. How these failures show up depends on the model's control board.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Stop the ice from growing.** Set the thermostat to Emergency Heat or Auxiliary Heat if you have it. Otherwise turn the system off and use other safe heat. Do not chip, scrape or pour hot water on the coil.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it is set to Heat in winter and Cool in summer, the set point is reasonable, and the batteries are fresh.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty. A clogged filter is a leading cause of summer icing.
- **Open your registers.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and not blocked by furniture.
- **Look above the unit.** Check for gutter leaks, icicles or roof runoff landing on the unit. Fixing the gutter often solves top icing for good.
- **Clear around the base.** Brush snow and leaves away from the sides of the unit by hand or with a soft broom. Keep about 2 feet clear, and do not strike the coil.
- **Check the breakers.** Confirm the breaker and the outdoor disconnect are on. If a breaker tripped, reset it once only. If it trips again, call a technician.
- **Check the exterior panels.** Make sure they are seated and secure.
- **Watch a defrost cycle.** If light frost clears within 1 to 2 hours of heating run time, the system is probably fine. If ice keeps building or comes back after a thaw, call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Ask about the pattern: top only, bottom only, the whole coil, or summer only.
- Inspect drainage, unit height, pad level and runoff from above.
- Check indoor airflow, the blower and the condition of the indoor coil.
- Measure refrigerant pressures and superheat/subcooling against Trane charging data. Leak-search if the charge is low.
- Force a defrost cycle on the board, using the test pins or service procedure for that board, to confirm the reversing valve shifts and the outdoor fan responds correctly.
- Test the coil temperature sensor or defrost thermostat and the outdoor air sensor against their specifications.
- Check the defrost interval setting on time/temperature boards. On communicating models, read the stored fault history.
- Check the reversing valve solenoid and the outdoor fan relay.

A good quote names a specific failed part or measured condition. "Add refrigerant" with no leak search should raise questions.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light white frost that clears periodically | Normal operation | None needed | None |
| Heavy ice on the top and fan grille | Gutter or roof drip | Fix the gutter, redirect runoff, use emergency heat until thawed | Inspect for fan or coil damage |
| Ice building up from the base | Poor drainage, unit too low, snow buildup | Clear snow and debris around the unit | Raise the unit on a stand, level the pad |
| Uneven ice, partial coil frosting | Low refrigerant charge | Switch to emergency heat | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Ice never clears, no defrost steam | Defrost board or sensor fault | Switch to emergency heat | Force defrost, test sensor and board, replace |
| Ice on the lines or unit in summer | Dirty filter, low charge, dirty indoor coil | Replace the filter, open registers, turn cooling off to thaw | Check charge, clean indoor coil, check blower |
| Defrost starts but ice remains | Reversing valve or fan relay issue | Switch to emergency heat | Test the valve solenoid and relay, replace the valve |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Air filter (DIY) | $10 – $40 |
| Gutter repair or downspout redirect | $0 – $300 |
| Riser stand or re-leveling the pad | $150 – $450 |
| Diagnostic visit | $100 – $250 |
| Coil temperature sensor or defrost thermostat | $150 – $400 |
| Defrost control board | $300 – $700 |
| Refrigerant leak search and recharge | $300 – $1,500+ (depends on refrigerant type, R-410A or R-454B, and where the leak is) |
| Reversing valve replacement | $1,000 – $2,000 |
| Outdoor coil replacement | $1,500 – $3,000 (parts may be covered under Trane's registered warranty) |
