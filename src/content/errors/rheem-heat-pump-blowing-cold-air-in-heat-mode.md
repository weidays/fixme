## What this code means

"Blowing cold air in heat mode" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. Your Rheem heat pump's indoor blower runs, but the air from the registers feels cool or room temperature instead of warm.

This page covers cold air while the heat pump itself appears to be running. If the outdoor unit isn't running at all, see "Rheem Heat Pump Not Heating." If the outdoor coil is iced over, see the defrost-related pages.

How the system reports this varies by model:

- **No fault code in most cases.** Many causes, such as thermostat setup, a reversing valve stuck in cooling, or low charge, don't trigger a fault code.
- **Fault light on some models.** Newer communicating or variable-speed models (EcoNet) may show a pressure-switch or other fault on the outdoor control board.
- **Reset behavior differs.** Pressure-switch lockouts on many Rheem defrost boards reset after a timed lockout or when power is cycled. Exact behavior depends on the board.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost cycle.** The unit temporarily reverses to melt frost. The blower may keep running and the air feels cool for a few minutes. If your system has auxiliary heat, it should temper this air.
2. **Thermostat setup or mode error.** Common versions include:
   - Thermostat set to "Fan On" instead of "Auto"
   - Reversing-valve output set wrong (Rheem uses O, energized in cooling)
   - Thermostat configured as a conventional furnace/AC system
   - Weak batteries causing erratic operation
3. **Dirty air filter or restricted airflow.** This lowers heating output and can trip the high-pressure switch in heat mode.
4. **Reversing valve or solenoid coil failure.** The valve stays stuck in the cooling position. Heat mode then delivers chilled air while the compressor runs.
5. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** Heat output drops sharply, especially in colder weather. The low-pressure switch may lock out the compressor while the blower keeps running.
6. **Compressor not running while the outdoor fan or indoor blower runs.** Possible causes include a failed capacitor, contactor, or overload, or a lockout from the outdoor control board.
7. **Failed auxiliary or backup heat strips.** These include sequencers, limits, or elements. The air goes cold during defrost or very cold weather, when the heat pump alone can't keep up.
8. **Duct leaks in unconditioned spaces.** Leaky ducts in an attic or crawlspace pull in cold air and dilute the warm supply.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Wait 10–15 minutes.** If you see steam rising from the outdoor unit and the warm air returns, you just saw a normal defrost.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's in Heat with the fan on Auto. Set it several degrees above room temperature and replace the batteries. If the thermostat was recently installed, check its setup menu for "heat pump" and "O/B: O." Use the thermostat's own manual for this.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it's dirty or overdue.
- **Breakers and disconnect:** Check the breakers for both the outdoor unit and the air handler. Separate breakers often feed the heat strips. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents:** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and unblocked.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear snow, leaves, or debris from around the cabinet. Look only; don't open the unit.
- **Condensate:** Make sure the air handler's drain line or pan isn't overflowing. A float switch tripped by water can shut down part of the system.
- **Exterior panels:** Confirm the air-handler access panels are fully seated.
- **Emergency Heat:** If the air stays cold, switch the thermostat to Emergency Heat (if available) to stay warm until service.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Verify the call for heat.** The tech confirms the thermostat calls for heat and checks O/B and W signals at the air handler and outdoor unit.
- **Measure temperature split.** The tech compares return and supply air temperatures. A split of about 20–30°F in heat mode is typical, depending on outdoor temperature.
- **Check the compressor.** This includes amp draw, capacitor, contactor, and overload.
- **Read the outdoor board.** The tech checks the defrost control board for fault flashes and lockout history.
- **Test the reversing valve.** This means testing the solenoid coil voltage and resistance, then checking line temperatures to confirm valve position.
- **Check the charge.** The tech attaches gauges and checks pressures, superheat, and subcooling. A low charge calls for leak search, not just a top-off.
- **Test auxiliary heat.** This includes the heat strips, sequencers or relays, and limit switches, including amp draw during an aux call.
- **Inspect ducts.** The tech may look for duct leakage if the equipment checks out.

A good quote names the specific failed part and the measurement that proved it. Be cautious of "add refrigerant" quotes with no leak diagnosis.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool air for a few minutes, steam from outdoor unit | Normal defrost | Wait; no action | None unless it lasts over 15 min |
| Cold air right after a thermostat install | O/B or system-type setting wrong | Check thermostat setup (O for Rheem) | Verify wiring at air handler/outdoor unit |
| Cold air, fan runs even when not heating | Fan set to On | Set fan to Auto | None |
| Weak, lukewarm air, dirty filter | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Check blower and coil if no improvement |
| Outdoor unit runs, air is chilled like AC | Reversing valve or coil stuck | Switch to Emergency Heat | Test coil; replace coil or valve |
| Lukewarm air, worse in cold weather, long runs | Low refrigerant | Switch to Emergency Heat | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor fan runs, compressor silent | Capacitor, contactor, or lockout | Reset breaker once | Test and replace electrical parts |
| Cold air only during defrost or deep cold | Failed aux heat strips | Check air-handler breakers once | Test elements, sequencers, limits |
| Some rooms cold, attic ducts | Duct leakage | Check visible register openings | Duct sealing and repair |

## Repair costs

- **Thermostat batteries, filter, settings:** $0–$40 DIY.
- **New thermostat:** $50–$250 DIY, or $150–$400 installed.
- **Service call and diagnosis:** $100–$250.
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400.
- **Contactor:** $150–$400.
- **Reversing valve solenoid coil:** $150–$400.
- **Reversing valve replacement:** $900–$1,800, including refrigerant recovery and recharge.
- **Refrigerant leak search and repair:** $300–$1,500+, depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A or R-454B).
- **Heat strip element:** $200–$600.
- **Sequencer or relay:** $150–$450.
- **Defrost control board:** $300–$700.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800–$3,500+. At this cost, many owners compare against replacing the whole system.
- **Duct sealing:** $300–$2,000, depending on access and scope.
