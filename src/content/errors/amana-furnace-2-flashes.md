## What this code means

Two flashes on your Amana furnace's diagnostic LED means the **pressure switch is stuck closed** — the control board saw the pressure switch contacts already closed *before* the inducer motor started.

Normally the pressure switch is open when the furnace is idle. When a call for heat arrives, the inducer motor spins up, creates negative pressure (draft), and *then* the switch closes to prove airflow. If the board sees the switch already closed at rest, it can't trust that draft is being proven correctly, so it refuses to start and flashes the fault.

This is almost always a **stuck switch** or a **shorted/pinched wire** in the pressure switch circuit — not a venting or gas-supply problem. On most Amana integrated furnace controls this fault holds the sequence until the switch opens; some boards will retry automatically once the condition clears, others hold until power is cycled. Confirm the exact behavior on the wiring/flash legend inside your blower compartment, since legends vary by board.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed pressure switch (stuck closed)** — the diaphragm or contacts have failed in the closed position. The single most common cause of this specific code.
2. **Shorted or pinched wiring in the pressure switch circuit** — a wire chafed against sheet metal or a damaged connector is bridging the circuit closed.
3. **Moisture or debris in the switch** — condensate or dust holding the diaphragm contacts closed, especially on high-efficiency (90%+) models.
4. **Wrong pressure switch installed** — a replacement switch with the incorrect trip rating that reads closed at rest.
5. **Faulty control board input** — rare, but a board that misreads the switch state can produce the same flash.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform. Everything inside the cabinet is technician work.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to Heat and above room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A severely clogged filter won't cause this code, but replace it if dirty — clean airflow rules it out.
- **Breaker/switch:** Cycle the furnace breaker (or the wall switch by the furnace) **once** to attempt a single reset. Do not reset repeatedly.
- **Visible vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents around the house are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate line (90%+ models):** If your furnace makes water, check that the condensate drain isn't clogged or backed up — standing water near the switch can hold it closed. Clear an obvious external clog only.
- **Panels:** Make sure the blower door and access panels are fully seated; a loose door switch can complicate startup.

If two flashes return after one reset, stop and call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A pro will verify the fault and pinpoint the cause so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Confirm the flash code** against the board's legend and the fault history.
2. **Meter the pressure switch at rest** — with the inducer off, the contacts should read open. A closed reading confirms a stuck switch.
3. **Inspect the switch wiring and connectors** for chafing, pinch points, or a short to the cabinet.
4. **Check for moisture or debris** in the switch and its sensing tube/port, common on condensing furnaces.
5. **Verify the switch rating** matches the furnace spec (wrong-rated replacements cause repeat faults).
6. **Test the board input** if the switch and wiring check out, to rule out a control fault.

A thorough tech tests the switch and wiring *before* recommending a new board — a board replacement should be a last resort here.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 flashes, no ignition attempt | Pressure switch stuck closed | Cycle power once; confirm flash code | Meter switch at rest, replace if stuck |
| Code returns after single reset | Shorted or pinched wiring | None — call a pro | Trace and repair/replace circuit wiring |
| Fault worse in humid weather | Moisture in switch (90%+) | Clear obvious condensate clog | Clean switch/port, correct drainage |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts plus labor:

- **Single reset (no parts):** $0 DIY if it doesn't return.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90 – $160.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150 – $300 including labor.
- **Wiring/connector repair:** $120 – $250 depending on access.
- **Cleaning switch and correcting condensate drainage:** $120 – $220.
- **Integrated furnace control board (rare for this code):** $350 – $650.

Most 2-flash repairs land in the **$150 – $300** range because a failed pressure switch or its wiring is the usual culprit.
