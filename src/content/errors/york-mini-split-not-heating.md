## What this code means

"Not heating" is a symptom, not a stored fault code. It means your York ductless heat pump runs, or appears to run, but the room doesn't warm up.

**If the indoor head is flashing a code or showing one on its display, look that code up first.** A displayed code narrows the cause far more than the symptom does. Code meanings differ between York mini-split series.

How this page fits with related pages:

- **Cooling problems:** see York Mini-Split Not Cooling.
- **Ducted York heat pumps:** see York Heat Pump Not Heating.

This page covers what is specific to ductless heads in heat mode:

- Remote and mode settings
- Multi-zone mode conflicts
- Cold-draft delay and defrost
- Low outdoor temperature limits
- Faults in the inverter, refrigerant circuit and reversing valve

Some fault conditions clear on their own once the triggering condition passes. Others hold until power is cycled. This varies by model. **A single breaker reset is reasonable. Repeated resets are not.**

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Remote or mode setting error.** Common examples:
   - The remote is set to Cool, Dry, Fan or Auto instead of Heat.
   - The setpoint is below room temperature.
   - Timer or sleep mode is active.
   - Remote batteries are weak, so commands aren't received.
2. **Normal operation mistaken for a fault.** This includes the 2–5 minute warm-up delay at startup and defrost cycles in cold, humid weather.
3. **Multi-zone mode conflict.** Another indoor head is set to cool or dry while this one calls for heat.
4. **Dirty indoor filters or coil.** Restricted airflow cuts heat output. It can also trigger high-pressure protection, which makes the unit cycle off.
5. **Outdoor unit blocked by snow or ice.** It may be buried in snow or iced over because drainage is poor or defrost is failing.
6. **Outdoor temperature below the model's heating range.** Capacity drops, or the unit enters protection mode.
7. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** The unit gives weak heat that gets worse over time.
8. **Sensor faults.** A failed indoor room sensor, coil sensor or outdoor sensor can give the board bad readings.
9. **Reversing valve or coil failure.** The unit cools when set to heat.
10. **Inverter board, outdoor fan motor or compressor failure.** These usually also show an error code.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Check the remote.** Confirm it shows Heat mode (sun icon). Set the temperature at least 3–4°F above room temperature. Cancel any timer and sleep settings.
2. **Replace the remote batteries.** Watch for the indoor unit's beep or light that confirms it received a command. Many heads also have a manual or emergency button under the front panel. Use it to test heat without the remote.
3. **Check the other zones (multi-zone systems).** Set every indoor head to Heat or Off.
4. **Wait out the startup.** Give it 5–10 minutes after turning on heat. If the outdoor unit steams and the indoor fan stops, that is defrost. Let it finish.
5. **Clean the indoor filters.** Lift the front panel and slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry fully, and reinstall. Do not spray or clean the coil or blower wheel yourself.
6. **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Gently remove snow from around and above it by hand or with a broom. Keep 12–24 inches clear. **Don't chip ice off the coil.** Fins and tubing are easy to puncture.
7. **Check the breaker and disconnect.** Confirm the breaker is on and the outdoor disconnect is in the On position. If the breaker has tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
8. **Check the condensate drain.** Water dripping from or behind the indoor head points to a drain problem, which a technician should address. Ice building up under the outdoor unit points to drainage problems in the outdoor pan.

If none of these restore heat, **stop.** Every remaining cause is inside the cabinet or the sealed refrigerant system.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Use this list to judge whether a quote is reasonable:

- **Reads stored error codes.** This may use the head's display, the remote's check function, or the LEDs on the outdoor board.
- **Measures supply air temperature.** In heat mode, air from the head typically runs 30–40°F or more above room temperature once the system is running.
- **Checks refrigerant pressures and temperatures with gauges.** On inverter units, readings are taken at a stable compressor speed. Low charge means finding the leak. **A recharge without a leak search is a red flag.**
- **Confirms the reversing valve shifts.** The tech checks coil voltage and line temperatures in heat mode.
- **Tests sensors.** Thermistors are checked for resistance against the manufacturer's chart.
- **Checks the outdoor fan motor and inverter board** for voltage and operation.
- **Checks defrost operation and the drainage of the outdoor base pan.**
- **Inspects flare connections.** Leaks on ductless systems are often found here.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit doesn't respond to remote | Dead batteries, remote fault | Replace batteries, try the manual button | Test the indoor receiver or board |
| Cold air for first few minutes | Normal warm-up delay | Wait 5–10 minutes | None needed |
| Heat stops, outdoor unit steams | Normal defrost | Wait for the cycle to end | Check defrost if it is excessive |
| One head heats, another won't | Multi-zone mode conflict | Set all heads to Heat or Off | Check the zone board if the conflict persists |
| Weak heat, airflow low | Dirty filters | Clean the filters | Clean the coil and blower wheel |
| Outdoor unit encased in ice | Failed defrost, blocked drainage | Clear snow, don't chip ice | Diagnose defrost sensor or board, base pan heater |
| Weak heat that worsens over weeks | Refrigerant leak | Nothing safe to do | Leak search, repair, evacuate, recharge |
| Blows cold in Heat mode | Reversing valve or sensor | Confirm Heat mode | Test valve coil, replace valve or sensor |
| Error code flashing | Sensor, fan, board or compressor fault | Note the code, reset breaker once | Diagnose per the code, replace parts |
| Stops heating in deep cold | Below model's low-ambient limit | Use backup heat | Confirm limits, discuss a cold-climate unit |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Settings, batteries, filter cleaning | $0 DIY |
| Diagnostic visit | $100–$250 |
| Professional coil and blower cleaning | $150–$400 per head |
| Temperature sensor or thermistor | $150–$350 |
| Leak search plus repair and recharge | $300–$1,200 (flare repairs at the low end, coil leaks at the high end) |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350–$750 |
| Reversing valve | $800–$1,500 (brazing, evacuation and recharge) |
| Indoor or inverter control board | $400–$1,000 |
| Compressor | $1,200–$2,500+ (often weighed against replacing the system) |

**Check your warranty before paying out of pocket.** York parts warranties often require registration. Proper installation records also matter.
