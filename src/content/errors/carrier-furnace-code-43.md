## What this code means

Carrier Furnace **Code 43** is a **pressure switch calibration fault**. On variable-speed condensing furnaces, the control board ramps the inducer motor to specific speeds and watches the **low-pressure switch** to confirm it opens and closes at the expected points. This "calibration" lets the board account for real-world venting resistance.

If the switch changes state at the **wrong inducer speed** — too early, too late, or not at all — the board cannot complete calibration and posts Code 43. The root cause is almost always **venting, the pressure-switch tubing, or the switch itself**, not the gas or ignition system.

Because the furnace typically will not fire until calibration succeeds, this is a **pro-level fault**: it protects against operating with restricted or unsafe venting. The board usually retries on the next heat call, but the code will keep returning until the physical cause is corrected.

> Note: exact code numbering and wording differ between board generations. Confirm the meaning on the fault-code legend printed inside your blower compartment door before ordering parts.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted vent/intake termination** — snow, ice, leaves, insect nests, or a bird screen clogged at the exterior PVC pipes changes the pressure the inducer sees.
2. **Pressure-switch tubing problems** — cracked, kinked, disconnected, or condensate-filled hoses give the switch a false reading during the calibration sweep.
3. **Blocked or sagging condensate drainage** — water backing up in the collector box or trap raises venting resistance and skews calibration.
4. **Failed or out-of-spec low-pressure switch** — a switch that trips at the wrong point (or sticks) can't be calibrated against.
5. **Inducer motor weak or out of range** — a variable-speed inducer that can't hit its target speeds prevents the board from finding a valid calibration.
6. **Vent pipe sizing/length or joint leaks** — improper installation or a loose coupling changes system pressure enough to fail calibration.
7. **Control board fault** — least common; the board's calibration logic or sensing circuit is faulty.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform:

- **Check the thermostat** — confirm it's set to Heat and the temperature is calling. Replace batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Replace a dirty air filter** — a badly clogged filter can disturb airflow and pressures.
- **Inspect exterior vent and intake pipes** — from outside, make sure the PVC terminations aren't blocked by snow, ice, leaves, or nests. Clear anything visible from the outside only.
- **Check the condensate line** — look for a clogged or overflowing drain or full trap; clear standing water at the accessible end if you can do so safely.
- **Reset once** — cycle the furnace switch or breaker off, wait 30 seconds, and turn it back on for **one** retry.
- **Seat the blower door** — make sure the access panel is fully closed; an ajar panel can disturb the door switch. **Do not remove the panel — only confirm from the outside that it is latched.**

If Code 43 returns after one reset, stop and call a technician. Do not open the cabinet or touch the pressure switch, tubing, or inducer.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Read the stored fault history and confirm Code 43 against the board legend.
- Inspect vent and intake piping end-to-end for blockage, slope, length, and leaking joints.
- Check the pressure-switch **tubing** for cracks, kinks, disconnection, or trapped condensate.
- Measure switch operation with a **manometer**, verifying it opens/closes at the rated pressure. There is no single universal value — the rated set point is printed on the switch body and varies by model, so it has to be read off the part in your furnace.
- Verify the **inducer motor** reaches its commanded speeds and draws normal current.
- Inspect the **condensate collector box and trap** for restriction.
- Confirm proper draft, then command a calibration cycle and watch the switch response.
- Replace the pressure switch, tubing, inducer, or board only after isolating the fault — not by guesswork.

Sanity check: a good quote names the specific failed component after testing, not a blanket board swap.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Code 43, furnace won't fire | Blocked vent/intake terminations | Clear visible snow/debris from outside pipes | Inspect full venting, remeasure draft |
| Code 43 after cold/windy weather | Ice or nest in exterior pipes | Clear only what's visible outside | Inspect terminations, verify slope/length |
| Code 43 with water near furnace | Blocked condensate drain | Clear accessible drain end | Clean collector box and trap |
| Code 43, inducer runs but won't validate | Cracked/kinked/full switch tubing | None — internal | Inspect and replace tubing |
| Code 43 persists after clearing vents | Failed pressure switch | None — internal | Manometer test, replace switch |
| Code 43 with weak/noisy inducer | Inducer out of speed range | None — internal | Test and replace inducer motor |
| Code 43 with all else good | Control board fault | None — internal | Verify sensing circuit, replace board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional variation applies):

- **DIY safe checks** (filter, clearing vent/condensate blockage): **$0–$40**
- **Pressure-switch tubing replacement:** **$120–$250**
- **Low-pressure switch replacement:** **$150–$350**
- **Condensate trap/collector cleaning:** **$120–$300**
- **Vent repair or re-piping:** **$200–$700+** depending on scope
- **Inducer motor replacement:** **$500–$1,200** installed — variable-speed/ECM inducer assemblies on condensing furnaces sit at the upper end of this band
- **Control board replacement:** **$500–$1,200** — variable-speed/Infinity boards are among the pricier furnace parts
- **Diagnostic/service call:** **$90–$180**, often credited toward the repair

Simple blockages cost little; component replacement is where bills climb. Always get the failed part identified by test before authorizing an inducer assembly or a board.
