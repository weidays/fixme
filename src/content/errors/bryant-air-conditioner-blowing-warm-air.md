## What this code means

"Blowing warm air" is a symptom, not a stored Bryant fault code. It means the indoor blower is moving air, but that air is at or above room temperature. That is different from air that is cool but not cold enough. In a Bryant split system, warm supply air almost always means one of three things:

- The **outdoor unit (compressor) isn't running** while the indoor fan is.
- The **furnace or electric heat is running** when it shouldn't be.
- **Hot attic or garage air is getting into the ducts**.

If your issue is weak cooling or a fan with no cold air, see the related pages listed below. This page covers the cases where the air is actually warm.

Reset behavior depends on the cause. A compressor's internal thermal overload resets by itself once it cools. On Evolution and some Preferred outdoor units, the control board can hold the compressor off for a set time, or lock it out until power is cycled, after repeated high- or low-pressure trips. Check your model's installation manual for the exact lockout logic.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat set wrong.** The thermostat is in Heat or Auto with a heat call, or the fan is set to On with no cooling call. With fan On, the blower moves unconditioned air between cycles, which feels warm in a hot attic or duct system.
2. **Outdoor unit has no power.** The breaker is tripped, the outdoor disconnect is pulled, or the low-voltage fuse on the furnace board has blown. The indoor fan still runs, so you get warm air.
3. **Failed run capacitor or contactor in the outdoor unit.** This is the most common component failure behind "fan runs inside, outside unit hums or sits silent." It is technician work.
4. **Compressor off on thermal overload.** A dirty condenser coil, a failed outdoor fan motor, or extreme heat can overheat the compressor. It shuts off, the indoor fan keeps running, and it may restart hours later.
5. **Pressure-switch trip or lockout from low refrigerant.** On units with a low-pressure switch, a leak can shut the compressor down entirely. The air turns warm rather than just weak. Evolution systems typically log this on the thermostat.
6. **Leaking or disconnected return or supply duct in the attic.** The return pulls in 130°F+ attic air, or the supply dumps cold air into the attic. This is common after attic work or with aging flex duct.
7. **Furnace heating in cooling mode.** A wiring fault, shorted thermostat wire, or a stuck gas valve or heat relay can cause this. It is rare, but if the furnace burner is lit during a cooling call, shut the system off.
8. **Failed compressor.** The compressor is seized, or its windings are open or shorted. This usually only becomes the verdict after a technician has ruled out the capacitor, contactor and overload.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set it to **Cool**, fan to **Auto**, and the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank. On Evolution thermostats, note any fault message shown.
- **Breakers:** At the main panel, check both the furnace or air handler breaker and the separate double-pole AC breaker. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and see Breaker keeps tripping.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Make sure the disconnect box by the condenser is fully seated or switched on. Do not open the condenser cabinet.
- **Listen at the outdoor unit:** During a cooling call, is the fan spinning and the compressor running? Silence, or a hum with no fan, is information to give the technician. Don't poke anything to make it spin.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it's dirty.
- **One power reset:** If the unit seems locked out, turn the AC breaker off for 5 minutes and back on once. Wait 5–10 minutes, because many Bryant controls have a built-in anti-short-cycle delay.
- **Visible vents and registers:** Confirm they are open and that return grilles aren't blocked.
- **Condensate:** Check that the drain line isn't overflowing. A tripped float switch on some installs can cut cooling.
- **Attic ducts (look only):** If you can safely see your attic ducts, note any obvious disconnected or crushed sections. Leave repairs to a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- They will confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor unit (the Y and C terminals) during a cooling call. They also check the furnace board's low-voltage fuse and transformer.
- They will check line voltage at the disconnect and contactor, and inspect the contactor points for pitting.
- They will test the run capacitor's microfarad rating against its label. A reading outside ±6% (or the stated tolerance) means it should be replaced.
- If the compressor is hot and silent, they will let the overload reset or cool it, then measure winding resistance (common to run, common to start) to separate a tripped overload from a failed compressor.
- They will connect gauges or probes to check refrigerant pressures, superheat and subcooling. If the charge is low, they should do a leak search, not just top it off.
- On Evolution or Preferred communicating units, they will read the fault history and outdoor board LED codes for pressure-switch or high-temperature lockouts.
- They will measure the temperature split across the indoor coil and inspect ducts for leaks or disconnections.
- They will confirm the furnace burners or heat strips are not energized during a cooling call.

A good quote names the failed part and the measurement that proved it. Be wary of "you need refrigerant" with no leak search, or "new compressor" without a capacitor test.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, outdoor unit completely silent | Tripped breaker, disconnect off, blown low-voltage fuse | Check thermostat mode, reset breaker once, seat disconnect | Find the cause of the blown fuse or trip, replace the fuse |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan doesn't spin | Failed capacitor or seized motor | Turn cooling off, call | Test and replace the capacitor or fan motor |
| Outdoor fan runs, compressor silent | Capacitor, contactor, overload or failed compressor | Turn off, call | Capacitor, contactor and winding tests |
| Cools, then turns warm after hours of running | Compressor overheating or pressure-switch trip | Replace filter, keep condenser area clear | Clean condenser coil, leak search and charge check |
| Air warm only at some vents, attic is hot | Duct leak or disconnection | Note which rooms are affected | Reconnect and seal ducts |
| Air hot, furnace burner lit or heat smell | Heat call or wiring fault | Set thermostat to Off, call | Trace thermostat wiring, check gas valve or heat relay |
| Air feels warm between cycles | Fan set to On | Set fan to Auto | None needed |
