## What this code means

On most Bryant furnaces with LED diagnostics, **Code 12 indicates a "blower on after power up" or a power-interruption event** — the control board detected that line voltage was lost and restored (or dropped momentarily) while there was an active call for heat or fan. The board runs the blower and reports the interruption so you know why the furnace behaved oddly.

In plain terms: something cut power to the furnace, even briefly, during a heating cycle. That could be a genuine utility blip, a breaker issue, a loose connection, or a component (transformer, door switch, or board) that can't hold power reliably.

> **Important:** The exact wording of Code 12 varies by control board generation. Some older Bryant/Carrier boards use flash codes differently. Always confirm the meaning against the diagnostic label printed inside your furnace's blower compartment door before acting.

Because a recurring Code 12 often traces back to an electrical fault inside the cabinet, this is rated **pro** severity — a one-time occurrence may be harmless, but repeat codes need a technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **A one-time power interruption** — a brief utility outage, a storm, or someone flipping the breaker. The most common and least serious cause.
2. **Tripped or loose breaker** — a breaker that's partially tripped or a loose lug feeding the furnace circuit causes intermittent power loss.
3. **Loose or corroded line-voltage wiring** — connections at the furnace junction box or disconnect switch that vibrate loose over time.
4. **Failing door safety (interlock) switch** — an intermittent blower-door switch that cuts power when the panel shifts or vibrates.
5. **Weak or failing 24V transformer** — a transformer that drops out under load can register as a power interruption.
6. **Failing control board** — the board itself losing or misreading power, especially on older units.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the gas valve, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Check your breaker.** Find the furnace circuit in your electrical panel. If it's tripped, flip it fully OFF, then back ON. A breaker that trips again immediately means an electrical fault — stop and call a pro.
- **Check the furnace switch.** Many furnaces have a light-switch-style shutoff on or near the unit. Make sure it's ON.
- **Confirm the blower door is fully seated.** The door must be closed and latched for the interlock switch to make contact. Re-seat it firmly.
- **Note recent power events.** Did the lights flicker or the power go out recently? A single Code 12 after a storm is usually nothing.
- **Replace a dirty filter and check the thermostat.** Fresh batteries in the thermostat and a clean filter rule out unrelated issues that can complicate diagnosis.
- **Watch for repeats.** Let the furnace run a cycle. If Code 12 doesn't return, you're likely fine. If it comes back, stop cycling it and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. **Read stored fault history** on the control board to see how often Code 12 has occurred and alongside which other codes.
2. **Measure incoming line voltage** at the furnace to confirm stable 120V under load.
3. **Inspect and tighten line-voltage connections** at the junction box, disconnect, and breaker.
4. **Test the door interlock switch** for continuity and reliable actuation.
5. **Check the 24V transformer output** to confirm it holds steady voltage during the blower and inducer draw.
6. **Evaluate the control board** for cold solder joints, burn marks, or intermittent power handling — replacing it only after ruling out wiring and transformer.

If a tech quotes a board replacement without first checking wiring, connections, and the transformer, ask them to confirm those were ruled out.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Code 12 once after a storm | Utility power blip | Cycle furnace; monitor | None if it doesn't recur |
| Code 12 with breaker tripped | Tripped/loose breaker | Reset breaker once | Diagnose repeat trips, tighten lugs |
| Code 12 when door is bumped | Intermittent door switch | Re-seat blower door | Test/replace interlock switch |
| Code 12 recurring, no outage | Loose wiring or weak transformer | Check furnace switch | Tighten wiring, test/replace transformer |
| Code 12 with erratic blower behavior | Failing control board | None | Inspect and replace board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined unless noted:

- **Reset breaker / re-seat door (DIY):** **$0**
- **Tighten loose wiring / connections:** **$100 – $200** (mostly labor / service call)
- **Door interlock switch:** **$100 – $200**
- **24V transformer:** **$150 – $300**
- **Control board replacement:** **$400 – $600**, occasionally higher on newer variable-speed boards

A typical diagnostic/service call runs **$90 – $180** and is often credited toward the repair. If your furnace is well out of warranty and needs a board, weigh the repair against the age of the unit.
