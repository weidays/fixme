## What this code means

"Loud noise" is a symptom, not a stored fault code. Most Carrier mini-splits will not display an error for noise alone. If the noise comes with a blinking light or an error code, write that code down. It often points straight to the failing part, such as a fan motor or compressor drive fault.

The first job is to find where the noise comes from: the **indoor wall head** or the **outdoor condenser**. The second job is to describe the sound:

- rattle
- grind
- squeal
- buzz
- bang
- gurgle

Some sounds are normal on inverter mini-splits:

- **Defrost whoosh:** a loud whoosh or hiss in heat mode while the unit defrosts.
- **Plastic ticking:** light ticking as the indoor housing warms or cools.
- **Compressor ramp-up:** a change in tone as the variable-speed compressor speeds up.

These normal sounds do not trigger or store a fault code.

If you hear a new, persistent mechanical sound, call a technician. That is why severity is marked **pro**. The causes are inside sealed cabinets, near fan blades, high-voltage inverter boards and refrigerant circuits.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

Start with the mode the unit was in when you heard the noise. **In heat mode,** begin at #1. Normal defrost and refrigerant-flow sounds are among the most common reasons homeowners report a loud noise. **In cooling mode,** defrost does not run, so start at #2.

1. **Normal defrost and refrigerant-flow sounds (mainly heat mode).** Whooshing, hissing or gurgling in heat mode or at startup. These are usually benign. Confirm the unit goes back to heating normally afterward.
2. **Loose panels, front grille, or mounting hardware.** The indoor head may be loose on its wall plate, or outdoor cabinet screws may have backed out. Either causes rattling or buzzing that changes with fan speed.
3. **Dirty filter or debris in the indoor blower wheel.** Restricted airflow causes whistling. Dust buildup on the cross-flow (tangential) blower wheel throws it off balance and causes a rhythmic thumping or wobble.
4. **Debris or ice in the outdoor fan.** Leaves, sticks or ice can strike the fan blade, causing ticking, scraping or a loud whack.
5. **Failing fan motor bearings.** This can be the indoor DC fan motor or the outdoor fan motor. Expect a grinding, squealing or growling sound.
6. **Worn compressor isolation mounts or a vibrating line set.** The rubber grommets under the compressor may be worn, or copper lines may touch the cabinet or wall. The result is buzzing or a vibration that rattles through the wall.
7. **Refrigerant problems.** A low charge can cause a steady hiss or bubbling at the indoor head. A restriction can do the same.
8. **Compressor or inverter board problems.** Loud knocking, clanking or harsh buzzing from the outdoor unit can come from internal compressor wear, or from the inverter drive struggling to start the compressor. This often comes with an error code.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Clean the filters.** Turn the unit off at the remote and lift the front panel. Slide out the mesh filters, rinse them in lukewarm water, let them dry, and reinstall them.
- **Check the remote settings.** "Turbo," "Powerful" or high fan speed is much louder than auto or quiet mode. Confirm the mode is set the way you want it.
- **Seat the front panel and louvers.** Make sure the indoor front panel clicks fully closed. Check that the louvers are not jammed or chattering.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove leaves, branches and snow from around and on top of the condenser. Do this with power off at the disconnect or breaker. Do not reach into the fan guard.
- **Check the exterior panels.** Confirm the outdoor service panel is in place. Do not remove panels.
- **Check the condensate line.** Look at the drain outlet. A gurgling indoor head with dripping can mean a clogged drain. See the leaking water page.
- **Note your model numbers.** Write down the model numbers from the nameplates on both the indoor head and the outdoor unit. They are different, and the technician will need both.
- **Try one power reset.** If the noise comes with an error light, turn the breaker off for 5 minutes, then back on. Do this once only. If the noise or code returns, leave the unit off and call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Locate and characterize the noise.** The tech will listen to both units in each mode and at each fan speed. This separates normal defrost and refrigerant sounds from mechanical faults.
- **Read fault history.** Many Carrier mini-splits store diagnostic codes. The tech can retrieve them from the indoor display, the remote's check function, or the outdoor board LEDs. Outdoor-board LED codes vary by series, so the tech will match them to the outdoor unit's model number from its nameplate.
- **Inspect the indoor blower.** With power isolated, the tech checks the cross-flow wheel for dust buildup, cracks and wobble. They also check the fan motor bearing for play and noise. Cleaning may involve a coil and blower wash.
- **Inspect the outdoor fan and motor.** They check blade clearance, the blade hub fastener, bearing condition and motor mount tightness.
- **Check vibration points.** This covers compressor grommets, line set contact points, wall-sleeve contact, and the mounting pad or wall bracket level.
- **Run electrical tests.** The tech measures fan motor and compressor current and voltage. On inverter units, they check the drive board and look for compressor phase faults.
- **Check refrigerant.** Gauges and superheat/subcooling readings show whether hissing or bubbling means low charge or a restriction. Leak search is done if needed.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whoosh or hiss in heat mode | Normal defrost cycle | Confirm heat resumes afterward | Only if defrost repeats constantly |
| Rattle that changes with fan speed | Loose front panel, grille or cabinet screws | Reseat the indoor front panel; check the outdoor panel is seated | Tighten fasteners and secure the wall plate or cabinet |
| Whistling at indoor head | Clogged filter | Clean the filters | Clean the coil and blower if heavily soiled |
| Thumping or wobbling indoor | Dirty or unbalanced blower wheel | Turn off; clean the filters | Clean or replace the blower wheel |
| Ticking or whacking outdoors | Debris or ice hitting the fan | Clear debris around the unit with power off | Remove obstruction; check blades and motor |
| Grinding or squealing | Fan motor bearings failing | Turn the unit off | Replace the indoor or outdoor fan motor |
| Buzz or vibration through the wall | Worn compressor mounts or line set contact | None | Replace grommets; isolate and re-clamp the lines |
| Constant hiss or bubbling indoors | Low charge or restriction | Turn the unit off | Leak search, repair and recharge |
| Loud knocking or clanking outdoors, often with a code | Compressor or inverter fault | One power reset, then call | Diagnose the board or compressor; repair or replace |
