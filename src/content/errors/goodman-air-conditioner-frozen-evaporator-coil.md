## What this code means

Goodman air conditioners don't usually flash a specific "frozen coil" error code — this is a **physical condition**, not a board fault. Ice forms on the indoor evaporator coil (inside your air handler or on top of the furnace) when the coil gets too cold and moisture in the air freezes onto it instead of draining away.

Once ice covers the coil, airflow drops to near zero, cooling stops, and you may see water pooling around the indoor unit as the ice melts. Left unchecked, a frozen coil can flood-back liquid refrigerant to the outdoor compressor — the single most expensive part in the system — which is why we rate this **pro-level**.

The two big buckets of causes are **not enough warm air moving across the coil** (airflow problems) and **not enough refrigerant** (leaks). Both make the coil run below freezing.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the most common cause by far. Restricted airflow lets the coil get too cold.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or blocked returns starve the coil of air.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm on the coil fins insulate it and kill airflow.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — low charge drops coil pressure and temperature below freezing. Needs a pro.
5. **Weak or failing blower motor / wheel** — a slow or dirty blower moves too little air.
6. **Running the AC in cool weather** — operating below about 60°F outdoors can freeze the coil.
7. **Stuck contactor or thermostat wiring fault** — the system runs cooling nonstop and overcools the coil.
8. **Clogged condensate drain** — a backed-up drain can trip a float switch or let water refreeze on the coil.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do them **before** paying for a service call.

- **Turn cooling OFF, fan ON.** Set your thermostat to OFF for cooling and the fan to ON. Running the blower with the compressor off thaws the coil safely. Do this first and let it fully melt.
- **Check the air filter.** Pull it out. If you can't see light through it, replace it. A dirty filter is the #1 cause.
- **Open your vents.** Walk the house and confirm supply registers and return grilles are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Check the thermostat.** Make sure it's set to a reasonable temperature (not, say, 60°F) and swap the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Check the breaker.** If the system tripped, that's a separate issue — reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Look for standing water in the drain pan or a full pan. Clearing a clogged drain line with a wet/dry vacuum at the outdoor termination point is a safe DIY step.
- **Look for melting ice.** If you see frost on the copper lines at the outdoor unit or ice on the indoor coil, confirm the thaw is complete before restarting cooling.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bend coil fins. Those require a licensed tech.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Goodman tech will work through this so you can sanity-check the visit:

1. **Confirm the coil is fully thawed** before testing — you can't measure a frozen system.
2. **Check airflow first** — filter condition, coil cleanliness, blower speed, and static pressure. This rules out the cheap causes.
3. **Attach gauges** and read suction/head pressures and superheat/subcooling to judge refrigerant charge. Correct values depend on your Goodman model's refrigerant (R-410A vs newer R-32/R-454B).
4. **If charge is low, leak-search** — with electronic detectors, UV dye, or nitrogen pressure test. Adding refrigerant without finding the leak is a temporary (and illegal-to-vent) fix.
5. **Inspect the blower motor and wheel** for dirt buildup or failure.
6. **Check the contactor and thermostat wiring** for a stuck-on cooling call.

If a tech wants to "just add a pound of refrigerant" without checking airflow or searching for a leak, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on indoor coil, weak airflow | Dirty filter / blocked vents | Replace filter, open vents, thaw | Verify static pressure, clean coil |
| Coil re-freezes after every thaw | Low refrigerant (leak) | Thaw and stop running cooling | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Water around indoor unit | Melting ice / clogged drain | Clear drain line, thaw coil | Clear drain, check float switch |
| Warm air from vents, unit running | Frozen coil blocking airflow | Turn off cooling, fan ON to thaw | Diagnose airflow vs refrigerant |
| Frost on outdoor copper lines | Low charge or low airflow | Thaw, replace filter | Measure charge, leak test |
| Coil freezes in cool weather | Running AC below ~60°F outdoors | Stop cooling until it's warmer | Confirm no other fault |
| Blower runs slow / noisy | Failing blower motor or dirty wheel | Replace filter, note noise | Clean/replace blower motor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Your Goodman's model, region, and refrigerant type affect the total.

- **Air filter replacement:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $0 DIY – $150 pro.
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$400.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$700 (ECM motors run higher).
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$450.
- **Refrigerant recharge:** $150–$600 depending on amount and refrigerant type — newer R-32/R-454B systems can cost more per pound.
- **Leak repair (line/coil braze):** $500–$1,000+.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500+ if the coil itself is leaking; sometimes covered under Goodman's parts warranty (labor is not).

If the coil freezing was caught early and the cause was just a filter, your total may be **$0–$40**. A leak-plus-recharge is where it climbs past **$1,000**.
