## What this code means

Goodman air conditioners don't display a "frozen evaporator coil" error code — there's no fault light for it. Instead you notice the **symptoms**: weak or no cold air, ice visible on the copper lines or the indoor coil, water pooling near the furnace or air handler, and a system that runs constantly without cooling.

The evaporator coil is the indoor part of your AC where refrigerant absorbs heat from your home's air. It only works right when **enough warm air keeps flowing across it**. If airflow drops or refrigerant pressure falls too low, the coil surface drops below freezing, moisture condenses and turns to frost, and the ice snowballs until the whole coil is blocked. A fully iced coil moves almost no air and can send liquid refrigerant back to the compressor — the expensive failure this problem is really about.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. A clogged filter starves the coil of airflow.
2. **Blocked return or supply vents / closed dampers** — furniture over returns, too many closed registers, or a collapsed flex duct restricts airflow the same way.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm insulate the coil so it can't shed cold, and it ices over.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — low charge drops coil pressure and temperature below freezing. Goodman systems don't "use up" refrigerant, so low charge always means a leak.
5. **Failing or slow blower motor / bad capacitor** — the blower can't push enough air even with a clean filter.
6. **Running the AC in cold weather** — cooling when it's below ~60°F outside can freeze the coil.
7. **Undersized or restrictive ductwork** — a chronic design issue that keeps a system on the edge.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all homeowner-safe. Do not open the sealed refrigerant system or bypass any switch.

- **Turn cooling OFF, fan ON.** On the thermostat set mode to OFF and fan to ON. This thaws the coil safely — expect 1–3 hours. Don't run cooling again until it's fully thawed.
- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray or you can't see light through it, swap it. Note the size printed on the frame.
- **Open and unblock vents.** Make sure supply and return grilles are open and not covered by rugs or furniture. Open closed registers in unused rooms.
- **Check the breaker.** After thawing, confirm the AC breaker in the panel hasn't tripped.
- **Check the thermostat.** Fresh batteries if it's battery-powered; confirm it's set to COOL at a reasonable setpoint (not, say, 60°F).
- **Look at the condensate drain and pan.** Melting ice overflows fast — clear standing water and make sure the drain line isn't clogged. A dropped float switch (from an overflowing pan) can also stop the system.

If the coil re-freezes after a full thaw and a clean filter, stop DIY and call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech confirms the cause rather than just recharging refrigerant. Expect them to:

- Confirm the coil is fully thawed, then run the system and **measure airflow / temperature split** across the coil.
- **Inspect the blower wheel and motor**, check the run capacitor, and verify fan speed settings.
- Check the **filter and evaporator coil** for dirt.
- Connect gauges to read **suction and head pressures** and calculate superheat/subcooling — this tells them if the charge is correct.
- If charge is low, perform a **leak search** (electronic detector, UV dye, or nitrogen pressure test) — a leak, not just a top-off, is the real fix.

Be cautious of any quote that adds refrigerant without mentioning a leak search. Topping off a leaking system just re-freezes weeks later.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on coil, filter is filthy | Clogged filter | Thaw, replace filter | Verify airflow, check duct sizing |
| Weak airflow, clean filter | Failing blower / bad capacitor | Thaw, confirm vents open | Test motor, replace capacitor/motor |
| Ice + hissing, warm air | Low refrigerant (leak) | Thaw, shut off cooling | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Coils dirty and gray | Dirty evaporator coil | None (call pro) | Clean coil, sanitize |
| Water on floor after thaw | Overflow / clogged drain | Clear standing water, flush drain | Clear line, test float switch |
| Freezes only in cool weather | Running AC below ~60°F | Don't cool below setpoint | Advise on outdoor low-temp limits |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **New air filter (DIY):** $8–$30
- **Thaw + diagnostic service call:** $80–$180
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Blower run capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Blower motor (ECM/PSC):** $400–$900
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$450
- **Leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,600 depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A units cost more if the coil itself leaks)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,500 if the coil is the leak source and out of warranty

Goodman's registered warranty often covers the coil part itself (commonly 10 years), but **labor and refrigerant are usually not covered** — that's where most of the cost lands.
