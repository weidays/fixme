## What this code means

A frozen evaporator coil is **not a code Goodman displays** — there's no blinking LED or fault number for it. It's a physical symptom you notice: ice on the copper coil or refrigerant lines inside your air handler or furnace cabinet, weak or warm airflow, and often water pooling under the unit as the ice melts.

The evaporator coil is where refrigerant absorbs heat from your indoor air. For that to work, warm air must keep flowing across the coil while the coil stays at the correct pressure and temperature. When **airflow drops** or **refrigerant pressure falls too low**, the coil surface drops below freezing, condensation turns to frost, and the frost snowballs into a solid block of ice. Once iced over, the coil can't absorb heat at all — so the house stops cooling even though the outdoor unit keeps running.

Because this applies to the coil and airflow path, it affects essentially all Goodman split-system air conditioners and heat pumps (GSX, GSXC, GSZ, ASX, ASXC and similar) paired with a matched indoor coil or air handler. The causes below are the same across the lineup.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. A restricted filter starves the coil of airflow and it freezes.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or furniture blocking returns reduces total airflow.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm on the coil fins insulate it and choke airflow.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — low charge drops coil pressure and temperature, causing ice. This is a repair, not a "top-off."
5. **Failing or slow blower motor / bad capacitor** — the blower can't move enough air across the coil.
6. **Running the AC in cool weather** — operating below roughly 60°F outdoors can freeze the coil on many systems.
7. **Clogged condensate drain backing up** — less common as a freeze cause, but often shows up alongside ice as water damage.
8. **Stuck or failing TXV/metering device** — restricts refrigerant flow and can ice the coil (technician diagnosis).

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. If they don't solve it, or the coil re-freezes, call a pro.

- **Turn off cooling first.** Set the thermostat to **OFF for cooling** and the **fan to ON**. Running the fan with the compressor off melts the ice safely. Give it 1–3 hours (up to 24 for heavy ice).
- **Check and replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, swap it. This alone fixes many freeze-ups.
- **Open your vents.** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or curtains.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to a reasonable temperature and not stuck in a mode that runs the compressor in cold weather.
- **Check the breaker.** If the unit tripped, don't reset repeatedly — reset once; if it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Look at the condensate drain.** Clear standing water in the pan and, if you're comfortable, clear the drain line with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor termination.
- **Do not chip or scrape the ice**, and do not touch the refrigerant lines. Let it thaw on its own.

After a full thaw, run the AC again and watch it. If it cools normally and stays ice-free, the filter or airflow was likely the cause. If it re-freezes, you have a deeper problem below.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Goodman tech won't just recharge refrigerant and leave. Expect them to:

- Confirm the system has fully thawed before testing.
- Inspect the filter, coil cleanliness, and blower wheel; measure **static pressure** to gauge airflow.
- Check blower motor amp draw and the **run capacitor**.
- Attach gauges to read **suction and head pressure** and measure **superheat/subcooling** to determine if the charge is correct or if there's a leak.
- If charge is low, perform a **leak search** (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test) rather than just adding refrigerant.
- Inspect the **TXV/metering device** and condensate drain.

If a quote jumps straight to "add refrigerant" with no leak search or airflow check, get a second opinion — topping off a leaking system is a temporary fix you'll pay for again.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on coil/lines, house not cooling | Dirty filter / low airflow | Set fan ON to thaw, replace filter, open vents | Measure static pressure, clean coil |
| Coil re-freezes after clean filter | Dirty evaporator coil or slow blower | Thaw again, verify filter and vents | Deep-clean coil, test blower motor & capacitor |
| Warm air, coil freezes, hissing | Low refrigerant from a leak | Turn off cooling, thaw, call pro | Leak search, repair, evacuate & recharge |
| Water pooling under air handler | Melting ice + clogged drain | Clear pan/line, replace filter | Clear drain, check pan, address freeze cause |
| Freezing only in cool weather | Running AC below ~60°F | Don't run cooling in cold weather | Confirm no other fault; advise on operation |
| Coil freezes, blower sounds weak | Failing blower motor / bad capacitor | Thaw system | Replace capacitor or blower motor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges. Actual prices vary by region and contractor.

- **Air filter replacement:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Coil deep-cleaning:** $150–$400.
- **Blower run capacitor:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Blower motor (ECM or PSC):** $400–$900 installed.
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250.
- **TXV / metering device replacement:** $400–$800.
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $100–$350.
- **Leak repair + evacuate and recharge:** $500–$1,500+, depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs newer refrigerants).
- **Full evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500+ if the coil itself is corroded or leaking.

If your Goodman system is under the original **10-year parts limited warranty** (registered within 60 days of install), the coil or motor part may be covered — but labor and refrigerant usually are not.
