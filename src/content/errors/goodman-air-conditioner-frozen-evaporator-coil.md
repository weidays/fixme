## What this code means

"Frozen evaporator coil" isn't a fault code on Goodman equipment — it's a physical condition you or a technician observe: a layer of ice or frost on the indoor coil (inside the air handler or above the furnace), and often on the copper refrigerant lines and even the outdoor unit.

Your evaporator coil is where refrigerant absorbs heat from your indoor air. For that to work, warm air has to keep flowing across the coil and the refrigerant charge has to be correct. When either of those breaks down, the coil surface drops below freezing, condensation turns to ice, and the ice snowballs — blocking airflow further until the whole coil is a block of frost.

The result is an AC that runs and runs but delivers little or no cool air, weak airflow, water pooling near the indoor unit when it thaws, and sometimes ice visible on the large copper line at the outdoor condenser.

Some Goodman systems with variable-speed or communicating air handlers may log a low-airflow or pressure-related fault, but a plain single-stage system will show nothing on a board — you just find the ice.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the #1 cause. A restricted filter starves the coil of warm return air, so it freezes.
2. **Blocked return vents or closed supply registers** — furniture over a return, too many closed vents, or a collapsed return duct reduces airflow.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm insulate the coil so it can't shed cold; airflow through the fins drops.
4. **Low refrigerant (a leak)** — undercharge lowers coil pressure and temperature below freezing. This always means a leak, not "topping off."
5. **Failing or slow blower motor / wrong fan speed** — a weak blower or wrong speed tap moves too little air.
6. **Running the AC when it's too cold outside** — running in cool weather (below ~60°F) can freeze the coil.
7. **Clogged condensate drain** causing a wet, restricted coil area, or a stuck blower relay.
8. **Kinked or undersized ductwork / dirty blower wheel** — long-term airflow restriction.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do these with the system in a safe state — no refrigerant handling, no opening sealed components.

1. **Turn the AC off at the thermostat, set the FAN to ON.** This stops the compressor while blowing room air over the coil to thaw it faster. Give it 1–3 hours (longer for heavy ice). Never chip or scrape ice.
2. **Check and replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, swap it. This alone fixes many frozen coils.
3. **Walk your vents.** Open all supply registers, and clear furniture, rugs, or boxes off return grilles. Don't run with more than a couple of registers closed.
4. **Check the condensate drain.** Look for standing water in the drain pan or a slow drip line. A safety float switch may have shut the unit off if the drain is clogged.
5. **Check the breaker.** If the air handler or furnace breaker tripped, the blower isn't running — reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
6. **After thawing, restart cooling and watch.** If it cools normally and the filter was the culprit, you're done. If it refreezes within a day, book a technician.

Do **not** add refrigerant, open the coil cabinet, or bypass the float switch yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech confirms the cause before quoting a repair — here's the sequence so you can sanity-check the visit:

- **Confirms the thaw** and inspects the coil, filter, and drain for the obvious airflow culprits first.
- **Measures airflow / static pressure** and checks blower speed. On variable-speed Goodman air handlers they'll read logged airflow faults.
- **Cleans or inspects the evaporator coil** and blower wheel if they're fouled.
- **Connects gauges and takes superheat/subcooling readings** to judge refrigerant charge. Low readings point to a leak — not a reason to just add refrigerant.
- **Performs a leak search** (electronic sniffer, UV dye, or nitrogen pressure test) if the charge is low, then locates the leak.
- **Checks the metering device (TXV or piston)** — a stuck or failed TXV can starve the coil and freeze it.

A quote that jumps straight to "you need refrigerant" without airflow checks or a leak search is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, ice on indoor coil | Dirty filter / blocked returns | Replace filter, open vents, thaw with fan | Measure static pressure, inspect ducts |
| Refreezes days after filter change | Dirty evaporator coil | Thaw, replace filter | Clean coil and blower wheel |
| Ice on outdoor copper line, warm air inside | Low refrigerant (leak) | Fan-only to thaw; stop compressor | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Blower barely spinning or silent | Failing motor / wrong speed | Reset breaker once; check filter | Test/replace blower motor, set speed |
| Water around indoor unit, unit shuts off | Clogged condensate drain / float switch | Clear visible clog, add drain tablets | Flush drain line, test float switch |
| Coil freezes on mild days only | Running AC when too cool out | Don't run below ~60°F outdoor | Verify charge and metering device |
| Ice returns despite good airflow & charge | Stuck TXV / metering device | None safe | Replace TXV or piston |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Regional rates and system size vary widely.

- **Filter replacement (DIY):** $10–$40.
- **Thaw + diagnostic service call:** $80–$200.
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$400.
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$250.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900 (more for variable-speed ECM motors, $600–$1,200).
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$450.
- **Leak repair + recharge (small, accessible leak):** $500–$1,200.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,500 depending on system size and whether it's under warranty (Goodman coils often carry a 10-year parts warranty if registered — labor is still on you).
- **Compressor damage from running frozen (worst case):** $1,500–$2,800+.

Registered Goodman systems frequently have a 10-year parts limited warranty on the coil and compressor — confirm registration before paying for a part.
