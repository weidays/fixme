## What this code means

Goodman air conditioners don't usually flash a specific "frozen coil" fault — most residential Goodman units have no display code for it. Instead you notice the symptoms: weak or no cool air, ice on the copper lines or the indoor coil, water pooling near the air handler or furnace, and a system that runs constantly without cooling.

A frozen evaporator coil happens when the indoor coil gets so cold that condensation on it freezes into ice. Two root problems cause this: **not enough warm indoor air moving across the coil (airflow)**, or **too little refrigerant (charge/leak)**. Either one drops the coil temperature below freezing.

This is filed as a **pro-level** problem because the most common repeat-offender cause — a refrigerant leak — requires EPA-certified handling. But several of the airflow causes are things you can safely check and fix yourself.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricts airflow across the coil.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers starve the coil of return air.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm insulate the coil and choke airflow.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the top cause of *repeat* freezing. Low charge means a colder-than-normal coil.
5. **Failing or slow blower motor / wrong blower speed** — reduced CFM across the coil.
6. **Running the AC when it's too cold outside** (below ~62°F) — common in spring/fall.
7. **Clogged condensate drain backing up onto the coil** — less common but possible.
8. **Stuck or failing metering device (TXV / piston)** — mostly diagnosed by a tech.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do these with **cooling turned OFF** so the coil can thaw:

- **Turn cooling OFF, fan ON.** Set the thermostat mode to OFF and the fan to ON to blow room air over the coil and speed thawing. Never chip at ice.
- **Check and replace the air filter.** A gray, matted, or overdue filter is the #1 culprit. Replace it — don't just look at it.
- **Open your vents.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and not blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed doors.
- **Check the breaker.** If the indoor blower breaker tripped, the coil freezes because air isn't moving. Reset it once.
- **Look at the condensate drain and pan.** If you see standing water or overflow, the drain may be clogged. Drain tablets and a wet/dry vac on the drain line outlet can help.
- **Give it time to thaw** — 1 to 3 hours minimum, sometimes overnight. Only restart cooling after the coil is fully thawed and dry.

If it freezes again within a day of thawing and a clean filter, stop and call a pro — that pattern points to refrigerant or blower problems.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech won't just add refrigerant. Expect them to:

- Confirm the coil has fully thawed before testing.
- Measure **airflow / temperature split** (return vs. supply air, typically aiming for an 18–22°F drop).
- Inspect the **filter, coil, and blower wheel** for restriction.
- Check **blower motor speed and amp draw**.
- Connect gauges to read **superheat and subcooling** — low readings suggest undercharge/leak.
- If charge is low, perform a **leak search** (electronic sniffer, UV dye, or nitrogen pressure test) rather than just topping off.
- Verify the **metering device (TXV/piston)** is feeding correctly.

If a tech proposes adding refrigerant without finding a leak, that's a red flag — you'll be paying to refill a system that leaks again.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on line/coil, weak air, filter is dirty | Clogged filter / low airflow | Turn off, replace filter, thaw fully | None if it stays fixed |
| Freezes again days after new filter | Low refrigerant (leak) | Turn off, call pro | Leak search + repair + recharge |
| Ice plus water on floor near furnace | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain, use tablets, vac line | Clear/replace drain, check pan |
| Weak air, coil visibly dirty/dusty | Dirty evaporator coil | Replace filter; light no-rinse cleaner on accessible coil | Full coil clean / pull-and-clean |
| Freezing in spring/fall, cool outside | Running AC below ~62°F outdoors | Wait for warmer weather | Confirm no other fault |
| Coil freezes, blower sounds weak/erratic | Failing blower motor or wrong speed | Check blower breaker | Test/replace motor, set correct CFM |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor, varies by region and model):

- **Air filter replacement:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Evaporator coil professional cleaning:** $150–$450
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900 (ECM motors cost more)
- **Refrigerant leak diagnosis:** $100–$350
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older R-22 systems cost far more)
- **TXV / metering device replacement:** $350–$700
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $800–$2,000+

A frozen coil caused by low refrigerant on an older, out-of-warranty Goodman can push you toward a coil or system replacement conversation — get that in writing with the leak location documented.
