## What this code means

Goodman air conditioners don't display a specific "frozen evaporator coil" fault code — this is a physical symptom you diagnose by sight, not a control-board readout. The evaporator coil is the indoor coil that sits on top of your furnace or inside your air handler. Under normal operation it stays cold (around 40°F) but never freezes.

When something reduces airflow across the coil or drops the refrigerant pressure too low, the coil surface falls below 32°F and moisture in the air freezes onto it. Ice builds up, blocks airflow further, and the problem snowballs until the coil is a solid block. You'll notice weak or no cool air, ice on the copper lines outside, or water pooling around the furnace once it starts to melt.

The freeze itself is a symptom. The real job is finding *why* the coil got cold enough to ice — and that root cause determines whether this is a $15 filter fix or a $1,500 refrigerant repair.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — the single most common cause. A clogged filter starves the coil of airflow, so it gets too cold and freezes.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers, blocked returns, or furniture over a vent restricts airflow the same way a dirty filter does.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm caking the coil fins insulate it and choke airflow, even with a clean filter.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — low charge drops the coil pressure and temperature below freezing. This is the most common cause of *recurring* icing and requires a technician.
5. **Failing blower motor or wrong fan speed** — a weak or miswired blower doesn't push enough air across the coil.
6. **Running the AC in cool weather** — running cooling when it's below ~60°F outside can freeze the coil; this is a usage issue, not a fault.
7. **Stuck or failing metering device (TXV/piston)** — feeds too much refrigerant into the coil, dropping its temperature. Less common, technician territory.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do these with the system in a safe state — set the thermostat cooling to **OFF** first so you're not running the compressor against ice.

- **Turn off cooling, run the fan.** On the thermostat, set the mode to OFF (or Heat off) and the fan to ON. This thaws the coil safely over 1–3 hours. Never chip or scrape ice off.
- **Check the air filter.** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many freeze-ups.
- **Open your vents.** Walk the house and make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or curtains.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm the fan setting and that a smart/programmable schedule isn't running cooling in cool weather.
- **Look at the condensate drain.** As the coil thaws, a lot of water drains out. Make sure the drain line and pan aren't overflowing onto your floor or furnace. A clogged drain can trip a float switch and shut the system down.
- **Check the breaker.** If the system tripped, leave the coil thawing (fan-only) before restoring cooling.

If the coil freezes again within a day of thawing and a clean filter, stop running cooling and call a technician — you almost certainly have a refrigerant or airflow problem that needs tools.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Goodman technician won't just recharge refrigerant and leave. Expect them to:

1. **Confirm the coil is fully thawed** before testing — readings are meaningless with ice present.
2. **Measure airflow** — check filter, blower speed taps, and static pressure to rule out an airflow restriction.
3. **Attach gauges and take superheat/subcooling readings** to see whether the refrigerant charge is correct, high, or low.
4. **Leak-search if the charge is low** — using electronic sniffers, UV dye, or nitrogen/bubble testing. Simply topping off a leaking system is a temporary fix and a red flag on a quote.
5. **Inspect the evaporator coil and metering device (TXV/piston)** for dirt or a restriction.
6. **Verify the outdoor unit** — condenser fan, contactor, and capacitor — since low airflow there can also skew pressures.

If a quote jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without any leak search or airflow check, ask why. Charge doesn't disappear on a sealed system without a leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on indoor coil, weak airflow | Dirty filter | Replace filter, run fan to thaw | Confirm airflow, check blower speed |
| Coil freezes again after clean filter | Low refrigerant / leak | Stop cooling, call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Ice on outdoor copper lines | Low charge or airflow | Thaw with fan; note if recurring | Gauge readings, leak test |
| Water around furnace after thaw | Normal melt or clogged drain | Clear drain, add drain tablets | Clear line, check float switch |
| Coil clean but still freezing | Dirty coil or bad TXV | None — internal | Clean coil, test/replace metering device |
| Freezes only in cool weather | Running AC when too cold out | Stop cooling below ~60°F | Verify no low-charge issue |
| Barely any air from vents | Weak/failing blower motor | Check filter/vents | Test blower motor and capacitor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. A frozen coil itself costs nothing to fix if it's a filter or airflow issue — the money is in the root cause.

- **Filter replacement (DIY):** $10–$40
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$200 pro; ~$15 DIY with tablets
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Blower motor capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Blower motor replacement:** $450–$900 (ECM motors cost more)
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$450
- **Refrigerant recharge:** $150–$600 depending on refrigerant type and amount (R-410A/newer blends vary)
- **Leak repair + evaporator coil replacement:** $800–$2,000+ depending on coil access and refrigerant

If your Goodman coil is under the 10-year parts warranty (registered systems), the coil part may be covered — but labor and refrigerant usually are not. Ask your technician to check the warranty by serial number.
