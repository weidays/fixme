## What this code means

Goodman air conditioners don't throw a specific "frozen coil" fault code — the indoor unit has no board logic that detects ice. A **frozen evaporator coil** is a physical condition where the indoor coil (inside your air handler or above your furnace) drops below freezing and builds a layer of ice or frost.

The coil is supposed to stay cold, but not *that* cold. Freezing happens when either **not enough warm air** moves across the coil, or the **refrigerant pressure is too low**, letting the coil temperature crash below 32°F. Once ice forms, it insulates the coil, cooling gets worse, and the problem snowballs.

You'll usually notice it as: warm air from the vents, ice visible on the copper lines or coil, water pooling around the furnace/air handler when it thaws, or the outdoor unit running constantly with no cooling.

**Severity: pro.** You can safely thaw the unit and rule out airflow problems yourself, but a coil that keeps refreezing almost always points to a refrigerant or blower issue that needs a licensed HVAC technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged or dirty air filter** — the #1 cause. A restricted filter starves the coil of warm return air, and it freezes over. Most common in summer when the system runs hard.
2. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — undercharge drops the coil's pressure and temperature. If your Goodman was fine for years and now freezes, suspect a slow leak, not a "top-off."
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm insulate the coil and block airflow through the fins, same effect as a dirty filter.
4. **Blower or fan problem** — a failing blower motor, wrong speed tap, or bad capacitor moves too little air. On Goodman variable-speed air handlers, a control fault can also reduce airflow.
5. **Closed, blocked, or too many closed vents/registers** — starving airflow in half the house can freeze the coil.
6. **Running the AC in cold weather** — running cooling below roughly 60°F outdoors can freeze the coil; not a defect.
7. **Kinked or restricted refrigerant metering (TXV/piston)** — a stuck metering device causes uneven, over-cold spots. Less common, technician-only.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do these first — many frozen coils are fixed with a filter change and patience.

- **Turn the system to OFF, then set the fan to ON.** This stops cooling but keeps air blowing to thaw the ice faster. Do NOT keep running cooling.
- **Check and replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, swap it. Note the size printed on the frame.
- **Check the thermostat.** Set it to a normal temperature (e.g., 74–76°F). Turning it very low won't cool faster and encourages freezing.
- **Open all supply and return vents.** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed doors aren't blocking return airflow. Aim for no more than 20% of vents closed.
- **Check the breaker.** After a thaw, if the unit won't restart, verify the AC and air handler breakers aren't tripped.
- **Check the condensate drain and pan.** A melting coil dumps a lot of water — make sure the drain line isn't clogged and flooding your floor. Clear a visible clog with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor drain exit.
- **Wait for a full thaw** (1–3 hours, up to 24 if solid) before running cooling again. **Never chip or scrape the ice** — you can puncture the coil.

If it freezes again within a day or two of a clean filter and clear airflow, stop and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Goodman tech will confirm the freeze is thawed, then work through the two big buckets — **airflow** and **refrigerant** — in order:

- **Measure static pressure and airflow** across the coil and blower; check filter, coil cleanliness, and blower speed settings.
- **Inspect the evaporator coil** for dirt, and inspect the blower wheel and motor. On variable-speed Goodman air handlers, they'll check control-board airflow commands and error history.
- **Attach gauges and take superheat/subcooling readings.** Low subcooling with high superheat points to undercharge/leak; this is the key measurement to sanity-check.
- **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — electronic sniffer, UV dye, or nitrogen/soap test. A reputable tech finds and fixes the leak rather than just adding refrigerant.
- **Check the metering device (TXV or piston)** for correct operation if airflow and charge look normal.

Be cautious of any quote that adds refrigerant without doing a leak search — an undercharged Goodman doesn't "use up" refrigerant unless it's leaking.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on indoor coil, dirty filter | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, thaw in fan mode | Verify static pressure, clean coil |
| Ice on outdoor copper lines, warm vents | Low refrigerant / leak | Thaw only; do not run cooling | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Refreezes after clean filter | Blower fault or dirty coil | Confirm vents open, then call pro | Test blower motor/capacitor, clean coil |
| Water on floor near furnace | Coil thawing + clogged drain | Vac out drain line, add pan tablets | Clear/replace drain, check pan |
| Coil freezes only on cool nights | Running AC in cold weather | Switch off cooling below ~60°F | Confirm no charge/airflow issue |
| Weak airflow from all vents | Blower speed or motor | Check for closed vents/filter | Correct blower tap, replace motor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Your actual price depends on region, coil access, and whether a leak is involved.

- **Filter replacement (DIY):** $10–$40.
- **Thaw + reset (DIY):** $0.
- **Professional coil cleaning:** $150–$450.
- **Blower capacitor replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Blower motor (ECM/variable-speed):** $500–$1,200+ parts and labor; Goodman ECM modules run high.
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$400.
- **Leak repair + recharge (minor):** $500–$1,200 depending on refrigerant type and amount. R-410A systems cost more than older R-22 to service.
- **Evaporator coil replacement (CAPT/CAUF):** $1,200–$2,800+; coil part may be under Goodman warranty if registered, but labor and refrigerant usually aren't.
- **TXV/metering device replacement:** $400–$900.

If you're facing a coil or compressor replacement on an older Goodman using discontinued refrigerant, ask whether a full system replacement is more cost-effective.
