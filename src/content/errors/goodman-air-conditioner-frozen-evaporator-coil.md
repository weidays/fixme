## What this code means

Goodman air conditioners don't display a specific "frozen coil" error code — this is a **symptom-based problem**, not a board fault code. You'll notice it as weak or no cold air, ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, water pooling near the furnace or air handler, or the system running non-stop without cooling.

A frozen evaporator coil means the indoor coil (inside your furnace/air handler cabinet) has dropped below freezing and moisture in the air has turned to ice on it. Once ice forms, airflow through the coil chokes off, which makes the coil even colder — a runaway loop that ends in a solid block of ice.

The root cause is almost always **too little heat reaching the coil** (poor airflow) or **too little refrigerant** to properly regulate coil temperature. We rank the specific causes below.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow drops coil temperature below freezing.
2. **Blocked return vents or closed supply registers** — furniture, rugs, or too many closed vents starve the coil of warm return air.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — years of dust build a blanket on the coil, insulating it and cutting airflow even with a clean filter.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — undercharged systems run the coil too cold. Common on older Goodman units; requires a technician (handling refrigerant is not DIY).
5. **Failing or weak blower motor / wrong blower speed** — an ECM or PSC blower that isn't moving enough air will freeze the coil.
6. **Running the AC in cold weather** — using cooling below ~60°F outdoor temperature can freeze the coil.
7. **Stuck or failed TXV/metering device** — overfeeds or restricts refrigerant flow to the coil.
8. **Clogged condensate drain (secondary effect)** — a backed-up drain can pool water and, combined with freezing, worsen the mess.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all safe for a homeowner. **First, turn the cooling OFF at the thermostat but set the fan to ON** — this melts the ice faster and warms the coil.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many freeze-ups.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's on COOL, and set the fan to ON to help thaw. After thawing, use AUTO.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house and open all supply registers; clear furniture, curtains, or rugs off return grilles.
- **Breaker:** If the outdoor unit tripped its breaker, note it — but don't repeatedly reset it. One reset is fine; repeated tripping means call a pro.
- **Condensate line:** Check for standing water in the pan or a clogged drain line. Add pan tablets to discourage sludge.
- **Let it fully thaw:** Wait until ALL ice is gone (1–3 hours, sometimes overnight) before restarting cooling. Place towels to catch meltwater.
- **Restart test:** Once dry, run cooling. If it freezes again within a day, stop and call a technician — you likely have a refrigerant or blower issue.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bend the coil yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Goodman tech will work through airflow first, then refrigerant:

1. **Confirm the ice is fully thawed** before testing — a frozen coil hides the true readings.
2. **Measure airflow / static pressure** across the coil and check blower amperage and speed tap (ECM vs PSC).
3. **Inspect the coil** for dirt buildup and check the filter and ductwork for restrictions.
4. **Take superheat and subcooling readings** with gauges to determine if the charge is correct — this tells them if it's a refrigerant issue vs airflow.
5. **Leak-search** if refrigerant is low (electronic detector, UV dye, or nitrogen pressure test) — adding refrigerant without finding the leak is a temporary fix.
6. **Test the TXV/metering device** for proper operation.

Sanity check: if a tech wants to "just add refrigerant" without checking airflow or looking for a leak, get a second opinion. A proper diagnosis includes both airflow and charge verification.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on indoor coil / copper lines | Dirty filter or low airflow | Turn off cooling, fan ON, replace filter, thaw fully | Measure static pressure, clean coil |
| Freezes again a day after thawing | Low refrigerant / leak | Stop running it; call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Weak airflow from vents | Clogged coil or blower issue | Check/replace filter, open vents | Clean coil, test blower motor/speed |
| Water around furnace/air handler | Melting ice or clogged drain | Clear drain, add pan tablets | Clear line, inspect pan and float switch |
| No cooling, outdoor unit runs | Frozen coil blocking airflow | Full thaw, then restart | Diagnose charge and metering device |
| Coil freezes only on cool nights | Running AC below ~60°F outdoors | Don't run cooling in cold weather | Verify no low-ambient control needed |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Your Goodman's exact model, refrigerant type, and local labor rates affect the total.

- **New air filter (DIY):** $10–$40
- **Thaw + filter swap (DIY):** $0
- **Professional coil cleaning:** $150–$450
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$450
- **Refrigerant recharge (R-410A):** $200–$700 depending on amount; R-454B systems may run higher as supply ramps up
- **TXV/metering device replacement:** $400–$800
- **Blower motor replacement (ECM):** $450–$900
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$1,800+ (parts often under warranty on registered Goodman units; you pay labor)
- **Compressor replacement (if freeze damaged it):** $1,300–$2,800+

Goodman offers a strong parts warranty (often 10 years on registered systems), so ask whether your coil or compressor is covered — you'd still owe labor, but parts can save hundreds.
