## What this code means

"Frozen evaporator coil" isn't a fault code on a display — Goodman split ACs don't flash a specific code for it. It's a physical condition: ice builds up on the indoor evaporator coil (the A-shaped coil inside your air handler or above your furnace).

The coil gets cold to pull heat out of your air. When something drops the coil temperature below freezing — either too little warm air blowing across it, or too little refrigerant inside it — condensation on the coil freezes instead of draining away. The ice grows, blocks airflow, and the problem snowballs. You'll notice weak or no cool air, higher humidity, water around the furnace, or a block of frost on the copper lines.

Because the two root causes (airflow vs. refrigerant charge) look identical from the outside, and because a charge problem means a refrigerant leak, this is flagged **pro-level**. You can safely thaw it and rule out airflow, but a recurring freeze needs a licensed technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. A choked filter starves the coil of the warm airflow it needs, and it ices over.
2. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak** — undercharged systems run the coil too cold and freeze it. On Goodman units this often shows up as gradual freezing that returns days after each thaw. Requires leak repair and recharge.
3. **Blocked or closed airflow** — too many closed supply registers, a crushed flex duct, or a blocked return grille reduces airflow across the coil.
4. **Failing or slow blower motor / capacitor** — a weak indoor blower moves too little air. Common on older Goodman/Amana air handlers with a failing ECM or PSC blower.
5. **Dirty evaporator coil** — years of dust film on the coil insulates it and disrupts airflow and heat transfer.
6. **Running the AC in cold weather** — operating below roughly 60°F outdoor temperature can freeze the coil.
7. **Stuck-closed or failed metering device (TXV/orifice)** — less common; a Goodman TXV that won't open properly starves the coil and freezes it.
8. **Clogged condensate drain backing up** — as ice melts, a plugged drain floods the pan and can trip a safety float switch, mimicking a "not cooling" complaint.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all homeowner-safe. **Do not open the refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.**

- **Turn cooling OFF, fan to ON.** At the thermostat, switch the system from Cool to Off and set the fan to On. This runs the blower without the compressor so the coil can thaw. Give it 1–3 hours.
- **Replace the air filter.** Pull the filter at the return or air handler. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of freeze-ups.
- **Open your vents.** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or filters stacked on top.
- **Check the breaker and thermostat batteries.** Confirm the AC breaker is on and, if your thermostat uses batteries, that they're fresh.
- **Check the condensate drain and pan.** Look for standing water or overflow near the furnace/air handler. If the pan is full, a wet/dry vac on the drain line outlet can clear a clog. A tripped float switch will keep the system off until cleared.
- **Let it fully dry before restarting.** Once all ice is gone and the drain flows, switch back to Cool. If it freezes again within a day or two, stop and call a pro — that points to refrigerant or blower problems you can't safely fix.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech won't just add refrigerant. Expect them to:

1. **Confirm airflow first** — inspect the filter, blower wheel, and static pressure. On Goodman ECM blowers they may check the motor module and programmed airflow (CFM) settings.
2. **Measure superheat and subcooling** — gauges on the service ports tell them whether the charge is correct, low (leak), or overcharged. This distinguishes airflow problems from refrigerant problems.
3. **Leak-search if charge is low** — with electronic sniffer, UV dye, or nitrogen pressure test. Simply "topping off" a leaking Goodman system without finding the leak is a red flag.
4. **Inspect the coil and metering device** — checking for a dirty coil or a failing TXV/orifice starving the coil.
5. **Verify the condensate path and float switch** function.

If a quote jumps straight to a recharge without measuring superheat/subcooling or searching for a leak, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on copper lines, weak airflow | Dirty filter / low airflow | Set fan ON to thaw, replace filter | Check blower, static pressure, coil |
| Freezes again days after thawing | Low refrigerant (leak) | Thaw and stop running it | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Water around furnace/air handler | Melting ice + clogged drain | Vac out drain line, clear pan | Clear drain, check float switch |
| Warm air, outdoor unit runs | Frozen coil blocking flow | Turn cooling off to thaw | Diagnose airflow vs. charge |
| Coil freezes in cool weather | Running AC below ~60°F | Turn AC off until warmer | Confirm no low-charge issue |
| Weak air even with clean filter | Failing blower / capacitor | None — call a pro | Test/replace ECM or capacitor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Your area, unit age, and refrigerant type (R-410A vs newer R-32) affect the total.

- **New air filter (DIY):** $10–$40.
- **Condensate drain cleaning (DIY):** $0–$25 with a vac and tablets; $100–$250 for a pro to clear and treat.
- **Blower capacitor replacement:** $150–$400.
- **ECM/blower motor replacement:** $400–$900+ depending on the Goodman air handler.
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$400.
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair:** $250–$800, depending on leak location.
- **Refrigerant recharge:** $150–$600+; R-410A and R-32 prices vary, and larger systems hold more.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $700–$1,800+ (part plus labor and recharge) if the coil itself is leaking. On units still under Goodman's parts warranty, the part may be covered but labor is not.
- **TXV / metering device replacement:** $350–$700.
