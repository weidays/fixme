## What this code means

Goodman air conditioners don't display a specific "frozen evaporator coil" fault code — this is a physical condition you diagnose by symptoms, not a board readout. The evaporator coil is the indoor coil inside your air handler or above your furnace. It gets cold to pull heat and humidity out of your air. When something goes wrong, that coil drops below freezing, and the moisture on it turns to frost, then a solid block of ice.

Once ice forms, it insulates the coil and blocks airflow, so cooling drops off fast and eventually stops. You'll often see water dripping from the indoor unit as the ice melts, or find the large copper suction line (the fat, insulated one) frosted over at the outdoor condenser.

Two root problems cause almost every frozen coil: **not enough air moving across the coil**, or **not enough refrigerant in the system**. Both make the coil colder than it should be.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow lets the coil overcool and freeze.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers, or furniture over returns, starves the coil of air.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm on the coil itself insulate it and cut airflow, same effect as a dirty filter.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — a leaking coil or line drops system pressure, making the coil run below freezing. Very common on older Goodman units.
5. **Failing or slow blower motor / bad capacitor** — if the indoor fan spins too slowly, airflow drops and the coil freezes.
6. **Running the AC in cool weather** — operating below roughly 60°F outdoors overcools the coil; common in shoulder seasons.
7. **Clogged condensate drain backing up** — a full drain pan and standing water near a cold coil can contribute to icing in some setups.
8. **Thermostat or control fan setting issues** — fan not running when it should, so air stalls over the coil.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all safe for a homeowner. Do them **after** the coil has fully thawed.

- **Turn off cooling, run the fan.** Set the thermostat to OFF for cooling and FAN to ON. Let the blower run 1–3 hours to melt all the ice before you inspect anything. Never run the compressor while iced.
- **Check the air filter.** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it. Do this monthly in summer.
- **Open your vents.** Walk the house and make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or curtains.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to COOL and, if it uses batteries, swap them. Set the fan to AUTO once the coil is fully thawed.
- **Check the breaker.** Look at the panel for a tripped AC or air-handler breaker. Reset it once; if it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Look for standing water in the drain pan or under the indoor unit. You can clear a clogged drain line with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor termination.
- **Don't run the AC in cool weather.** If it's below about 60°F outside, leave cooling off.

If the coil refreezes after a clean filter and open vents, you have a refrigerant or airflow problem that needs a technician. **Do not add refrigerant yourself** — that's illegal without EPA certification and won't fix an underlying leak.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works the airflow-vs-refrigerant fork in a logical order. Here's what a legitimate diagnosis looks like:

1. **Fully thaws the coil** and confirms the ice pattern (whole coil vs. partial suggests airflow vs. refrigerant issues).
2. **Measures airflow** — inspects filter, coil, blower wheel, and blower speed; checks static pressure across the system.
3. **Cleans and inspects the evaporator coil** if it's dirty or biofilmed.
4. **Attaches gauges** to read suction and head pressure, and takes superheat/subcooling readings to judge refrigerant charge.
5. **Leak-searches** with an electronic detector or dye if the charge is low — Goodman coils and line sets are common leak points.
6. **Checks the blower motor and capacitor** for correct speed and amp draw.

A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search is a red flag. Refrigerant doesn't get used up — if it's low, it leaked, and topping it off is a temporary patch.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on indoor coil, weak airflow | Dirty filter | Replace filter, thaw coil | Confirm airflow, check static pressure |
| Frost on outdoor suction line | Low refrigerant / leak | Turn off AC, call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Coil refreezes after new filter | Dirty coil or low charge | Thaw again, call pro | Deep-clean coil or check charge |
| Water pooling under air handler | Melting ice / clogged drain | Clear drain line with wet/dry vac | Inspect pan, float switch, drain |
| AC freezes only in cool weather | Low outdoor temp | Don't run cooling below 60°F | Verify no other fault present |
| Warm air, blower sounds slow | Blower motor / capacitor | Thaw, call pro | Test motor, replace capacitor/motor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Diagnostic/service call fees ($90–$180) often apply on top and may be credited toward the repair.

- **Air filter replacement:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150–$250 professional.
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$450 depending on access.
- **Blower capacitor replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Blower motor (ECM or PSC) replacement:** $400–$900.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $200–$450 for a minor fix and top-off; more if the leak is at a fitting or coil.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$1,600 installed, higher with older R-22 systems where a matched coil is scarce.
- **Full charge / recovery on R-410A:** $100–$300 depending on system size.

Older Goodman units using R-22 refrigerant can push leak repairs sharply higher because R-22 is expensive and being phased out — a tech may recommend replacing the coil or the whole system instead.
