## What this code means

Your Goodman AC has no display code for this problem. It is a visible symptom: frost or ice forming on the refrigerant lines, usually at the outdoor unit or where the lines enter the indoor coil.

A split system has two copper lines:

- **The larger, insulated suction line** should feel cold and sweaty, like a cold soda can.
- **The smaller liquid line** should be warm or near outdoor temperature.

Ice forms when the refrigerant inside the suction line drops below 32°F. That happens when the system either doesn't have enough heat to absorb or doesn't have enough refrigerant.

This page covers ice on the lines themselves. If the ice is mainly on the indoor coil, see Goodman AC Frozen Evaporator Coil. The two problems often occur together, and line ice is frequently the first thing homeowners notice.

**There is no reset.** Standard Goodman condensers have no lockout for this condition. They keep running while frozen until the root cause is fixed. Some communicating models may flag a related fault, but behavior varies by control board.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Restricted indoor airflow.** Too little warm air crosses the evaporator coil, so it gets too cold and the suction line ices. Common causes:
   - A dirty air filter (most common)
   - Closed or blocked registers
   - Blocked return grilles
   - A dirty evaporator coil
   - A failing or wrong-speed blower

2. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Low charge drops the suction pressure and temperature. Ice typically starts near the outdoor service valve or at the indoor coil. Goodman coils, like other brands, can develop formicary (pinhole) corrosion leaks.

3. **Metering device problems.** A sticking or failed TXV, or a wrong-size or partly blocked piston, starves the coil of refrigerant.

4. **Liquid-line restriction.** A clogged filter-drier or a kinked line causes frost at the restriction point, often on the small line.

5. **Running in cool outdoor temperatures.** Cooling below about 60–65°F outdoors can drop coil pressure enough to freeze, especially if the unit lacks a low-ambient kit.

6. **Blower problems at the air handler or furnace.** If the outdoor unit runs while the indoor blower is off or slow, ice builds quickly. Causes include a failed blower motor, a bad capacitor, a control board fault, or an incorrect speed tap. This overlaps with Goodman AC Fan Runs But No Cold Air.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Stop the freezing.** Set the thermostat to OFF for cooling and the fan to ON. This thaws the ice and protects the compressor. Place towels near the indoor unit, because thawing makes a lot of water.

2. **Check the air filter.** If it's gray or clogged, replace it with the correct size. A very restrictive filter in a system not designed for it can also choke airflow.

3. **Open all supply registers and clear return grilles.** Move furniture, rugs, and curtains away from them. Don't close off unused rooms.

4. **Check the thermostat.**
   - Replace weak batteries.
   - Confirm the fan setting works. With the fan set to ON, you should feel air at the vents.
   - Avoid setting the temperature extremely low on cool days.

5. **Check the condensate drain.** Make sure the drain line and pan aren't backed up. A full pan can trip a float switch, and a clogged drain will overflow during the thaw.

6. **Restart and watch.** After everything is fully thawed and dry, turn cooling back on and check the lines after 30–60 minutes. If frost returns, shut the system off and call a technician.

Do not chip at the ice, open the cabinet, or add refrigerant.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Airflow check.** Inspects the filter, blower, and evaporator coil. Measures the temperature drop across the coil, which is normally about 16–22°F, and may measure static pressure.
- **Blower verification.** Confirms the blower motor, capacitor, and speed tap are correct for the condenser's tonnage.
- **Charge check.** Connects gauges and measures superheat (piston systems) or subcooling (TXV systems) against the Goodman charging chart on the unit.
- **Leak search.** If the charge is low, uses an electronic detector, bubbles, or a nitrogen pressure test. Common leak spots include the evaporator coil, Schrader valves, service valves, and flare or braze joints.
- **Restriction check.** Measures the temperature drop across the filter-drier and line set. A noticeable drop at one point indicates a restriction.
- **TXV or piston evaluation.** Checks the TXV bulb mounting and insulation, and confirms the piston size matches the condenser.

**Red flag:** A quote that only says "add refrigerant" with no leak search or charge measurements.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on large line and indoor coil, weak airflow at vents | Dirty filter or blocked returns | Replace filter, open registers, thaw | Clean coil if the problem persists |
| Ice on large line, good airflow, high bills | Low refrigerant from a leak | Turn cooling off, thaw, call a pro | Leak search, repair, evacuate, recharge |
| Frost on small line at one spot | Clogged filter-drier or kinked line | Turn cooling off and call | Replace drier or repair line |
| Ice returns quickly after thaw, warm air | Failed TXV or wrong piston | Turn cooling off and call | Replace TXV or correct piston |
| Outdoor unit runs, no air from vents | Indoor blower failure | Confirm fan ON setting, check breaker | Test or replace blower motor, capacitor, or board |
| Freezes only on cool days | Low ambient operation | Don't run AC below about 60°F outdoors | Install low-ambient control if needed |
