## What this code means

"Not heating" is a symptom, not a single error code. It means your Carrier ductless mini-split is on in HEAT mode but isn't warming the room. You might see the indoor fan running with cool or lukewarm air, the fan not running at all, or the indoor unit lit up while the outdoor unit sits idle.

Some causes are normal behavior that clears on its own:

- **Cold-draft prevention:** after startup, the indoor fan waits until the coil is warm.
- **Defrost cycles:** the unit pauses heating to melt frost off the outdoor coil.

Real faults include refrigerant loss, an electronic expansion valve (EEV) or reversing valve problem, sensor faults, and inverter or board failures.

If the indoor unit flashes an error code or a blinking light pattern, write it down before you do anything else. Codes and blink patterns differ by series, so your outdoor model's service manual is the definitive reference. Whether a fault clears itself depends on the fault and the model:

- Many protection stops, such as high or low pressure or overcurrent, clear and retry automatically after a delay.
- Some faults lock the unit out until power is cycled after repeated trips.

This page covers ductless units only. For ducted systems, see the Carrier Heat Pump Not Heating page.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Wrong mode or settings.** The remote is in COOL, AUTO, DRY or FAN instead of HEAT, or the setpoint is below room temperature. A dead remote battery can also mean the last command never reached the unit. Check for a schedule, timer, or "eco" setting that is limiting output.
2. **Normal defrost or cold-start delay.** The unit pauses heat during defrost, and the indoor fan waits for a warm coil at startup. This is often mistaken for a fault in cold, humid weather.
3. **Dirty indoor filters or coil.** Clogged mesh filters restrict airflow. That triggers high-pressure or indoor-coil overheat protection in heat mode, which cuts compressor speed or stops it.
4. **Blocked or iced outdoor unit.** Snow, drifted leaves, or ice buildup the defrost can't clear (often from a poor drain path or the unit sitting too low) starve the outdoor coil of airflow.
5. **Outdoor temperature below the unit's rating.** Standard models lose a lot of capacity in deep cold. This varies widely between standard and cold-climate outdoor units.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** Flare connections are a well-known leak point on ductless systems. Low charge causes weak heat, frosting, and low-pressure trips.
7. **Sensor faults.** A failed coil or room thermistor, or the outdoor defrost or ambient sensor, can cause wrong defrost timing or early shutoff. These usually show an error code.
8. **EEV or reversing (4-way) valve fault.** A stuck reversing valve means the unit can run but can't switch into heat. A sticking EEV starves or floods the coil.
9. **Inverter board, communication, or compressor failure.** The outdoor unit doesn't respond at all, often with a communication or inverter error code. This is the least likely cause and the most expensive.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Remote and mode:** Replace the remote batteries. Confirm HEAT mode (the sun icon) and set the temperature at least 3–5°F above room temperature. Cancel any timers, sleep mode, or schedules.
- **Wait it out:** After a change, give the unit 10–15 minutes. If the outdoor unit is steaming or the indoor fan stopped briefly, let the defrost finish.
- **Indoor filters:** Lift the front panel and slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry fully, and reinstall. Do not spray or touch the coil fins behind them.
- **Outdoor unit clearance:** Clear snow, leaves, and debris from around the outdoor unit and the path where defrost water drains away. Don't chip ice off the coil, and don't pour hot water on it.
- **Power:** Check the breaker and the outdoor disconnect. Do **one** power cycle: switch it off for about 5 minutes, then back on.
- **Indoor airflow:** Make sure curtains or furniture aren't blocking the indoor unit's intake or louvers.
- **Condensate:** In heat mode the indoor unit normally drains little or no water. If you see water dripping indoors, note it for the technician.
- **Note any error code** or blink pattern before you call.

If it still won't heat after these checks, or it shows an error code, stop and call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Read the active and stored fault codes from the indoor display, outdoor board LEDs, or a service tool for that series.
- Measure the supply-air and return-air temperatures in heat mode.
- Verify that the outdoor unit gets a run command and that indoor/outdoor communication is working.
- Attach gauges and check operating pressures against the manufacturer's charging chart. If pressures are low, do a leak search, starting at the flare joints.
- Check that the reversing valve actually switches. This includes coil voltage and the temperature behavior of the lines.
- Test the EEV coil and confirm the valve's movement.
- Check the thermistors' resistance against the temperature charts.
- Test the inverter board, the compressor windings, and the fan motor.
- Inspect the outdoor coil and drain path for defrost problems.

A good quote names a specific failed component or a confirmed leak location. Be wary of a quote that says "add refrigerant" without a leak search, since refrigerant doesn't get used up.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool air, setpoint low, or wrong icon | Wrong mode or setting | Set HEAT, raise the setpoint, replace remote batteries | None |
| Fan stops, outdoor unit steaming | Defrost cycle | Wait 10–15 minutes | None unless it happens very often |
| No warm air at first startup | Cold-draft prevention | Wait a few minutes | None |
| Weak heat, dusty filters | Restricted airflow | Clean the filters | Deep coil and blower cleaning |
| Outdoor unit buried or heavily iced | Blocked coil, poor drainage | Clear snow and debris | Raise the unit on a stand, fix drainage, check defrost |
| Weak heat, frosty lines, gets worse over weeks | Refrigerant leak | Turn off and call | Leak search, repair, evacuate, recharge |
| Blows cool, outdoor unit runs normally | Reversing valve or EEV fault | Call | Test and replace valve or coil |
| Error code shown, stops repeatedly | Sensor, pressure, or board fault | One power cycle, note the code | Test thermistors, board, compressor |
| Outdoor unit never starts | Communication or inverter fault | Check breaker and disconnect | Diagnose wiring, board, or compressor |
