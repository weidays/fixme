## What this code means

"Frozen and not defrosting" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. It means the outdoor coil of your Amana heat pump has built up ice that the unit's defrost cycle is failing to clear. In heating mode, the outdoor coil runs below freezing and naturally collects frost. The defrost control should periodically reverse the system briefly (running in cooling mode with the outdoor fan off) to warm the coil and melt the frost. When that cycle does not start, starts but cannot finish, or cannot keep up, the ice keeps growing until the coil, and sometimes the whole cabinet, is encased.

This page covers the **outdoor coil icing in heating mode**. If the ice is on the indoor coil or refrigerant lines during cooling season, see Amana AC Frozen Evaporator Coil instead.

**Reset behavior:** Most Amana defrost controls do not latch a lockout for a missed defrost. The board simply keeps trying on its timer or demand schedule. Some newer communicating models log a fault on the outdoor control board or thermostat. Others show nothing, so the ice itself is the main sign. Clearing the ice does not fix the root cause. If the unit refreezes, the fault is still there.

**Ice versus normal frost:** A thin, even white frost that melts every 30–90 minutes, with steam rising and a "whoosh" sound when defrost ends, is normal. Thick ice, ice on the top or fan grille, or ice that persists for hours means the system needs service.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or poorly attached defrost sensor (coil thermostat).** The sensor clips onto a coil tube and tells the board when the coil is cold enough to need defrost. If it has failed, has loosened, or reads warm, the board never starts defrost. This is the most common cause of a heat pump that ices up and stays iced.
2. **Defrost control board fault.** The board's timer, sensor input, or defrost relay can fail. On demand-defrost boards, an ambient or coil sensor fault can also stop defrost. Behavior varies by board generation.
3. **Reversing valve or its solenoid coil not shifting.** Defrost depends on the reversing valve switching the system into cooling mode. A burned-out solenoid coil or a stuck valve means the board calls for defrost but nothing happens.
4. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** An undercharged system runs the outdoor coil colder than designed. It can ice faster than defrost can clear, and a defrost cycle may not warm the coil enough to melt it.
5. **Outdoor fan not stopping, or relay and wiring faults in the defrost circuit.** If the outdoor fan keeps running during defrost, heat blows away and the cycle may end before the ice melts. Damaged sensor wires or connectors cause similar symptoms.
6. **Environmental and installation factors.** These include water dripping from a roof or gutter onto the unit, a unit sitting too low so melt water refreezes around the base, or blocked airflow from snow, leaves, or shrubs. These overwhelm a working defrost system rather than break it.
7. **Restricted indoor airflow.** A badly clogged filter or closed registers raise system pressures in heating and reduce performance. This is a contributing factor rather than a primary cause of outdoor icing.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Switch to emergency or auxiliary heat.** Set the thermostat to "EM Heat" or "Aux" so the home stays warm and the outdoor unit stops running while ice melts naturally. Do not chip, pry, or hose the coil with tools or pressure.
- **Check thermostat settings and batteries.** Confirm the thermostat is in Heat with a sensible setpoint and the fan is on Auto. Replace batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** A clogged filter at the air handler or furnace restricts airflow and worsens performance.
- **Open supply and return vents.** Make sure registers are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Clear snow and debris from around the outdoor unit.** Gently brush snow away from the sides and top, by hand or with a soft broom. Keep about 2 feet of clearance. Do not touch the fins with tools.
- **Look for dripping water.** If a gutter or roof edge drips onto the unit, note it for the technician. Fixing that drainage is often the permanent cure.
- **Check the breakers and disconnect.** If the outdoor unit is completely dead, check the breaker and the outdoor disconnect switch. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a pro.
- **Make sure exterior panels are seated.** Loose access panels can affect operation, so press them firmly into place.

If the unit refreezes after a full thaw with these items addressed, stop there. The remaining causes are all inside the cabinet or the refrigerant circuit.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent technician will generally:

- **Thaw the coil safely,** often by running the unit in cooling mode briefly or letting it melt on emergency heat, then inspect the coil for damage.
- **Force a defrost cycle.** Many Amana defrost boards have test pins or a test function that speeds up or forces defrost. The technician watches whether the reversing valve shifts, the outdoor fan stops, and auxiliary heat energizes.
- **Test the defrost sensor.** They check that it is clipped tightly to the correct coil tube and measure its resistance or continuity against the manufacturer's temperature chart.
- **Check the board.** They verify the board receives power and sensor input and outputs to the reversing valve and fan relay during defrost, and they read any stored fault codes on newer controls.
- **Test the reversing valve solenoid coil** for voltage and resistance, and check for a stuck valve by comparing line temperatures and pressures.
- **Measure refrigerant pressures, superheat, and subcooling.** If the charge is low, a proper leak search should follow, not just a top-off.
- **Check site conditions,** including unit height, drainage, gutter drip, and clearance.

**Quote sanity check:** If you're told you need a new compressor or a whole new system for a unit that simply won't defrost, ask what tests ruled out the sensor, board, and reversing valve solenoid first. Those are the usual culprits. A refrigerant top-off with no leak search is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid ice on the coil and the unit never goes into defrost (no steam, no fan stop) | Failed or loose defrost sensor, or board fault | Switch to EM heat and let the ice melt | Test and reattach or replace the sensor; force defrost; replace the board if needed |
| Defrost "sound" occurs but ice remains | Reversing valve not shifting, or outdoor fan not stopping | EM heat, then call for service | Test the solenoid coil and valve; check the fan relay circuit |
| Ice builds fast and defrost can't keep up, with weak heat indoors | Low refrigerant charge or leak | EM heat; don't run iced | Pressure, superheat, and subcooling check; leak search and repair; recharge |
| Ice concentrated on the top or one side | Water dripping from the roof or gutter | Note it; fix gutters after thaw | Confirm defrost works; recommend a drip shield or relocation |
| Ice at the base climbing up the coil | Unit too low, melt water refreezing | Clear snow around the base | Raise the unit on a stand or riser; check drainage |
| Weak airflow indoors plus icing | Clogged filter or closed vents | Replace the filter and open vents | Check the blower and system charge if no improvement |
| Outdoor unit dead, coil iced from earlier | Tripped breaker or disconnect | Reset the breaker once | Find the cause of the trip; test electrical components |

Amana's lifetime unit replacement and parts warranties on many models can cover failed components if the unit was registered. Labor is usually not covered, so ask your technician to check warranty status by serial number.
