## What this code means

"Breaker keeps tripping" is not a fault code displayed by the Trane control board. It is an electrical protection event at your home's panel. The circuit breaker opens because the heat pump or air handler drew more current than the breaker allows, or because there is a short to ground.

Most Trane split systems use two separate circuits:

- **Outdoor unit (condenser) circuit.** This is typically a 240V double-pole breaker sized to the nameplate "Max Fuse / Breaker" rating. It powers the compressor and outdoor fan.
- **Indoor air handler circuit(s).** This powers the blower and the electric auxiliary or emergency heat strips. Larger heat kits often have their own breakers, sometimes mounted on the air handler itself.

A breaker does not reset itself. Once tripped, it stays off until someone switches it back on. Treat a breaker that trips repeatedly as a real fault, not a nuisance. On inverter-driven models (XV series), the drive's own fault codes may also be logged on the board or the communicating thermostat. A technician can read these to narrow down the cause.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

These are ranked by how often each is found on residential heat pumps. Trane does not publish a single ranked list for breaker trips.

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor (outdoor unit).** The compressor or fan motor struggles to start and draws locked-rotor current until the breaker trips. This is very common and is often accompanied by a humming outdoor unit.
2. **Electric auxiliary or emergency heat fault (air handler breaker).** Possible faults include a shorted heat element, a welded or failed sequencer or relay, or burned terminals on the heat kit. This trips mostly in cold weather or in emergency heat mode.
3. **Compressor problems.** A grounded or shorted winding trips instantly. A "hard-starting" or locked-rotor compressor trips within seconds. This is the most expensive cause.
4. **Loose, corroded or burned wiring connections.** These can occur at the breaker, the disconnect, the contactor or the heat kit lugs. Heat at a loose connection can trip the breaker or damage it.
5. **Pitted or failing contactor.** Chattering or welded contacts can cause arcing and overcurrent.
6. **Failing outdoor fan motor or indoor blower motor.** Worn bearings or shorted windings cause high current draw.
7. **High head pressure.** A badly clogged outdoor coil, overcharge or a restriction makes the compressor draw excessive amps. A failed outdoor fan can cause the same problem.
8. **Weak or wrong-sized breaker.** Breakers wear out after many trips. A breaker that is undersized versus the nameplate "Min Circuit Ampacity / Max Breaker" rating will trip under normal load.
9. **Moisture or ground fault.** Water in the disconnect, whip or control box, or rodent-chewed wiring, can cause a short to ground.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Note which breaker tripped.** Is it the outdoor unit, the air handler, or the heat strip? Note when it trips: instantly, after minutes, or only in cold weather or emergency heat. This information saves diagnostic time.
2. **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty or clogged.
3. **Check the thermostat.** Make sure it isn't set to "Emergency Heat" unintentionally. Replace its batteries if the display is weak.
4. **Clear visible obstructions.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked. Clear leaves, snow or debris from around the outdoor unit. Do not open the unit.
5. **Look, don't touch.** Look for burning smell, scorch marks or melted insulation at the panel or the outdoor disconnect. If you see any of these, leave the breaker off and call a professional.
6. **Reset once.** Turn the thermostat off and switch the breaker fully OFF, then ON. Wait 5 minutes, then restore the thermostat call. This delay lets refrigerant pressures equalize and protects the compressor.
7. **Stop if it trips again.** If it trips a second time, leave it off and call a technician. Switching to emergency heat is only reasonable if the trip is on the outdoor breaker and the air handler breaker holds.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should:

- **Inspect for damage.** Check the breaker, the disconnect, and the wiring for heat damage or loose lugs. They will torque connections and replace any burned components.
- **Test the capacitor.** They will measure capacitance (µF) against the rating and look for bulging or leaking.
- **Test the compressor windings.** They will measure the resistance of common, start and run windings. They will also test for a short to ground with an ohmmeter or megohmmeter. A grounded compressor usually means compressor or unit replacement.
- **Measure running and starting amps.** They will use a clamp meter on the compressor, fan and blower, and compare readings to nameplate RLA/LRA figures.
- **Check the heat kit.** They will test the element resistance and the sequencer or relay operation, and measure the amperage of each stage.
- **Inspect the contactor.** They will look for pitting or welding.
- **Check refrigerant pressures and coil condition.** This applies if the trip is delayed and amps are high.
- **Verify the breaker.** They will confirm the breaker size against the nameplate and test the breaker itself.
- **Read the board or drive fault history.** This applies to communicating and inverter (XV) systems.

Be cautious of a quote for a new compressor or system without documented winding or ground readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outdoor breaker trips within seconds, unit hums | Failed capacitor or locked compressor | Reset once; leave off if it repeats | Test and replace capacitor; add hard-start kit or diagnose compressor |
| Outdoor breaker trips instantly, no hum | Grounded compressor or wiring short | Leave breaker off | Megohm test; repair wiring or replace compressor |
| Air handler breaker trips in cold weather or emergency heat | Shorted heat strip or failed sequencer/relay | Check filter; leave breaker off if it repeats | Test elements and sequencers; replace heat kit parts |
| Breaker trips after running 10–30 minutes | High head pressure, dragging fan motor, or weak breaker | Clear debris around outdoor unit; replace filter | Clean coil, check charge, measure amps, replace motor or breaker |
| Breaker warm, burning smell, discoloration | Loose or burned connection | Leave off; call promptly | Replace breaker or lugs; repair wiring |
| Trips after rain or snow | Moisture in disconnect or control box | Leave off; keep area clear | Dry, seal and repair components; test for ground |
| Contactor chattering before trip | Pitted contactor or low control voltage | None | Replace contactor; check control transformer and wiring |
