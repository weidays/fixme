## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a numbered fault code. It describes a symptom: your Bryant air conditioner starts, runs only briefly, shuts off, and repeats. The house never gets properly cool or dry.

The repeated stopping is usually caused by one of these:

- **The thermostat ending the call for cooling too early.**
- **A safety device inside the system stopping the compressor.** This includes the high- or low-pressure switches and the compressor's internal overload.
- **A system that is too large for the house.**

On most Bryant units, the control enforces a roughly 5-minute delay before the compressor can restart, then tries again automatically. Systems with an Evolution or communicating control may show a fault message on the wall control. They may also lock out after repeated trips. Exact counts and timing vary by model, so check your unit's manual.

If the outdoor unit is not running at all, or the system blows warm air, see the related pages listed at the end instead.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat problems.** Common examples:
   - weak batteries
   - poor placement, such as near a supply vent, in sunlight or near a heat source
   - incorrect cycle-rate or staging settings
   - a loose thermostat wire
2. **Restricted airflow.** A dirty filter, closed registers or a dirty evaporator coil reduce airflow. This can trigger the low-pressure switch or let the coil freeze.
3. **Dirty or blocked outdoor coil.** Heat cannot escape, so pressure rises until the high-pressure switch or the compressor overload trips.
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** This can trip the low-pressure switch and causes erratic, short runs.
5. **Failing electrical components.** A weak run capacitor or a pitted contactor can overheat the compressor or make it drop out.
6. **Oversized equipment.** The system reaches the set temperature in minutes, then shuts off before removing humidity.
7. **Control board or sensor faults.** On Evolution and communicating models, faulty outdoor sensors or control boards can interrupt cycles. Behavior depends on the model.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat.** Replace the batteries. Confirm it is set to COOL with the fan on AUTO. Check whether a nearby supply vent, a lamp or direct sun could be giving it a false reading.
- **Air filter.** Replace it if it looks dirty. Make sure it is the correct size and that the airflow arrow points toward the furnace or air handler.
- **Vents and registers.** Open all supply registers. Clear furniture and rugs away from return grilles.
- **Outdoor unit.** Clear leaves, grass clippings and plants so there are at least 2 feet of open space around it. Look, but do not open the unit.
- **Condensate line.** If water is backing up into the drain pan, a float switch may be cutting power. Clear the line at its outside end or at the access port.
- **Breaker and power.** Confirm the breaker and the indoor service switch are on, and that all exterior panels are securely seated.
- **One reset.** If an Evolution control shows a lockout, you may reset it once. If short cycling returns, stop and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will usually work through these steps:

- **Watch a full cycle.** They note run times and whether the thermostat call, a safety switch or the overload is ending each cycle.
- **Check thermostat wiring and settings.** This includes cycle rate and anticipator settings.
- **Measure refrigerant performance.** They check suction and discharge pressure, superheat and subcooling against the charging chart for your model.
- **Test electrical parts.** They test the run capacitor against its rating, inspect the contactor, and measure compressor amp draw.
- **Inspect the coils.** They look for dirty indoor and outdoor coils and check airflow and blower settings.
- **Test safety switches.** They check the high- and low-pressure switches for continuity.
- **Read stored faults.** On Evolution and communicating systems, they pull fault history from the wall control or board LEDs.
- **Check sizing if needed.** If they suspect the system is oversized, they may recommend a Manual J load calculation.

A quote that says "low refrigerant" should also include leak detection, not just a refill.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cycles every few minutes, blank or erratic display | Thermostat batteries, setting or location | Replace batteries, check settings, note the location | Rewire, relocate or replace the thermostat |
| Short runs, weak airflow, frost on lines | Dirty filter or blocked returns | Replace filter, open vents | Clean evaporator coil, check blower and charge |
| Outdoor unit runs briefly, then stops; coil is dirty | High-pressure or overload trip | Clear debris around the unit | Clean outdoor coil, test fan motor and capacitor |
| Short runs, poor cooling, hissing or oily spots | Low refrigerant or leak | None | Find and repair leak, recharge to specification |
| Clicking, hard starts, humming | Weak capacitor or worn contactor | None | Test and replace capacitor or contactor |
| Cools fast, but the house stays humid | Oversized system | None | Load calculation, staging or airflow adjustment, or a resized system |
| Lockout message on Evolution control | Repeated safety trips | One reset, then call | Read fault history, repair the root cause |
| Water in the drain pan, unit stops | Float switch tripped | Clear the condensate line | Clear the drain, check pump and pitch |
