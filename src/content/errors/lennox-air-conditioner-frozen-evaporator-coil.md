## What this code means

"Frozen evaporator coil" is a condition, not a numbered Lennox fault code. Most Lennox split-system air conditioners have no sensor that reports it directly. You notice it by its symptoms:

- ice or frost on the indoor coil, which sits above the furnace or in the air handler
- ice on the large insulated copper line at the outdoor unit
- weak or warm airflow from the vents
- water around the furnace as the ice melts

The coil freezes when its surface drops below 32°F. This happens when too little warm indoor air passes over it, or when the refrigerant in it runs too cold or too low. Lennox communicating systems (iComfort, S30/S40) and some variable-speed equipment may show a related low-airflow, low-pressure or low-charge alert. Those alerts usually clear on their own once the condition goes away. There is generally no lockout requiring a reset.

This page covers the frozen indoor coil specifically. For a Lennox AC that simply isn't cooling, see the not-cooling page. For outdoor-coil icing on heat pumps in heating mode, see the heat pump pages.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Restricted airflow from a dirty air filter.** This is the most common cause by far. A clogged filter starves the coil of warm air.
2. **Closed or blocked supply registers or return grilles.** Too many closed vents, or furniture over a return, cuts airflow below what the coil needs.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil.** Dust and biofilm on the coil fins insulate the coil and block air even when the filter is clean. This is common on older systems or those run without filters.
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Low charge drops coil pressure and temperature below freezing. Lennox aluminum and copper coils can develop formicary or pinhole leaks over time.
5. **Blower problems.** Possible faults include a failing blower motor or capacitor, a slipping or wrong speed tap, or an incorrect airflow setting on variable-speed ECM motors. Setup and blower settings vary by furnace or air handler model.
6. **Metering device or refrigerant restriction.** A stuck TXV, a clogged filter-drier or a kinked line set can cause the coil to run too cold.
7. **Running the AC in very cool weather.** Operating below about 60–65°F outdoors can drop coil pressure enough to freeze, especially on systems without low-ambient controls.
8. **Undersized or restrictive ductwork.** This is a design issue, often revealed after an equipment change.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Stop cooling and thaw.** Set the thermostat to OFF and the fan to ON. This blows room air across the coil and speeds melting.
   - Put towels near the furnace in case the drain pan overflows.
   - Never chip or scrape ice.
2. **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it's gray or clogged, or if you can't see light through it. Use the correct size, which is printed on the filter frame. Very restrictive high-MERV filters can contribute to the problem, so check your equipment's recommendation.
3. **Open registers and clear returns.** Make sure most supply vents are open and no return grille is blocked by furniture, rugs or curtains.
4. **Check the thermostat.**
   - Confirm it's set to COOL with a sensible setpoint. Very low setpoints in mild weather make freezing more likely.
   - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
5. **Check the condensate line.** Look at the drain line exit and the condensate pump, if you have one. Make sure water drains once the coil thaws. A clogged drain won't freeze the coil, but it can cause water damage during the thaw.
6. **Make sure panels are seated.** The furnace or air handler blower door must be fully closed. On many units the blower won't run otherwise, and air leaks can affect airflow.
7. **Restart and watch.** After the coil is fully thawed and dry, return to COOL. Check the indoor coil area and the large copper line at the outdoor unit over the next several hours. If frost returns, turn the system off and call a technician.

Do not add refrigerant, open the coil cabinet or adjust blower settings yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician works through these steps:

- **Confirm the thaw is complete.** Accurate readings are impossible on an iced coil.
- **Measure airflow.** This includes total external static pressure, the temperature drop across the coil (typically 16–22°F when working properly), and blower speed or airflow settings against Lennox specs for your furnace or air handler.
- **Inspect the coil.** They'll look at the evaporator coil and blower wheel for dirt, which may require removing a coil access panel.
- **Connect gauges or digital probes.** They'll check suction pressure, superheat and subcooling.
  - Low suction with high superheat suggests low charge or a restriction.
  - Low suction with normal superheat points toward an airflow problem.
- **Leak-search if charge is low.** Methods include an electronic detector, bubble solution, UV dye or a nitrogen pressure test. EPA rules and good practice call for finding the leak, not just topping up.
- **Test the blower motor and capacitor.** They'll check amperage and verify ECM programming or dip-switch settings.
- **Check for restrictions.** They'll look at the TXV and filter-drier using temperature drops across components.
- **Read alert history.** On iComfort/S30/S40 systems, they'll review stored alerts.

Be wary of a quote that adds refrigerant with no leak check, or that skips airflow testing entirely.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on coil, weak airflow, gray filter | Clogged air filter | Thaw (fan ON), replace filter | None if it doesn't refreeze |
| Coil freezes in rooms with closed vents | Blocked registers/returns | Open vents, clear returns | Duct evaluation if it persists |
| Clean filter, still freezes, low airflow | Dirty evaporator coil or blower wheel | Thaw, keep filter changed | Coil and blower cleaning |
| Ice on large outdoor copper line, long run times, rising bills | Low refrigerant from leak | Turn off cooling, call pro | Leak search, repair, evacuate, recharge |
| No or very weak air from vents, humming | Blower motor or capacitor failure | Check breaker and blower door | Test and replace capacitor or motor |
| Freezes after new install or equipment change | Wrong blower speed/airflow setting | None | Verify and reset airflow settings |
| Normal airflow, frosted line near indoor coil | TXV or filter-drier restriction | Turn off cooling, call pro | Replace TXV or drier, recharge |
| Freezes only on cool nights | Low-ambient operation | Raise setpoint, avoid cooling below ~60°F outdoors | Low-ambient kit if cooling is needed |
| Water on floor after freeze | Melting ice overflowing pan or clogged drain | Clear condensate line, towels | Pan or drain repair |
