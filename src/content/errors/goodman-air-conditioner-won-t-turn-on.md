## What this code means

"Won't turn on" is not a Goodman error code. It describes a system that shows no sign of life when cooling is called for. The indoor blower doesn't start, the outdoor unit stays silent, and often the thermostat screen is blank or unresponsive.

This page covers that **total no-start**. If any part of the system runs, these related pages fit better:

- **The indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit doesn't:** see Compressor not running or Fan runs but no cold air.
- **The outdoor fan is still but you hear humming:** see Outdoor fan not spinning.

On a Goodman split system, the 24-volt control power for the AC comes from the transformer in the indoor furnace or air handler, not the outdoor unit. That means a fault indoors can stop the entire AC.

Standard single-stage condensers do not latch a lockout. Once power and the thermostat call are restored, they restart, usually after a short anti-short-cycle delay. Communicating and variable-speed models may hold a fault until power is cycled, and the behavior varies by model.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat problem.** Possible issues include dead batteries, the thermostat set to Heat or Off, the setpoint above room temperature, or a Wi-Fi thermostat with a lost configuration or a lost C-wire.
2. **Tripped breaker or open disconnect.** The AC typically has its own 240V breaker and an outdoor disconnect. The furnace or air handler has a separate 120V/240V breaker and a service switch, often mistaken for a light switch.
3. **Tripped condensate safety (float) switch.** A clogged drain fills the pan or the secondary drain. The float switch then cuts the 24V circuit to prevent water damage, so the system stays off.
4. **Blower door safety switch open.** If the indoor cabinet panel isn't seated after a filter change, the door switch cuts power to the board.
5. **Blown low-voltage fuse on the furnace or air handler board.** This is usually a 3A or 5A automotive-style fuse. It is often blown by a shorted thermostat wire, for example one chewed or nicked at the outdoor unit.
6. **Failed 24V transformer.**
7. **Failed control board or loose or burnt wiring.** This includes failures of the outdoor board on two-stage and communicating models.
8. **Pending time delay.** This is not a fault. After a power outage or a setting change, some boards wait about 3–5 minutes before starting the AC.

If the breaker for the AC itself trips repeatedly, see Breaker keeps tripping instead.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set the mode to Cool and the fan to Auto. Set the temperature at least 3°F below room temperature. Replace the batteries if the thermostat uses them. Wait 5 minutes for any time delay to finish.
- **Breakers:** Check the panel for both the AC/condenser breaker and the furnace or air handler breaker. Turn any tripped breaker fully off, then back on, **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Service switch:** Make sure the switch at the furnace or air handler is on. Also make sure the outdoor disconnect is fully inserted or switched on. Do not open the disconnect box beyond its normal pull-out handle.
- **Panels:** Confirm the indoor unit's blower door is fully seated and secured, especially if you just changed the filter.
- **Condensate:** Look for water in the drain pan, the secondary pan, or the float switch area. If the line is clogged, clear it from the outside end with a wet/dry vac. The system should restart once the float drops.
- **Filter and vents:** Replace a dirty filter and open the supply registers. These won't usually cause a total no-start, but they prevent repeat problems.
- **Power cycle:** If you have a communicating or variable-speed model, power-cycle it at the breakers **one time**. If it doesn't recover, call for service.

Do not remove the outdoor service panel, touch the capacitor, or replace fuses.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirm line voltage.** The tech checks for 120V or 240V at the furnace or air handler and 240V at the condenser disconnect.
- **Measure 24V at the transformer.** The tech checks the R and C terminals on the board. If there is no output while line voltage is present, the transformer is bad.
- **Inspect the board fuse.** If it's blown, the tech looks for the short before replacing it. Usual suspects are the thermostat cable, the outdoor low-voltage wiring, and a shorted contactor coil.
- **Check the safety circuit.** The tech tests continuity through the door switch and the float switch in the R or Y circuit.
- **Jumper R to Y and G at the board.** This tells whether the thermostat or its wire is at fault.
- **Read the board LED.** On two-stage and communicating outdoor boards, the tech reads the LED or ComfortBridge app codes.
- **Finish with wiring checks.** The tech inspects for burnt terminals and loose connections.

A good quote names the specific failed part and the measurement that proved it.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat screen blank | Dead batteries, no 24V | Replace batteries, check breakers | Test transformer and fuse, run C-wire |
| Screen works, nothing runs | Wrong mode or setpoint, open float switch | Set to Cool, clear condensate line | Check safety circuit, jumper test |
| Worked until filter change | Blower door not seated | Reseat panel | Replace door switch if faulty |
| Water in pan, system dead | Float switch tripped | Clear drain line | Clean pan, check trap and pump |
| Dead after storm or outage | Tripped breaker, blown fuse, board damage | Reset breaker once | Replace fuse after finding short, board or transformer |
| Fuse blows repeatedly | Shorted thermostat wire or contactor coil | None | Locate and repair short |
| Communicating model shows fault | Board or communication error | One power cycle | Read codes, repair wiring or board |
