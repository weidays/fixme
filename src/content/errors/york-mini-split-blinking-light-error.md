## What this code means

A York mini-split does not have a single "blinking light error." The flashing lamp on the indoor unit is how the unit reports its status or a fault code. Some patterns mean normal operation. Others mean the controller has found a problem.

Because York ductless systems are built by OEM partners, the details vary by model:

- **Which lamp blinks.** It may be the operation/run lamp, the timer lamp, or a dedicated defrost or alarm lamp.
- **How many flashes** mean what.
- **Whether a numeric code** also appears on the display.

There are two broad categories:

- **Normal status blinks.** These include defrost or anti-cold-air warm-up in heat mode, and on some models a filter-clean reminder. The unit recovers on its own.
- **Fault blinks.** The unit stops or limits operation and repeats a flash pattern. Common categories are:
  - Indoor/outdoor communication loss
  - Temperature sensor (thermistor) faults
  - Control board memory (EEPROM) errors
  - Fan motor speed faults
  - Outdoor-unit protection trips, such as high or low pressure, high discharge temperature, inverter module/IPM protection, or abnormal voltage

**Reset behavior varies.** Some protection conditions clear automatically and the unit retries after a delay. Most sensor, EEPROM and persistent communication faults hold until power is cycled. Because a true fault involves sealed-system or high-voltage inverter components, diagnosis is technician work.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost or anti-cold-air warm-up (heat mode).** The lamp blinks while the outdoor coil defrosts or the indoor coil warms up. Airflow pauses, then resumes. This is not a fault.
2. **Filter-clean reminder (some models).** A lamp blinks after a set number of run hours. It clears by cleaning the filter and resetting the reminder per the manual.
3. **Indoor/outdoor communication error.** The boards can't talk to each other. Typical causes are a loose or miswired interconnect cable, damaged wiring, a tripped outdoor breaker, or a failing board. This is one of the most common true fault blinks on ductless systems.
4. **Temperature sensor (thermistor) open or shorted.** This can affect the room, indoor coil, outdoor coil, outdoor ambient or discharge sensor. Corroded connectors and damaged sensor leads are frequent culprits.
5. **Outdoor protection trip.** Possible causes include:
   - High pressure or high discharge temperature, often from a blocked outdoor coil or low refrigerant
   - Low pressure, often from refrigerant loss
   - Inverter module (IPM) overcurrent
   - Supply voltage too high or too low

   Some of these self-clear and retry. Repeated trips may lock the unit out.
6. **Indoor or outdoor fan motor fault.** The board can't detect proper fan speed feedback because of a failed motor, an obstruction, or a wiring/connector problem.
7. **Control board fault (including EEPROM).** This is the least common cause and is usually confirmed only after wiring and sensors check out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Note the pattern first.** Write down which lamp blinks, how many flashes occur between pauses, the operating mode, and the outdoor temperature. This often lets a tech identify the problem before arriving.
- **Check whether it's normal defrost.** In heat mode on a cold day, wait 10–15 minutes. If warm air resumes and the blinking stops, the unit is working normally.
- **Clean the indoor filters.** Open the front panel and slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them, let them dry fully, and reinstall. If your model has a filter reminder, reset it as described in your manual.
- **Check the remote.** Replace its batteries and confirm the mode and setpoint are correct, for example that it isn't set to heat in summer.
- **Check power.** Confirm the breaker for the system isn't tripped and the outdoor disconnect is on. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Do one reset.** Turn the unit off at the remote, cut power at the breaker for about five minutes, then restore it. If the same pattern returns, do not keep resetting.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove leaves, snow or debris from around the outdoor unit and make sure about 2 feet of clearance. Look only; do not open panels.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Make sure the drain hose outlet isn't kinked or blocked.
- **Seat the panels.** Confirm the indoor front panel is closed and properly seated.

Do not open the outdoor unit, disconnect the interconnect cable, or touch the inverter board. Inverter capacitors can hold a dangerous charge after power is off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Read the code.** The tech counts the flash pattern or uses the display or remote check function, then looks it up in the service manual for your exact model.
- **Communication faults.** The tech verifies supply voltage at the outdoor unit and the communication voltage across the interconnect terminals. They also inspect for loose, corroded or miswired terminals and check the cable for damage, such as rodent chewing or line-set damage.
- **Sensor faults.** The tech unplugs the suspect thermistor and measures its resistance against the manufacturer's temperature/resistance chart.
- **Protection trips.** The tech:
  - Checks outdoor coil cleanliness and airflow
  - Measures operating pressures and superheat/subcooling
  - Checks for refrigerant leaks
  - Verifies line voltage under load
- **Fan faults.** The tech checks that the motor spins freely, then checks the motor windings, connectors and feedback signal.
- **Board faults.** A board is replaced only after wiring, sensors and power are confirmed good.

If a quote calls for a new board without any wiring or sensor checks, ask questions.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lamp blinks in heat mode, fan pauses, then resumes | Defrost or anti-cold-air warm-up | Wait 10–15 minutes | None needed |
| Lamp blinks but unit runs normally | Filter reminder (some models) | Clean filters and reset reminder per manual | None needed |
| Unit dead or stops soon after start, steady repeating pattern | Indoor/outdoor communication error | Check breaker and disconnect, do one reset | Test communication voltage, repair interconnect wiring, replace board if needed |
| Pattern returns right after reset, unit won't start | Thermistor open or shorted | One reset only | Measure sensor resistance, replace sensor |
| Unit runs, stops, retries, then locks out | High pressure or temperature, or low refrigerant protection | Clear debris around outdoor unit, clean filters | Clean coil, find and repair leak, recharge, check pressures |
| Fault in hot weather or during voltage dips | Inverter/IPM or voltage protection | Do one reset | Measure line voltage, test inverter board |
| Indoor or outdoor fan not turning, fault blinks | Fan motor or feedback fault | Do one reset | Test and replace fan motor or connector |
| Persistent code with wiring and sensors good | Control board or EEPROM fault | None | Replace indoor or outdoor board |
