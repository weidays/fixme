## What this code means

"Not heating" is a symptom, not a single fault code. Your American Standard ductless mini-split is a heat pump. To heat, it reverses the refrigerant flow, so the indoor coil becomes the hot side. When it fails to heat, the possibilities range from simple to serious:

- **Normal operation:** a warm-up delay or a defrost cycle
- **Simple setting problems:** wrong mode, setpoint or timer
- **Real faults:** the reversing valve, refrigerant charge, sensors, the outdoor fan or the compressor

**How it behaves when something is wrong:**

- If the unit has detected a fault, the indoor unit usually blinks an indicator light or shows an error code. The meaning of that code is model-specific, so look it up in the service manual for your exact model.
- Many protection stops reset on their own once conditions return to normal.
- Some lockouts hold until power is cycled. This behavior varies by model.

**If you also have cooling problems:** heating faults often overlap with them. See the related pages listed at the end of this article.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Mode or setpoint error.** The unit is set to Cool, Dry, Fan or Auto instead of Heat. Other possibilities are a setpoint below room temperature, an active timer, or dead remote batteries, so the command never reached the unit.
2. **Normal defrost or warm-up.** Heat pauses during defrost, and the indoor fan is held off until the coil warms. Owners often mistake this for a failure.
3. **Dirty indoor filters or a blocked outdoor unit.** Clogged filters cut airflow and heat output. Snow, ice or debris around the outdoor coil chokes heat absorption.
4. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** Heat output is weak, and you may see icing patterns or protection trips. The leak must be found and repaired.
5. **Reversing valve or its coil failed.** The unit cools when set to Heat, or blows lukewarm air. The valve is stuck, or its solenoid or control signal is bad.
6. **Sensor or control board fault.** A bad coil or ambient thermistor, or a fault in the indoor or outdoor board, causes wrong defrost decisions or protection shutdowns. These faults usually come with an error code.
7. **Outdoor fan motor or compressor problem.** The outdoor fan is not running, or the compressor will not start or keeps tripping on protection. Inverter compressor drive faults also fall in this category.
8. **Cold weather beyond the model's rating.** A standard model in extreme cold may simply not keep up. Cold-climate models are rated for lower temperatures.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the remote settings.**
  - Confirm the remote is set to **Heat** mode (often a sun icon).
  - Set the temperature a few degrees above the room temperature.
  - Cancel any timer or sleep settings.
- **Replace the remote batteries.** Then point the remote directly at the indoor unit and listen for the beep that confirms it received the signal.
- **Wait 10–15 minutes.** This rules out the normal warm-up delay or a defrost cycle. Steam from the outdoor unit with no indoor airflow is usually defrost.
- **Clean the indoor filters.**
  - Turn the unit off.
  - Lift the front panel and slide out the mesh filters.
  - Rinse them with lukewarm water and let them dry fully.
  - Reinstall them.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.**
  - Remove snow, leaves and debris from around the cabinet. Keep about 2 feet of clearance.
  - Do not chip ice off the coil. You can damage the fins.
  - Gently clear snow from the top of the cabinet.
- **Check the breaker or disconnect.** If the breaker has tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.
- **Do one power reset.** Turn the unit off at the breaker for about 5 minutes, then restore power. Do this only once. Repeated resets of a unit that keeps faulting can mask or worsen the problem.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Make sure the drain line outlet is not frozen or blocked, so meltwater from defrost can drain away.
- **Check the panels.** Confirm the indoor front panel is fully seated and the louvers are open.
- **Write down any error code or blink pattern.** Your technician will need it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Read the fault code.** They check the indoor display or blink pattern, and the outdoor board's LED codes if your model has them. They then compare these against the service manual for your model.
- **Confirm the mode and test heating operation.** They measure the temperature split between the return and supply air at the indoor unit.
- **Check refrigerant pressures and temperatures in heat mode.** This includes superheat and subcooling, compared with the manufacturer's charge chart. If the charge is low, they perform leak detection, typically with an electronic detector or bubble test, followed by a nitrogen pressure test after repair.
- **Test the reversing valve.**
  - They check the valve coil's voltage and resistance.
  - They compare the temperatures of the valve's lines to see whether it actually shifts.
- **Test the sensors.** They measure thermistor resistance against the manufacturer's temperature/resistance table. This covers the indoor coil, outdoor coil, ambient and discharge sensors.
- **Check the outdoor fan motor and the compressor.** They test windings and current draw, and check the inverter board's output.
- **Review the defrost operation.** They confirm the unit enters defrost and exits it correctly.

**Watch out for vague quotes.** A quote that says "needs a recharge" with no leak search, or "replace board" with no fault code or test results, deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit blows cool air in Heat mode | Wrong mode, or reversing valve stuck | Confirm Heat mode and a setpoint above room temperature | Test reversing valve coil and valve; replace if failed |
| No airflow for several minutes after start | Normal warm-up (anti-cold-draft) | Wait 10 minutes | None if it then heats |
| Heat stops, outdoor unit steams | Normal defrost cycle | Wait 5–15 minutes | Check defrost sensors and logic if it defrosts constantly |
| Weak, lukewarm heat | Dirty filters or low refrigerant | Clean filters; clear the outdoor unit | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Outdoor unit encased in ice | Defrost fault, sensor fault, or blocked drainage | Clear snow from around the unit; check that the drain isn't frozen | Diagnose defrost sensor, board or outdoor fan |
| Unit blinks or shows an error code and stops | Sensor, board, fan or compressor protection | Note the code; do one power reset | Fault-code diagnosis per the service manual |
| Breaker trips when heating | Compressor or electrical fault | Do not keep resetting | Electrical and compressor diagnosis |
| Struggles only in deep cold | Below the model's low-ambient rating | Check your model's rated range | Advise on capacity or supplemental heat |

## Repair costs

These are typical US price ranges, including parts and labor. Actual prices vary by region and model.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service call / diagnostic | $90 – $250 |
| Filter cleaning, setting fix | $0 DIY |
| Professional coil cleaning (indoor and outdoor) | $150 – $400 |
| Thermistor / sensor replacement | $150 – $400 |
| Reversing valve coil (solenoid) | $150 – $350 |
| Reversing valve replacement (brazing and recharge) | $800 – $1,800 |
| Leak repair plus recharge | $400 – $1,500 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350 – $800 |
| Indoor or outdoor control/inverter board | $400 – $1,200 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500 – $2,500+ (replacing the outdoor unit is often weighed against this cost) |

**Check your warranty first.** Registered American Standard units often have longer parts coverage, sometimes 10 years or more, which can cut costs sharply. Labor coverage depends on your installer or any extended plan.
