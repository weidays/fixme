## What this code means

Lennox Code **E209** means the **high-fire (high-stage) pressure switch failed to close** when the control expected it to. On a two-stage or modulating furnace, the inducer motor speeds up on high fire, which should generate enough draft (negative pressure) to pull the high-fire pressure switch closed. When the control commands high fire but that switch stays open, it posts this fault.

The pressure switch is a safety device. It confirms the inducer is actually moving combustion gases before — and while — the burners fire on high. If the switch never closes on high fire, the control will not allow high-stage operation, and the furnace typically drops back to low fire or shuts down.

**Verify the code on your own unit first.** Lennox uses several control generations, and not all of them display the same alphanumeric codes — some report faults through LED flash or blink patterns instead. Before acting on anything here, confirm the E209 designation and its meaning against the diagnostic code table printed on the label inside your blower door, or in the installation/service manual for your specific model series (for example the SLP98V or EL296V manual code table). If your furnace's own table shows something different for this code, go with the table.

**Reset behavior:** On many Lennox integrated controls, a fault of this type auto-clears once the switch closes correctly on the next high-fire call. Repeated faults can escalate to a hard lockout requiring a power cycle. Exact thresholds and reset logic vary by control generation — again, check the label inside your blower door.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted flue/intake vent** — snow, ice, nests, or debris in the concentric or PVC vent termination reduces draft so the inducer can't build enough pressure on high fire.
2. **Clogged condensate drain or trap** — a full trap or plugged drain line backs up water into the pressure switch tubing path, preventing the switch from closing under high-fire draft.
3. **Cracked, pinched, or disconnected pressure switch hose** — the small rubber tube between the inducer and the high-fire switch leaks or is kinked.
4. **Inducer motor weak or failing** — the inducer spins but no longer produces the higher airflow needed for high fire, so the low-fire switch closes but the high-fire one doesn't.
5. **Failed high-fire pressure switch** — the switch diaphragm or contacts are defective and won't close even at correct pressure.
6. **Water or debris in the inducer pressure taps/collector box** — condensate pooling in the collector box restricts pressure sensing on high fire.
7. **Control board or wiring fault** — loose connectors or a control that misreads the high-fire switch (least common).

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do — all outside the sealed cabinet:

- **Check the thermostat:** Confirm it's set to Heat and the setpoint is above room temperature. A weak or dying thermostat can misbehave on staging calls; replace batteries if it uses them.
- **Replace a dirty air filter:** A clogged filter won't hold a high-fire pressure switch open, so it isn't the cause of this code — but a clean filter is basic maintenance and rules out unrelated airflow complaints while you're at it.
- **Inspect the outdoor vent terminations:** Look outside for the PVC intake/exhaust pipes. Clear away snow, ice, leaves, or any nest or debris blocking the openings. This is the single most common fixable cause of this fault.
- **Look at the condensate drain:** If you see standing water near the furnace or a full condensate pan, the drain line may be clogged. Do not open the cabinet — the trap and collector box are technician territory. If your system has an accessible exterior drain outlet or a capped cleanout port you can reach safely, you can check that it is draining freely and clear obvious debris from the opening.
- **Cycle the breaker or furnace switch once:** Turn the furnace disconnect off, wait 30 seconds, and back on to attempt one reset. Do not repeatedly reset a locked-out unit.

If the code returns after clearing the vent and cycling power, stop here — the rest is technician work.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will systematically confirm where the draft is failing:

- **Measure pressure switch operation** with a manometer, reading actual inducer negative pressure on both low and high fire and comparing to the switch's rated set point.
- **Inspect and test the pressure switch hoses** for cracks, moisture, or blockage, and check the collector box/taps for condensate.
- **Check the inducer motor** amperage and airflow to confirm it reaches high-fire speed and output.
- **Verify the flue and condensate system** are fully clear, including the internal trap.
- **Test the switch electrically** — confirming it opens and closes at spec, and checking control connectors and voltage.
- **Confirm the control stages** to high fire correctly before condemning the switch or inducer.

A good quote names the specific failed part (switch, hose, inducer, or drain) — not just "pressure switch problem."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furnace heats weakly, won't reach high fire | High-fire switch not closing | Clear vent terminations | Manometer test, replace switch |
| Standing water near furnace, code appears in cold | Clogged condensate drain/trap | Check accessible exterior drain outlet only | Clear internal trap, check collector box |
| Code after snow/ice storm | Blocked vent termination | Clear snow/debris from pipes | Verify vent sizing and slope |
| Inducer runs but stays on low fire | Weak inducer motor | None — cabinet work | Test inducer amps/output, replace motor |
| Rattling or whistling hose sound near inducer | Cracked/loose pressure hose | None — cabinet work | Inspect and replace hose |
| Code persists after clearing vent | Failed switch or control board | One power cycle only | Electrical test of switch and board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Clearing a blocked vent termination:** $0 DIY, or $80–$180 on a service call.
- **Clearing a clogged condensate drain/trap:** $0–$150 depending on access.
- **Pressure switch hose replacement:** $80–$180.
- **High-fire pressure switch replacement:** $150–$300 installed.
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $400–$1,200 installed, depending on model.
- **Control board replacement (rare for this code):** $400–$1,200 installed.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** typically $90–$180, often credited toward the repair.

On two-stage and modulating Lennox equipment, inducer assemblies and unit-control boards are OEM parts that are frequently dealer-sourced, so quotes for either job commonly land above $700. A quote in that range is not automatically inflated — ask which part failed and how it was confirmed.

Because this code is frequently caused by a blocked vent or drain, always insist the tech rule those out before quoting a pressure switch or inducer.
