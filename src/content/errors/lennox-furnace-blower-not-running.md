## What this code means

"Blower not running" is a symptom rather than a single Lennox fault code — the indoor blower fails to spin up when the furnace calls for heat (or for fan-only "G" operation). On most Lennox furnaces the control board energizes the blower a short time after the burners light, and again for a set cool-down period after they shut off. When the blower never starts, the furnace either overheats and trips the high-limit switch or simply blows no air at the registers.

Depending on the board and motor, you may see a slow-flashing status LED, a blower-specific fault, or **no code at all** (for example, an open blower-door safety switch usually kills the whole furnace without storing a fault). Variable-speed-equipped models (primarily SLP and EL series) may log a motor-communication fault, while older PSC-motor furnaces typically show nothing. Because a stalled blower quickly leads to overheating and limit trips, this is a **pro-level** repair once the safe homeowner checks below are ruled out.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blown run capacitor (PSC-motor furnaces).** The most common cause on belt-less direct-drive PSC blowers. A weak or failed dual/single run capacitor leaves the motor humming or dead.
2. **Failed blower motor or seized bearings.** Worn bearings, an open winding, or a tripped internal thermal overload stop the motor. Common on aging furnaces.
3. **Failed ECM/variable-speed motor module.** On variable-speed-equipped models (primarily SLP and EL series), the electronic module on the motor fails while the motor itself may be fine.
4. **Control board blower relay failure.** The board's blower (HEAT/COOL) relay contacts can be burned or pitted so they no longer close, or the relay coil fails to pull in — either way the board loses its blower output.
5. **Open blower-door safety switch.** The interlock switch behind the blower access panel must be closed; a panel that isn't fully seated, or a failed switch, cuts power to the blower and often the whole furnace.
6. **Loose or disconnected motor wiring / connector.** Vibration can back out a spade terminal or an ECM harness plug.
7. **Blown low-voltage or main board fuse.** A shorted component can pop the board fuse, killing blower control.
8. **Thermostat wiring / fan setting fault.** A miswired or failed "G" (fan) signal prevents fan-only operation.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform. If the blower still won't run, stop and call a licensed technician.

- **Check the thermostat.** Set it to HEAT and raise the setpoint several degrees. Then try the fan setting on **ON** — if the blower runs continuously on ON but not during heating, note that for your technician.
- **Replace thermostat batteries** if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Check the air filter.** A clogged filter can cause overheating and limit trips that mimic a blower fault. Replace a dirty filter with the correct size.
- **Confirm the blower access panel is fully seated.** Push the lower door firmly into place so the door-interlock switch is engaged. Do **not** tape, wedge, or bypass the switch.
- **Check the breaker** for the furnace and the furnace switch (looks like a light switch near the unit). If tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off and call a pro.
- **Look at supply and return registers** to be sure they're open and unblocked.
- **Check the condensate drain** on high-efficiency models — a full drain pan or clogged line can trip a float switch and shut the furnace down.

Do **not** open the cabinet, touch the capacitor, test wiring, or reset the furnace repeatedly.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically:

- Read the control board's diagnostic LED and any stored blower/motor fault.
- Verify 120 V reaching the board and confirm the door-interlock switch is closed.
- Check the board's low-voltage fuse and the "G" fan signal from the thermostat.
- On PSC motors, measure the run capacitor with a meter and confirm blower voltage output from the board relay.
- On ECM/variable-speed motors, check the 5-pin/16-pin harness, motor power and communication, then isolate the motor module from the motor.
- Test the blower motor windings and check for seized bearings or a tripped internal overload.
- Inspect wiring, connectors, and the blower relay contacts on the board.
- Confirm proper airflow and check the high-limit switch, since a stalled blower usually trips it.

Ask for the specific failed component and its measured reading — this lets you sanity-check the quote against the parts below.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burners light, blower never starts, furnace shuts off on limit | Bad run capacitor (PSC) | Replace filter; confirm door seated | Test/replace capacitor, verify motor |
| Motor hums but won't spin | Weak capacitor or seized bearings | None | Replace capacitor or blower motor |
| No air on heat or fan ON; furnace otherwise dead | Open door switch or blown board fuse | Reseat blower panel; reset breaker once | Test door switch, replace board fuse |
| Variable-speed model, no blower, comm fault logged | Failed ECM motor module | None | Test/replace ECM module or motor |
| Blower dead only in fan-ON mode | Thermostat "G" wiring fault | Check fan setting, replace batteries | Repair thermostat wiring / board relay |
| Blower ran, now nothing, no code | Loose motor connector | None | Reseat/repair motor wiring |
| Intermittent no-blower + limit trips | Loose connector, failing motor, airflow restriction or failing board relay | Replace filter | Diagnose blower circuit; replace board if relay output is confirmed faulty |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter (DIY):** $10 – $40
- **Thermostat batteries (DIY):** $5 – $15
- **Blower run capacitor:** $150 – $300
- **Blower-door safety switch:** $80 – $180
- **Control board low-voltage fuse (with diagnosis):** $90 – $200
- **Control board / integrated furnace control:** $400 – $900+
- **ECM motor module (variable-speed):** $300 – $600
- **PSC blower motor:** $450 – $900 installed (more if the blower wheel and housing must be pulled)
- **ECM/variable-speed blower motor:** $700 – $1,500+ installed
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $100 – $225 (often applied to the repair)

Prices vary by region, motor type, and Lennox series. Variable-speed (SLP/EL) parts run higher than Merit-series PSC parts, and Lennox OEM control boards are not cheap.
