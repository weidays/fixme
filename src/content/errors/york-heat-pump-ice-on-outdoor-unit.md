## What this code means

"Ice on outdoor unit" is not a numbered fault code. It is a visible symptom. Some York communicating systems may log a related defrost or sensor fault at the thermostat or control board, but most units show no code at all.

In heating mode, the outdoor coil runs colder than the outside air, so moisture freezes on it. That is expected. York heat pumps clear it with a defrost cycle. The board briefly reverses the system to cooling mode and stops the outdoor fan, which melts the frost from the inside.

Your defrost behavior depends on the board:

- **Demand defrost:** Many current boards start a defrost when sensors detect frost-like conditions.
- **Time-temperature defrost:** Older boards check at a set interval of 30, 60 or 90 minutes and defrost if the coil is cold enough.

A defrost usually ends on coil temperature or a maximum time limit, roughly 10–14 minutes on many boards. Normal operation then resumes automatically. There is no lockout to reset for ice itself.

When this is a problem: ice is thick or clear and glassy, it covers the whole coil or the top and fan, or it is still there after an hour or more of operation.

Ice comes from one of two sources. Either water is reaching the unit from outside, or the unit isn't removing frost as fast as it forms. If ice builds even though defrost never seems to run, see the dedicated defrost page listed under Related codes.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal frost mistaken for a fault.** Light frost that clears every 30–90 minutes is correct operation, especially in freezing fog or drizzle near 32–40°F.
2. **External water freezing on the unit.** Common sources include:
   - a gutter overflowing or dripping onto the top of the unit
   - roof runoff with no gutter above it
   - a downspout discharging nearby

   Ice built from the top down usually points here.
3. **Poor drainage or a buried base.** If the unit sits on the ground, or snow is drifted against it, defrost meltwater can't drain away. It refreezes in the base pan and on the lower coil, and builds up with each cycle. York installation instructions call for elevating the unit above expected snow levels in cold climates.
4. **Restricted airflow.** A dirty indoor filter, a dirty outdoor coil, or blocked registers can all contribute. So can debris, plantings or a cover around the outdoor unit. Low airflow makes the coil run colder and frost faster.
5. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** Undercharge drops coil temperature and causes uneven icing, often starting at one section or the bottom of the coil.
6. **Defrost control faults.** These include a failed defrost board, defrost or coil temperature sensor, ambient sensor, or defrost relay. On older boards, an interval set too long for local conditions can also be the issue. The unit frosts but defrost doesn't start or ends too early.
7. **Outdoor fan problems.** A failed fan motor or capacitor, or a fan that doesn't shut off during defrost, can cause icing. If the fan runs during defrost, the cycle can't melt the coil effectively.
8. **Reversing valve or its solenoid failing to shift.** If the valve doesn't switch, defrost can't send heat to the outdoor coil. This usually comes with poor indoor heating as well.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Watch one cycle.** Note the time and check back over 60–90 minutes. A whoosh, steam, and a mostly clear coil means defrost is working. Thin frost that keeps returning is normal.
- **Switch to emergency/auxiliary heat** at the thermostat if the coil is solidly iced. This protects the outdoor unit while you wait for help. Expect higher electric bills while it runs.
- **Replace or check the air filter.** A clogged filter is a cheap, common contributor.
- **Open supply registers** and make sure return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Check thermostat batteries and settings.** Confirm it is set to Heat, not Cool or Emergency Heat by mistake, and replace the batteries if the display is weak.
- **Look above the unit.** Look for a dripping gutter, missing gutter section or downspout aimed at the unit. Clearing a clogged gutter from the ground or redirecting a downspout is a reasonable fix.
- **Clear snow and debris from around the unit** by hand or with a broom. Keep about 2 feet of clearance and remove any cover. Clear only around the outside of the unit. Do not chip ice from the coil or reach into the fan.
- **Check the breaker and outdoor disconnect.** If the unit is completely dead, check the breaker and the outdoor disconnect. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Make sure the exterior access panels are seated** and secured.

Do not pour boiling water on the unit, use tools on the ice, or open any panel to inspect the board or sensors.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirm the source.** The tech will check whether ice is top-down (runoff) or coil-wide (an operating problem), and check unit elevation and base drainage.
- **Check airflow.** This means measuring indoor static pressure and checking the filter, blower and outdoor coil condition.
- **Force a defrost.** Most York defrost boards have test pins or a test mode that starts defrost on demand. The tech confirms three things during the forced defrost:
  - the reversing valve shifts
  - the outdoor fan stops
  - the auxiliary heat energizes
- **Test the sensors.** Coil and ambient sensors are checked against York's temperature-resistance chart. The board's outputs and defrost interval setting are also verified.
- **Check refrigerant charge** with gauges or digital probes, using superheat and subcooling per the unit's charging chart. If the charge is low, they should leak-search before adding refrigerant.
- **Check the outdoor fan motor and capacitor,** and the reversing valve solenoid coil.

A good quote names the specific failed part and test result, for example "coil sensor out of spec" or "valve won't shift on forced defrost." Be wary of a quote to simply "add refrigerant" with no leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light frost that clears every 30–90 min | Normal operation | None | None |
| Thick ice on top or one side, worse near roofline | Gutter or roof runoff | Fix or redirect gutter/downspout; switch to emergency heat until melted | Install drip shield or roof diverter if needed |
| Ice building up from the base, unit in snow | Poor drainage, unit not elevated | Clear snow around unit by hand | Install riser stand or wall brackets |
| Whole coil iced, weak airflow indoors | Dirty filter or restricted airflow | Replace filter, open registers | Clean coil, check blower and static pressure |
| Patchy ice, bottom of coil, long run times | Low refrigerant (leak) | Switch to emergency heat | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Ice keeps growing, never hear defrost | Defrost board or sensor fault | Switch to emergency heat | Force-defrost test; replace board or sensor |
| Fan keeps spinning during defrost, or fan not running | Fan motor, capacitor or relay | Switch to emergency heat | Replace motor, capacitor or relay |
| Ice plus little or no heat indoors | Reversing valve or solenoid | Switch to emergency heat | Replace solenoid coil or valve |
