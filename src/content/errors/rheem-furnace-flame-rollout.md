> **Emergency first:** If you smell gas, see scorching or smoke, or your CO alarm is sounding, follow these steps in order:
> 1. Leave the house now. Do not flip switches or use phones inside.
> 2. From outside, call your gas utility's emergency line or 911.
> 3. Do not go back in until responders say it is safe.
>
> If there is no gas smell and no CO alarm:
> 1. Set the thermostat to OFF.
> 2. Turn off the furnace power switch or breaker.
> 3. Open windows to ventilate.
> 4. Keep the furnace off until a licensed technician inspects it.

## What this code means

"Flame rollout" means the furnace's **rollout limit switch** opened. This is a heat-sensing safety switch mounted near the burners or burner box. It trips when flames or extreme heat spill out of the normal combustion path. When it opens, the control board shuts off the gas valve.

On many Rheem and Ruud boards, an open rollout switch shows up as one of these:
- a flashing LED limit or rollout fault code
- a furnace that runs the inducer but never lights

The exact flash pattern differs by board, so check the code chart on your blower door.

Most Rheem rollout switches are **manual-reset**. They do not reset on their own. Some boards also hold a lockout until power is cycled.

The trip itself is not the problem. It is a symptom. Something forced flame out of the burner area, and that something must be found before the furnace runs again.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted flue/vent.** Bird nests, debris, ice, a disconnected vent pipe, or a blocked chimney liner stop combustion gases from leaving. Flames then push back out of the burner area. This is the most common cause.
2. **Restricted heat exchanger passages.** Soot or debris buildup in the heat exchanger cells blocks flue gas flow. This often follows long-term poor combustion.
3. **Cracked or failed heat exchanger.** A crack lets blower air disturb the flames and push them out of the burner area. This also creates a carbon monoxide risk. It is common in older units.
4. **Inducer or draft problems.** A weak inducer, a damaged inducer wheel, or improper venting reduces draft.
5. **Combustion air or burner problems.** Any of these can cause lazy, rolling flames:
   - misaligned or dirty burners
   - an over-fired gas valve
   - wrong orifices, for example after an LP/natural gas conversion
   - not enough combustion air in a closed utility room
6. **Faulty rollout switch.** This is less common. A technician confirms it only after ruling out the causes above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do **not** reset the rollout switch or open the burner compartment. With the furnace powered off, you can safely do the following:

- **Thermostat:** Leave it set to OFF until the furnace has been inspected.
- **Breaker/switch:** Keep the furnace switch off. Do not cycle power repeatedly to "try again."
- **CO alarm:** Make sure working CO alarms are on each level of the home. Replace the batteries if needed.
- **Visible vents:** From outside, look at the furnace exhaust and intake terminations for any of these:
  - nests
  - snow or ice
  - leaves
  - a crushed or disconnected pipe

  You can clear loose snow or debris you can reach from the outside. Do not take apart any vent piping. Tell the technician what you found.
- **Air filter:** A badly clogged filter can contribute to overheating. Replacing it is fine, but it rarely causes rollout on its own.
- **Combustion air:** If the furnace sits in a closet, make sure any louvered doors or vents are open and not blocked by stored items.

Do not attempt a reset of a tripped rollout switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified technician will typically:

- Confirm which safety tripped, using the board flash code and a continuity check of the rollout circuit.
- Inspect for scorching, melted wires, or discoloration around the burner box.
- Check the vent system end to end for blockage, disconnection, improper slope, and correct sizing.
- Check inducer operation and pressure switch readings.
- Inspect the heat exchanger. This may involve a camera and a combustion analysis looking for CO and O2 changes when the blower starts.
- Check gas pressure, orifice size, and burner alignment against the rating plate.
- Watch flame behavior at startup and when the blower starts.
- Only after correcting the root cause, reset or replace the rollout switch and test.

If a quote is only "replace the rollout switch" with no vent or heat exchanger check, ask what caused the trip.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inducer runs, no ignition, rollout code | Rollout switch open | Shut off furnace, call technician | Find root cause, then reset or replace switch |
| Trip after snow or storm | Blocked vent termination | Clear visible outside snow or debris, keep furnace off | Verify venting, inspect, reset |
| Repeated trips, soot around burners | Restricted heat exchanger or bad combustion | Keep furnace off | Clean burners and exchanger, adjust combustion |
| Flames dance when blower starts | Cracked heat exchanger | Keep off, check CO alarms | Camera and combustion test, exchanger or furnace replacement |
| Trip after gas conversion or valve change | Over-firing or wrong orifices | None | Verify orifices and manifold pressure |
| Scorched wires near burner box | Severe rollout | Keep power off | Repair wiring, fix cause, full safety check |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic visit | $100–$200 (more for after-hours emergency calls) |
| Clear vent or flue blockage | $150–$400; chimney liner work $500–$2,500 |
| Rollout switch replacement | $150–$350 |
| Burner and heat exchanger cleaning, combustion tune-up | $150–$400 |
| Inducer motor replacement | $350–$800 |
| Gas valve or orifice correction | $200–$650 |
| Heat exchanger replacement | $1,500–$4,500+ (parts may be covered under Rheem's limited heat exchanger warranty, labor usually not) |
| Furnace replacement | $3,500–$7,500+ |
