## What this code means

"Leaking water" is not a stored error code. It's a symptom: water dripping, running down the wall, or spraying from the indoor head of a Trane ductless mini-split.

In cooling and dry mode, the indoor coil pulls moisture from the air. That water collects in a drain pan inside the head and leaves through a condensate hose. The hose either drains by gravity to the outside or feeds a small condensate pump. When water can't leave fast enough, the pan overflows and water comes out of the bottom or front of the unit.

On some installations, a condensate pump has a float switch that shuts the system off and may show a fault on the indoor unit. Whether a code appears, and what it is, depends on the model and on how the installer wired the pump. If your head is flashing lights, see Trane Mini-Split Blinking Light Error.

Nothing "resets" here. The leak continues until the cause is fixed.

This page covers the ductless indoor head only. Ducted Trane air conditioners and furnaces have separate pages, listed under Related codes.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged condensate drain line.** Algae, slime, dust and insect nests block the hose or the drain pan outlet. This is the most common cause by far, especially after the first humid weeks of the season.

2. **Dirty air filters, leading to coil icing.** Clogged mesh filters cut airflow, so the coil freezes. When the ice melts, the water overwhelms or misses the drain pan.

3. **Failed or unplugged condensate pump.** This only applies where a pump is installed, typically when the head is on an interior wall or the hose can't slope downhill. Common pump problems are:
   - a stuck float
   - a dead motor
   - a clogged reservoir
   - lost power

4. **Improper drain slope or a kinked, sagging hose.** The hose must run continuously downhill. Low spots trap water and debris, and a hose crushed behind the wall restricts flow. Leaks that start soon after installation usually point here.

5. **Indoor head not level, or tilted the wrong way.** The pan must tilt slightly toward the drain outlet. A bent mounting plate or a unit that has shifted lets water pool at the opposite end and spill.

6. **Dirty indoor coil or blower wheel.** Built-up grime can make water spit off the blower wheel ("spitting" from the louvers). It can also redirect condensate past the pan.

7. **Low refrigerant charge.** Low charge causes coil icing and then meltwater overflow. It's less common than the causes above, and it needs a licensed tech to find and repair the refrigerant leak.

8. **Cracked drain pan or a loose drain hose connection inside the head.** This is rarer. It's more likely on older units or after a previous service visit.

9. **Very high indoor humidity combined with a cold surface.** Sometimes the "leak" is condensation forming on the outside of the cabinet or on uninsulated refrigerant lines at the wall penetration. Poor line-set insulation is a technician fix.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Turn the system **off with the remote** before checking anything. If water is near an outlet or the unit's electrical cord, turn the mini-split's **breaker** off as well.

1. **Clean the filters.**
   - Lift the front panel; Trane heads hinge open without tools.
   - Slide out the mesh filters.
   - Rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry fully, and reinstall.
   - Replace any filter that is torn.
   - Do not remove any other covers.

2. **Look for ice.** If you can see frost on the coil behind the filters, leave the unit off, or run it in fan-only mode, until the ice is completely gone. That can take a few hours. Then restart in cooling mode with clean filters.

3. **Check the outdoor end of the drain hose.** Follow the hose to where it exits outside.
   - Make sure the end isn't buried in mulch, submerged in a puddle, frozen, or blocked by debris or a wasp nest.
   - Gently clear the opening.
   - A wet/dry vacuum held over the hose outlet for 1–2 minutes can often pull out a clog.
   - Don't push wire or rods up the hose; it can puncture it or dislodge the connection inside the head.

4. **Check for visible kinks.** Look at any exposed section of the hose, under the head or along the exterior wall. Straighten kinks and low sags you can reach without removing anything.

5. **If you have a condensate pump**, it is usually a small box near the head or in a line-set cover.
   - Confirm it's plugged in and the outlet has power.
   - If it's full and silent, it needs service.
   - Don't open sealed pump covers or alter its wiring.

6. **Check the head is visibly level.** Use a level on top of the cabinet. If it's obviously tilted or loose on the wall, stop using the unit and call a pro. Don't try to re-hang it yourself; the refrigerant lines and drain are attached behind it.

7. **Restart once and watch.** After cleaning the filters and clearing the drain outlet, run cooling for 30–60 minutes. If water still drips, shut the unit off and schedule service.

Do not open the indoor cabinet, remove the louver or chassis cover, or pour chemicals or bleach into the head. Any refrigerant work requires a certified technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically work through these steps:

- **Ask about the pattern.** Did the leak start right after install? Only on humid days? After a long period of heavy use? Does it come with weak cooling?
- **Inspect filters and coil.** They'll look for icing, dirt, or a matted coil face.
- **Test the drain.** The tech removes the front cover, pours a measured amount of water into the drain pan, and watches it drain. If it doesn't, they flush or blow out the line from the pan outlet. They may use nitrogen, a pump flush, or a drain-cleaning tool.
- **Check pan and connection.** They'll look for cracks, sludge, and a loose drain-hose coupling inside the head.
- **Check level and slope.** The tech verifies the head tilts toward the drain side and checks the mounting plate. They'll trace the hose route for traps or sags.
- **Test the condensate pump** (if fitted). This covers float operation, motor, check valve, discharge tubing, and the safety switch wiring.
- **Check the blower wheel.** They'll inspect it for buildup that causes spitting. A full deep clean may involve removing the blower or using a bag-style coil cleaning kit.
- **Check refrigerant only if icing or poor cooling suggests low charge.** This means measuring superheat/subcooling or pressures, then leak-searching before adding refrigerant.

**Red flags in a quote:**
- Adding refrigerant without finding the leak.
- Recommending a new system for what is clearly a clogged drain.
- Charging for "coil replacement" without showing evidence of coil damage or a confirmed refrigerant leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady drip from bottom of head in cooling mode | Clogged condensate drain | Clear hose exit outside; wet/dry vac at outlet | Flush pan outlet and line; treat for algae |
| Water plus weak airflow, frost visible on coil | Dirty filters causing icing | Clean filters; thaw before restarting | Clean coil; check charge if icing recurs |
| Leak started soon after installation | Bad drain slope or kinked hose; head not level | Check visible hose for kinks | Re-route drain with proper slope; re-level mounting plate |
| Unit shuts off or flashes; small pump box is full | Failed condensate pump or float switch | Confirm pump is plugged in and powered | Clean or replace pump; verify safety switch |
| Water spits from louvers with air | Dirty blower wheel or coil | Clean filters | Deep clean blower wheel and coil |
| Repeated icing and poor cooling even with clean filters | Low refrigerant charge | Turn off; no DIY | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Drips from outside of cabinet or the wall where lines enter | Condensation on uninsulated lines or cabinet | Lower indoor humidity; check visible insulation | Re-insulate line set; seal wall penetration |
| Leak from one end only, unit looks tilted | Head not level or drain pan cracked | Use a level to confirm; stop using | Re-level head or replace drain pan |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges. Labor rates vary by region, and Trane parts sourced through dealers may cost more than generic parts.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Cleaning filters, clearing drain outlet yourself | $0 |
| Replacement filters (model-specific) | $15–$50 |
| Service call / diagnostic | $90–$200 |
| Drain line clearing and flush | $100–$250 |
| Deep clean of indoor head (coil + blower wheel) | $200–$450 per head |
| Re-routing drain line or re-leveling head | $150–$400 |
| Condensate pump replacement (installed) | $200–$450 |
| Drain pan replacement | $200–$500 |
| Line-set insulation repair | $100–$300 |
| Refrigerant leak search, repair and recharge | $400–$1,200+ (more if the coil itself must be replaced) |
| Indoor coil replacement (out of warranty) | $800–$1,800+ |

Trane ductless systems typically carry a parts warranty when registered. Check whether pump, pan or coil parts are covered before you pay out of pocket. Labor coverage depends on the installer.
