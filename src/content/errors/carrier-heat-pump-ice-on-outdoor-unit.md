## What this code means

"Ice on outdoor unit" is not a numbered fault code. It is a visible symptom. The key question is whether you are looking at **normal frost** or **problem ice**.

**Normal frost**
- In winter, the outdoor coil runs colder than the outside air, so moisture freezes on it.
- Your Carrier heat pump clears this with a defrost cycle. It briefly reverses into cooling mode and the outdoor fan stops.
- During defrost, the indoor system may blow cooler air or turn on auxiliary heat.
- You may hear a "whoosh" and see steam rising from the unit. That is normal.

**Problem ice**
- Thick, clear or glassy ice that covers the coil and does not melt between cycles.
- Ice built up on the top panel or around the fan blades.
- A solid block of ice at the base of the unit.
- Ice that forms in summer cooling mode. This is always a fault.

This page covers ice from causes **other than** a total defrost failure, plus how to tell the cases apart. If the unit never enters defrost at all, also see "Frozen and not defrosting."

There is nothing to reset. Icing clears when the underlying cause is fixed. Once the ice melts, the system resumes normal operation.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Water dripping or pooling onto the unit.** This is the most common cause of heavy ice on otherwise healthy units. Examples include a leaking or overflowing gutter, a roof drip line directly above the unit, or a unit sitting too low so that defrost meltwater refreezes around the base. Carrier installation instructions call for the unit to be raised above expected snow and ice levels and kept away from roof runoff.
2. **Restricted airflow.** Snow drifts, leaves, plants or covers can block the outdoor coil. A clogged indoor air filter or closed supply registers can also lower coil temperatures and pressures. Indoor airflow problems matter most in summer icing.
3. **Defrost control problems.** Possible issues include:
   - A defrost thermostat or outdoor coil sensor that is failing or reading poorly
   - A defrost interval set too long for the local climate on time-temperature boards
   - A sensor that has slipped out of position on the tubing

   The unit still defrosts, but too late or too briefly to keep up.
4. **Low refrigerant charge (a leak).** Low charge makes the coil run abnormally cold. A common sign is ice on only part of the coil, often the bottom rows or near the refrigerant line connections.
5. **Outdoor fan motor or fan capacitor failure.** If the fan doesn't spin in heating mode, the coil frosts heavily within minutes.
6. **Reversing valve or defrost relay problems.** The board calls for defrost, but the system does not fully switch over, so the coil never warms. This overlaps with "Frozen and not defrosting."
7. **Metering device or restriction issues.** A sticking TXV, a blocked piston or a clogged filter-drier causes uneven, localized frosting. This is less common.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Watch a full cycle.** Note the time. Heat pumps typically defrost after 30 to 90 minutes of runtime. If you see steam and the fan stops for a few minutes, defrost is working.
- **Look above the unit.** Check for overflowing gutters, missing downspouts or roof drip lines aimed at the unit. Gutter repair or a diverter is a normal homeowner or roofer job and does not involve opening the unit.
- **Clear loose snow and debris** from around and on top of the unit by hand or with a soft broom. Keep about 2 feet of clearance. **Do not chip or scrape ice.**
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty, and make sure indoor supply and return registers are open and unblocked.
- **Check thermostat settings.** Confirm the thermostat is in Heat (or Auto) with a sensible setpoint. Replace batteries if the display is weak or blank.
- **Check the breakers.** Confirm the outdoor unit disconnect and the breakers are on. If a breaker has tripped, you may reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **If the unit is heavily iced,** switch to **Emergency Heat** and call a technician. Do not keep resetting the unit or running it encased in ice.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Visual inspection.** The technician looks at the ice pattern. Uniform heavy ice points toward defrost or airflow problems. Partial or bottom-row ice points toward charge or metering problems. Ice at the base points toward drainage or the unit's height.
- **Outdoor fan check.** They verify the fan motor and capacitor at the unit.
- **Defrost board test.** On time-temperature boards, they use the test pins to force a defrost. They then check the reversing valve shift, the fan stop and the defrost termination. They also confirm the interval setting is appropriate.
- **Sensor check.** They measure the defrost thermostat or coil sensor resistance and confirm it is mounted correctly. On Infinity and Greenspeed systems, they review the fault history and sensor readings on the Infinity control.
- **Refrigerant check.** They attach gauges and check pressures, superheat and subcooling against Carrier's charging charts. If the charge is low, they perform leak detection before recharging.
- **Airflow check.** They confirm indoor airflow, especially if icing occurs in cooling mode.

A sound quote names a specific failed part or measured condition. Be cautious of "just add refrigerant" without leak detection.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thin, even frost that clears with steam periodically | Normal operation | None | None |
| Ice heavy on the top or one side, under the roofline | Gutter or roof runoff | Fix the gutter or add a diverter; clear snow | Relocate or raise the unit if needed |
| Ice block around the base | Meltwater can't drain; unit too low | Clear snow around the base | Install a riser or stand; improve drainage |
| Entire coil iced; fan still runs | Defrost interval, sensor or board issue | Switch to Emergency Heat | Test the board, sensor and interval setting |
| Only the bottom rows iced | Low refrigerant charge | Switch to Emergency Heat | Find and repair the leak; recharge to spec |
| Fan not spinning; rapid frosting | Fan motor or capacitor failure | Turn the unit off; use Emergency Heat | Replace the capacitor or motor |
| Ice in summer cooling mode | Dirty filter, low airflow or low charge | Replace the filter; open registers; turn off the system to thaw | Check airflow and charge |
| Defrost starts but the coil stays iced | Reversing valve or defrost relay problem | Switch to Emergency Heat | Diagnose the valve, solenoid or board |

These are typical US ranges. Your price will depend on your region and your unit's warranty status.

| Unit riser or stand | $150 – $500 |
