## What this code means

"Not heating" is a symptom, not a single stored fault code. On American Standard heat pumps it means the outdoor unit is not delivering warm air in heat mode. The air may be cool or lukewarm, or the system may not run at all.

What you see depends on the model:

- **Communicating systems** (Platinum series, AccuLink/Link controls): these often log a specific alert on the thermostat or outdoor control. Write it down before resetting anything, because some alerts clear on power-cycle and others hold until the fault is fixed.
- **Conventional 24V systems** (most Silver and Gold units): these may show only an LED pattern on the defrost board inside the outdoor unit. A technician reads it.

Behavior also varies by model. Some faults, such as high- or low-pressure trips, auto-retry after a delay. Repeated trips can hold a lockout until power is cycled. Your installation manual and the wiring diagram inside the panel are the authority for your model.

Two things are normal and are **not** failures:

- **Defrost cycles.** The unit steams and air runs cool for a few minutes.
- **Lower supply-air temperature than a furnace.** In very cold weather the auxiliary heat strips should help out.

If the house cannot hold the thermostat setting, the cause is usually one of the items below.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat setup or settings.** The thermostat is in cool or off, or it is a replacement configured for the wrong reversing-valve (O/B) setting. American Standard heat pumps energize the reversing valve in cooling (O). A thermostat set to B produces cold air in heat mode.
2. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow.** Low airflow raises head pressure in heat mode. That can trip the high-pressure switch or reduce output.
3. **Outdoor coil iced over or a defrost problem.** A failed defrost control board, defrost sensor/thermostat or outdoor fan lets ice build until the unit stops heating.
4. **Failed run capacitor or contactor in the outdoor unit.** The indoor fan runs but the compressor or outdoor fan does not.
5. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** Output drops gradually, coils frost unevenly, or low-pressure trips occur.
6. **Reversing valve stuck or its solenoid coil failed.** The unit runs but blows cold air steadily in heat mode.
7. **Auxiliary/backup heat not working.** Tripped strip breakers, failed sequencers or open limits leave the home short of heat in deep cold even when the heat pump itself is fine.
8. **Compressor failure.** This is the least common cause and the most expensive.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** set it to heat (not auto or cool) and set the temperature 3–5°F above room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Recent thermostat swap:** if heating failed right after a new thermostat went in, check its installer menu for heat pump with O reversing valve. If you are unsure, call the installer.
- **Air filter:** replace it if it is dirty. Make sure the registers and return grilles are open and unobstructed.
- **Breakers:** check both the outdoor unit breaker and the air handler/electric heat breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **One reset:** turn the thermostat to off and switch off the outdoor disconnect or breaker for 5 minutes. Restore power, then return to heat. Do this only once.
- **Outdoor unit:** clear snow, leaves and debris from around the unit (keep about 2 feet of clearance) and confirm the panels are seated. Never chip ice off the coil, and do not pour hot water on it.
- **Watch for defrost:** steam with the fan stopped for up to about 10 minutes is normal.
- **Condensate:** check that the indoor condensate drain or pump is not overflowing. Some air handlers shut down on a float switch.
- **Stopgap:** if the house is getting cold, switch to **emergency heat** and call for service.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Read any stored alerts on communicating controls or the defrost board LED.
- Verify thermostat configuration and 24V signals (Y, O, W/aux) at the air handler and outdoor unit.
- Check line voltage, the contactor and the capacitor (measured in microfarads against the rating).
- Confirm compressor and outdoor fan amperage.
- Connect gauges or probes and measure pressures, superheat and subcooling in heat mode.
- Check the reversing valve by monitoring line temperatures and testing the solenoid coil.
- Test the defrost sensor, the board, and a forced defrost cycle.
- Check backup heat strips, sequencers, limits and the heat breakers.
- If refrigerant is low, perform a leak search. Expect leak detection to be quoted separately from the recharge.

A sound quote names the failed component and the measured reading that proves it. Be wary of "just add refrigerant" with no leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold air constantly in heat mode | Thermostat O/B setting or stuck reversing valve | Confirm heat mode and the heat pump setting on the thermostat | Test the reversing valve solenoid and replace the valve or coil |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Failed capacitor or contactor, or tripped breaker | Check and reset the breaker once | Test and replace the capacitor or contactor |
| Outdoor coil encased in ice for hours | Defrost board or sensor failure, outdoor fan fault, low charge | Clear snow and debris; use emergency heat | Diagnose the defrost circuit and fan; check the charge |
| Lukewarm air, long run times | Dirty filter, low refrigerant | Replace the filter | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Fine in mild weather, cold on frigid days | Aux heat strips not working | Check the air handler heat breakers | Test strips, sequencers and limits |
| Unit starts, then shuts off repeatedly | Pressure switch trips from airflow or charge | Replace the filter and open the registers | Check pressures, blower and charge |
| Loud hum, no compressor start | Bad capacitor or failing compressor | Turn the unit off and do not keep resetting | Capacitor or compressor diagnosis |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter or thermostat battery (DIY) | $0–$40 |
| Thermostat reconfiguration (service call) | $100–$250 |
| Run capacitor | $150–$400 |
| Contactor | $150–$350 |
| Defrost sensor | $150–$350 |
| Defrost control board | $300–$700 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350–$800 |
| Leak search and recharge (R-410A or R-454B) | $300–$1,500+ |
| Reversing valve solenoid coil | $150–$350 |
| Reversing valve replacement (brazing, evacuation, recharge) | $1,000–$2,500 |
| Heat strip element or sequencer | $200–$600 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800–$3,500+ |

Recharge cost depends on leak size and refrigerant price. Many units are under a 10-year registered parts warranty, so check yours before a big repair. Labor is usually not included in that warranty.
