## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a stored fault code. Most single-stage Amana heat pumps have no fault display. Some two-stage and communicating (ComfortBridge) models report specific fault codes through outdoor board LEDs or the thermostat. If yours shows a code, note it for the technician.

Unlike a straight AC, a heat pump cools only when its reversing valve shifts into cooling position. On Amana and Goodman equipment, the thermostat energizes the valve through the **O** terminal in cooling. So a heat pump that blows warm air while set to Cool often points to a valve, control or thermostat-configuration problem, not just low refrigerant.

There is no single lockout to clear. The compressor does have a short-cycle delay, typically a few minutes, before it restarts after power loss or a thermostat change.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat setting or configuration.** The thermostat is set to Heat or Auto with the wrong setpoint, or a replacement thermostat is configured for "B" (energize in heat). Amana needs "O" (energize in cool). The result is warm air whenever you call for cooling.
2. **Outdoor unit not running.** Common reasons are a tripped breaker or pulled disconnect, a failed run capacitor, or a pitted or stuck contactor. The indoor fan runs but the air isn't cold.
3. **Restricted airflow.** A clogged filter, closed registers, or a dirty indoor coil can cause the indoor coil to freeze, which reduces cooling.
4. **Dirty outdoor coil or failed outdoor fan motor.** This drives up head pressure, so the high-pressure switch (where fitted) or the compressor's internal overload trips.
5. **Reversing valve fault.** The solenoid coil may have failed, or the valve may be mechanically stuck in heat position. Typical signs are warm indoor air, a cold outdoor coil, and the unit otherwise running.
6. **Low refrigerant charge (a leak).** This causes weak cooling, iced lines, and possible low-pressure switch trips on equipped models.
7. **Defrost control board fault.** A failed board can hold the unit in defrost (heat) mode or fail to energize the outdoor unit. Behavior varies by board generation.
8. **Compressor failure.** The compressor may hum, trip on overload, or not run at all. This is the least likely cause and the most expensive.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set the mode to Cool and the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature, with the fan on Auto. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Wait out the delay:** After any change, give the system about 5 minutes before judging it.
- **Breakers and disconnect:** Check the indoor and outdoor breakers, and the disconnect box near the outdoor unit. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **One power reset:** Turn the thermostat off, cut power at the breaker for about 5 minutes, then restore it. Do this only once.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it is dirty. If the indoor lines or coil are visibly iced, set the thermostat to Off and the fan to On for a few hours to thaw before trying cooling again.
- **Registers and returns:** Open the supply vents and clear any furniture blocking return grilles.
- **Outdoor unit, from outside the cabinet only:** Clear leaves, grass, and anything stored within about 2 feet. Rinse the fins gently with a garden hose, with power off.
- **Condensate:** Check that the drain line isn't backed up. A full pan with a float switch can shut the system off.
- **Panels:** Make sure the air handler access panels are fully seated.

Don't open the outdoor electrical panel, touch the capacitor, or try to "push" the contactor. Capacitors hold a dangerous charge even with the power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- They will confirm the thermostat is configured with O/B set to O for cooling, and check for 24V on Y and O at the outdoor unit.
- They will check that the outdoor contactor pulls in. They will test the run capacitor with a meter (microfarad reading against its rating) and confirm amp draw on the compressor and fan.
- They will feel or measure line temperatures to see which mode the valve is in. They will test the reversing valve coil for continuity and voltage, then decide whether the coil or the valve body has failed.
- They will attach gauges or digital probes to check suction and liquid pressures, superheat, and subcooling against the Amana charging chart. If the charge is low, they should leak-search rather than just top it off.
- They will inspect the defrost board for stuck defrost and read any fault LEDs or ComfortBridge codes.
- They will measure compressor winding resistance and insulation to ground before condemning the compressor.
- They will check airflow: filter, blower operation, static pressure, and the condition of the indoor coil.

A quote that recommends a new valve or compressor without voltage, coil and pressure readings deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air in Cool mode, outdoor unit running | Thermostat O/B wrong, or reversing valve stuck or coil failed | Confirm Cool mode. Check the thermostat's O/B setting in its installer menu if the manual allows. | Test the valve coil and voltage on O. Replace the coil or valve. |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Breaker, capacitor, contactor, or no 24V signal | Check breakers and disconnect. Do one reset. | Test and replace the capacitor or contactor. Trace control wiring. |
| Outdoor unit hums but the fan doesn't spin | Failed capacitor or fan motor | Turn power off and call a technician. | Replace the capacitor or fan motor. |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Dirty filter, low airflow, or low charge | Replace the filter. Run fan only to thaw. | Find and repair the leak, recharge, and clean the coil. |
| Cools weakly and runs constantly | Dirty coils or low charge | Clear around the unit and hose off the outdoor coil. | Clean the coils, check the charge, and leak-search. |
| Unit stops and water appears near the air handler | Clogged condensate line or float switch tripped | Clear the drain line and empty the pan. | Clear the trap and verify float switch operation. |
| Runs briefly, then shuts off repeatedly | High head pressure, overload, or a pressure switch | Clean around the outdoor unit. Do not keep resetting. | Diagnose the fan, charge, switches, and compressor. |

| Reversing valve solenoid coil | $200–$450 |

Amana's parts warranties can cover major components, and some models carry a lifetime compressor or unit-replacement warranty if registered. Labor is usually not covered, so check your registration before approving a large repair.
