## What this code means

"Won't ignite" is not one specific fault code. It's a symptom: the thermostat calls for heat, but the burners never light. This page covers how to narrow that symptom down.

On an Amana hot surface ignition furnace, every startup follows the same sequence:

1. The thermostat calls for heat.
2. The inducer motor starts.
3. The pressure switch proves that the vent is drawing.
4. The igniter heats up and glows.
5. The gas valve opens.
6. The flame sensor proves that a flame is present.

A failure at any step stops ignition. The board records which step failed as an LED flash code.

Before you treat this as a generic problem, look through the sight glass on the blower door and count the flashes:

- **1 flash** points to ignition lockout.
- **3 flashes** point to the pressure switch staying open.
- **8 flashes** point to an igniter fault (on boards that use that code).

Each of those codes has its own page. This page is for when you don't know the code yet, or when the furnace simply sits silent or keeps cycling without lighting.

**Reset behavior:** Most Amana boards try to ignite several times, usually three or four. After that they go into a lockout, which typically clears itself after about one hour. You can also clear it by cutting power. Retry counts and timing differ by board, so check the label on your furnace's door.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

These follow Amana's own startup sequence, ordered by how often each one causes a no-ignition call.

1. **No call for heat reaching the furnace.** The thermostat has dead batteries, is set wrong, or the furnace power switch or breaker is off. The furnace stays completely silent.
2. **Pressure switch not closing.** This is often caused by a clogged condensate trap or drain on 90%+ efficiency models, or by a blocked intake or exhaust pipe. The inducer runs, but the igniter never glows. Usually shows as 3 flashes.
3. **Failed hot surface igniter.** The inducer runs and the pressure switch closes, but there's no glow. Igniters wear out, and they crack if anything touches them.
4. **Gas supply problem.** The gas valve switch is off, the gas meter is off, or a propane tank is empty. The igniter glows, but no flame appears, and the furnace locks out after its retries.
5. **Dirty flame sensor.** The burners light for a few seconds and then shut off. Homeowners often describe this as "won't ignite." Usually shows as 1 or 7 flashes.
6. **Blower door or safety circuit open.** An unseated blower door, an open rollout switch, or an open limit switch stops the sequence. The board shows the matching code.
7. **Failed gas valve or control board.** These are less common. A technician confirms them only after ruling out everything above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

**If you smell gas, skip this list.** Leave the house, don't operate any switches, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

If there's no gas smell, these checks are safe for a homeowner:

- **Thermostat:** Set it to HEAT, about 5°F above room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is blank or faint.
- **Power:** Make sure the furnace service switch (it looks like a light switch near the unit) is ON. Then check the breaker and reset it once if it has tripped.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it's dirty. A badly clogged filter can trip the limit switch and keep the furnace from relighting.
- **Blower door:** Make sure the lower panel is fully seated. The door switch won't let the furnace run if it isn't.
- **Vent pipes outside:** On high-efficiency models, check that the two PVC pipes outside aren't blocked by snow, ice, leaves, or nests.
- **Condensate line:** Check that the drain line or pump isn't kinked or overflowing.
- **Read the code:** Count the LED flashes through the sight glass and write down the number.
- **One reset:** Cut power for about 30 seconds, restore it, and watch one full startup attempt.

If the furnace still won't light after that single reset, stop and call a technician. Do not keep resetting it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing these steps helps you sanity-check a quote:

- **Read the stored fault codes** and watch a full startup sequence to see which step fails.
- **Inducer and pressure switch:** Check inducer operation, measure vent pressure with a manometer, and inspect the pressure switch hose, the condensate trap, and the vent piping.
- **Igniter:** Measure igniter resistance and confirm the board is sending it about 120V during the warm-up period.
- **Gas:** Check the gas supply, the inlet pressure, and the manifold pressure. Confirm the valve is getting 24V when it should open.
- **Flame signal:** Measure the flame signal in microamps, then clean or replace the flame sensor if the reading is weak.
- **Safety circuits:** Test the limit, rollout, and door switches for continuity.
- **Board or valve:** Replace the control board or gas valve only after the inputs to each have been verified.

Be wary of anyone who recommends a new board or gas valve without showing you these measurements first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Completely silent, no LED | No power or no call for heat | Check thermostat batteries, the service switch, and the breaker | Check the transformer, fuse, and wiring |
| Inducer runs, no glow, 3 flashes | Pressure switch not closing | Clear snow or debris from the outdoor vent and check the condensate line | Clean the trap, test the switch, inspect the inducer |
| Inducer runs, switch closes, no glow | Failed igniter | None. Do one reset only | Test and replace the igniter |
| Igniter glows, no flame, then lockout | Gas supply or gas valve | Confirm other gas appliances work. Check the propane level | Check gas pressure and replace the valve if needed |
| Lights for 2–10 seconds, then shuts off | Weak flame sensing | One reset | Clean or replace the flame sensor and check grounding |
| LED code for limit or rollout | Overheating or flame rollout | Replace the filter and open the supply registers | Find the root cause before resetting the rollout switch |
