## What this code means

**Safety first:** If you smell gas, see smoke or scorch marks, or your carbon monoxide alarm is sounding, get everyone out of the house now. From outside, call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line. Do not flip switches or try to restart the furnace.

If none of those warning signs are present, turn the furnace off at the thermostat and at its service switch. Open a few windows. Leave it off until a technician inspects it.

A flame rollout switch is a heat-sensing safety switch mounted near the burners. It trips when flames or very hot gases spill out of the burner box instead of being drawn into the heat exchanger and up the vent. When it opens, the control board shuts off the gas valve.

On most Trane and American Standard furnaces, the rollout switch is wired in series with the high-limit circuit. That means the board usually shows **4 flashes (open limit)** rather than a separate rollout code. Some older boards display it differently.

Most Trane rollout switches are **manual reset**. They stay open until someone presses the button on the switch, and cycling power will not clear them. Some models use auto-reset rollout devices. Either way, a rollout trip is never "just a glitch." The switch did its job, and the cause must be found.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted vent or flue.** Bird nests, debris, ice or snow, a disconnected vent pipe, or a poorly sized chimney liner on 80% models can trap combustion gases so flames push outward.
2. **Restricted heat exchanger or secondary heat exchanger.** Soot, rust scale or debris can partially block the passages. On 90%+ condensing models, the secondary heat exchanger is a known clog point.
3. **Dirty, rusted or misaligned burners.** Flames that don't go straight into the heat exchanger cells can roll out of the burner box.
4. **Weak or failing inducer draft.** A worn inducer motor or a damaged inducer wheel may move too little air to pull flames in, even if the pressure switch still closes.
5. **Overfiring.** Manifold gas pressure set too high, the wrong orifices, or an incorrect LP/natural gas conversion can produce flames too large for the combustion chamber.
6. **Cracked heat exchanger.** A crack disturbs airflow inside the cells and can cause rollout. It also creates a carbon monoxide risk.
7. **Inadequate combustion air or negative pressure.** An 80% furnace in a closet that's too tight, or strong exhaust fans pulling against the vent, can starve combustion.
8. **Faulty rollout switch (least common).** A weak or heat-fatigued switch can trip on its own. Technicians replace the switch only after ruling out the causes above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do not reset the rollout switch, remove the burner door, or restart the furnace repeatedly. These checks are safe because they happen outside the cabinet:

- **Thermostat:** Set it to OFF so the furnace doesn't keep calling for heat. Replace the batteries if the display is weak.
- **Power:** Turn off the furnace service switch (it looks like a light switch on or near the unit) and leave it off.
- **Exterior vent terminations:** On high-efficiency models with PVC pipes through a wall or roof, look from the ground for snow, ice, nests or debris. Clear snow by hand only if you can reach it safely from the ground. Do not take pipes apart.
- **Air filter:** Check it and replace it if it's dirty. A clogged filter is not a typical rollout cause, but the technician will want it clean.
- **Look without touching:** Note any scorch marks, melted wire insulation or soot around the furnace door. Tell your technician what you saw.
- **CO alarm:** Make sure you have a working carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Confirm the blink code and check which safety switch is open: rollout switch, high limit or auxiliary limit.
- Inspect the burner area for soot, scorching and heat damage to wiring.
- Inspect the vent system end to end for blockage, sag, disconnection and proper sizing. On 80% models, they will run a draft test.
- Check the inducer motor and wheel. Measure draft pressure with a manometer.
- Measure inlet and manifold gas pressure. Verify the orifice size and the fuel type setup.
- Pull the burners and inspect the heat exchanger. This often includes a camera inspection, and on condensing units, checking the secondary heat exchanger.
- Run a combustion analysis and watch the flame during startup for lifting or rolling.
- Only after fixing the cause, reset the rollout switch, or replace it if it tests faulty. Then run the furnace through several cycles while monitoring.

A quote that only says "replaced rollout switch," with no explanation of why it tripped, is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 flashes, furnace off, trouble began after a snowstorm | Blocked exterior vent | Turn the furnace off; clear snow from the vent termination only if reachable from the ground | Inspect the vent, verify draft, reset the rollout switch |
| Soot or scorch marks near the burner door | Restricted heat exchanger or flue | Leave the furnace off; check your CO alarm | Clean or inspect the heat exchanger, clear the flue, check combustion |
| Flames looked lazy, yellow or wandering before shutdown | Dirty or misaligned burners, or poor combustion air | Leave the furnace off | Clean and align burners, check combustion air supply |
| Rollout trips again soon after a reset | Overfiring, cracked heat exchanger or weak inducer | Do not reset again; call for service | Gas pressure test, heat exchanger inspection, inducer test |
| CO alarm sounding or gas smell | Combustion leak or gas leak | Leave the house; call 911 or the gas utility from outside | Full safety inspection before restart |
| No visible cause, single trip | Weak rollout switch, or an intermittent blockage | Leave the furnace off; replace the filter | Test the switch, rule out other causes, replace the switch |
