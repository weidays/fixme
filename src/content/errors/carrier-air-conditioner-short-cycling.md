## What this code means

"Short cycling" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. Your Carrier air conditioner starts cooling, shuts off within a few minutes, and then starts again. The house never feels evenly cool or dry.

On many Carrier outdoor units, a safety device stops the compressor mid-cycle. That device might be the high-pressure switch, the low-pressure switch, or the compressor's internal overload. The control board then holds a time delay of roughly 5 minutes before allowing a restart.

On many boards, repeated trips within one thermostat call lead to a soft or hard lockout:

- **Soft lockout:** often clears on its own after a set period.
- **Hard lockout:** may hold until power is cycled.

The exact trip count, timing and reset behavior vary by model and control board. Two-stage and variable-speed Infinity systems record the event in the Infinity or Côr service menu. Other units show it as an LED flash code on the outdoor control.

This page covers the cooling side. For heat-mode cycling, see the heat pump and furnace short-cycling pages.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat problems.** The thermostat may sit in a bad location, such as direct sun, near a supply register, or above a lamp or TV. Other possibilities are weak batteries, a wrong cycles-per-hour setting, or a loose thermostat wire. A dead or flickering display can drop the cooling call.
2. **Restricted airflow from a dirty filter or closed registers.** Low airflow can make the evaporator run too cold. This can trip the low-pressure switch or freeze-protection logic and stop the compressor early.
3. **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil.** Debris, cottonwood, dryer lint or tight shrubbery trap heat. Head pressure then climbs until the high-pressure switch or compressor overload opens.
4. **Incorrect refrigerant charge.** A low charge from a leak commonly trips the low-pressure switch. An overcharge can trip on high pressure. Either way, the unit runs briefly and shuts down.
5. **Oversized equipment.** A unit too large for the home cools the air near the thermostat quickly and shuts off before a full cycle. This is a design issue rather than a failure. It is common when a replacement was sized by tonnage instead of a Manual J load calculation.
6. **Failing electrical components.** A weak run capacitor, pitted contactor, or overheating compressor can trip on internal overload. A failing outdoor fan motor also causes this, because the condenser can't reject heat.
7. **Faulty pressure switch or control board.** A switch can open prematurely even when pressures are normal. A defect in the outdoor or indoor control can also interrupt the call. Both are less common.
8. **Condensate safety switch trips.** A clogged drain line raises water in the pan or secondary pan. A float switch then cuts power to the cooling circuit and may restore it as water drains, which mimics short cycling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

**Thermostat**
- Replace the batteries.
- Confirm the thermostat is set to Cool with a reasonable setpoint.
- Note whether sun or a nearby supply vent hits the thermostat.
- If your model has a cycle-rate or "cycles per hour" setting, check that it matches the owner's manual default. Don't change installer settings you don't understand.

**Air filter**
- Replace a dirty filter with the correct size.
- Avoid very high-MERV filters unless your system was designed for them.

**Vents and registers**
- Open all supply registers.
- Clear furniture and rugs off returns.

**Outdoor unit (view only)**
- Look from outside without removing panels.
- Clear leaves, grass clippings and plants at least 2 feet away.
- Gently rinse the outside of the fins with a garden hose on low pressure, with power OFF at the outdoor disconnect.

**Condensate line**
- Check the indoor drain pan area and any secondary pan for standing water.
- If accessible, clear the drain line opening, for example with a wet/dry vacuum at the outdoor end.

**Breaker and reset**
- Confirm the AC and air handler breakers are on.
- If the unit appears locked out, you may cycle power **once**: turn off the thermostat, switch off the breaker for 5 minutes, then restore.
- If short cycling returns, stop and call a technician. Repeated resets can damage the compressor.

**Panels**
- Make sure the indoor unit's access panels are fully seated, since a loose door switch can drop power.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will do most of the following:

- **Read stored faults.** They check the outdoor control LED flash codes, or the Infinity or Côr service menu fault history. This shows whether high pressure, low pressure, or another fault stopped the compressor.
- **Watch a full cycle.** They time on and off periods and confirm whether the thermostat call drops (a thermostat or wiring issue) or the outdoor unit stops while the call is still present (a safety trip).
- **Measure refrigerant pressures, superheat and subcooling** against Carrier's charging chart for your model and metering device. If the charge is low, a leak search should come before any recharge.
- **Test electrical components.** This includes capacitor microfarads, contactor condition, compressor amperage and outdoor fan operation.
- **Check airflow.** They inspect the filter, blower speed, static pressure and evaporator coil cleanliness.
- **Inspect the condenser coil** and clean it if needed.
- **Test the pressure switches and condensate float switch** for proper operation.
- **Evaluate equipment sizing** if everything tests fine. They compare tonnage to a load calculation, or to the home's square footage and insulation as a rough check.

Be cautious of a quote that only "adds refrigerant" without a leak check, or recommends a new system without any measurements.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat display blinks or goes blank, unit stops | Weak thermostat batteries or loose wiring | Replace batteries | Check thermostat wiring and terminals, replace thermostat if faulty |
| Unit runs 2–5 min, house reaches setpoint fast but feels clammy | Oversized system or thermostat near a supply vent | Note run times, block sun on thermostat | Load calculation, relocate thermostat, consider correct-size or variable-speed system |
| Shuts off on hot afternoons, outdoor unit very hot | Dirty condenser coil or failing outdoor fan (high-pressure trip) | Clear debris, gently hose fins with power off | Coil cleaning, fan motor and capacitor test, pressure check |
| Short cycles with weak airflow, ice on lines | Dirty filter or low charge (low-pressure trip) | Replace filter, open registers | Measure charge, leak search and repair, check blower |
| Stops and restarts, water in the drain pan | Clogged condensate line tripping float switch | Clear the drain line, empty the pan | Flush drain, verify float switch, fix pan slope |
| Compressor hums, clicks off, restarts after a delay | Weak capacitor, failing contactor, or compressor overload | One power-cycle reset only | Test and replace capacitor or contactor, check compressor amps |
| Outdoor LED flashing a fault code | Pressure switch trip or board fault | Record the flash pattern | Read the code against the unit's wiring diagram, repair root cause |
