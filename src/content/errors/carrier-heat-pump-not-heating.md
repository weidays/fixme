## What this code means

"Not heating" is a symptom, not a Carrier fault code. It means the heat pump runs, or tries to run, in heat mode but the house doesn't warm up, or the system doesn't start at all on a heat call.

There are two very different situations, and they have different causes:

- **The outdoor unit runs but the air is cool.** This points to defrost, reversing-valve, refrigerant or airflow problems.
- **The outdoor unit doesn't run at all.** This points to power, thermostat, control-board or compressor/capacitor problems.

How the system reports faults depends on the model:

- **Infinity / Greenspeed systems** usually log a specific fault (for example, a pressure, temperature or communication fault) on the Infinity or Côr wall control. Read and note it before calling.
- **Non-communicating Comfort / Performance units** may show a flash code on the defrost board inside the outdoor unit. Reading that board is technician work.

Lockout behavior also varies by board. Many Carrier boards hold a lockout after repeated high- or low-pressure trips until power is cycled. Some faults clear automatically after a delay. Check your model's service literature.

If your backup heat is running nonstop, that is a related but different problem. See "Carrier Heat Pump Aux Heat On Constantly."

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat setting or setup.** The thermostat may be in the wrong mode, set below room temperature, or have dead batteries. A newly installed non-Carrier thermostat may be configured for the wrong reversing-valve logic. Carrier energizes the reversing valve in cooling, so it uses O, not B.
2. **Normal defrost cycle or cold-weather limits.** The unit pauses heating during defrost. Below roughly 25–35°F, a standard heat pump's output drops, and the system relies on aux heat. This is expected, not a failure.
3. **Restricted airflow.** A clogged filter, closed registers, or a blocked outdoor coil (snow, ice, leaves, a too-close shrub) cuts heat output and can trip pressure limits.
4. **Tripped breaker or outdoor disconnect.** If the outdoor unit has no power, only the indoor fan or aux strips run.
5. **Defrost control problems.** A failed defrost board, defrost thermostat or coil sensor can leave the outdoor coil iced over solid.
6. **Failed run capacitor or contactor.** The outdoor fan or compressor won't start or hums and stops.
7. **Stuck or failed reversing valve, or a failed solenoid coil.** The unit runs but delivers cool air.
8. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Heat output is weak, and you may see icing or low-pressure lockouts.
9. **Compressor failure.** This is the least likely cause and the most expensive.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Thermostat.** Confirm it is set to HEAT (not COOL, OFF or AUTO with a wrong setpoint) and that the setpoint is a few degrees above room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
2. **Wait out defrost.** If the outdoor unit is steaming and its fan has stopped, give it 10–15 minutes.
3. **Air filter.** Replace it if it's dirty, and make sure it's installed with the airflow arrow pointing toward the blower.
4. **Registers and returns.** Open supply registers and clear furniture or rugs away from returns.
5. **Outdoor unit surroundings.** Gently brush off loose snow and leaves from the outside of the unit and keep about 2 ft of clearance. Do not chip ice off the coil. You'll bend the fins. If it's iced solid, switch to EM HEAT and call.
6. **Breakers and disconnect.** Check the breakers for both the indoor and outdoor units. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.
7. **One reset.** If the unit appears locked out, turn the thermostat off, wait 5 minutes, then return it to HEAT. Do this **only once**. Repeated resets can damage the compressor.
8. **Condensate line.** In heat mode the indoor coil drains little, but a full float-switch pan can still shut the system down. Check that the drain line isn't blocked.
9. **Panels.** Make sure the indoor unit's access panels are fully seated.

While you wait for service, use EM HEAT to stay warm.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Use this list to sanity-check what a technician does and quotes:

- Reads stored fault codes from the Infinity/Côr control or the defrost-board LED.
- Verifies thermostat wiring and configuration (O terminal, staging, aux lockout settings).
- Checks line and control voltage at the outdoor contactor, and tests the run capacitor's microfarad rating.
- Measures supply/return temperature split. A typical heat pump rise is roughly 20–30°F, depending on conditions.
- Connects gauges or probes to check suction/discharge pressures, superheat and subcooling for charge. If the charge is low, they leak-search before adding refrigerant.
- Energizes the reversing valve solenoid and checks for a proper changeover. Confirms the coil is getting voltage and that the valve shifts.
- Tests the defrost sensor resistance and forces a defrost cycle from the board.
- Checks compressor amperage and winding resistance before condemning the compressor.

A quote for a compressor or reversing valve should be backed by these measurements, not by a guess.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing runs, blank thermostat | Dead batteries, tripped breaker | Replace batteries; reset breaker once | Diagnose transformer, fuse or wiring |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Outdoor breaker/disconnect off, contactor or capacitor fault | Check outdoor breaker once | Test and replace contactor or capacitor |
| Outdoor unit steams briefly, fan stopped | Normal defrost | Wait 10–15 minutes | None needed |
| Outdoor coil iced solid for hours | Defrost board or sensor fault, low charge | Switch to EM HEAT; clear loose snow | Test defrost controls; check charge |
| Unit runs, air is cool | Thermostat O/B setup, stuck reversing valve, low charge | Verify HEAT mode and setpoint | Check O/B wiring, test valve, leak-search |
| Weak heat, long run times | Dirty filter, blocked outdoor coil, low charge | Replace filter; clear around unit | Clean coil; measure charge |
| Outdoor unit hums, then trips off | Failed capacitor or compressor issue | Leave it off after one reset | Test capacitor and compressor |
| Fault displayed on Infinity control | Model-specific fault | Write the code down | Diagnose per Carrier service manual |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges and include parts and labor.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Thermostat batteries / filter (DIY) | $0–$40 |
| Service call / diagnostic | $90–$250 |
| Thermostat reconfiguration or rewiring | $100–$250 |
| Run capacitor | $150–$400 |
| Contactor | $150–$350 |
| Defrost sensor or thermostat | $150–$400 |
| Defrost control board | $300–$700 |
| Infinity/Greenspeed outdoor control board | $600–$1,500 |
| Reversing valve solenoid coil | $150–$350 |
| Reversing valve replacement | $900–$2,000 |
| Leak search + repair + recharge | $400–$1,800+ (depends on refrigerant type and leak location) |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800–$3,500+ |
| Outdoor coil replacement | $1,500–$3,000+ |

Check your warranty before approving major work. Many registered Carrier units carry a 10-year parts warranty, but labor is usually not covered. On older units, a compressor or coil failure often makes replacing the system the better value.
