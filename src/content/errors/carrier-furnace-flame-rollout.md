## What this code means

**If you smell gas:**

1. Leave the house now and take everyone with you.
2. Do not touch the thermostat, the furnace service switch, light switches, or any other electrical switch or device on the way out. Any of them can make a spark.
3. Once you are outside and away from the house, call your gas utility's emergency line or 911.

**If your CO alarm is sounding, or you see scorch marks or flame outside the burners, and you do not smell gas:**

1. Set the thermostat to OFF.
2. Turn off the furnace's service switch.
3. Leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from outside.

If you have any doubt about whether you smell gas, skip the shutdown steps and leave.

If none of those signs are present, still leave the furnace off and call a technician. Treat this as an emergency.

Every Carrier gas furnace has one or more **flame rollout switches** mounted on the burner box. Each is a heat-sensitive safety switch. It opens if flame or very hot gas "rolls out" of the burner area instead of being drawn through the heat exchanger and up the vent.

When it opens, the control board shuts off the gas valve. On many boards, the inducer and blower keep running for a while. The board then flashes a limit-circuit code, usually **Code 33**. Some boards flash **Code 13** if the fault persists.

The switch is **manual reset**. It will not close again on its own, and cycling power does not reset it. A rollout trip almost always means something is blocking or misdirecting combustion gases. Assume there is a real problem, not a nuisance trip, until a technician proves otherwise.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

Carrier's guidance lists these causes without ranking them. The order below puts blocked vents first because field experience treats them as at least as common as a restricted heat exchanger.

1. **Blocked or undersized vent or flue.** Possible causes include bird or animal nests, ice or snow on PVC terminations, a disconnected or sagging flue pipe, or a chimney blockage on 80% units.
2. **Restricted heat exchanger.** On 90%+ condensing models, the secondary heat exchanger can clog with debris or corrosion. On 80% models, the primary cells can fill with soot or rust scale. Either way, gases back up into the burner area.
3. **Burner problems.** Misaligned, rusted or dirty burners or crossovers can push flame sideways out of the burner box.
4. **Cracked or failed heat exchanger.** A crack disturbs the airflow and draft. This is a serious finding and often leads to replacing the furnace on older units.
5. **Incorrect gas pressure or orifices.** Overfiring from high manifold pressure, wrong orifices, or a botched propane/natural gas conversion can cause rollout.
6. **Weak draft.** A degraded inducer can produce insufficient draft. This often shows up together with pressure-switch codes.
7. **Failed rollout switch.** A switch that is weak or heat-fatigued can trip on its own. Technicians should rule out every other cause before blaming the switch.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do these only if there is **no gas smell, no CO alarm, and no visible scorching**:

- **Thermostat:** Set it to OFF so the furnace does not keep trying to restart. Do not try to force heat on.
- **Breaker or service switch:** Leave the furnace powered off until the technician arrives. Do not repeatedly cycle power to clear the code.
- **Visible vent terminations:** Outside, look at the intake and exhaust pipes (PVC on condensing units) or the chimney cap. Check for nests, snow, ice or debris. If ice or snow is blocking them, clear it gently by hand, then tell the technician about it. Do not restart the furnace yourself.
- **Condensate line (condensing models):** If water is pooling or the visible drain tube is obviously kinked, note it for the technician.
- **CO alarm:** Make sure you have a working carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas and near the furnace.
- **Front panels:** Make sure the panels are seated, but **do not open the burner compartment or press the rollout reset button**.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent technician should:

- **Read the code:** Read the stored fault history on the board to confirm Code 33 or 13 and how often it has occurred.
- **Inspect the switches and area:** Check which rollout switch tripped, and look for scorched wiring, discolored burner box paint, or melted insulation.
- **Inspect the vent:** Check the full vent and flue path, and the chimney liner on 80% units, for blockage, slope and connections.
- **Inspect the burners:** Remove and check burners for alignment, rust and soot. They should be cleaned if needed.
- **Inspect the heat exchanger:** Use a camera on the primary and, on condensing units, the secondary heat exchanger. Look for blockage or cracks.
- **Measure gas pressure:** Measure inlet and manifold gas pressure against the rating plate, and verify the orifice size for your fuel type.
- **Check draft:** Verify inducer operation and pressure readings.
- **Check combustion and CO:** Run a combustion and CO test after repairs.
- **Reset and observe:** Only after the cause is fixed should they reset the switch and watch a full heating cycle for flame lift or rollout.

**Be wary of a quote that only says "reset rollout switch" or "replace switch" with no explanation of what caused the trip.**

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Code 33 or 13, no heat, furnace won't relight | Rollout switch open | Thermostat OFF, leave unit off, call a technician | Find the cause, then manually reset the switch |
| Rollout after snowstorm, condensing unit | Iced or blocked PVC vent termination | Clear visible snow or ice outside, do not restart | Verify the vent, reset the switch, test combustion |
| Soot or scorch marks near burners | Restricted or cracked heat exchanger, dirty burners | Shut down, call a technician, check the CO alarm | Camera inspection, cleaning or heat exchanger replacement |
| Repeated trips, older 80% furnace | Blocked chimney or flue, rusted heat exchanger | Keep off, check the chimney cap from the ground | Flue and chimney inspection, heat exchanger evaluation |
| Rollout after propane conversion or gas work | Wrong orifices or high manifold pressure | Keep off | Correct the orifices and set gas pressure |
| Gas smell | Possible gas leak | Leave now, touch no switches, call the gas utility or 911 from outside | Full safety inspection before any restart |
| CO alarm, no gas smell | Possible combustion leak | Thermostat OFF, service switch off, leave and call 911 or the gas utility from outside | Full safety inspection before any restart |
| Trips with no other findings | Weak rollout switch | None | Replace the switch only after every other cause is ruled out |
