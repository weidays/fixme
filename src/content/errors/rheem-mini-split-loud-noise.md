## What this code means

"Loud noise" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. Rheem mini-splits do not display a specific error when they become noisy. If your unit also shows a flashing light or code, see Rheem Mini-Split Blinking Light, because a stored fault may explain the noise.

This page covers ductless systems specifically: the wall-mounted or cassette indoor head and its matching inverter outdoor unit. For central systems, see the separate Rheem AC Loud Noise and Rheem Heat Pump Loud Noise pages.

Noise does not trigger a lockout and does not auto-reset. It continues until the cause is fixed. The first step is to tell apart normal operating sounds from mechanical problems:

- **Usually normal:** light ticking as plastic warms or cools, refrigerant hiss or gurgle, and a whoosh or steam burst during heat-mode defrost.
- **Not normal:** grinding, screeching, banging, metallic rattling, loud buzzing, or vibration you can feel through the wall.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

Rheem does not publish an official troubleshooting order for noise. The ranking below reflects how often each cause shows up in ductless service calls.

1. **Dirty filter or debris in the indoor blower wheel.** Restricted airflow causes whistling and a louder fan. Dust buildup on the cross-flow blower wheel can unbalance it and cause a wobble or thump.
2. **Loose panels, front cover, louvers or mounting.** Rattling or buzzing often comes from a loose front panel, a loose outdoor cabinet panel, or an indoor head that has shifted on its wall bracket.
3. **Outdoor unit vibration or debris.** Leaves, sticks or ice contacting the fan blade cause loud ticking or scraping. Worn or missing rubber isolation pads, or an unlevel pad or wall bracket, let vibration carry into the house.
4. **Line set contact.** Refrigerant lines touching the wall, the siding or each other can transmit compressor vibration as a hum or rattle.
5. **Failing fan motor bearings, indoor or outdoor.** These cause grinding, squealing or a growling hum that gets worse over time.
6. **Cracked or damaged fan blade or blower wheel.** These cause rhythmic thumping or knocking.
7. **Compressor or inverter problems.** Loud knocking, rattling at startup, or a harsh buzz from the outdoor unit can mean internal compressor wear, a loose compressor mount, or inverter board issues. These usually cause performance problems too.
8. **Refrigerant issues.** A persistent loud hiss or bubbling, especially with poor cooling or heating, can indicate low charge or a restriction. See Rheem Mini-Split Not Cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Identify the source.** Note whether the noise comes from the indoor head or the outdoor unit, what it sounds like, and when it happens (startup, constant, or heat mode only).
- **Clean the filter.** Turn the unit off with the remote, lift the front panel, slide out the mesh filters, rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry fully, and reinstall.
- **Seat the front panel.** Make sure the indoor front cover clicks fully closed and the filters sit flat in their slots.
- **Check the remote.** Confirm the fan is not set to Turbo or High, which is louder by design. Replace the batteries if the unit is not responding properly.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** With the system off at the breaker or disconnect, remove leaves, sticks and snow from around and on top of the unit, from the outside only. Keep about 2 feet of clearance. Do not reach into the fan or remove panels.
- **Check visible exterior panels.** If an outdoor service panel is visibly loose, press it back into place without removing screws or opening it.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Gurgling from the indoor head can come from a partly blocked drain line. Make sure the outdoor end of the drain hose is clear and not kinked.
- **Reset once.** Turn the breaker off for 5 minutes, then back on. If the noise returns, stop and call a pro. Do not keep power-cycling the unit.

If you hear grinding, screeching or banging, turn the system off and leave it off until it is inspected.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified HVAC technician will typically:

- **Listen to the unit running** in cooling, heating and different fan speeds to isolate the source, often with the panels removed.
- **Check the indoor blower wheel** for dirt, imbalance or cracks, and spin the motor by hand (power off) to feel for bearing drag.
- **Inspect the outdoor fan blade and motor** for blade damage, a loose hub, or worn bearings.
- **Check compressor mounts, isolation grommets and pads**, and level the unit.
- **Reposition or secure the line set** and add insulation or clamps where lines contact surfaces.
- **Read any stored fault codes** from the indoor and outdoor boards. Code availability varies by model.
- **Measure pressures and temperatures** if refrigerant noise is suspected, and leak-check if the charge is low.
- **Test the compressor and inverter board** electrically if outdoor knocking or buzzing persists.

A good quote will name the specific failed part, not just say "noise repair."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whistling or loud airflow from indoor head | Dirty filter | Clean filters | Clean blower wheel and coil if heavily soiled |
| Rattle or buzz from indoor head | Loose front panel or filter | Reseat panel and filters | Tighten mounting bracket and check internal parts |
| Rhythmic thump from indoor head | Dirty or cracked blower wheel | Turn off, clean filter | Clean or replace blower wheel |
| Grinding or squealing (indoor or outdoor) | Failing fan motor bearings | Turn system off | Replace fan motor |
| Scraping or ticking from outdoor unit | Debris or ice hitting fan | Clear debris from outside with power off | Inspect and replace damaged fan blade |
| Vibration felt through wall | Line set contact or worn pads | Note when it happens | Secure lines, replace isolation pads, level unit |
| Loud knock or rattle from outdoor unit at startup | Compressor mount or internal wear | Turn off and call | Inspect mounts, diagnose compressor and inverter |
| Gurgling from indoor head | Partly blocked condensate drain | Check outdoor drain hose end | Clear drain line or pump |
| Constant loud hiss with poor cooling | Refrigerant leak or restriction | Turn off and call | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Whoosh or steam from outdoor unit in winter | Normal defrost | None needed | None unless it is excessive |
