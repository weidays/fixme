## What this code means

"Clicking but not starting" is not a flash code — it's the sound a Trane furnace makes when the control board receives a call for heat but the startup sequence stalls before ignition. The clicking is almost always a relay on the integrated furnace control (IFC) energizing and dropping out, or the draft inducer motor attempting (and failing) to spin up.

On a normal cycle, a Trane furnace should: energize the inducer, prove the pressure switch, glow the igniter, open the gas valve, and light. A repeated click with no follow-through means the sequence is breaking down at the very beginning — the board can call for the next step but something isn't responding.

Because there's no single flash code tied to this symptom, the diagnosis depends on **where** the sequence stops. Watch the LED on the control board through the sight window: if it settles into a specific flash pattern (2, 3, 4 flashes, etc.), follow that code's page instead. If it just clicks with no consistent pattern, this page applies. Behavior varies by board revision, so confirm the LED reading before assuming a part is bad.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Weak or failing transformer / low control voltage.** The board's relays need a steady 24V to pull in and stay in. A tired transformer or a marginal 24V circuit causes relays to chatter — click in, drop out, click again — without completing the sequence.
2. **Failing relay on the integrated furnace control (IFC).** The inducer or gas-valve relay contacts can weld, pit, or fail to hold. You hear the coil energize (the click) but the contacts don't pass power to the next component.
3. **Stalled or failing draft inducer motor.** A worn inducer bearing or bad inducer capacitor can leave the motor buzzing/clicking without spinning. The pressure switch never proves, so the sequence halts.
4. **Loose or corroded low-voltage connections.** Poor connections at the board, transformer, or thermostat wiring create intermittent voltage that makes relays click without a clean pull-in.
5. **Thermostat wiring / call-for-heat glitch.** An intermittent thermostat or R-to-W connection can send a flickering call, causing the board to click on and off.
6. **Control board fault.** After the above are ruled out, a failed IFC itself can produce clicking with no start.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do — none involve opening the sealed cabinet or touching wiring:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to HEAT and the setpoint is well above room temperature. If it's a battery model, install fresh batteries — a dying thermostat can send a weak or flickering call for heat.
- **Air filter:** A severely clogged filter can trip limits and interrupt normal operation. Replace it if it's dirty.
- **Breaker / furnace switch:** Turn the furnace breaker OFF for 30 seconds, then back ON. This is your **one reset** — cycle power once. Also confirm the wall switch (often looks like a light switch near the furnace) is ON.
- **Visible vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return registers aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate line (high-efficiency models):** If the drain is clogged and the pan is full, a float switch can interrupt operation. Check for standing water and a blocked drain line.

If it still clicks and won't start after one power cycle, **stop and call a technician.** Repeated resets on a no-start unit are not safe.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

1. **Read the control board LED** through the sight glass to catch any flash code that appears just before or during the click.
2. **Measure transformer output and 24V control voltage** under load to confirm the board has stable power for its relays.
3. **Verify the call for heat** at the R and W terminals and check thermostat wiring for intermittent connections.
4. **Watch the startup sequence step by step** — does the inducer relay click and the inducer spin? Does the pressure switch close?
5. **Test the draft inducer motor and its capacitor** if the inducer is buzzing or not spinning.
6. **Inspect relay contacts on the IFC** and check for chattering or welded contacts.
7. **Confirm the board itself** only after supply voltage, wiring, and the inducer are verified good.

A good quote names the specific failed component and the voltage or continuity reading behind that conclusion — not just "replace the board."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rapid clicking, no inducer spin | Weak transformer / low 24V | Cycle power once; replace thermostat batteries | Measure transformer output, replace transformer |
| Single click then nothing | Failing IFC relay | Cycle power once | Test relay contacts, replace control board |
| Buzzing/click, inducer won't turn | Stalled draft inducer or bad capacitor | None safe | Test and replace inducer motor/capacitor |
| Intermittent clicking, random | Loose low-voltage wiring | Confirm thermostat is on HEAT | Tighten/repair board and thermostat connections |
| Click stops when thermostat replaced | Faulty thermostat call | Replace batteries; check settings | Test R-W call, replace thermostat |
| Clicking with standing water present | Clogged condensate / float switch | Check and clear visible drain blockage | Clear drain, test float switch |
