## What this symptom means

"Ice on outdoor unit" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. During heating, an American Standard heat pump pulls heat from outdoor air. That makes the outdoor coil colder than the air around it, so moisture freezes on the coil. A defrost control briefly reverses the system to melt the frost.

Most American Standard heat pumps in service today use a demand defrost control. It reads outdoor-air and coil sensors and starts defrost only when frost has built up, so the time between cycles varies with the weather. Very old units may use a time-temperature defrost board instead, which defrosts on a fixed schedule, typically every 30 to 90 minutes, while the coil is cold.

**Normal:** light white frost that clears during defrost. You'll often see steam and hear a whoosh when the reversing valve shifts.

**Not normal:** heavy clear ice, a solid ice block at the base, ice on the fan or top grille, or a coil that never clears.

This page covers icing caused by water, airflow and weather, and how to tell normal frost from a real problem. If the unit clearly never enters defrost, see the separate page American Standard Heat Pump Frozen, Not Defrosting: Fixes.

**Reset behavior:**
- No standalone ice code exists to reset on most models.
- On AccuLink communicating systems, any related alert clears or persists according to that system's own logic.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Water dripping onto the unit from above.** A leaking or overflowing gutter, roof edge, or downspout drips onto the top. This water refreezes faster than defrost can melt it, and it is the most common cause of heavy top-down ice.
2. **Poor drainage or snow buildup at the base.** Defrost meltwater can't drain away and refreezes into a block around the bottom coil. Typical reasons are a unit sitting too low, a sunken pad, or drifted snow.
3. **Freezing rain or ice storms.** Freezing precipitation can coat the unit faster than defrost removes it. This usually clears once the weather improves.
4. **Low refrigerant charge (a leak).** A low charge drives coil temperature down and causes uneven, patchy icing, often frost on only part of the coil. This requires a technician.
5. **Defrost system faults.** Examples include a failed defrost sensor or thermostat, a defrost control board fault, a reversing valve that doesn't shift, or an outdoor fan relay problem. These overlap with the "not defrosting" page.
6. **Outdoor fan motor or capacitor failure.** If the fan isn't moving air, the coil ices rapidly.

If there is no external water source, such as gutter drip, drifted snow, or freezing rain, defrost faults and low refrigerant charge become the leading suspects.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Switch the thermostat to Emergency Heat (EM HEAT or AUX)** if the unit is heavily iced. This stops the outdoor unit and heats with backup strips while you wait for service. Backup heat costs much more to run than the heat pump, so use EM heat only as a stopgap until the repair is done. Leaving it on for weeks will raise your electric bill.
- **Confirm the thermostat is in Heat mode** and the setpoint is reasonable. Replace thermostat batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty. A clogged filter is not a typical cause of outdoor icing, but it is a quick check before a service call.
- **Open supply registers and clear return grilles** of furniture and rugs.
- **Check the breakers.** Make sure the indoor and outdoor breakers aren't tripped. If one has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call.
- **Look above the unit.** Note any gutter overflow or roof drip landing on the outdoor unit. Fixing gutters, or adding a downspout extension that directs water away, is ordinary home maintenance.
- **Gently clear loose snow from around the base and sides** by hand or with a broom so meltwater can drain. Do not touch the coil fins, and never chip or pry ice off the unit.
- **Watch one cycle from a safe distance.** If you see steam and the frost clears within about 10 minutes, the defrost cycle is working.

Do not open the cabinet, pour hot water on the coil, or repeatedly turn the unit off and on.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Visual pattern.** Top-down ice suggests dripping water. Ice only at the base suggests drainage. Partial-coil frost suggests low charge. Uniform heavy ice suggests defrost or fan failure.
- **Force a defrost cycle** using the board's test pins or the service menu on AccuLink. This verifies that the reversing valve shifts, the outdoor fan stops, and the backup heat energizes.
- **Test the defrost sensor or thermostat** for resistance at a known temperature, check its placement on the coil, and confirm board outputs.
- **Check the outdoor fan motor and run capacitor** for proper operation.
- **Measure refrigerant pressures, superheat and subcooling.** If the charge is low, expect leak detection before a recharge. A recharge alone is not a proper repair.
- **Read stored alerts** on communicating AccuLink systems.
- **Inspect the pad height and drainage clearance.** They may recommend a riser stand.

A quote should name a specific failed part or a measured low charge. Be skeptical of a "recharge" with no leak search, or a board replacement that wasn't preceded by a forced-defrost test.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thick ice on top, worse under roof edge | Gutter or roof drip | Fix gutter or redirect water; use EM heat meanwhile | Usually none; may advise relocating or a cover deflector |
| Ice block around the base | Poor drainage or snow drift | Clear loose snow away by hand; use EM heat | Raise unit on riser or pad; verify defrost |
| Whole unit glazed after ice storm | Freezing rain | Use EM heat until weather clears | Inspect if ice persists after weather improves |
| Frost on only part of the coil | Low refrigerant charge | Use EM heat; call for service | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Heavy ice, no steam or defrost ever seen | Defrost sensor, board, or reversing valve | Use EM heat; call for service | Test sensor and board; replace failed part |
| Ice with fan not spinning | Fan motor or capacitor | Turn unit off at thermostat; call | Replace capacitor or fan motor |
