## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single York fault code. It describes a symptom: the air conditioner starts, runs for only a few minutes, and shuts off before your home reaches the thermostat setting. Then it repeats the pattern.

On York split systems, the shutdowns come from one of three sources:

- **The thermostat ending the call too early.** This can be caused by placement, settings or wiring.
- **A safety device opening.** Examples include the high- or low-pressure switch or the compressor's internal overload.
- **The system satisfying the thermostat too fast.** This happens when the unit is oversized for the home.

Behavior after a trip varies by model:

- **Many York boards use a built-in anti-short-cycle delay.** This is typically about 5 minutes and prevents immediate restarts.
- **Some pressure-switch circuits lock out after repeated trips.** These may stay locked until power is cycled.
- **The compressor's internal overload resets on its own** once the compressor cools down, which can take 30 minutes or more.

Check your unit's wiring diagram or control-board label for the specific logic.

This page covers the cooling-mode condenser and air conditioner. Furnace and heat pump short cycling are covered on separate York pages.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat issues.** Common problems include:
   - Placement near a supply register, in direct sun or near a heat source
   - Weak batteries
   - A low cycle-rate or swing setting
   - Loose low-voltage wiring

   All of these can end the call within minutes.
2. **Restricted airflow.** A dirty filter, closed registers or a dirty evaporator coil drops suction pressure. That can trip the low-pressure switch or start coil icing, which can lead to a frozen coil.
3. **Dirty or blocked outdoor coil.** Heat cannot leave the system, so head pressure rises. This opens the high-pressure switch or overheats the compressor until its internal overload trips.
4. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** Low suction pressure trips the low-pressure switch, and the compressor may overheat from poor cooling of its windings.
5. **Failing run capacitor or outdoor fan problem.** A weak capacitor or a fan that isn't moving enough air makes the compressor run hot and trip on overload. The outdoor fan not spinning is covered on its own page.
6. **Electrical and control faults.** These include:
   - A pitted contactor
   - A failing control board
   - Loose high-voltage connections
   - A faulty pressure switch
7. **Oversized system.** The unit cools the air near the thermostat too quickly and shuts off before running long enough. The giveaway is a clammy, humid house even though temperatures look fine.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:**
  - Confirm the thermostat is set to COOL with the fan on AUTO.
  - Set the temperature at least 3°F below room temperature.
  - Replace the batteries.
  - Look for sun, lamps or a supply vent blowing directly on the thermostat.
- **Air filter:** Replace the filter if it is gray or clogged, and confirm the airflow arrow points toward the blower.
- **Registers and returns:**
  - Open all supply registers.
  - Make sure furniture or rugs don't block the return grilles.
- **Outdoor unit:**
  - Clear leaves, grass clippings and debris from around the condenser.
  - Keep plants at least 2 ft away.
  - Look from outside only. Do not remove the panels.
- **Breaker and disconnect:** If a breaker is tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Condensate:** Check that the drain line is flowing and that any float/overflow safety switch isn't triggered by a full pan. A tripped float switch can interrupt cooling.
- **One restart:** Turn the system off at the thermostat, wait 5 minutes, and restart it one time. If it still cycles every few minutes, turn it off and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will typically work through these checks:

- **Watch a full cycle.** They time the run and note which component drops out first:
  - Thermostat call
  - Contactor
  - Compressor alone while the fan keeps running, which suggests an overload trip
- **Check the thermostat signal.** They verify 24V at the Y terminal, check the thermostat's cycle settings, and may relocate or replace the thermostat.
- **Measure airflow.** They check static pressure, the blower setting and the condition of the evaporator coil.
- **Read refrigerant pressures with gauges.** They measure superheat and subcooling to confirm the charge. If the charge is low, they should find and repair the leak, not just top it off.
- **Test electrical components.** They test the run capacitor against its rating, measure compressor amp draw, and inspect the contactor and high-voltage wiring.
- **Check the safety switches and board.** They check the high- and low-pressure switches for continuity, and read any fault LED on the control board or communicating thermostat.
- **Run a load calculation (Manual J).** If everything tests fine but cycles stay short, they evaluate whether the unit is oversized.

**Red flag:** Be wary of a quote that adds refrigerant with no leak search, or one that recommends a new compressor without capacitor, amp-draw and pressure readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2–5 min, house never cools | Thermostat placement or settings | Check settings, replace batteries, shade the thermostat | Relocate the thermostat or repair its wiring |
| Short runs and weak airflow at vents | Dirty filter or blocked returns | Replace the filter, open registers | Clean the evaporator coil, check the blower |
| Outdoor unit stops while the indoor fan keeps running | High pressure or compressor overload | Clear debris around the condenser | Clean the coil, test the capacitor and fan motor |
| Short cycles plus ice on the lines | Low charge or low airflow | Replace the filter, turn the system off to thaw | Leak search, repair and recharge |
| Clicking at the outdoor unit, compressor hums then stops | Weak capacitor or contactor | None beyond one restart | Replace the capacitor or contactor |
| Cool but clammy house, frequent short runs | Oversized unit | None | Load calculation, airflow or staging adjustment, or replacement |
| Cycles off and won't restart for a long time | Pressure-switch lockout | Cycle power once | Diagnose the switch and pressure fault |
