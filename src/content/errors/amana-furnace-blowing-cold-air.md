## What this code means

"Blowing cold air" is a symptom, not an Amana fault code. The blower is moving air, but the burners either aren't lit or keep shutting off. When this happens, the Amana control board almost always stores a reason. Read that code before you open or power down anything, because a reset clears it.

How you read the code depends on your board:

- **Flash-LED boards (most models):** Look through the small window in the lower blower door and count the LED flashes. Count the flashes between pauses, and watch at least two cycles to be sure.
- **Display boards (some communicating/variable-speed models, such as the AMVC96 and AMVM97):** These show letters and numbers on a 7-segment display instead of a flash count. Write down exactly what the display shows. The flash counts on this page don't apply to these boards.

On either type, **the code label inside your blower door is the authority.** Code meanings differ between Amana board versions. The same flash count can mean different faults on different furnaces.

There are two broad situations:

- **The burners never lit.** The thermostat isn't calling for heat, or the furnace failed its ignition sequence and locked out. In a lockout, the blower may run alone.
- **The burners lit, then shut down.** A safety switch opened, such as the high-limit or rollout switch. The board then runs the blower on purpose to cool the heat exchanger.

A short burst of cool air at the start of a cycle is normal. Amana boards delay the blower until the heat exchanger warms up. Continuous cold air is the problem.

**Reset behavior varies by board:**
- Most Amana integrated boards try ignition several times, then enter a lockout. That lockout typically clears on its own after about 1 hour.
- Cycling power or the thermostat clears it immediately.
- Limit-switch faults generally reset automatically once the furnace cools. Rollout switches are typically manual-reset and need a technician.

Check the diagram label inside your blower door for your board's exact behavior.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

Any flash counts below are **board-dependent examples only.** They don't apply to display boards, and your blower-door label governs.

1. **Thermostat fan set to ON.** The blower runs continuously, so it pushes unheated air between burner cycles. This is the most common no-cost cause.
2. **Thermostat setting or power issue.** Possible problems include:
   - The thermostat isn't in HEAT.
   - The setpoint is below room temperature.
   - Batteries are dead.
   - A heat-pump thermostat was set up wrong for a gas furnace.
3. **Dirty air filter causing high-limit trips.** Restricted airflow overheats the heat exchanger. The limit opens, the burners shut off, and the blower keeps running. On many flash-LED boards, this is the 4-flash limit-circuit code. Check your label.
4. **Ignition failure or lockout.** Examples include a dirty flame sensor, a failed hot surface igniter, or a gas supply problem. The furnace tries, fails and locks out. Depending on the board, these may show as 1, 7 or 8 flashes, but the meanings vary, so confirm on your label.
5. **Pressure switch or venting problem.** A blocked intake or exhaust pipe, a failing inducer motor, or a blocked condensate trap on condensing models can stop ignition. On many boards, this is a 3-flash code. Some two-stage boards use a separate code for the high-stage switch. Check your label.
6. **Flame rollout switch open.** This points to a blocked heat exchanger or venting problem. It needs professional inspection. Don't rely on a particular flash count to identify rollout. On many Amana boards, an open rollout switch reports through the limit circuit, so it can look like a limit fault. Treat any code your label lists as limit or rollout as a reason to stop after one reset.
7. **Control board or gas valve fault.** These are less common and are diagnosed by a technician after other causes are ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Read the code first.** On a flash-LED board, count the flashes through the blower door sight glass. On a display board, note the characters shown. Write it down and match it to the blower-door label. Do this before cycling power, because a reset clears the code.
- **Thermostat:**
  - Set it to HEAT.
  - Set the fan to AUTO, not ON.
  - Raise the setpoint 3–5°F above room temperature.
  - Replace the batteries if the display is blank or faint.
- **Air filter:** Pull it out and replace it if it's gray or clogged. Buy the size printed on the edge of your existing filter. Install the new one with the airflow arrow pointing toward the furnace.
- **Breaker and service switch:** Confirm the furnace breaker is on and the light-style switch near the furnace is on.
- **Doors and panels:** Make sure the blower door is fully seated. A loose door opens the door safety switch and the furnace won't run.
- **Vents and registers:** Open the supply registers. Make sure the return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Exterior pipes (high-efficiency models):** Check the PVC intake and exhaust outside for snow, ice, leaves or nests. Clear only what you can reach by hand from outside.
- **Condensate line:** Make sure the drain hose outside the cabinet isn't kinked, frozen or disconnected.
- **One reset only:** Turn the power off for 30 seconds, then back on, and call for heat. If it blows cold again or shows a code, stop and call a technician. If your label says the code means limit or rollout, don't reset again.

If you smell gas, don't touch switches. Leave the home and call your gas utility's emergency line or 911.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically work through these steps:

1. **Read the stored fault code** and the board's fault history, if your board keeps one.
2. **Watch a full ignition sequence:** inducer start, pressure switch close, igniter glow, gas valve open, flame proving, then blower-on delay.
3. **Measure flame signal in microamps**, and clean or replace the flame sensor if the reading is low.
4. **Check the igniter** with resistance testing and the board's output voltage.
5. **Check the pressure switch** by testing manometer readings against the switch rating and inspecting hoses, inducer, vent and condensate trap.
6. **Measure temperature rise** against the range on the rating plate, and check static pressure if limit trips are suspected.
7. **Inspect for rollout**, including heat exchanger condition and burner alignment.
8. **Verify gas pressure** at the valve, inlet and manifold.
9. **Check thermostat wiring**, confirming 24V on W and the correct fan behavior.

Be cautious of a quote to replace the control board without documented voltage tests. Boards are rarely the first cause.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

Flash counts in this table are **board-dependent examples** for flash-LED boards only. Your blower-door label governs. On display boards, match the displayed code to the label instead.

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blower runs nonstop, air lukewarm or cool | Thermostat fan set to ON | Set fan to AUTO | None usually needed |
| No heat, thermostat display blank | Dead batteries or thermostat power loss | Replace batteries; check breaker | Check 24V transformer or thermostat wiring |
| Warm briefly, then cold; label lists code as limit (example: 4 flashes on many boards) | High-limit trips from restricted airflow, or rollout on boards that report it through the limit circuit | Replace filter; open registers and returns; one reset only | Check temperature rise, blower, limit and rollout switches |
| Cold air; label lists code as ignition lockout or flame sense (examples: 1 or 7 flashes on some boards) | Ignition lockout or weak flame signal | One power reset | Clean or replace flame sensor; check gas supply |
| Cold air; label lists code as igniter fault (example: 8 flashes on some boards) | Igniter circuit fault | None beyond one reset | Test and replace hot surface igniter |
| Inducer hums, no ignition; label lists code as pressure switch (example: 3 flashes on many boards; some two-stage boards use a separate high-stage code) | Pressure switch open from venting or condensate issue | Clear outside vent pipes; check drain line | Test switch, inducer, hoses, trap and venting |
| Cold air; label lists code as rollout (count varies by board) | Flame rollout switch open | Don't reset; call a pro | Find cause, inspect heat exchanger, reset or replace switch |
| No code, burners never light | Board, gas valve or wiring fault | Confirm thermostat call and power | Voltage diagnostics; replace failed component |
