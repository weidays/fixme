## What this code means

"Blowing cold air in heat mode" is a symptom, not a numbered Carrier fault code. The thermostat is calling for heat and the indoor blower is running. The air coming out of the registers, however, is room temperature or colder.

This page covers the case where the heat pump runs but delivers the wrong air. It does not cover a system that won't heat at all or a unit stuck in defrost; those have their own pages, listed under Related codes.

Some cold-feeling air can be normal:

- **Short defrost cycles:** brief cool air during defrost is expected.
- **Lower supply temperature:** heat pump air is cooler than furnace air, typically about 90–105°F.

Some Carrier defrost boards and Infinity/Côr controls will log a fault and flash an LED code in certain cases, such as a pressure switch trip or lockout. This depends on the model. Many conventional systems simply keep running with no code displayed.

Reset behavior also varies. Low-pressure and high-pressure lockouts on many Carrier units clear by themselves after a short delay or a thermostat cycle. Others hold until power is cycled. Check the model's installation instructions or the label on the control board.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost cycle or heat pump supply temperature.** The unit reverses for several minutes to clear ice from the outdoor coil. If auxiliary heat doesn't come on during defrost, the air feels cold. Normal heating air at about 95°F can also feel "cool."
2. **Thermostat setup or settings.** Common setup problems:
   - Mode left on Cool, Auto or Fan On.
   - After a thermostat replacement, the reversing-valve setting is wrong. Carrier uses O, energized in cooling; a thermostat set to B runs the unit backward.
   - The thermostat is configured for a conventional AC and furnace instead of a heat pump.
   - Dead batteries.
3. **Outdoor unit not running while the indoor blower runs.** The outdoor unit can be off because of:
   - A tripped outdoor breaker or pulled disconnect.
   - A failed contactor or capacitor.
   - A seized fan or compressor.
   - A pressure-switch lockout.

   When this happens, the blower circulates unheated air. If auxiliary heat isn't staged in, the air comes out cold.
4. **Reversing valve fault.** A failed solenoid coil, a broken O wire, or a mechanically stuck valve can leave the system in cooling mode. You get cold air and a warm outdoor coil.
5. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** The unit runs but moves little heat. Supply air is lukewarm or cool, and frosting or low-pressure trips are common.
6. **Severe airflow or outdoor coil problems.** These include:
   - A clogged filter.
   - A heavily iced outdoor coil.
   - A coil blocked by debris or snow.

   These sharply reduce heating capacity.
7. **Auxiliary/emergency heat not working in cold weather.** Possible causes are a failed heat strip, a tripped heat-strip breaker, or a sequencer problem. Below the heat pump's balance point, the air then feels cool.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:**
  - Confirm the mode is set to Heat and the setpoint is several degrees above room temperature.
  - Set the fan to Auto, not On.
  - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Recent thermostat swap:** If the problem started after a new thermostat was installed, check its installer settings. Make sure it is configured as a heat pump with the changeover valve energized in cooling (O), per the thermostat's manual. If you're unsure, leave this for a technician.
- **Watch for defrost:** Look at the outdoor unit. If it is steaming, the fan is stopped, and it resumes normal operation within about 10 minutes, that was a defrost cycle.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it's dirty.
- **Breakers:**
  - Check the outdoor unit breaker, the air handler breaker, and the separate heat-strip breaker(s) in the main panel.
  - If one is tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
  - Check that the outdoor disconnect is fully seated.
- **One reset:** If the unit seems locked out, turn the thermostat off for 5 minutes, then back to Heat. Do this only once.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear snow, leaves and debris from around the unit, keeping about 2 feet clear, without opening the unit.
- **Indoor airflow:** Make sure registers are open and return grilles are unblocked.
- **Condensate:** Check that the drain line isn't backed up. Some air handlers have a float switch that shuts the system down when the drain is blocked.
- **Exterior panels:** Make sure the access panels are fully seated.

Do not open the outdoor unit or the air handler cabinet. Do not touch the reversing valve, wiring or refrigerant lines.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically:

- **Check the thermostat:** confirm the call and wiring at the thermostat and air handler, including O/B, Y, W/aux and the heat pump configuration.
- **Check the outdoor unit:**
  - Verify the contactor pulls in.
  - Confirm the compressor and fan run.
  - Test the capacitor.
  - Read any LED fault codes on the defrost or control board.
- **Measure temperatures:**
  - Supply and return air temperatures. A healthy heat pump typically shows a temperature rise of roughly 15–30°F, depending on outdoor temperature.
  - Line temperatures: in heating, the large vapor line should be hot.
- **Check the reversing valve:**
  - Test coil voltage and resistance.
  - Try energizing and de-energizing the valve.
  - Listen and feel for the shift.
- **Check refrigerant:** connect gauges or probes to check pressures, superheat and subcooling for low charge or a leak.
- **Check defrost operation:** inspect the defrost board, coil sensor and defrost termination.
- **Check auxiliary heat:** test the heat strips, sequencers and limit switches.

A sound quote should name the specific failed component and the measurements that point to it. A "recharge" with no leak search is not a real diagnosis.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool air for 5–10 min, outdoor unit steaming | Normal defrost | Wait; note how often it happens | Check defrost board/sensor only if defrost runs too long or too often |
| Cold air began after new thermostat installed | O/B or heat pump setting wrong | Check thermostat installer settings per its manual | Correct wiring and configuration |
| Blower runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, contactor, capacitor, lockout | Check breakers/disconnect; reset once | Test and replace contactor/capacitor; diagnose lockout cause |
| Outdoor unit runs, air cold, large line cold | Reversing valve stuck or coil/wire failed | None | Test coil and wiring; replace coil or valve |
| Lukewarm air, long run times, frosting | Low refrigerant charge | Replace filter; clear outdoor unit | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Cool air only on very cold days | Aux heat strips or breaker failed | Check heat-strip breakers | Test strips, sequencers and limits |
| Weak airflow, cool air | Clogged filter or blocked returns | Replace filter; open registers | Inspect blower and indoor coil |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including labor; prices vary by region:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Thermostat reconfiguration / service call | $100–$250 |
| New thermostat installed | $150–$500 (Carrier Infinity/Côr controls cost more) |
| Run capacitor | $150–$400 |
| Contactor | $150–$350 |
| Reversing valve solenoid coil | $200–$450 |
| Reversing valve replacement (recover, braze, evacuate, recharge) | $1,000–$2,500 |
| Leak search and repair plus refrigerant recharge | $300–$1,500+, depending on leak location and refrigerant (R-410A or R-454B) |
| Defrost control board or coil sensor | $250–$700 |
| Heat strip element or sequencer | $200–$600 |

Check your warranty before approving major work. Many registered Carrier units carry a 10-year parts warranty, which can cover the valve or board but not the labor.
