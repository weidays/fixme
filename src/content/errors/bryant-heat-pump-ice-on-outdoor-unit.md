## What this code means

"Ice on outdoor unit" is not a numbered fault code. It is a visible symptom. In heating mode, the outdoor coil of a Bryant heat pump runs below freezing, so frost forms on it in humid weather between roughly 25°F and 40°F. The outdoor control board clears that frost with a periodic defrost cycle:

- **Standard Bryant boards** use timed defrost, typically selectable at 30, 60 or 90 minutes of run time, combined with a defrost thermostat on the coil.
- **Evolution and many variable-speed models** use demand defrost, which reads an outdoor coil temperature sensor.

**What normal looks like:**
- Light, even white frost on the coil.
- Periodic defrost cycles. During defrost, the fan stops, the unit gets louder, steam rises and water drains.
- Defrost cycles usually end within about 10 minutes, depending on the model.

**What abnormal looks like:**
- Thick or clear ice.
- The whole unit, including the top or fan, encased in ice.
- Ice building up from the base.
- Frost that never clears after an hour or more.

This page focuses on telling normal frost apart from a problem, and on external causes such as drainage, snow and roof runoff. If the coil is solidly frozen and the unit never goes into defrost, see "Bryant Heat Pump Frozen, Not Defrosting" for the defrost-control side in depth.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal frost mistaken for a fault.** Light frost that clears every 30 to 90 minutes is expected operation.
2. **Water dripping onto the unit.** A missing or overflowing gutter, or a roof edge or deck above the unit, drips water that refreezes on the top and fan.
3. **Poor drainage or a buried base.** Snow drifts, a sunken pad, or a unit set too low leaves melt water with nowhere to go. It refreezes and builds upward each cycle.
4. **Freezing rain or ice storms.** These glaze the whole cabinet faster than defrost can clear it.
5. **Defrost control problem.** This can be a failed defrost thermostat, a failed coil temperature sensor (demand-defrost models), or a failed defrost control board. On Evolution systems, this may log a fault.
6. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** A leak makes the coil run colder and frost unevenly, often patchy or only on part of the coil.
7. **Outdoor fan or reversing valve problems.** A fan that fails to restart, or a reversing valve that doesn't shift in defrost, prevents normal frost removal.
8. **Restricted indoor airflow.** A dirty filter or closed registers reduce heating performance and can lengthen frost buildup. This is a contributing factor, not usually the root cause.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Watch one full cycle.** Note whether, within about 90 minutes, the fan stops and steam rises (defrost) and the frost clears. If it does, the unit is probably working normally.
- **Switch to Emergency or Auxiliary heat if the unit is encased.** Do this at the thermostat if the coil and fan are fully iced. It keeps the house warm and stops the outdoor unit from running iced.
- **Do not chip, scrape or pry ice.** Do not pour boiling water on the unit or aim a heat gun at it either.
- **Clear snow by hand or with a soft broom from around the base and sides.** Keep about 12 to 18 inches clear. Do not touch the fan or open any panel.
- **Look above the unit.** Check for dripping gutters, downspouts or roof edges. Redirecting a downspout away from the unit is a fair DIY step.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty, and make sure indoor supply registers and return grilles are open.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it is in Heat mode with a sensible setpoint. Replace the batteries if it has them.
- **Reset power once if needed.** If the outdoor unit isn't running at all, check the breaker and the outdoor disconnect, then cycle power one time. Do not repeat the reset if the problem returns.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirm defrost actually occurs.** They will check the outdoor board's defrost interval setting and run or force a test defrost. Many Bryant boards have speed-up or test terminals for this.
- **Test the defrost thermostat or outdoor coil temperature sensor** against the manufacturer's temperature and resistance chart.
- **Check the defrost board outputs.** In defrost, the reversing valve solenoid should energize and the outdoor fan should shut off.
- **Read stored faults on Evolution systems.** These appear in the wall control's service menu.
- **Measure refrigerant pressures and superheat or subcooling** to check charge. If the charge is low, they will leak-search before adding refrigerant.
- **Test the outdoor fan motor and capacitor**, and confirm the fan restarts after defrost.
- **Check installation factors.** These include pad height, clearance, and whether a riser stand is needed.

**Red flags in a quote:** refrigerant top-off without a leak search, or a board replaced without first testing the sensor.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light white frost that clears every 30–90 min with steam | Normal defrost operation | None; keep the area clear | None needed |
| Thick ice on top and fan, coil sides OK | Water dripping from gutter or roof | Redirect water; use Emergency heat until melted | Check fan blade/motor for ice damage |
| Ice building from the base up | Snow drift, sunken pad, unit too low | Clear snow by hand; use Emergency heat | Raise the unit on a riser or stand; level the pad |
| Whole unit glazed after a storm | Freezing rain | Use Emergency heat; wait for thaw | Inspect if it doesn't recover after the thaw |
| Solid ice, never defrosts | Defrost sensor or thermostat, or board fault | Use Emergency heat; one power reset | Test the sensor and board; replace the failed part |
| Patchy frost on part of the coil | Low refrigerant (leak) | Use Emergency heat | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Fan stays off after defrost; ice returns quickly | Fan motor or capacitor, or board | Turn the system off or use Emergency heat | Test and replace the motor or capacitor |
| No steam and no reversing sound at defrost | Reversing valve or solenoid | Use Emergency heat | Test the solenoid coil; replace the valve if stuck |

## Repair costs

These are typical US price ranges.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Snow clearing, downspout redirect, filter | $0–$40 DIY |
| Gutter repair or extension above the unit | $100–$400 |
| Riser stand or pad re-leveling | $150–$500 |
| Defrost thermostat or outdoor coil temperature sensor | $150–$350 |
| Defrost or outdoor control board | $300–$700 (higher for Evolution and variable-speed boards) |
| Outdoor fan capacitor | $150–$400 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $400–$900 |
| Refrigerant leak search and repair, plus recharge | $300–$1,500+ (depends on leak location and refrigerant type) |
| Reversing valve replacement | $1,200–$2,500+ |

Parts covered under warranty can lower the cost, but you will usually still pay for labor.
