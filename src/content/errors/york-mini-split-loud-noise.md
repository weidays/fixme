## What this code means

"Loud noise" isn't a diagnostic code that your York mini-split displays. It's a symptom. The system usually keeps running and won't lock itself out because of noise. If your model also shows a blinking light or error code, see the related page on blinking lights.

Mini-splits are designed to run quietly. Normal sounds include:
- a soft fan whoosh from the indoor head,
- a varying hum from the inverter compressor outside,
- clicks or creaks from plastic expanding and contracting,
- brief hissing during defrost in heat mode.

Sounds that are not normal include grinding, screeching, banging, steady rattling, loud buzzing, or gurgling that keeps going. These usually point to a fan, mounting or refrigerant problem that needs a technician. Noise behavior and normal decibel levels vary by York series and model, so compare against your installation manual.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty filters or blower wheel (indoor head).** Restricted airflow causes whistling. Dirt buildup on the cross-flow (barrel) blower wheel can unbalance it and cause wobbling or a rhythmic thump.
2. **Debris or loose parts in the outdoor unit.** Leaves, sticks, or a loose cabinet panel or fan guard can rattle or tick.
3. **Loose mounting or vibration transfer.**
   - An outdoor unit on a wall bracket or uneven pad, or with worn vibration pads, can buzz or drone through the house.
   - An indoor head that isn't secure on its mounting plate can rattle.
   - Line sets touching the wall can transmit vibration.
4. **Failing fan motor bearings (indoor or outdoor).** Grinding, squealing or growling that gets louder over time.
5. **Damaged or rubbing fan blade or blower wheel.** A cracked or warped blade, or a wheel shifted on its shaft, causes scraping or ticking.
6. **Ice buildup.** Ice on the indoor coil or outdoor coil can make the fan hit ice or make cracking noises. This is often linked to airflow or refrigerant problems.
7. **Refrigerant-related noise.** Continuous hissing, bubbling or gurgling (outside of normal defrost and startup) can mean low charge, a leak or a restriction.
8. **Electrical buzzing.** A failing contactor or relay, a loose connection, or a problem with the inverter board components can cause buzzing.
9. **Compressor mechanical problems.** Loud knocking or clanking at startup, or harsh metallic noise under load. This is the least common and most expensive cause.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn it off if the sound is grinding, banging or screeching.** Use the remote or the disconnect, then call a technician.
- **Clean the indoor filters.** Lift the front panel, slide the mesh filters out, rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry completely, and reinstall. Don't run the unit without filters.
- **Check the remote settings.** High fan or "Turbo/Powerful" modes are louder by design. Try Auto or a lower fan speed, or Quiet/Sleep mode if your remote has it. Replace weak remote batteries if commands aren't registering.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from outside the cabinet.**
  - Clear leaves, grass and debris from around the unit. Keep about 12–24 inches of clearance.
  - Check that nothing is touching the cabinet, such as shrubs, toys or a fence.
- **Seat exterior panels.** If an exterior access panel or cover is visibly loose, press it back into place or snug its existing exterior screws. Don't open the cabinet.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Gurgling at the indoor head can come from a partly blocked drain. Make sure the drain hose outlet outside is clear and not kinked or submerged.
- **Reset once if needed.** If the unit is acting abnormally, turn off the breaker or disconnect for 5 minutes, then restore power once. If the noise returns, stop and call a professional.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Isolate the source.**
  - Is it the indoor head or the outdoor unit?
  - Does it happen with the fan only or with the compressor running?
  - Is it in heat mode, cool mode or defrost?
  - Does it happen at startup or steady state?
- **Inspect the blower wheel and fan blades.** The tech checks for dirt, cracks, rubbing and set-screw slippage. They spin parts by hand with power off to feel for bearing roughness.
- **Check mounting.** This includes the outdoor wall bracket or pad level, vibration isolators, the indoor mounting plate, and line-set contact points.
- **Measure fan motor amperage.** The reading is compared to the nameplate, and the tech checks motor bearings for play.
- **Check the refrigerant system.** The tech measures pressures and temperatures (superheat/subcooling), leak-checks if the charge is low, and looks for restrictions. Only EPA-certified techs may handle refrigerant.
- **Check electrical components.** This includes the contactor or relays, connections, and the inverter board and compressor drive. The tech also reads any stored fault codes on the board or display.
- **Evaluate the compressor.** The tech listens under load, checks winding resistance and current, and looks for liquid slugging causes.

A good quote will name the specific component and how it was confirmed. Be wary of a quote that jumps straight to "replace the outdoor unit" without testing.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whistling from indoor head | Clogged filters, restricted airflow | Clean filters; lower fan speed | Clean blower wheel and coil |
| Rhythmic thump or wobble indoors | Dirty or unbalanced blower wheel | Turn off; clean filters | Clean, re-seat or replace blower wheel |
| Grinding or squealing (indoor or outdoor) | Worn fan motor bearings | Turn unit off | Replace fan motor |
| Scraping or ticking | Fan blade or wheel rubbing, debris | Clear debris around outdoor unit | Realign or replace blade/wheel; remove internal debris |
| Rattling or buzzing through walls | Loose panel, mounting, worn isolators | Seat exterior panels; clear contact points | Tighten bracket, level pad, replace vibration pads |
| Gurgling at indoor head | Partly blocked condensate drain | Check drain hose outlet is clear | Clear drain line, check slope or pump |
| Continuous hiss or bubbling (not defrost) | Low charge or leak, restriction | Turn off; note when it occurs | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Cracking noise; ice visible | Coil icing | Clean filters; turn off to thaw | Diagnose airflow or refrigerant cause |
| Loud electrical buzz outdoors | Contactor or relay, loose connection, board | Turn off at disconnect | Test and replace electrical part or board |
| Knocking or clanking at startup | Compressor mechanical issue | Turn off | Compressor diagnosis, replacement |
