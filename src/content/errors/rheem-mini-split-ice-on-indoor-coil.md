## What this code means

"Ice on indoor coil" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. Frost or ice forms on the fins of the indoor head's evaporator coil. You usually see it behind the front grille or louver, and it often comes with weak or warm airflow, dripping, or a hissing or crackling sound.

The coil freezes when its surface temperature drops below 32°F. That happens when too little warm room air crosses the coil, or when the refrigerant inside runs too cold because of low charge or a metering fault.

How the unit responds depends on the model:
- Many Rheem mini-split boards use the indoor coil sensor for anti-freeze protection. They temporarily stop the compressor and resume on their own once the coil warms up. Where this happens, it **auto-resets** and does not hold a lockout.
- Some models display a protection code. Others give no indication at all.

If the unit keeps refreezing after you've addressed airflow, a technician is needed.

This page covers the indoor head of a ductless mini-split. For ducted systems, see the Frozen Evaporator Coil and Ice on Refrigerant Line pages.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filters.** This is by far the most common cause on ductless heads. The mesh filters behind the front panel load up with dust and pet hair within weeks, and the airflow drop lets the coil freeze.
2. **Dirty indoor coil or blower wheel.** Dust, mold or biofilm on the coil fins and the cylindrical blower wheel restricts airflow even when the filters are clean. This is common in units that are a few years old.
3. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Flare connections at the indoor head and line set are a well-known leak point on mini-splits. Low charge drops the coil pressure and temperature below freezing.
4. **Indoor fan running too slowly or failing.** Causes include a failing DC fan motor, a fan-speed feedback fault, or the unit being left on the lowest or "quiet" fan speed in very low setpoint or humid conditions.
5. **Faulty indoor coil (pipe) temperature sensor or control board.** If the sensor reads incorrectly, freeze protection never kicks in.
6. **Operating outside the design range.** Long cooling runs at a very low setpoint when the room or outdoor air is cool (for example, mild spring nights) let the coil run below freezing.
7. **Restriction in the refrigerant circuit.** Examples are a sticking electronic expansion valve (EEV) or a kinked or crushed line set. This is less common, and frost often starts at one spot rather than covering the whole coil evenly.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Stop cooling and thaw.** Set the unit to OFF or FAN only with the remote. Do not chip or scrape ice; the fins and tubing are easily punctured. Lay towels under the head.
- **Clean the air filters.** Turn the unit off first. Lift the front panel (consult your owner's manual) and slide out the mesh filters. Vacuum or rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry completely, then reinstall. Repeat every 2–4 weeks in heavy use.
- **Check the remote settings.** Use a reasonable setpoint (around 72–78°F), with fan on AUTO or medium rather than the lowest speed. Confirm the mode is COOL or DRY as intended, and replace the remote batteries if the display is dim.
- **Clear the airflow path.** Make sure curtains, furniture or shelves aren't blocking the intake or the outlet louvers. Let the louvers swing freely; don't force them by hand.
- **Check the condensate drain.** After the thaw, make sure water exits where the drain hose terminates outdoors or at the pump, and that the hose isn't kinked or clogged at its end.
- **Power reset once.** If the unit shows a code or won't restart, switch it off at the breaker or disconnect for 5 minutes, then restore power. Do this **one time only**.
- **Run and watch.** Run cooling for an hour and check for new frost. If ice returns with clean filters and normal settings, call a licensed HVAC technician. Do not open the head's cabinet or touch the refrigerant fittings.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirm airflow.** The tech inspects the filters, the coil face and the blower wheel, often with a light and mirror or a borescope. They check the fan speed (RPM) and fan motor feedback.
- **Read the stored fault codes** and check the indoor coil and room temperature sensors against a thermometer, comparing their resistance to the manufacturer's temperature/resistance chart.
- **Measure system performance.** This includes gauges or digital probes on the outdoor unit's service port (suction pressure, superheat), plus the temperature split across the indoor coil.
- **Leak search if the charge is low.** Expect an electronic leak detector and/or bubble solution at the flare nuts, coil and line set. A nitrogen pressure test may follow.
- **Repair and recharge.** After a leak repair, the system is evacuated with a vacuum pump to a micron target and recharged **by weight** to the nameplate spec. Topping off without a leak repair is a red flag on a quote.
- **Deep cleaning.** If the coil or blower wheel is dirty, the tech uses a coil-cleaning bag kit or removes the blower wheel.
- **EEV or board check** if the charge and airflow are normal but the coil still runs cold.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Even frost across the coil, weak airflow | Clogged filters | Thaw, clean filters | None if it resolves |
| Clean filters, musty smell, black specks from vents | Dirty coil or blower wheel | Thaw, clean filters | Coil and blower wheel deep clean |
| Ice returns within hours, hissing, poor cooling | Refrigerant leak / low charge | Shut off, call pro | Leak search, flare repair, evacuate, weigh-in charge |
| Airflow very weak even when thawed | Indoor fan motor or speed fault | Set fan to AUTO/high, one power reset | Fan motor or control diagnosis and replacement |
| Freezes with no protection shutdown | Coil sensor or board fault | One power reset | Test and replace sensor or board |
| Frost only on one section or at the line connection | Restriction or EEV issue | Shut off, call pro | Check EEV, line set, repair the restriction |
| Freezes only on cool nights at a low setpoint | Out-of-range operation | Raise the setpoint, use DRY mode or a higher fan speed | Usually none |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter cleaning, settings adjustment | $0 (DIY) |
| Service call / diagnostic | $90 – $200 |
| Indoor coil and blower wheel deep cleaning | $150 – $400 per head |
| Coil temperature sensor replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Indoor fan motor replacement | $300 – $700 |
| Leak search plus flare repair and recharge | $400 – $1,200 |
| Indoor coil replacement or major leak repair | $1,000 – $1,800+ |
| Indoor control board | $300 – $700 |
| EEV coil or valve replacement | $350 – $900 |

Refrigerant cost varies with type (R-410A, or the newer R-32/R-454B) and with how much charge is needed. Check the warranty if the unit is registered, since parts are often covered for 10 years.
