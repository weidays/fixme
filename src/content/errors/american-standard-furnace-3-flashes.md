## What this code means

Three flashes on an American Standard furnace control board indicate a **pressure switch error**. The control board is telling you one of two things:

- The pressure switch is **open when the inducer motor is running** (the board expected it to close and prove draft, but it didn't), or
- The pressure switch is **closed when the inducer is off** (the board expected it open at rest, but it's stuck closed).

The pressure switch is a safety device. Its job is to confirm the inducer motor is actually moving combustion air and venting exhaust before the board allows the igniter and gas valve to fire. If the switch doesn't behave the way the board expects, the furnace won't light — this protects you from operating with a blocked or failed vent.

Because this is a **safety-circuit fault**, the burners stay off until the switch reads correctly. On most American Standard integrated furnace control (IFC) boards the code clears automatically once conditions are normal, though some board revisions latch after repeated faults and need a power cycle. Confirm the exact behavior on the legend inside your blower door.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted vent / intake termination** — snow, ice, leaves, insects, or a bird nest at the exterior PVC pipe reduces airflow so the switch never closes.
2. **Clogged condensate drain or trap** — a full or blocked condensate line on high-efficiency (condensing) models backs up water and trips the pressure switch open.
3. **Weak or failing inducer motor** — a worn inducer can't generate enough draft to close the switch.
4. **Cracked, pinched, or disconnected pressure tubing** — the rubber hose between the inducer and switch can split, kink, or fill with condensate.
5. **Blocked heat exchanger or flue passages** — soot or debris restricting exhaust flow.
6. **Failed pressure switch itself** — the switch diaphragm sticks open or closed and no longer responds to pressure.
7. **Loose or corroded wiring at the switch or board** — an intermittent connection mimics an open switch.

We've ordered this list by how often each cause turns up and how cheap it is to rule out: check venting and condensate first, then confirm the inducer is actually producing draft with a manometer, and only then suspect the switch itself. Working outside-in this way avoids replacing a switch that was reporting a real restriction.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform. Everything else lives inside the cabinet and is technician work.

- **Check the air filter.** A severely clogged filter can disrupt airflow and pressure. Replace it if it's dirty — read the size printed on the edge of the filter you pull out, since American Standard cabinets take a range of sizes.
- **Verify the thermostat** is set to Heat with a target above room temperature. Replace thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Inspect the exterior vent and intake pipes.** Look for snow, ice, leaves, or nests blocking the PVC terminations outside. Clear anything you can safely reach from the ground.
- **Check the condensate drain** on high-efficiency models. If you see standing water near the furnace or a full drain pan, the line may be clogged — clear the visible external portion if you can.
- **Reset the furnace once.** Cycle the furnace switch or breaker off for 30 seconds, then on. Do this **only once** — if the 3-flash code returns, stop and call a pro.
- **Confirm interior registers and returns are open** so airflow isn't strangled.

Do **not** open the cabinet, disconnect tubing, bypass the switch, or attempt to test the switch yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech follows a repeatable sequence, which helps you sanity-check any quote:

1. **Reads the flash code and board legend** to confirm it's a pressure switch fault and note whether the board latched.
2. **Inspects vent and intake terminations** and the full flue path for blockage.
3. **Checks the condensate trap and drain** for water backup that could open the switch.
4. **Connects a manometer** to measure the actual pressure the inducer produces and compares it to the spec printed on the unit.
5. **Inspects the pressure tubing** for cracks, kinks, or condensate, and confirms it's seated at both ends.
6. **Tests the inducer motor** for correct amperage and airflow.
7. **Bench-tests or ohm-checks the pressure switch** to confirm it opens and closes at the rated pressure.
8. **Inspects wiring and connectors** at the switch and control board for corrosion or looseness.

A trustworthy diagnosis distinguishes a *blocked vent* (cheap fix) from a *failed inducer* (expensive part) — beware quotes that jump straight to replacing the switch without a manometer reading.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inducer runs, burners never light | Switch stays open (blocked vent, weak inducer) | Clear exterior vent; replace filter; one reset | Manometer draft test, inspect inducer & tubing |
| No heat after ice storm or snowfall | Vent/intake termination blocked | Clear snow/ice from PVC pipes outside | Confirm flue is clear, check for water damage |
| Water pooling near furnace, no heat | Clogged condensate drain trips switch open | Clear visible external drain line | Clear trap, verify condensate path, test switch |
| Short-cycling with intermittent 3 flashes | Cracked tube or intermittent wiring | One reset; note if it recurs | Replace tubing, repair connectors, retest |
| Fault present with inducer off | Switch stuck closed | None — internal | Replace pressure switch |
| Recurring code, inducer sounds weak | Failing inducer motor | None — internal | Replace inducer assembly |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor, varies by region and model):

- **Clearing a blocked vent or intake:** $0 DIY (if you can reach it) – $150 for a service call.
- **Clearing a clogged condensate line:** $0 DIY – $200 professional.
- **Replacing pressure tubing:** roughly $75 – $180 in parts and time, but it's rarely a standalone ticket — tubing is almost always replaced as part of a diagnostic visit or a pressure switch job rather than billed on its own.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150 – $350.
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $400 – $1,200, depending on whether the OEM inducer assembly or a universal motor is used and on regional labor rates.
- **Diagnostic service call:** $90 – $180, often credited toward the repair.

If a tech recommends replacing the pressure switch, ask whether they measured inducer draft first — replacing the switch won't fix a weak inducer or a blocked vent.

## Related codes

- **American Standard Furnace 2 Flashes: Causes & Fixes** — on most American Standard/Trane IFC boards, 2 flashes indicates a system lockout after failed ignition attempts. Legends vary by board revision, so check the board legend to be sure which code you're seeing before troubleshooting.

Always confirm the flash-code meaning against the diagnostic legend printed inside your furnace's blower door, since legends can differ between board revisions.
