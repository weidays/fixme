> **Emergency — do this first.** If you smell gas (rotten egg / sulfur), hear hissing, hear a CO alarm, or anyone feels dizzy, nauseated, or has a headache, **leave the house now** and, from outside, call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line. Do not flip switches, do not restart the furnace, and do not go back inside until you are told it is safe.
>
> **If there is no gas smell and no one feels ill:** turn the furnace **off** at its service switch (the light-switch-style switch on or near the cabinet), **leave the furnace door closed**, and call a licensed HVAC technician. **Do not reset the rollout switch and do not try to restart the unit** — a 6-flash code is exactly the kind of fault that can accompany carbon monoxide.

## What this code means

Six flashes on your Amana furnace's diagnostic LED means the **flame rollout (auxiliary limit) switch is open** — it has tripped. This is a small manual-reset safety switch mounted near the burner box. It is designed to sense heat or flame in a place where flame should never be, and cut off the gas valve when that happens.

When flame "rolls out" of the burner compartment instead of being drawn cleanly into the heat exchanger, it usually means combustion airflow is disrupted — a blocked flue, plugged heat exchanger passages, or a **cracked heat exchanger**. Because rolled-out flame can send carbon monoxide into your living space, this is one of the most serious codes an Amana furnace produces.

**This code does not auto-reset.** The rollout switch is a manual-reset device mounted on the burner compartment, so clearing it is technician work performed *after* the cause is found and corrected — never a homeowner step. The switch did its job; the reason it tripped is still there.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted flue / venting** — a bird nest, ice, debris, disconnected vent pipe, or blocked termination backs up combustion gases and forces flame out of the burner box.
2. **Dirty or clogged heat exchanger / burners** — soot and debris obstruct the normal flame path, causing flame to spill forward and trip the switch.
3. **Cracked or failed heat exchanger** — a crack changes airflow around the burners and lets flame roll out. This is a replace-or-condemn condition.
4. **Failed or weak inducer (draft) motor** — if the inducer can't move enough combustion air, flame won't be drawn in properly. (Note: a fully failed pressure-proving path usually shows as 2 or 3 flashes; rollout is about flame escaping, not just draft.)
5. **Misaligned, warped, or corroded burners** — burners firing at the wrong angle can direct flame toward the rollout sensor.
6. **A genuinely faulty rollout switch or its wiring** — least common; the switch can degrade, but technicians rule this out last because the switch is usually correct.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Keep it to these — everything inside the cabinet is technician work, and none of these steps involve restarting the furnace:

- **Confirm it is safe.** No gas smell, no soot around the furnace, no one feeling ill. If any of those are present, stop and call your gas utility / 911 from outside.
- **Check the thermostat.** Set to Heat and above room temperature; replace batteries if it's battery-powered. (This won't fix a rollout, but it rules out a false "no heat" complaint.)
- **Inspect your air filter.** A severely clogged filter chokes airflow and overheats the system. Replace it if it's dirty.
- **Look at outdoor vent terminations.** From outside, check that the plastic intake/exhaust pipes or metal flue are clear of snow, ice, leaves, or nests. Do **not** disassemble any pipe.
- **Check the furnace switch and breaker.** Note whether the breaker has tripped — that's useful information for your technician.
- **Confirm supply/return registers are open** so airflow isn't starved.
- **Condensate line (high-efficiency models):** a full condensate trap or clog can back up and cause faults — verify the drain isn't overflowing.

**Do not reset the rollout switch, and do not power-cycle the furnace to try to clear a 6-flash lockout.** The switch is mounted on the burner compartment, which must stay closed. Leave the furnace off at its service switch and have it diagnosed before any reset is attempted.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified technician should:

1. Verify the 6-flash code and check the rollout switch continuity with a multimeter.
2. **Inspect the heat exchanger** with a camera/mirror and combustion analyzer for cracks — this is the critical step given the emergency severity.
3. Perform a **combustion analysis** measuring CO in the flue and around the cabinet.
4. Inspect and clean the **burners** and check flame pattern for rollout evidence (scorching, sooting).
5. Confirm the **flue is clear** and properly pitched, and check the inducer motor draft.
6. Only after clearing mechanical causes, replace a genuinely defective rollout switch — never bypass it.

A good quote names the *root cause*, not just "reset the switch." If a tech offers to reset it and leave, that's a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 flashes, no heat, gas smell or CO alarm | Flame rollout / possible cracked heat exchanger | Evacuate, call 911 / gas utility from outside | Full combustion & heat exchanger inspection |
| 6 flashes after cold snap or storm | Blocked/iced flue or vent termination | Check outdoor vent is clear of snow/ice | Clear & inspect venting, verify draft |
| Soot or scorch marks near burners | Dirty burners or bad flame pattern | None — do not open cabinet | Clean/align burners, analyze combustion |
| 6-flash lockout stays on the board | Ongoing rollout condition | Leave it off; do not reset the switch | Diagnose flue/exchanger/inducer, then reset |
| Furnace short-cycled, then locked at 6 flashes | Restricted airflow / weak inducer | Replace clogged filter, open registers | Test inducer, check heat exchanger passages |
| Rollout trips with clean flue & burners | Failing rollout switch or wiring | None | Test and replace switch, verify circuit |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts + labor:

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $200
- **Flue/vent clearing:** $100 – $300
- **Burner cleaning & flame adjustment:** $150 – $400
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $400 – $1,200
- **Flame rollout switch replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Heat exchanger replacement:** $1,500 – $3,000+ (often approaching the cost of a new furnace)
- **Full furnace replacement (if exchanger is cracked and unit is aged):** $3,500 – $8,000+

For an older furnace with a confirmed cracked heat exchanger, replacing the whole unit is frequently the smarter choice — ask your tech to price both.

## Related codes

Flash-code legends vary between board generations, so **confirm the code chart on your furnace door label** before assuming any of these meanings apply to your unit:

- **Amana Furnace Code 1 Flashes: Ignition Lockout Fixes** — failure to prove flame after ignition attempts.
- **Amana Furnace 2 Flashes: Pressure Switch Stuck Closed Fix** — draft-proving switch closed when it shouldn't be.
- **Amana Furnace 3 Flashes: Pressure Switch Stuck Open Fix** — draft/venting not proving, often flue-related.
- **Amana Furnace 4 Flashes: Open High-Limit Switch Fix** — overheating from airflow restriction (often confused with rollout).
- **Amana Furnace 5 Flashes: Flame Sensed, No Call for Heat** — flame detected when there should be none.
