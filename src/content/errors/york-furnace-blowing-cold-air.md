## What this code means

"Blowing cold air" is a symptom, not a single York flash code. Your furnace fan is moving air, but the air isn't warm. On York units this almost always means one of two things: the blower is running when the burners aren't lit (often just a thermostat fan setting), or the furnace is **trying** to heat but failing to light or sustain a flame.

When the control board detects an ignition or flame-proving problem, it stores a flash code on the LED behind the lower access panel and may shut down the gas while the blower keeps running to purge heat. Most York/Source 1 style controls use a **soft lockout**: after the allowed retries fail, the board stops trying, waits roughly one hour, and then automatically restarts the ignition sequence by itself. A hard lockout that stays down until power is cycled is the exception, not the rule — so in most cases you do not need to cycle the breaker on a gas appliance that shut itself down for a reason. If the furnace locks out repeatedly, stop and call a technician.

Flash patterns are not universal across York equipment. Read the actual flash count through the sight glass and compare it to the **diagnostic label printed inside your own furnace door** — the chart differs by board revision, and a count that means one thing on one board can mean something else on another.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat settings or dead batteries** — the fan set to ON instead of AUTO runs the blower 24/7 and moves cool air between heat cycles; a dim or blank screen, dead batteries, or the wrong mode (COOL/off) means no heat is being requested at all. Together these are the single most common cause of "cold air" with no fault in the furnace.
2. **Dirty air filter or closed vents** — severe airflow restriction can cause the limit switch to trip the burners while the blower keeps cooling the heat exchanger.
3. **Failed ignition (no flame established)** — igniter, flame sensor, or gas-proving fault. The board stores a fault code and the blower purges, and after the retries fail the control goes into lockout.
4. **Flame loss during the cycle** — a dirty flame sensor or weak flame lets the board shut gas mid-run, so air turns cold partway through heating.
5. **Pressure or limit switch fault** — an open pressure switch or tripped limit stops burners but leaves the blower running on cool-down.
6. **Control board or inducer failure** — the board can't sequence ignition correctly, or the inducer isn't proving draft, so burners never fire.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Set the thermostat fan to AUTO** (not ON) and the mode to HEAT, with the setpoint several degrees above room temperature.
- **Replace the thermostat batteries** if it's a battery-powered model and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Replace a dirty air filter** — a clogged filter is a frequent cause of overheating and short, cold cycles.
- **Confirm all supply registers and return vents are open and unblocked** by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Check the breaker and the furnace service switch** (looks like a light switch near the unit); reset a tripped breaker once.
- **Check the condensate drain** on high-efficiency units — a clogged line can trip a safety and stop heating. Limit yourself to the **external drain line and its termination** and to making sure the floor drain or condensate pump basin isn't backed up. Leave the trap on the furnace itself to a technician.
- **If the unit is locked out**, give it time first — most York controls retry on their own after about an hour. If you do reset power at the breaker, do it **once**. If it locks out again, stop and call a pro; repeated resets of a gas appliance that keeps shutting down are unsafe.

Do not open the burner compartment, touch wiring, bypass any switch, or open the gas valve. Those are technician tasks.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

- Read the stored flash code at the board LED and cross-reference the diagnostic chart for that specific board.
- Watch a full call for heat: inducer start, pressure switch closing, igniter glow, gas valve opening, and flame establishment.
- Measure **flame-sensor microamps** to see if the flame signal is dropping out (common cause of mid-cycle cold air).
- Test the **limit and pressure switches** with a multimeter and check inducer draft.
- Inspect the **igniter** for cracks and the **burners** for carbon or misalignment.
- Verify **115 VAC polarity** and ground, and check the control board for proper 24 V outputs and sequencing.
- Confirm airflow and temperature rise are within York's rated range.

If a quote jumps straight to "replace the board" without reading the flash code and testing flame signal, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan runs constantly, air never warm | Fan set to ON | Switch thermostat to AUTO | None needed |
| No heat at all, fan runs | Thermostat settings or dead batteries | Replace batteries, set to HEAT | Check thermostat wiring, 24 V |
| Weak airflow, short cold cycles | Clogged filter / closed vents | Replace filter, open vents | Check limit switch, temp rise |
| Igniter glows but no flame | Failed ignition / lockout | Wait for the auto-retry; at most one breaker reset | Test igniter, flame sensor, gas valve |
| Warms then goes cold mid-cycle | Flame loss / dirty sensor | None safe | Clean/replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Clicks, never fires | Pressure switch open / inducer | Check external condensate drain line | Test pressure switch, inducer, draft |
| Flash code repeats after reset | Board or component lockout | Stop resetting, call pro | Full diagnostic per the furnace's own flash chart |

## Repair costs

- **Thermostat setting / filter / batteries:** $0–$40 DIY
- **Service diagnostic call:** $90–$180
- **Flame sensor cleaning only:** often nothing beyond the diagnostic fee, since it's a short bench-free cleaning done during the visit
- **Flame sensor replacement:** $100–$250 installed
- **Hot surface igniter:** $150–$350
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350
- **Limit switch:** $150–$350
- **Inducer motor:** $400–$1,200 installed
- **Control board:** $400–$1,200 installed

Prices vary by region, model, and whether parts are under York's warranty. Labor is typically the larger share on no-heat calls.
