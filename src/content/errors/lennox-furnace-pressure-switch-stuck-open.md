## What this code means

Your Lennox furnace uses a **pressure (draft) switch** to confirm that the inducer motor is pulling air through the burner and venting combustion gases before the gas valve opens. On each call for heat, the control board energizes the inducer, then checks that the pressure switch has **closed**. If the switch stays **open** — meaning the board never sees adequate draft — the furnace will not ignite and the control reports a "pressure switch stuck open" (switch failed to close) condition.

This is a safety interlock working as designed: no proven draft, no gas. **How the fault is reported depends on your control board.** Some Lennox boards flash an LED pattern; others display or log a numeric fault code. The numbering is not the same across every board generation, so read the code from the diagnostic chart printed on your furnace's door label, or from the owner's/installation manual for your specific model, rather than assuming a number you found online applies to your unit. Whatever the display, the behavior is the same: on many Lennox controls the fault will **retry** several times, then **hold in lockout** until power is cycled. Because diagnosing it involves the venting and combustion system, it is **pro-level** work.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted vent/intake pipe** — snow, ice, debris, a bird nest, or a frozen condensate drip at the termination prevents proper draft.
2. **Clogged condensate trap or drain line** (condensing/90%+ furnaces) — a backed-up trap holds water in the pressure switch sensing line or inducer housing.
3. **Cracked, loose, or kinked pressure-switch hose** — the small rubber/silicone tube between the inducer and switch leaks or pinches off.
4. **Water or debris in the sensing port** — condensate or dust blocks the port on the inducer housing or switch.
5. **Weak or failing inducer motor** — the motor spins but doesn't move enough air to close the switch.
6. **Failed pressure switch** — the diaphragm sticks open or the switch is out of calibration.
7. **Blocked heat exchanger passages** — restricts airflow enough to prevent switch closure (less common).

Note: this is a **draft/venting** fault, not an ignition or gas-supply fault. Gas pressure and igniter problems are reported separately by the control.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform:

- **Check your thermostat** — set to Heat and the setpoint above room temperature; replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Reset power once** — flip the furnace switch or breaker off, wait 30 seconds, and back on to clear one lockout. If the fault returns, **stop** and call a pro.
- **Inspect visible vent terminations outdoors** — safely clear snow, ice, leaves, or a nest from the intake/exhaust pipes where you can reach them from the ground. Do not disassemble anything.
- **Look at the condensate line** — if you see standing water or a visibly clogged drain near the furnace, note it for your technician.
- **Confirm supply and return registers are open** throughout the home.
- **General maintenance (not a fix for this fault):** replace a dirty air filter if it's due. A restricted filter does not hold the pressure switch open — it causes other problems, such as limit trips — but a clean filter is good housekeeping before a service visit. Use the correct size and arrow direction.

Do **not** open the furnace cabinet, bypass the pressure switch, disconnect hoses, or reset the unit repeatedly.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Put a **manometer** on the pressure switch sensing port to read actual inducer draft against the setpoint printed on the switch body.
- Inspect the **pressure-switch hose** for cracks, kinks, moisture, or blockage, and clear the sensing ports.
- Check the **condensate trap and drain** for blockage and standing water.
- Inspect the full **vent and intake run** for restriction, slope, and blockage, including the termination.
- Test the **inducer motor** amperage and verify it reaches full speed; check the inducer housing for soot or debris.
- Verify the **switch itself** opens and closes at spec with the correct draft present.
- Inspect the heat exchanger passages if airflow restriction is suspected.

This sequence lets them separate a cheap hose/vent fix from an inducer or switch replacement — useful for sanity-checking a quote.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inducer runs, furnace never lights, then locks out | Switch not closing (vent/draft issue) | Clear visible vent blockage; reset once | Measure draft, trace venting restriction |
| Fault worse in cold/snow | Frozen vent termination or condensate | Clear snow/ice from exterior pipes | Thaw/clear line, check slope and trap |
| Gurgling or water near furnace | Clogged condensate trap/line | Note standing water | Clear trap and drain line |
| Inducer sounds weak or noisy | Failing inducer motor | None | Test amperage, replace inducer |
| Fault with clear vents and good draft | Failed/miscalibrated pressure switch | None | Test and replace pressure switch |
| Cracked/loose hose at inducer | Damaged sensing tube | None | Replace or reseat hose |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor, varies by region and model):

- **DIY vent/filter/condensate clearing:** **$0** if you clear a reachable blockage yourself.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** **$100 – $250**.
- **Pressure-switch hose replacement:** **$120 – $220**.
- **Condensate trap/drain clearing:** **$100 – $250**.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** **$150 – $350**.
- **Inducer motor/assembly replacement:** **$400 – $1,200 installed**, with the higher end typical on condensing and variable-speed models where the inducer assembly is proprietary and expensive.
- **Vent re-slope or repair:** **$150 – $450** depending on access and length.

Because the sensing ports, hose, switch, and inducer are all inside the cabinet, replacement is technician work — there are no homeowner-installable parts for this fault.
