## What this code means

"Leaking water" is a symptom, not a numbered error code. On a Carrier ductless system, it almost always means condensate from the indoor head is not reaching the drain hose.

Condensate is the water the indoor coil pulls out of the air in cooling and dry modes. It collects in a drain pan behind the louver. From there it flows by gravity, or through a small pump on some models, to a hose that exits through the wall. When that path is blocked, tilted or overwhelmed, water spills out of the bottom or front of the head.

This page covers the ductless indoor head specifically. For ducted central systems, see the separate Carrier AC leaking water page.

Whether the unit reports anything depends on the model:

- **Wall-mount heads:** Most gravity-drain heads simply leak and keep running. They show no code.
- **Cassette and slim-duct units with a built-in pump:** These usually have a float switch. When it trips, the unit stops cooling and may show a water-level alarm on the display. That alarm normally clears once the water drains and power is cycled, but this varies by series. Check your manual.

Water dripping from the **outdoor** unit is normal, especially during defrost in heat mode.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged condensate drain or drain pan.** Algae, slime, dust and insect nests block the pan outlet or the hose. This is the most common cause by far.
2. **Kinked, crushed, sagging or disconnected drain hose.** The hose needs a continuous downward slope. A dip that traps water, a kink behind the head, or a hose that has slipped off the pan spigot all cause leaks. The outlet end can also be buried in mulch, soil or ice.
3. **Indoor head not level, or drain tilted the wrong way.** Wall-mount heads are usually installed slightly lower on the drain side. A head that is level or tipped away from the drain lets water pool and spill. This is common after a new install or when the mounting plate shifts.
4. **Dirty filters or a dirty indoor coil.** Restricted airflow lets the coil drop below freezing. The ice later melts faster than the pan can drain it. Heavy dirt can also clog the pan directly.
5. **Failed condensate pump or stuck float switch.** This applies to cassette and ducted units, and to any head installed with an add-on pump.
6. **Low refrigerant charge causing coil icing.** This usually comes with weak cooling and frost on the lines. See the related icing pages.
7. **Wall-sleeve or line-set sweating.** Poorly insulated copper lines or an unsealed wall penetration can drip condensation. This is often mistaken for a drain leak.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Turn the unit off** with the remote if water is reaching walls, floors or outlets. Move valuables and lay down towels.
2. **Clean the filters.** Open the front panel the way the owner's manual shows, then slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them in lukewarm water, let them dry completely, and reinstall them. Clean them every 2 to 4 weeks in heavy use.
3. **Find the drain outlet outside.** It is usually a flexible hose near where the line set exits the wall.
   - Make sure the end is not buried, crushed, frozen or plugged with debris or insects. Clear any debris from the opening.
   - Check that the hose runs downhill along its whole visible length, with no kinks or upward loops.
4. **Watch the outlet while the unit runs in cooling mode.** On a humid day it should drip steadily within 15 to 30 minutes. If it never drips but the head does, the blockage is upstream and needs a technician.
5. **Eyeball the head with a small level** placed on top of the housing. Do not remove the head or bend the mounting plate. Just note what you see and tell the technician.
6. **Look for frost.** Check for ice on the visible copper fittings or inside the louver. If you see any, switch the unit to fan-only mode to thaw it, then call for service.
7. **If the unit stopped and shows a water-level alarm,** cycle power **once** at the breaker or disconnect after the water has drained. If the alarm returns, stop resetting it and call a pro.

Do not open the head's casing, remove the drain pan, or push wires or brushes into the pan outlet. Pans and their spigots crack easily, and the control board sits nearby.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Confirm where the water is coming from: the pan overflow, the drain spigot, sweating lines or the wall sleeve.
- Remove the front cover and louver assembly. Inspect and flush the drain pan, and clear the outlet and hose with a hand pump, nitrogen or a wet vac at the outside end.
- Check the hose's slope and connections all the way through the wall, and re-secure or replace insulated drain hose as needed.
- Check level and mounting-plate alignment, and reset the head's slope toward the drain side.
- Inspect and clean the evaporator coil and blower wheel. These may need a coil-cleaning bag and a low-pressure rinse.
- On pump-equipped units, test the pump, the float switch and the alarm wiring, and read any stored fault codes.
- If icing is present, measure airflow, refrigerant pressures, superheat and subcooling. If the charge is low, they should perform a leak search **before** adding refrigerant.

Expect a clear explanation of which item was at fault. A quote for refrigerant "top-off" alone, with no leak search, is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drips from head in cooling mode, no water at outside outlet | Clogged pan outlet or hose | Check outlet end for debris or crushing; turn unit off | Flush pan and line, clean pan |
| Water spills from one end of head | Head tilted away from drain | Check with a level and report it | Re-level head or mounting plate |
| Leak plus musty smell, black specks on louver | Slime or mold in pan and blower | Clean filters | Deep-clean coil, pan and blower wheel |
| Ice on coil or lines, then dripping | Dirty filters, low airflow or low charge | Clean filters; run fan-only to thaw | Airflow check, leak search, charge |
| Cassette or ducted unit stops, shows water alarm | Failed pump or stuck float | One power cycle after draining | Test or replace pump and float |
| Wet wall near line-set exit | Sweating lines or unsealed sleeve | Check for visible gaps | Re-insulate lines, seal penetration |
| Water from outdoor unit in winter | Normal defrost drainage | Keep area clear so ice doesn't build up | None unless ice blocks the unit |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges and vary by region and access.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter cleaning, clearing outside outlet (DIY) | $0 |
| Service call and diagnosis | $90 – $200 |
| Drain line and pan flush | $100 – $250 |
| Full indoor head deep clean (coil, pan, blower wheel) | $200 – $450 per head |
| Drain hose replacement or re-routing | $150 – $400 |
| Re-level or re-mount indoor head | $150 – $350 |
| Condensate pump replacement (cassette, ducted or add-on) | $200 – $500 |
| Line-set insulation repair or wall-sleeve sealing | $100 – $300 |
| Refrigerant leak search, repair and recharge | $400 – $1,500+ |

Refrigerant repairs depend on the leak location and on whether parts are covered under the Carrier warranty. Warranty coverage typically requires registration and proof of professional installation.
