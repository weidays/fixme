## What this code means

"Screen blank" is not a numbered error code. It means the display has gone dark. This page covers the **intermittent or situational** version, where the screen blanks under certain conditions or comes and goes.

If your thermostat is completely dead and never comes back, see the separate page Honeywell Thermostat Blank Screen: Fixes & Costs.

First, rule out display sleep. Many touchscreen and Wi-Fi models dim or turn off the screen after a period of inactivity. If the display is not simply sleeping, an intermittent blank screen almost always means power to the thermostat is unstable or is being interrupted. Which kind of interruption can blank your screen depends on how your model is powered. See the next section.

Once a temporary power problem clears, the display comes back automatically. There is no lockout to clear. A failed thermostat or a blown control board fuse will not recover on its own, though. Whether your settings survive depends on the model. Most current Honeywell models keep the schedule in memory, but some older battery models lose the clock.

## Is your model battery-powered, hardwired, or both?

How your thermostat gets power determines which causes below can actually blank the screen. Check your model's installation guide if you are unsure.

- **Battery-powered:** battery-only RTH models. The display runs on the batteries, so weak batteries are the main cause of blanking. If a float switch, door switch, breaker or transformer cuts 24V, the heating or cooling stops, but the screen usually stays lit.
- **Both (24V with battery backup):** FocusPRO/Pro units with batteries installed as backup. Weak batteries are again the main cause of blanking. If 24V is cut upstream, the batteries keep the display lit while the system stops.
- **Hardwired 24V only, no battery backup:** for example the T5, RTH6580WF, RTH8580WF and RTH9585WF. These models have no batteries to hold the display. Anything that interrupts 24V upstream can blank the screen, including a float switch, door switch, breaker, transformer, loose wiring or C-wire problem.

On a battery-powered or battery-backed model, the screen may stay on while the system has stopped. In that case, the upstream causes below may still explain the stopped system, even though they are not what is blanking your display.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Display sleep or backlight timeout.** Touchscreen and Wi-Fi models, such as the T-series and RTH9585, dim or turn off the display after inactivity, depending on settings. This is harmless and should be ruled out first. *Applies to:* touchscreen and Wi-Fi models.
2. **Weak or low-quality batteries.** The screen may dim or blank under load before the low-battery warning appears. Honeywell recommends replacing batteries with fresh alkaline cells. *Applies to:* battery-only RTH models and FocusPRO/Pro models with batteries installed as backup. This cause does not apply to 24V-only models without batteries.
3. **Condensate float switch tripping.** This happens in cooling season. A clogged drain lets water rise in the pan or line, and the float switch cuts 24V. The AC stops. As water drains, power may return, so the problem comes and goes. *Applies to:* blanking mainly on 24V-only models without battery backup. On battery-powered or battery-backed models, the AC stops but the screen usually stays lit.
4. **Missing, loose or unreliable C-wire on a hardwired model.** Most current Honeywell Wi-Fi models require a C-wire (or the Honeywell C-wire adapter) rather than power-stealing. If one was installed without a working C-wire, or the C-wire connection is poor, the thermostat can lose power when the equipment calls for heat or cooling. The result is a screen that blanks or flickers when the system starts. Power-stealing behavior and C-wire requirements vary by model. *Applies to:* hardwired models. Battery-powered models do not draw power from the heating or cooling circuit, so this cause does not apply to them.
5. **Furnace or air handler door not fully seated.** The door safety switch cuts 24V to the thermostat. A loose panel that shifts with blower vibration can blank the screen intermittently. *Applies to:* blanking mainly on 24V-only models without battery backup.
6. **Breaker or service switch issues.** A breaker that is tripping or a furnace service switch that is partly off will interrupt power. *Applies to:* blanking mainly on 24V-only models without battery backup.
7. **Loose thermostat wiring or a failing transformer or fuse (technician).** A connection that makes and breaks, or a transformer near failure, causes random blanking. *Applies to:* blanking mainly on 24V-only models without battery backup.
8. **Failing thermostat.** This is less common. *Applies to:* all models. On battery models, suspect it only after fresh batteries do not help. On hardwired models, suspect it only when a steady 24V supply is confirmed.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Display sleep:** Touch the screen or press a button. If the display wakes right away, it was likely sleeping. Check the display or backlight settings in your thermostat's menu or owner's manual if you want it to stay on longer.
- **Batteries (battery-powered or battery-backed models):** Pull the thermostat off its wallplate (most models lift straight off) and install fresh alkaline batteries. Do not use rechargeables unless your manual allows them.
- **Breaker and switch:** Confirm that the HVAC breaker is fully on and that the light-switch-style service switch near the furnace or air handler is on. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Condensate line:** Look at the drain pan and the condensate line at the indoor unit. If there is standing water, clear the line from the accessible cleanout. A wet/dry vac at the outdoor drain end works well.
- **Air filter:** A badly clogged filter can freeze the coil. The meltwater then overwhelms the drain and trips the float switch. Replace the filter if it is dirty.
- **Panels:** Press the furnace or air handler blower door firmly into place so the door switch is fully engaged. Do not tape or hold the switch closed.
- **Note the pattern:** Write down when the screen blanks, such as when heat or cooling starts, after rain, after a period of inactivity, or randomly. Also note whether your model has batteries. This helps a technician pinpoint the cause quickly.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Measure 24V between R and C at the thermostat and at the control board, both idle and during a heat or cooling call. This shows power stealing or voltage sag.
- Check the transformer output and the control board fuse (usually 3–5A). A blown or intermittent fuse often points to a shorted thermostat wire or a component on the low-voltage circuit.
- Test the condensate float switch and the door switch for continuity.
- Inspect thermostat wire connections at both ends for loose or corroded conductors.
- If there is no C-wire, recommend running a spare conductor or installing a common-wire adapter (Honeywell sells one for some models).

A credible quote should name the specific failed part or condition. "Replace thermostat" without a voltage test is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screen goes dark after inactivity, wakes on touch | Display sleep or backlight timeout (touchscreen and Wi-Fi models) | Adjust display settings | None needed |
| Screen dims or blanks, then returns | Weak batteries (battery-powered or battery-backed models) | Install fresh alkaline batteries | None needed |
| Blanks in summer, AC stops, water in pan | Condensate float switch tripped (24V-only models) | Clear the condensate line, replace the filter | Clear a deep clog, check the float switch and coil |
| Blanks or flickers when the system starts | Missing or unreliable C-wire (hardwired models) | Note the pattern | Add a C-wire or adapter |
| Blanks when the blower runs | Loose blower door (24V-only models) | Seat the panel firmly | Test or replace the door switch |
| Random blanking, breaker trips | Electrical fault (24V-only models) | Reset the breaker once | Find the short, replace the fuse or transformer |
| Stays blank with confirmed power | Failed thermostat | Replace with a compatible model | Install and wire the thermostat |
