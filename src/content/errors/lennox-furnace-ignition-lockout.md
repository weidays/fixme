## What this code means

An **ignition lockout** means your Lennox furnace tried to light its burners several times, failed to prove a stable flame each time, and then shut itself down as a safety measure. The control board closes the gas valve and stops trying so raw gas can't accumulate in the combustion chamber/burner box and vent system.

This is the furnace working *as designed*—the lockout is a symptom, not the root problem. Something in the ignition sequence (igniter, flame proving, gas delivery, or airflow) failed the required number of consecutive attempts.

On most Lennox controls the lockout is **timed and auto-resets after about one hour**, or you can clear it immediately by cycling power at the thermostat or breaker. Newer Lennox boards display a **numeric alert code**—read the legend on the wiring-compartment label to see what that code means on your control. Older boards flash an LED pattern instead. Because the display, code list, and trial count all vary by board generation, the label inside the wiring compartment and your unit's installation manual are the authoritative reference for your model.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty flame sensor** — The most common cause. A carbon- or oxide-coated flame rod can't conduct enough current to "prove" the flame, so the board assumes ignition failed and locks out.
2. **Weak or failed hot surface igniter** — A cracked or aged igniter may not reach ignition temperature, causing repeated failed trials.
3. **Insufficient gas supply** — A partially closed manual gas valve, low supply pressure, or a fault at the gas valve prevents a clean light-off.
4. **Blocked flue or combustion air / pressure switch issue** — Restricted venting or an inducer problem can prevent the pressure switch from closing, aborting ignition before or during the trial.
5. **Dirty or misaligned burners** — Debris, rust, or spider webs disrupt flame travel across the burners so the flame never reaches the sensor.
6. **Failed control board or gas valve** — Less common; a board that mis-times the ignition sequence or a valve that won't fully open will trigger repeated lockouts.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform. If they don't restore heat, call a licensed technician.

- **Replace a dirty air filter.** Severe restriction can trip limit and airflow faults that interrupt ignition. A clean filter is the cheapest fix.
- **Confirm the thermostat is set to HEAT** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Replace the thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Cycle power once.** Turn the furnace switch or breaker off for 30 seconds, then back on—this clears the lockout for **one** reset. If it locks out again, stop and call a pro.
- **Check that supply and return vents/registers are open** and unobstructed throughout the house.
- **Look at the condensate line** (on high-efficiency models). A clogged or frozen drain can back up and trip safety switches; clear any obvious external blockage.
- **Confirm the furnace door/panel is fully seated.** A loose front panel can hold open the door interlock switch and prevent operation.

Do **not** repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace, open the gas valve, or attempt any work inside the cabinet. If you look at the exterior vent termination outside the house, check only what is visible from outside—never reach into vent piping or take any part of the venting apart.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. **Pull the fault history** from the control board and confirm the lockout code and trial count.
2. **Watch a full ignition cycle**—verifying the inducer starts, the pressure switch closes, the igniter glows, and the gas valve opens on schedule.
3. **Measure flame current (microamps)** with a meter at the flame sensor to see if a weak or dirty sensor is failing to prove flame.
4. **Test the hot surface igniter** for resistance and visible cracking.
5. **Check gas supply and manifold pressure** against the rating plate.
6. **Inspect and clean burners and the flame sensor**, and verify venting and the pressure switch operate correctly.
7. **Confirm the fix** by running several complete heat cycles with no lockout.

This sequence lets you sanity-check a quote: a good tech isolates the *cause* of the failed ignition rather than just clearing the code.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furnace clicks and igniter glows but no sustained flame | Dirty flame sensor | One reset; replace filter | Clean/replace flame sensor, verify microamp reading |
| Igniter never glows | Failed hot surface igniter | One reset | Test and replace igniter |
| Burners light briefly then shut off, repeats then locks out | Flame not proving / low gas | One reset | Check gas pressure, clean burners, test flame sensor |
| Inducer runs but no ignition attempt | Pressure switch not closing / blocked flue | Look at the exterior vent termination only, from outside, for obvious blockage (nest, snow, debris) — never reach into or disassemble vent piping | Test pressure switch, inspect inducer and venting |
| Locks out immediately, no attempt | Control board or gas valve fault | One reset | Test board and gas valve, replace if faulty |
| Lockout after a very dirty filter | Airflow/limit interaction | Replace filter, reset once | Verify limit switch and airflow |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US pricing including parts and labor. A diagnostic visit is often $80–$180 and may be credited toward the repair.

- **Reset / no parts needed:** $0 DIY (or the diagnostic fee if a tech visits)
- **Flame sensor clean or replacement:** $80–$250
- **Hot surface igniter replacement:** $150–$350
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$350
- **Gas valve replacement:** $400–$900 installed
- **Control board replacement:** $400–$1,200 installed, depending on the board and whether it's a communicating control
- **Burner cleaning / inducer service:** $150–$450 depending on scope

Prices vary by region, model, and whether the part is under Lennox's parts warranty. Communicating and higher-end boards sit at the top of the range, so a quote near the high end isn't automatically inflated.
