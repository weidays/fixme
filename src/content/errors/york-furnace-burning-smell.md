## What this code means

"Burning smell" is not a York flash code. It's a symptom, and it can range from harmless to dangerous. **Treat it as an emergency until you've ruled out the serious causes.**

**Do this first if the smell is anything more than faint dust:**

1. **Smoke, visible flame, or a strong electrical or plastic odor:** set the thermostat to OFF and switch off the furnace at its service switch or breaker. Leave the house and call **911**.
2. **Rotten-egg or sulfur smell:** this is a gas leak, not a burning smell. Do not touch switches. Leave the house now and call your **gas utility's emergency line or 911** from outside. See the York Furnace Gas Smell page.
3. **Headache, nausea or dizziness, or a carbon monoxide alarm:** get everyone outside and call 911.

Once everyone is safe, the odor itself helps narrow down the cause:

- **Dusty or burnt-dust smell:** usually the first startup of the season. Normal if it fades within an hour or so.
- **Hot electrical, burning plastic or fishy smell:** an overheating motor, wiring, capacitor or board. This is serious.
- **Burning rubber:** often a failing blower motor or a melting wire jacket.
- **Smoky or exhaust smell:** possible venting or heat exchanger problem. Shut down and call for service.

Before cutting power, note any LED flash code through the sight glass on the blower door. Shutting off power clears the stored code on most York boards.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dust burn-off at the start of the season.** Dust settles on the heat exchanger and burners over summer. It's harmless and fades within the first few cycles.
2. **Clogged air filter.** Restricted airflow overheats the heat exchanger and strains the blower motor. It often comes with 4 flashes (open limit) on York boards.
3. **Overheating blower motor or failing run capacitor.** Worn bearings or a weak capacitor make the motor run hot. You'll notice a hot-electrical or burning-rubber odor, sometimes with humming. Relevant to PSC-motor models; ECM-equipped models fail differently.
4. **Electrical component or wiring overheating.** A loose connection, a shorted transformer, or a burnt spot on the control board.
5. **Foreign object or debris.** Items stored against the furnace, something dropped into a floor register, or a plastic item near the cabinet.
6. **Flame or venting problem.** Combustion products or rollout scorching wiring near the burners. See the York Furnace Flame Rollout page. Any exhaust smell needs immediate professional attention.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Only do these with the furnace **turned off**, and only if there is no smoke, flame, gas odor or CO alarm.

- **Judge the smell and its timing.** Faint dust smell in the first hour of the first cold day? Open a window and let it run while you watch it. If it doesn't fade within a few hours, shut the furnace down.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it's gray, clogged, or more than about 3 months old.
- **Clear around the furnace.** Remove anything stored against or on top of the cabinet. Look into floor registers for dropped items. Don't reach into ductwork beyond what you can see.
- **Check the visible vents.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and unblocked. Outside, make sure intake and exhaust pipes on high-efficiency models are clear of debris or snow.
- **Confirm the blower door is seated.** If it isn't, the door safety switch keeps the furnace from running properly. Don't remove the panel to look inside.
- **Reset only once.** If the smell was clearly dust and the furnace locked out, you may restore power **once**. If any electrical or plastic smell returns, turn it off and leave it off.

Don't open the cabinet, touch wiring, or try to test the motor or capacitor yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- They'll ask what the smell was like, when it started, and whether there was a flash code.
- They'll inspect the cabinet for discoloration, melted insulation, burnt connectors, and scorch marks on the control board or transformer.
- They'll test the blower motor's amp draw against its nameplate, check the run capacitor's microfarads, and spin the wheel to feel for bearing drag.
- They'll check temperature rise against the range on the rating plate, and check static pressure for airflow restriction.
- They'll inspect the burners and rollout switches for signs of flame rollout.
- They'll inspect the venting, and may run a combustion and CO test. If they suspect the heat exchanger, they'll inspect it visually or with a camera.

A legitimate quote should name the specific failed part and show evidence of it, such as a burnt connector, a failed capacitor reading, or high motor amps.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dusty smell on first cold-weather startup | Dust burn-off | Ventilate and watch for 1–2 hours | None, unless the smell persists |
| Hot smell plus weak airflow, 4 flashes | Clogged filter or overheating | Turn off, replace the filter, one reset | Check temperature rise, static pressure and limit switch |
| Burning rubber or electrical smell, humming | Blower motor or capacitor failing | Shut off power and call | Test and replace the capacitor or motor |
| Burning plastic, possible smoke | Wiring, transformer or board overheating | Power off; call 911 if smoke | Repair wiring, replace the board or transformer |
| Smell only after running a while, item near the unit | Object against the cabinet or in a register | Remove visible items, clear registers | Inspect if the smell continues |
| Smoky or exhaust smell, CO alarm | Venting or combustion problem | Evacuate and call 911 | Vent repair, combustion test, heat exchanger check |

## Repair costs

| Repair | Typical US cost |
|---|---|
| Dust burn-off | $0 |
| Air filter (DIY) | $10–$40 |
| Service call or diagnostic | $90–$200 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $120–$250 |
| PSC blower motor replacement | $400–$800 |
| ECM blower motor replacement | $700–$1,500 |

| Transformer replacement | $150–$300 |
| Control board replacement | $300–$700 |
| Rollout switch or vent repair | $150–$600 |

Costs vary by region, model and warranty status. York parts warranties often cover motors and boards, but labor may not be covered.
