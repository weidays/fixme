## What this code means

"Ice on outdoor unit" is not a fault code that an Amana board displays. It is a symptom you see. Use this page to answer one question first: is this normal frost or a problem?

**Normal frost**
- Below about 40°F in humid weather, a heat pump's outdoor coil runs colder than the air around it. Frost builds up on the coil.
- Amana defrost controls handle this automatically. On conventional models, the board checks a coil sensor at a set run-time interval, typically 30, 60, 90 or 120 minutes, set at the factory or by the installer.
- When defrost starts, the reversing valve shifts and the outdoor fan stops. The coil warms and melts the frost, often with a cloud of steam. Normal heating then resumes.

**Abnormal ice**
- Any of these signs means something is wrong:
  - Thick, clear ice that covers the coil solidly
  - Ice on the top panel or fan grille
  - Ice built up from the base pan upward
  - Ice that is still there after several hours
- The pages listed below already cover two specific failures:
  - The defrost system not working (Amana Heat Pump Frozen, Not Defrosting)
  - A stopped outdoor fan (Amana AC Outdoor Fan Not Spinning)

This page covers the full set of ice causes, including installation and site problems.

**Reset behavior:** There is no lockout to reset for ice itself. Inverter and communicating models may log their own fault codes alongside the ice. Those codes follow the reset rules in that model's service manual.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal frost in a defrost cycle that hasn't run yet.** A light, even frost that clears within an hour or two is expected operation.
2. **Water dripping or splashing onto the unit.**
   - Roof runoff, a missing gutter, or a leaking downspout over the unit causes heavy top ice.
   - Defrost cannot clear this kind of ice, because water keeps refreezing on the cabinet top and fan.
3. **Unit sitting too low or blocked by snow and drifts.**
   - Amana installation instructions call for raising the unit above the expected snow line so meltwater can drain.
   - A unit set on the ground lets defrost water refreeze in the base pan and climb into the coil.
4. **Defrost control problem.** The cause may be a failed or loose coil (defrost) thermostat or sensor, a failed defrost board, or a defrost interval set too long for your climate. The board and sensor are covered in detail on the not-defrosting page.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.**
   - Low charge drops coil temperature and pressure, so frost builds faster.
   - It is often uneven, with patchy ice on part of the coil or the lower circuits.
6. **Reversing valve or outdoor fan relay problems during defrost.**
   - The valve may not shift.
   - The fan may keep running during defrost and blow away the heat that should melt the coil.
7. **Low indoor airflow while heating.** A clogged filter or closed registers can upset system pressures and contribute to icing. This is a less common primary cause.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Watch for a defrost cycle.** Leave the system in Heat and observe for up to 90–120 minutes. Steam rising off the coil and frost clearing means the system is likely working normally.
2. **Check the thermostat.**
   - Confirm it is set to Heat with a sensible setpoint.
   - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
   - If the ice is heavy, switch to **Emergency/Aux Heat** to protect the compressor while the ice melts.
3. **Replace or check the air filter.** A filthy filter restricts airflow. Install a fresh one.
4. **Open the supply registers and clear return grilles.** Make sure furniture and rugs are not blocking them.
5. **Look above the unit.**
   - Check for dripping eaves, overflowing gutters, or a downspout aimed at or near the unit.
   - Redirecting a downspout or clearing a clogged gutter is safe and often solves top-down icing.
6. **Clear snow from around the unit.** Use a broom or your hands, never a shovel against the coil. Keep at least 12 inches clear on all sides.
7. **Check the breaker and outdoor disconnect.** Make sure both are on. If a breaker has tripped, you may reset it **once**. If it trips again, call a technician.
8. **Confirm the access panels are seated.** Panels should be in place and secure, but do not open them.

**Do not** chip, pry, or hose ice off with high pressure. **Do not** open the cabinet. Refrigerant, the defrost sensor, the board, and capacitors are technician items.

## How a technician will diagnose it

1. **Confirm the ice pattern.**
   - Even frost suggests normal operation.
   - Top-down ice suggests water intrusion.
   - Bottom-up ice suggests drainage or elevation problems.
   - Patchy ice suggests a charge problem.
2. **Force a defrost cycle.** Amana defrost boards have test pins that shorten the timing so the tech can verify:
   - The reversing valve shifts
   - The outdoor fan stops
   - Auxiliary heat comes on
   - The cycle ends once the coil warms
3. **Test the defrost thermostat or coil sensor.** The tech checks continuity or resistance against the spec for that model and confirms the sensor is clamped tightly to the correct tube.
4. **Check the defrost interval jumper.** On conventional boards, this setting may be changed for humid climates.
5. **Measure refrigerant pressures and temperatures** (superheat and subcooling, or the manufacturer's heating charge chart). If the charge is low, the tech looks for a leak.
6. **Check fan motor and relay operation** during defrost.
7. **Check inverter and communicating models.** On models such as AVZC, the tech reads stored fault codes from the outdoor control or thermostat.
8. **Recommend site corrections.** These may include a riser stand, gutter changes, or base-pan drainage fixes.

If a quote names "add refrigerant" without mentioning a leak search, ask about the leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thin white frost that clears with steam | Normal defrost operation | None; keep observing | None needed |
| Thick ice on top and fan grille | Roof or gutter runoff | Fix the gutter or redirect the downspout; use Emergency Heat until melted | Install a protective hood or relocate the unit if needed |
| Ice building up from the base | Unit too low, snow drift, poor drainage | Clear snow around the unit | Install a riser stand and clear the base-pan drains |
| Whole coil encased, never defrosts | Defrost sensor or board failure | Switch to Emergency Heat | Test the sensor and board; replace the failed part |
| Fan keeps running during defrost | Defrost board or fan relay fault | Switch to Emergency Heat | Diagnose and replace the board or relay |
| Patchy ice on part of coil | Low refrigerant charge or leak | Switch to Emergency Heat | Find the leak, repair it, evacuate, and recharge |
| No steam, no defrost sound, cold air indoors | Reversing valve or solenoid fault | Switch to Emergency Heat | Test the solenoid coil; replace the valve if it is stuck |
| Weak airflow indoors plus icing | Dirty filter or closed registers | Replace the filter and open the registers | Check the blower if airflow stays low |
