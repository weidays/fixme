## What this code means

"Ice on indoor coil" is not a numbered fault code. It means frost or ice has formed on the evaporator coil inside your Bryant ductless head. You may see white frost behind the front grille or louvers. Other signs include weak or warm airflow and water dripping as the ice melts.

The coil ices when it runs below 32°F. That happens for two reasons:
- **Too little warm air crosses the coil.** Common causes are dirty filters, a clogged blower wheel, or a low fan speed.
- **The refrigerant inside is too cold.** Common causes are low charge, a metering valve problem, or a bad sensor reading.

Most Bryant ductless boards watch an indoor coil temperature sensor. When the coil gets too cold, the board runs freeze protection. It shuts down or slows the compressor until the coil warms, then restarts on its own. Whether you also see a blinking light or display code depends on the model and board. Some units signal it and many give no code at all.

This page covers icing on the **indoor ductless head**. The following topics have their own pages:
- Ducted central-AC freezing: "Frozen Evaporator Coil"

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty indoor mesh filters.** This is by far the most common cause on ductless heads. The filters clog in weeks in dusty homes or homes with pets.
2. **Dirty blower wheel or coil face.** The squirrel-cage blower wheel in wall heads builds up dust, mold, and biofilm. That cuts airflow even after the filters are cleaned. This is very common on units older than 2 to 3 years without professional cleaning.
3. **Operating conditions.** Any of these keeps the coil very cold for long periods:
   - Setpoint at the minimum, around 61–62°F
   - Fan locked on Low or Quiet
   - Long Dry-mode run times
   - Cooling at night when the outdoor temperature is low
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Flare connections at the indoor head or outdoor service valves are the classic leak points on mini-splits. Brazed joints and coils can also leak.
5. **Indoor coil temperature sensor fault.** A drifted or failed sensor can stop the board's freeze protection from working.
6. **Indoor fan motor or fan-control problem.** A motor running slow or stopping intermittently, or a board not driving the fan correctly, reduces airflow.
7. **Multi-zone metering problems.** On multi-zone systems, these can send too much refrigerant to one head:
   - A sticking electronic expansion valve (EEV)
   - Crossed wiring or piping between zones
8. **Restricted refrigerant circuit.** Examples are a kinked line set or a restriction at a flare or strainer. This is less common.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Thaw first.** Switch to Fan-only or turn the unit off, and let the ice melt naturally. Lay towels under the head. Don't chip or heat the coil.
2. **Wash the filters.**
   - Lift the front panel as your owner's manual shows and slide out the mesh filters.
   - Rinse them with lukewarm water, or use a mild soap if they're greasy.
   - Let them air-dry completely, then reinstall.
   - Never run the unit without filters.
3. **Check the remote settings.**
   - Make sure the unit is in Cool, not Dry.
   - Set the fan to Auto or Medium/High instead of Low or Quiet.
   - Raise the setpoint to a normal 72–76°F.
   - Replace the remote batteries if the display is dim or settings don't stick.
4. **Clear the airflow path.** Move curtains, furniture, or shelves that block the head's intake on top or the discharge louvers. Make sure the louvers are not closed down.
5. **Check the condensate drain.** Once thawed, confirm water drains from the outside end of the drain hose. Clear any obvious blockage at the outlet and any kinks you can see.
6. **Reset power once.** If the unit shows a code or won't restart after thawing:
   - Turn off the breaker or disconnect for about 5 minutes, then restore power.
   - Do this only once.
   - If it ices or faults again, stop and call a pro.
7. **Watch it run.** After thawing and clean filters, run Cool with the fan on High for a few hours. Frost returning within a day points to an internal cause.

Do not remove the head's cover or touch the coil, blower wheel, or wiring. Do not use spray-in coil cleaners or "bag" cleaning kits yourself. Overspray onto the control board and motor is a common way to cause expensive damage.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should:
- **Confirm airflow.** Inspect the blower wheel and coil face with a light. Check fan speed and the motor's operation.
- **Read system pressures and temperatures.** Measure superheat and subcooling with the system fully thawed. Readings taken on an iced coil are meaningless.
- **Leak-check if the charge is low.** Use an electronic detector or bubble solution, starting with the indoor and outdoor flare joints. Nitrogen pressure testing may be needed for slow leaks.
- **Test the indoor coil sensor.** Check its resistance against the temperature chart in the Bryant service manual for your model.
- **Pull stored fault history** where the board supports it.
- **On multi-zone systems:**
  - Run each zone alone.
  - Verify EEV operation.
  - Confirm piping and wiring go to matching ports.

Be wary of a quote that offers to "top off" refrigerant without a leak search. Also question a full system replacement before airflow and charge have been checked.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frost on coil, weak airflow, gray filters | Clogged mesh filters | Thaw, wash and dry filters | None if this solves it |
| Clean filters but still weak, musty airflow | Dirty blower wheel or coil | Thaw; set fan to Auto/High | Remove and deep-clean blower wheel and coil |
| Ices overnight or on minimum setpoint | Low setpoint, Low fan, long Dry runs | Raise setpoint, fan to Auto, use Cool not Dry | Check settings if it persists |
| Ices plus poor cooling, oily spot at a fitting | Refrigerant leak | Thaw and turn off; don't keep running it | Leak search and repair, vacuum, weighed charge |
| Ices repeatedly, airflow normal | Faulty indoor coil sensor or board | One power reset | Test sensor against chart, replace sensor or board |
| Fan stops or surges, then coil frosts | Indoor fan motor or fan-control fault | Note the pattern for the tech | Test and replace motor or board |
| Only one zone of a multi-zone ices | EEV, cross-wiring or piping mismatch | Note which zone and when | Verify zone wiring and piping, service the EEV |
| Water dripping as ice melts | Overflow during thaw, possibly a partial drain clog | Towels; clear drain outlet | Clean pan and drain line |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter washing (DIY) | $0 |
| Professional maintenance visit and coil rinse | $100 – $250 per head |
| Deep clean of blower wheel and coil (wheel removal or wrap-and-wash) | $200 – $500 per head |
| Re-make a leaking flare connection, with vacuum and recharge | $250 – $700 |
| Leak search plus repair on line set or coil, with recharge | $500 – $1,500+ |
| Indoor evaporator coil replacement | $800 – $2,000+ (often weighed against replacing the head) |
| Indoor coil temperature sensor | $150 – $350 |
| Indoor fan motor | $300 – $700 |
| Indoor or outdoor control board | $350 – $900 |
| Multi-zone EEV coil or valve | $300 – $900 |

Check warranty coverage before paying for parts. Many registered Bryant ductless systems carry 10-year parts coverage, though labor is usually separate.
