## What this code means

"Not heating" is a symptom, not a single fault code. It means your Rheem ductless mini-split is running in heat mode but isn't delivering warm air, or isn't delivering enough of it.

Because a mini-split is a heat pump, heating relies on several parts working together:
- the reversing valve,
- the inverter-driven compressor,
- correct refrigerant charge,
- clear airflow at both the indoor and outdoor units.

Some causes are normal and temporary:
- the warm-start delay when heat mode starts,
- defrost cycles in cold, damp weather,
- reduced output below the model's rated low-ambient limit.

Others are genuine faults that need a licensed technician.

If the indoor unit shows an error code or a flashing LED pattern, write it down. Code meanings vary by Rheem series, and the code tells the technician where to start.

Whether a fault clears on its own or holds until power is cycled also varies by model and by the specific fault. Your owner's manual or the installer's service documentation is the authority here.

This page covers ductless mini-splits only. Ducted systems are covered separately. See "Rheem Heat Pump Not Heating" and "Rheem Heat Pump Blowing Cold Air in Heat Mode."

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Wrong mode or setting.** The remote is set to Cool, Auto, Dry or Fan. Or the setpoint is below room temperature. Or a timer or eco/sleep mode is limiting output.
2. **Normal warm-start delay or defrost cycle.** The indoor fan pauses while the coil warms up. During defrost, heating stops for several minutes. Both are normal.
3. **Dirty indoor filters or blocked airflow.** Clogged mesh filters, or furniture or curtains blocking the indoor head, reduce heat output. They can also trigger high-pressure or overheat protection in heat mode.
4. **Outdoor unit blocked by snow, ice or debris.** If the outdoor coil can't absorb heat, output drops. Heavy icing that won't clear is a fault. See "Rheem Heat Pump Frozen, Not Defrosting."
5. **Outdoor temperature below the model's heating range.** Non-cold-climate models lose much of their capacity in deep cold.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** This is common at flare connections on mini-splits. It causes weak heat, icing and eventually protection shutdowns.
7. **Sensor faults.** A failed indoor room or coil thermistor, or an outdoor coil, ambient or discharge thermistor, gives the control board wrong readings. These often show up as an error code.
8. **Reversing valve problem.** The valve or its coil sticks in cooling position. The usual sign is cool air in heat mode while the unit cools normally.
9. **Inverter board, outdoor fan motor or compressor failure.** These are less common but expensive. They usually come with an error code.
10. **Indoor/outdoor communication error.** Wiring problems between the units, or a board fault, stop the outdoor unit from running. This typically displays a code.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the remote.** Confirm it's set to Heat (often a sun icon). Set the temperature at least 3–5°F above room temperature. Cancel any timers, and turn off sleep or eco modes temporarily.
- **Replace the remote batteries.** Point the remote at the indoor head and listen for the beep that confirms it received the signal.
- **Wait out the start-up and defrost periods.** Give the unit 10–15 minutes after switching on. If the outdoor unit is steaming or the indoor fan has paused, it is probably defrosting. Let it finish.
- **Clean the indoor filters.** Lift the front panel as your owner's manual shows, then slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them in lukewarm water, let them dry fully, and reinstall. Do this monthly during heavy use.
- **Clear around both units.** Move furniture or drapes away from the indoor head. Gently brush loose snow away from the outdoor unit. Keep 2 feet clear around it. Never chip at ice or spray the coil with water.
- **Check the breaker and outdoor disconnect.** If a breaker has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Do one power reset.** If an error code shows, turn off the breaker for about 5 minutes, then restore power once. If the code returns, don't keep resetting.
- **Check the condensate drain in heat mode.** In heat mode, water drains from the outdoor unit. Make sure the drain path isn't frozen into a sheet of ice. Don't try to remove the cabinet or panels.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified technician will typically:

1. **Read and record the error code** or diagnostic LED pattern, and confirm what it means for your exact model and board.
2. **Measure temperatures.** They check supply versus return air temperature at the indoor head. They also measure outdoor ambient and outdoor coil temperatures.
3. **Verify the reversing valve** is getting its signal and actually shifting, by comparing line temperatures.
4. **Check refrigerant pressures**, along with superheat or subcooling where the manufacturer provides data for that. If the charge is low, they should leak-search the flare joints and coils with electronic detection or bubble solution. Simply "topping off" without a leak search isn't a proper repair.
5. **Test the thermistors** against the resistance-versus-temperature chart for that model.
6. **Check voltage and communication** between the indoor and outdoor units, and inspect the interconnect wiring.
7. **Evaluate the inverter board, outdoor fan motor and compressor** if codes or readings point there.

Ask what code or measurement led to the diagnosis. Be cautious about a compressor or board replacement quoted without pressure readings, temperature readings or a code.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool air right after turning on | Warm-start delay or wrong mode | Confirm Heat mode and wait 10 minutes | None if it resolves |
| Heat stops, outdoor unit steaming | Normal defrost | Wait 5–15 minutes | None unless defrost repeats constantly |
| Weak heat, low airflow | Dirty filters or blocked indoor head | Clean filters and clear the area | Clean the blower wheel and coil if heavily soiled |
| Weak heat only in very cold weather | Below the model's rated range | Add supplemental heat | Assess sizing or recommend a cold-climate model |
| Outdoor coil iced solid | Defrost fault, low charge or fan issue | Clear snow around the unit; don't chip ice | Test defrost sensors, fan motor and charge |
| Cool air in heat mode, cools fine | Reversing valve or valve coil | None | Diagnose and replace the coil or valve |
| Weak heat that gets worse over weeks | Refrigerant leak | None | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Error code shown, no heat | Sensor, communication, board or compressor fault | One power reset | Diagnose by code and repair the component |
| Remote doesn't respond | Dead batteries or remote fault | Replace batteries | Check the indoor receiver or board |

## Repair costs

These are typical US price ranges including labor. Actual prices vary by region and by whether the system is under warranty.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Settings, batteries or filter cleaning (DIY) | $0–$20 |
| Service call or diagnostic fee | $100–$250 |
| Professional indoor coil and blower cleaning | $150–$400 per head |
| Thermistor or sensor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Reversing valve solenoid coil | $150–$350 |
| Leak search and repair with recharge | $300–$1,200+ (more if the leak is in a coil) |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350–$800 |
| Reversing valve replacement | $700–$1,500 |
| Inverter or control board | $400–$1,200 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500–$3,000+ (often weighed against replacing the outdoor unit) |

Rheem parts warranties usually require that the system was registered and professionally installed. Check your coverage before you approve major repairs.
