## What this code means

Eight flashes on your American Standard furnace's diagnostic LED means the control board is seeing a **low flame-sense signal** — the tiny electrical current the flame sensor detects when a real flame is present is too weak.

The furnace proves a flame is burning by passing a small microamp current through the flame to the sensor rod. When that signal is strong, the board keeps the gas valve open. When it reads low — from a dirty or corroded sensor, a poor connection, or an actually weak flame — the control questions whether it has a safe, stable flame and can drop the burner and post this code.

In practice, this is one of the most common and least alarming furnace codes. The overwhelming cause is a **flame sensor coated in oxidation or dust**. It is not a gas-supply lockout and not a control-circuit failure — those are different codes. On most American Standard integrated furnace controls the unit will retry ignition, but a persistent weak signal will hold the furnace in a soft lockout until the underlying issue is cleaned or repaired.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or oxidized flame sensor** — by far the most likely. A thin coating on the sensor rod chokes the microamp signal. Routine and inexpensive to correct.
2. **Loose or corroded flame-sensor wire/connection** — a poor connection at the sensor or board terminal weakens the signal.
3. **Sensor positioned wrong or partially out of the flame** — a bent or shifted rod reads a weaker flame.
4. **Weak or lifting flame from partially blocked/dirty burners** — less flame area over the rod means less current.
5. **Poor furnace ground / control board ground** — flame sensing relies on a good chassis ground; a bad ground reads as low current.
6. **Failing flame sensor** — cracked ceramic insulator or a worn rod that no longer conducts reliably.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do. Everything involving the sensor, burners, or wiring is inside the cabinet and is technician work.

- **Check the air filter.** A clogged filter can starve airflow and disturb the flame; replace a dirty one.
- **Confirm the thermostat is set to Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Replace thermostat batteries if it uses them.
- **Check the furnace breaker** and the service switch (looks like a light switch) on or near the furnace. Reset a tripped breaker once.
- **Look at supply and return registers** throughout the house — make sure they are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Check the condensate drain** (high-efficiency models) for standing water or a blocked line; a full trap can trigger unrelated shutdowns.
- **Cycle power once.** Turn the furnace off at the switch for 30 seconds, then back on, to clear a single soft lockout. If it returns, call a pro — do not keep resetting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will confirm the 8-flash code, then:

- **Measure flame current in microamps** with a meter in series with the flame-sense circuit while the burner is lit, comparing it to the board's minimum spec (often around 0.5–1.0 µA minimum, healthy readings frequently 3–6 µA).
- **Inspect and clean the flame sensor rod** with fine abrasive, then re-measure to confirm the signal recovered.
- **Check the sensor wire, terminal, and connector** for corrosion or looseness, and reseat or repair.
- **Verify furnace ground** at the chassis and control board, since grounding is essential to flame sensing.
- **Inspect the burners and flame pattern** to confirm a strong, steady flame is reaching the rod.
- **Replace the flame sensor** if cleaning doesn't restore an adequate signal.

If a tech quotes a whole control board or gas valve for an 8-flash code without first measuring flame current and cleaning the sensor, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furnace lights then shuts off after a few seconds, repeats | Dirty flame sensor | Cycle power once; replace filter | Clean sensor, measure flame current |
| 8 flashes after a new install or recent service | Sensor wire loose or misplaced rod | None — leave the cabinet closed | Reseat wire, reposition sensor |
| Intermittent heat, code comes and goes | Corroded connection or marginal ground | Cycle power once | Repair connection, verify ground |
| Code returns soon after a sensor cleaning | Worn or cracked flame sensor | None | Replace flame sensor |
| Weak, lazy, or yellow-tipped flame | Dirty burners reducing flame current | Replace dirty filter | Clean burners, inspect combustion |
| Locks out after several retries | Persistent low flame signal | One reset only | Full flame-sense diagnosis |

## Repair costs

- **Flame sensor cleaning (service call):** $100 – $180 in most areas, often the whole fix.
- **Flame sensor replacement:** $150 – $250 installed; the part alone is typically $15 – $40.
- **Wiring/connection or ground repair:** $100 – $200 depending on access.
- **Burner cleaning/tune-up:** $150 – $350 if the flame itself is weak.
- **Air filter (DIY):** $10 – $30.

Most homeowners land at the low end — a dirty flame sensor cleaned during a routine service call. Replacing a control board or gas valve is rarely necessary for this code.
