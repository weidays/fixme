## What this code means

Six flashes on your Goodman furnace control board means an **open rollout (auxiliary limit) switch**. This is a manual-reset safety device mounted near the burners. It trips when flames "roll out" of the burner box instead of staying inside the heat exchanger where they belong.

This is one of the most serious codes your furnace can throw. A tripped rollout switch is not a nuisance fault — it is the furnace's last line of defense against flames and combustion gases escaping into your equipment cabinet and, potentially, your home. The switch is **manual-reset**: the furnace holds in lockout and will **not** clear by cycling power. Only a technician should reset it, and only after finding and fixing the cause.

**Do not reset this switch repeatedly.** If the rollout switch keeps tripping, flames are escaping the burner box on each cycle — a direct carbon monoxide hazard.

**If you smell gas, smell burning, or a CO alarm sounds:** shut the furnace off at the switch or breaker, leave the house, and call your gas utility's emergency line or 911 from outside. Do not go back inside to troubleshoot.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted flue / vent** — A clogged or obstructed vent (bird nest, debris, ice, disconnected pipe) forces combustion gases to back up, pushing flames out of the burner box. This is the most common trigger.
2. **Cracked or corroded heat exchanger** — A crack changes pressures and airflow around the burners, causing flames to roll out. This is a serious, no-repair-the-part condition — a cracked heat exchanger must be replaced (or the furnace replaced).
3. **Burner problems** — Dirty, misaligned, or rusted burners, or debris in the burner ports, can cause uneven flames that reach the rollout switch.
4. **Blocked inducer or condensate passages** — On high-efficiency models, blockages that reduce combustion draft can cause flames to spill.
5. **Failed rollout switch itself** — Occasionally the switch is faulty or has degraded, but a technician must rule out the genuine safety causes first before assuming this.

Goodman's own guidance treats this as a serious fault requiring inspection — not a switch to simply reset and forget.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Keep to these safe, outside-the-cabinet checks only:

- **Air filter** — A severely clogged filter restricts airflow and contributes to overheating. Replace it if it's dirty.
- **Thermostat** — Confirm it's set to Heat and the temperature is above room temp. Replace dead batteries if applicable.
- **Breaker / furnace switch** — Confirm the furnace breaker isn't tripped and the service switch is on.
- **Return and supply registers/vents** — Make sure they're open and unblocked to allow proper airflow.
- **Visible flue/vent termination outside** — From the ground, look for obvious blockage at the outdoor vent cap (nest, debris, snow). Do not disassemble venting.
- **Condensate line (high-efficiency models)** — If the drain is obviously overflowing or clogged, a backed-up condensate system can affect operation.

**Do not** open the burner compartment, reset the rollout switch, or touch any wiring, burners, or sensors. Those are technician tasks.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should:

- Confirm the 6-flash code and verify the rollout switch is actually open with a multimeter.
- **Inspect the heat exchanger** for cracks, holes, or corrosion (visual, camera scope, or combustion analysis).
- Check the flue/vent for blockage or improper slope and the inducer draft.
- Inspect and clean the burners, checking flame pattern and alignment.
- Run a **combustion analysis** to measure CO and confirm safe operation.
- Only after correcting the root cause will they manually reset the rollout switch and verify the furnace fires safely through several cycles.

If a tech offers to reset the switch without inspecting the heat exchanger and flue, that's a red flag — insist on a full combustion inspection.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 flashes, no heat, furnace locked out | Open rollout switch tripped | Shut furnace off; replace dirty filter; call a pro | Diagnose root cause, inspect heat exchanger, reset switch |
| Burning smell near furnace | Flames rolling out of burner box | Shut down, ventilate, call for service | Combustion analysis, burner/flue inspection |
| CO alarm sounding | Combustion gases escaping | Leave home, call gas utility/911 from outside | Full safety inspection before restart |
| Code returns after any reset | Cracked heat exchanger or blocked flue | Do not reset again; call a pro | Heat exchanger inspection, flue clearing/replacement |
| Vent cap outside blocked (nest/ice) | Restricted flue | Note it; report to technician | Clear/repair venting, verify draft |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; varies by region and model):

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90 – $200
- **Flue/vent clearing or repair:** $150 – $600
- **Burner cleaning / adjustment:** $150 – $400
- **Rollout switch replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Inducer motor (if draft is the cause):** $300 – $700
- **Heat exchanger replacement:** $1,500 – $2,500+ (often more than half the cost of a new furnace)
- **Full furnace replacement:** $3,500 – $8,000+ (frequently the better value if the heat exchanger is cracked and the unit is older)

Because a cracked heat exchanger is a life-safety issue, many technicians and manufacturers will red-tag and shut down the furnace until it's replaced. If your unit is 12+ years old, weigh replacement against repair.
