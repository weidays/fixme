## What this code means

On most Trane (and rebranded American Standard) gas furnaces, **4 flashes** on the control board LED means an **open temperature limit circuit**. The furnace's safety string includes the **high-limit switch** and one or more **rollout switches**, and one of them has opened.

- The **high-limit switch** opens when the air inside the furnace gets too hot — almost always because airflow is restricted. This switch normally **auto-resets** when the furnace cools, so the code may clear on its own once the cause is fixed.
- A **rollout switch** opens when flames "roll out" of the burner box instead of drawing into the heat exchanger. This is a **manual-reset** device — it holds the fault until a technician physically resets it and diagnoses why it tripped.

Because a rollout trip can indicate a cracked heat exchanger or a blocked flue — both serious safety issues — this code is rated **pro**. A homeowner can safely rule out the simple airflow causes, but anything beyond that belongs to a licensed technician.

> **Legend note:** Flash-code meanings differ between board generations. Always confirm 4 flashes against the diagnostic label printed inside your furnace's blower compartment door.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the #1 cause. Restricted return air lets heat build up until the high-limit switch opens.
2. **Blocked or closed supply registers / return grilles** — too many closed vents or furniture blocking returns starves airflow the same way a dirty filter does.
3. **Restricted or dirty blower wheel / failing blower motor** — the blower can't move enough air across the heat exchanger.
4. **Dirty evaporator (A-coil) restricting airflow** — a coil packed with debris raises static pressure and traps heat.
5. **Faulty or drifted high-limit switch** — the switch itself opens prematurely even with adequate airflow.
6. **Tripped rollout switch from flame rollout** — caused by a blocked flue, cracked heat exchanger, or dirty/misaligned burners. Manual reset only.
7. **Wiring, connector, or control-board fault in the limit circuit** — an open connection reads as an open limit.

Standard practice is to verify airflow (filter, registers, blower, coil) before condemning the limit switch or chasing a rollout cause, so work the list top-down.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

**Read the emergency callout at the top of this page first.** If there is any gas smell, burning/soot smell, scorching near the burners, or a sounding CO alarm, skip everything below, get out, and call the gas utility or 911.

Otherwise, these are the only steps a homeowner should perform. Everything else is inside the cabinet and is technician work.

- **Replace the air filter** if it's dirty or older than 1–3 months. Match the size printed on your existing filter. This alone clears most 4-flash events.
- **Open all supply registers** and make sure returns aren't blocked by rugs, furniture, or closed doors.
- **Confirm the thermostat** is set to Heat with the setpoint above room temperature; replace the thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Check the furnace breaker** and the service switch (looks like a light switch near the unit). You may cycle power **once** to attempt a reset after fixing airflow.
- **Clear the area around the furnace** so it can draw return air freely.

Do **not** open the burner compartment, press the rollout reset button yourself, bypass any safety switch, or reset the furnace repeatedly. Repeated resets on a rollout condition can be dangerous.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. Read the flash code and check the diagnostic label to confirm meaning for your board.
2. Measure **temperature rise** and **static pressure** across the furnace to verify airflow is within the rated range on the unit's data plate.
3. Inspect the **filter, blower wheel, and evaporator coil** for restriction, and test the blower motor and capacitor.
4. Test the **high-limit switch** for continuity and correct trip point with a multimeter.
5. If a **rollout switch** is tripped, investigate why — inspecting burners, the heat exchanger for cracks, and the flue/venting for blockage **before** resetting it.
6. Check limit-circuit wiring, connectors, and the control board.

If the tech proposes replacing a limit switch without first checking airflow and static pressure, ask why — the switch is usually the symptom, not the cause.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Code clears after filter change | Dirty filter restricting airflow | Replace filter, cycle power once | None if it stays resolved |
| Weak airflow at registers | Closed vents, dirty blower or coil | Open all registers/returns | Clean blower wheel and coil, test motor |
| Furnace short-cycles then flashes 4 | High-limit opening on heat buildup | Verify airflow, replace filter | Measure temp rise / static pressure, test limit switch |
| Code won't clear after one reset | Manual-reset rollout switch tripped | Stop; do not reset repeatedly | Inspect burners, heat exchanger, flue; reset only after diagnosis |
| Burnt/soot smell near burners | Flame rollout event | **Emergency: shut off at the service switch, leave the house, call the gas utility or 911 from outside** | Full combustion and heat-exchanger inspection |
| Intermittent code, airflow fine | Faulty limit switch or wiring | None | Test/replace limit switch, repair wiring |

## Repair costs

| Repair | Typical US cost |
|---|---|
| Air filter (DIY) | $10 – $40 |
| Thermostat batteries (DIY) | $5 – $15 |
| Professional airflow diagnosis / service call | $90 – $200 |
| Blower wheel cleaning | $150 – $350 |
| High-limit switch replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Rollout switch replacement + diagnosis | $200 – $450 |
| Blower motor replacement | $450 – $1,500+ |
| Control board replacement | $400 – $700 |
| Cracked heat exchanger (repair/replace) | $500 – $2,000+ (often furnace replacement) |

Blower motor pricing varies widely: a standard PSC motor sits near the low end, while the ECM/variable-speed motors common in higher-end variable-capacity furnaces land at the top of the range or above, since the motor and its module cost considerably more than a PSC replacement.

A clogged filter costs nothing but the filter. Costs climb fast if the rollout trip traces back to a heat-exchanger crack or flue blockage, which is why this code warrants professional eyes.

## Related codes

Flash-code legends vary by board vintage, so confirm any of these against the diagnostic label inside your own blower door before acting on them.

- **Trane Furnace 1 Flash** — system lockout after failed ignition attempts or repeated flame dropouts.
- **Trane Furnace 2 Flashes** — pressure switch stuck closed.
- **Trane Furnace 3 Flashes** — open pressure switch circuit (venting/draft side, distinct from the limit circuit).
