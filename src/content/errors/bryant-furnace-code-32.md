## What this code means

Bryant Furnace **Code 32** means the **low-stage (low-pressure) switch did not close when expected, or opened during operation**. On two-stage and multi-stage Bryant boards, this switch confirms the inducer motor is pulling proper draft through the heat exchanger and vent before and during the low-stage firing rate.

This is a **draft/inducer proving fault** — it is **not** an airflow, blower, or high-limit problem, and it is not a gas or ignition fault. The control watches the pressure switch to be sure exhaust gases are being pulled correctly. If it can't confirm that, it will not (or will stop) firing on low stage.

**Legends vary by board.** The meaning above is the manufacturer-documented meaning for two-stage/multi-stage boards. Always confirm against the **fault-code legend printed on your furnace door label** — that legend governs your specific unit. On some single-stage boards Code 32 is not used at all.

Most Bryant boards will **retry automatically** once the switch reads correctly, but repeated Code 32 faults escalate to a **hard lockout that holds until power is cycled**.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted vent/intake** — debris, ice, insects, or a nest in the exhaust or combustion-air pipe reduces draft so the switch never closes.
2. **Cracked, disconnected, or pinched pressure-switch hose** — the small rubber tube between the inducer and the switch is loose, split, or kinked.
3. **Condensate blockage (90%+ condensing models)** — a plugged trap or drain backs water up into the inducer or pressure tubing, preventing the switch from closing.
4. **Weak or failing inducer motor** — the inducer spins but doesn't move enough air to develop draft for the low-stage switch.
5. **Water or debris inside the pressure switch or inducer housing** — sediment and moisture keep the switch from sensing proper negative pressure.
6. **Failed low-stage pressure switch** — the switch itself is out of calibration or stuck open.
7. **Wiring or connector fault** at the pressure switch or inducer — corroded terminals or a loose molex.

Bryant's own troubleshooting order starts at the venting and hose, then the inducer, and finally the switch itself — so don't replace the switch first.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should attempt:

- **Check the air filter.** A severely clogged filter won't cause Code 32 directly, but replace it if dirty as part of good maintenance.
- **Confirm thermostat settings** — set to Heat with the setpoint above room temperature, and replace thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Check the breaker** for the furnace and the furnace power switch; flip it fully off and on once.
- **Inspect visible vent terminations outside** — clear snow, ice, leaves, or nests from the exhaust and intake pipes where you can safely reach them from the ground.
- **Check the condensate drain** (condensing models) — if you see standing water at the furnace or a full trap, a clogged drain is a likely contributor; clear the visible drain line if you can.
- **One reset only:** cycle furnace power once. If Code 32 returns, stop and call a technician — do not keep resetting a locked-out unit.

Do **not** open the cabinet, disconnect hoses, jump the pressure switch, or test any component with a meter. That is technician work.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. Read the stored fault history and confirm the door-label legend for your board.
2. Inspect the vent and combustion-air piping for blockage, slope, and length.
3. Check the pressure-switch hose for cracks, water, and correct routing.
4. Measure inducer draft with a manometer and compare to the switch's rated set-point.
5. Verify inducer motor amp draw and RPM against spec.
6. Check the condensate trap and drain on condensing units for blockage.
7. Test the pressure switch's open/close pressure electrically, and inspect wiring/connectors.

If the draft is correct but the switch won't close, they'll replace the switch; if draft is weak, they'll address the inducer or venting instead.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Code 32, inducer runs but no ignition | Vent/intake blockage | Clear visible outdoor vent debris/ice | Manometer draft test, clear internal blockage |
| Code 32 after wind or storm | Blocked or iced vent terminal | Clear snow/ice from terminals | Verify vent slope, add screen/guard |
| Standing water near furnace | Plugged condensate trap/drain | Clear visible drain line | Clean trap, flush drain, inspect inducer |
| Inducer sounds weak or noisy | Failing inducer motor | None | Test amps/RPM, replace inducer |
| Code 32 with clean venting | Cracked/loose pressure hose | None | Inspect and replace hose |
| Recurring hard lockout | Failed pressure switch or wiring | One power reset only | Test switch pressure, replace switch/wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor installed, except where noted):

- **DIY vent/condensate clearing:** $0
- **Air filter replacement:** $15 – $40
- **Pressure-switch hose replacement:** $80 – $180
- **Low-stage pressure switch replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Inducer motor assembly replacement:** $400 – $1,200
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90 – $180 (often applied to the repair)

Inducer assemblies are the widest range on the list. A basic single-speed inducer on an older two-stage furnace lands near the bottom of that range, while the **variable-speed/ECM inducers used on condensing and modulating equipment cost noticeably more** — those jobs commonly finish at or near the top end, and quotes above $1,000 are not automatically a red flag on that kind of furnace. Emergency, after-hours, or difficult-access work can push any of these numbers higher.

A simple blocked-vent or hose fix is at the low end; a full inducer replacement is at the high end. Get an itemized quote that separates part and labor — and if a tech wants to replace the pressure switch without checking draft or venting first, ask them to verify the switch is actually failing.
