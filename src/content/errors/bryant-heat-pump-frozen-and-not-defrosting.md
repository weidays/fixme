## What this code means

"Frozen and not defrosting" is not a numbered Bryant error code. It describes a heating-mode problem: the **outdoor coil** of your heat pump is covered in thick ice and the unit is not clearing it.

In heat mode, the outdoor coil runs colder than the outside air, so moisture freezes on it. Bryant heat pumps handle this with a defrost cycle. The control board briefly reverses the unit into cooling mode, which warms the outdoor coil and melts the ice. During that cycle:

- the outdoor fan stops
- the indoor auxiliary or strip heat usually comes on, so you don't feel cold air

When defrost never starts, ends too early, or can't keep up, ice builds until the coil is a solid block. Heating capacity then drops to almost nothing.

How defrost is triggered depends on the model:

- **Time-and-temperature boards** (common on older and Legacy units) check the coil at a fixed interval, often jumper-selectable at 30, 60 or 90 minutes. They defrost only if a coil thermostat reads cold.
- **Demand-defrost boards** (many Preferred and Evolution units) compare coil and outdoor-air sensor readings and defrost only when needed.

This is different from the indoor problem covered in "Bryant AC Frozen Evaporator Coil." That page is about ice on the indoor coil in cooling mode. This page is about ice on the outdoor coil in heating mode.

Defrost is automatic and is not a lockout, so it does not "reset." If a part has failed, the problem will keep coming back until it is repaired.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed defrost sensor or coil thermostat.**
   - This is the most common cause.
   - If the sensor never reports a cold enough coil, the board never starts defrost.
   - If it is mounted loosely or in the wrong spot on the tubing, defrost can end too early.

2. **Faulty defrost control board, or a failed relay on the board.**
   - The board may never start the timer or demand logic.
   - It may also fail to energize the reversing valve or stop the outdoor fan.

3. **Reversing valve or its solenoid coil not shifting.**
   - The board calls for defrost, but the valve stays in heat mode, so the coil never warms.

4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.**
   - Low charge makes the outdoor coil run abnormally cold, so it ices faster.
   - Defrost also becomes too weak to clear the ice.

5. **Outdoor fan motor or capacitor problem.**
   - If the fan doesn't run, or the board doesn't stop it during defrost, icing can't be managed.

6. **Site and drainage problems.**
   - Snow drifts, a unit sitting too low, or a missing riser stand can trap meltwater, which refreezes around the base and coil.
   - Roof runoff or a leaking gutter dripping onto the unit can encase it in ice that no defrost cycle can handle.

7. **Restricted indoor airflow.**
   - A clogged filter or closed registers raise head pressure and can upset defrost timing on some systems.
   - This is a contributing factor more often than the root cause.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Switch to emergency or auxiliary heat.** At the thermostat, select EM HEAT or Emergency Heat. This stops the compressor and keeps the house warm while the ice melts on its own. Expect a higher electric bill until the repair.

2. **Check the thermostat.**
   - Confirm it is set to Heat.
   - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
   - On Evolution or communicating controls, write down any fault message shown.

3. **Replace the air filter** at the furnace or air handler if it is dirty.

4. **Open supply registers and clear return grilles** of furniture and rugs.

5. **Look at the outdoor unit without touching the coil.**
   - Clear snow from around and on top of the unit, keeping about 2 feet clear.
   - Note whether a gutter or roof edge drips onto it. Fixing that runoff is a common, inexpensive cure.

6. **Watch for a defrost cycle** once the ice has melted, which can take several hours on emergency heat.
   - Switch back to normal heat.
   - Within about 30 to 90 minutes of frosting up, you should see the fan stop and steam rise.
   - If heavy ice builds again with no defrost, call a technician.

7. **Do one power reset if needed.** Turn the outdoor disconnect and breaker off for 5 minutes, then back on. Do this once only. If icing returns, stop and call for service.

Do **not**:
- chip or scrape the ice
- pour hot water onto the coil
- open the unit's service panel
- jump the defrost test pins

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically:

- Confirm heavy ice and check the indoor airflow and filter.
- Read the board's status or fault LEDs, or the Evolution fault history.
- Use the board's defrost test or speedup terminals to force a defrost. Then they watch whether:
  - the reversing valve shifts
  - the outdoor fan stops
  - auxiliary heat comes on
- Test the defrost sensor or thermostat resistance against Bryant's temperature chart, and check that it is clamped tightly to the correct tube.
- Check voltage at the reversing valve solenoid. If voltage is present but the valve doesn't shift, the valve itself is suspect.
- Check the outdoor fan motor and capacitor.
- After the coil thaws, attach gauges and check superheat and subcooling. If the charge is low, they should leak-search rather than just "top off."
- Look at site issues: elevation, drainage and roof runoff.

**Red flags in a quote:**
- recommending a new system without testing the sensor and board first
- adding refrigerant without any leak search

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid ice, fan never stops, no steam ever | Defrost sensor or board not starting defrost | Switch to emergency heat, call for service | Test sensor and board, force defrost, replace failed part |
| Unit "whooshes" but ice remains | Reversing valve not shifting, or defrost ending too early | Emergency heat | Check solenoid voltage and valve, reposition sensor |
| Ice heaviest at the bottom of the coil | Poor drainage, unit too low, snow buildup | Clear snow, keep area clear | Add riser or stand, correct drainage |
| Ice on top or one side, dripping water | Roof or gutter runoff | Fix gutter or add a drip diverter | Inspect coil for damage |
| Frequent icing plus weak heat and long runtimes | Low refrigerant charge | Emergency heat | Leak search, repair, evacuate, weigh in charge |
| Outdoor fan not spinning in heat mode | Fan motor or capacitor | One power reset only | Test and replace motor or capacitor |
| Icing along with a dirty filter | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, open registers | Check blower and duct static |

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter, battery and snow-clearing fixes | $0–$40 |

Costs often fall into the warranty-parts range on registered Bryant equipment, so check your registration before approving a repair.
