## What this code means

"Loud noise" is not a fault code on an American Standard heat pump. It is a symptom, and the control board usually logs nothing for it. Your first job is to sort normal heat-pump sounds from real mechanical trouble.

This page covers noises that only heat pumps make. These include defrost-cycle sounds, reversing-valve shifts, and outdoor-unit noises during heating season. For general cooling-season noise from a split air conditioner, see the separate AC loud noise page. For a ductless system, see the mini-split loud noise page.

**Normal sounds (no action needed):**
- A loud whoosh or hiss when defrost starts or ends.
- Steam rising from the outdoor unit during defrost.
- A brief stop of the outdoor fan during defrost.
- A soft click from the contactor at start and stop.
- A mild hum while running.
- Variable-speed AccuComfort models ramping their speed up and down. This is a design feature.

**Abnormal sounds (call a pro):**
- Grinding or screeching
- Repeated banging or clanking
- Rattling or buzzing that won't stop
- A loud hum with no fan or compressor movement
- Continuous hissing or gurgling outside of defrost

A noise problem does not auto-reset or lock out on its own. However, the underlying fault may eventually trip a pressure or overload safety. If that happens, the unit stops and restarts on its own once the safety resets, or a board may hold a lockout depending on the model.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost and reversing-valve operation.** This is the most common "loud noise" call in heating season. The whoosh at defrost start and end, plus the fan pause, are expected behavior.
2. **Ice buildup hitting the outdoor fan blade.** Ice can build up from a blocked unit, a defrost problem, or snow and ice falling from the roof. The result is scraping or clattering. If the unit is iced over and not clearing, see the frozen-and-not-defrosting page.
3. **Debris or a loose panel or fan guard.** Sticks, leaves, a loose top grille or a loose access panel can cause rattling and vibration.
4. **Failing outdoor fan motor bearings.** These cause squealing, grinding or a growl that rises with fan speed.
5. **Contactor chatter or a weak capacitor.** These cause buzzing or loud humming at the outdoor unit. A failing capacitor may hum while the fan or compressor struggles to start.
6. **Compressor mechanical noise.** Signs include loud knocking, clanking, rattling at startup or shutdown, or a high-pitched whine. Sources can be worn internal parts, failed mounting grommets, or liquid refrigerant returning to the compressor.
7. **A reversing valve that is sticking or failing to shift fully.** This causes ongoing hissing or a buzzing solenoid. It often comes with poor heating.
8. **Refrigerant-circuit issues.** Gurgling or continuous hissing outside of defrost can point to a leak or a restriction.
9. **Indoor blower or ductwork noise.** Sources include a blower wheel that is out of balance or dirty, or ducts that pop and bang as they expand. A clogged filter and closed registers make whistling and duct noise worse.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Identify when the noise happens.** If it is a whoosh lasting about a minute, with steam and a paused fan in cold weather, it is almost certainly defrost. Watch one full cycle before calling.
- **Check the air filter.** A clogged filter makes the indoor blower strain and whistle. Replace it if it is dirty.
- **Open supply registers and return grilles.** Make sure furniture or rugs are not blocking them. Closed registers can cause whistling and duct booming.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from outside the cabinet.** Clear leaves, snow drifts and debris from around the unit, keeping about 2 feet of clearance. Clear roof runoff dripping onto the unit if you can do so safely. Do not reach through the fan grille.
- **Seat the exterior panels.** If an access panel or the top grille is visibly loose, press it back into place. Tighten only the existing exterior screws you can reach without opening the unit.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm the mode and setpoint are correct, and replace the batteries if low. This rules out rapid on/off cycling that sounds like repeated clunks.
- **Stop the system if the sound is harsh.** For grinding, screeching, loud banging or a hum with nothing spinning, turn the system off at the thermostat. Call a technician, and don't keep restarting it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirm the source.** The tech listens at the outdoor unit, the indoor air handler and the line set, in both heat mode and a forced defrost. This separates normal defrost noise from faults.
- **Check the outdoor fan.** With power off, they check the blade for ice damage, cracks or a loose hub. They spin it by hand to feel for bearing roughness, then check motor amp draw.
- **Test electrical components.** They test the capacitor (rated vs. measured microfarads) and inspect the contactor for pitting, chatter and low control voltage.
- **Assess the compressor.** They measure compressor amps and start behavior, and inspect the mounting grommets. Listening at different points helps pinpoint internal noise.
- **Check refrigerant pressures and the reversing valve.** They read pressures and superheat/subcooling, and test reversing valve operation, including the solenoid coil, voltage, and temperature differences across the valve. They also check for leaks if a charge problem is suspected.
- **Check defrost operation.** They verify the defrost control and sensor, especially if ice is reaching the fan.
- **Inspect the indoor side.** They look at the blower wheel, motor mounts, and duct connections.

Expect a diagnostic fee before any repair quote. Ask which specific test pointed to the part they want to replace.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loud whoosh, steam, fan stops briefly in cold weather | Normal defrost / reversing valve shift | None; observe one full cycle | None needed unless it repeats constantly |
| Scraping or clattering from outdoor fan in winter | Ice contacting fan blade | Clear snow and roof runoff from around the unit; turn off if loud | Check defrost control and sensor; replace a damaged blade |
| Rattling from outdoor cabinet | Debris, loose panel or grille | Clear debris outside the unit; seat the exterior panels | Secure internal parts, check fan guard and mounts |
| Squeal or grinding that rises with fan speed | Worn fan motor bearings | Turn off system | Replace outdoor fan motor (often with capacitor) |
| Buzzing or loud hum, fan or compressor slow to start | Weak capacitor or chattering contactor | Turn off system | Test and replace capacitor and/or contactor |
| Banging or knocking at compressor start or stop | Compressor mount or internal wear, liquid floodback | Turn off system | Check mounts, charge and metering; compressor diagnosis |
| Continuous hissing or gurgling outside defrost | Reversing valve or refrigerant issue | None | Pressure test, leak search, reversing valve diagnosis |
| Whistling at registers or booming ducts | Clogged filter, closed registers | Replace filter, open registers | Check blower and static pressure, fix ductwork |

## Repair costs

Typical US installed prices, including parts and labor. Costs vary by region, model, and whether the unit is under warranty. American Standard often covers parts for 10 years when the unit is registered, but labor usually isn't covered.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service call / diagnostic | $80 – $200 |
| Normal defrost noise (education only) | $0 – $150 |
| Clearing debris, securing panels (tech visit) | $100 – $250 |
| Run or dual capacitor | $120 – $350 |
| Contactor | $150 – $350 |
| Outdoor fan blade | $150 – $350 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350 – $800 |
| Defrost control board or sensor | $200 – $650 |
| Compressor mounting grommets | $150 – $400 |
| Reversing valve solenoid coil | $150 – $350 |
| Reversing valve replacement (brazing and recharge) | $900 – $2,000 |
| Refrigerant leak repair and recharge | $300 – $1,500+ |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800 – $3,500+ |
| Indoor blower motor or wheel | $400 – $1,200 |

On older units, a compressor or reversing valve failure out of warranty often justifies pricing a new system.
