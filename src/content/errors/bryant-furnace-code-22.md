## What this code means

Bryant Furnace **Code 22** is an **Abnormal Flame-Proving Signal**: the control board is sensing flame at the flame sensor *while the gas valve is de-energized* — in other words, flame is present when the board has commanded the gas off.

Because gas should not be burning when the valve is closed, the board responds defensively. The **inducer motor runs until flame is no longer sensed**, purging the heat exchanger. This is a safety response to a potentially serious condition.

This is **not** an ignition failure and it is **not** a "no flame" fault. Code 22 is the opposite problem — flame where there should be none. That almost always points to a **leaking or stuck-open gas valve**, or to **residual flame** lingering after shutdown. It does not point to gas-supply problems, dirty sensors that fail to sense, or ignition timing (those belong to other codes).

Because the root cause involves the gas valve and burners, **Code 22 is a professional-severity fault**. Do not repeatedly reset the unit trying to make it run.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Leaking or stuck-open gas valve.** The most likely and most serious cause. The valve is not fully sealing when de-energized, allowing gas to keep feeding the burners so flame persists after shutdown. This is a gas-train component and is technician-only.
2. **Residual or lingering flame at shutdown.** Flame that does not extinguish promptly when gas is cut — often related to valve seating or a slow-closing valve.
3. **Flame sensor or wiring fault reporting false flame.** A shorted flame-sense circuit or miswired sensor lead can make the board believe flame is present when it is not. Diagnosis requires meter tests inside the cabinet.
4. **Control board misreading the flame signal.** Less common, but a failing board can generate a false flame-proving signal. Confirmed only after the gas valve and sensor circuit are ruled out.

Where boards differ: some Bryant models display the numeric **Code 22**, while others print a text description such as "flame sensed with gas valve off." The underlying meaning is the same. If your board's legend differs, follow the printed description.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Keep these strictly to what a homeowner can safely do. Do **not** open the burner compartment or touch the gas valve.

- **Confirm the thermostat is set correctly** (Heat mode, setpoint above room temperature) — this rules out a stray call for heat.
- **Check or replace the air filter** if it is visibly dirty; poor airflow can complicate any furnace fault.
- **Reset the furnace ONCE** by cycling the furnace switch or breaker off, waiting 30 seconds, then on. If Code 22 returns, stop and call a technician — do not keep resetting.
- **Check the breaker** serving the furnace hasn't tripped.
- **Look at visible supply and return registers** to confirm they are open and unblocked.
- **Inspect the condensate line/drain** (on high-efficiency models) for obvious blockage or overflow.

⚠️ If you ever smell gas, do not troubleshoot. Leave the house, avoid switches, and call your gas utility's emergency line or 911 from outside.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

- Read the stored fault history on the control board to confirm Code 22 versus a neighboring flame code.
- **Watch the burners at shutdown** to see whether flame extinguishes promptly or lingers after the valve de-energizes.
- **Test the gas valve** — verifying it closes fully and doesn't leak by, using manometer/pressure checks and inspecting for a stuck or worn valve seat.
- **Meter the flame-sense circuit** to confirm whether a real flame signal or a false signal (shorted sensor/wiring) is reaching the board.
- Inspect flame-sensor lead routing and connectors for shorts or pinched wiring.
- If the valve and sensor circuit test good, **evaluate the control board** for a false flame-proving reading.

This order lets you sanity-check a quote: a tech who jumps straight to replacing the control board without verifying the gas valve seals is skipping the most likely cause.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inducer keeps running after heat call ends | Flame still sensed with valve off | One reset; if it recurs, stop | Verify valve closes fully; test gas train |
| Burner flame lingers after shutdown | Leaking/stuck-open gas valve | None — shut down, call pro | Replace or reseat gas valve |
| Code 22 with no visible flame | False flame signal / shorted sensor circuit | None inside cabinet | Meter flame-sense circuit and wiring |
| Code 22 repeats every cycle | Faulty gas valve or control board | Single reset only | Diagnose valve, then board |
| Faint gas smell with the fault | Possible gas leak | Leave home, call gas utility/911 | Emergency gas-train service |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Actual price varies by region, model access, and whether parts are in warranty.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90 – $180.
- **Gas valve replacement:** $300 – $600+ installed (the most common Code 22 repair).
- **Flame sensor replacement:** $100 – $250 installed, if a false-signal sensor is the cause.
- **Flame-sense wiring/connector repair:** $100 – $250 depending on labor.
- **Control board replacement:** $400 – $700+ installed, only if the valve and sensor circuit test good.

DIY parts cost for a homeowner here is effectively **$0** — every genuine fix for Code 22 is inside the cabinet and gas-related, so it is technician work.
