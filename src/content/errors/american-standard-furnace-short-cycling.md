## What this code means

"Short cycling" is a symptom, not a single flash code. It means your American Standard furnace starts a heating cycle, runs only briefly, shuts off, and then restarts. The house never reaches the thermostat setting, or it does so unevenly while the furnace wears itself out.

On American Standard furnaces, short cycling almost always comes from one of three places:

- **A safety control ending the cycle.** Most often this is the high-limit switch opening because the furnace is overheating.
- **The flame-proving circuit dropping the flame.** The flame lights, then shuts off within seconds.
- **The thermostat or its wiring.** It tells the furnace to stop too early.

**Does it reset on its own?** That depends on the cause:

- **High limit (open limit circuit):** The board typically shuts the burners, keeps the blower running, and retries once the limit closes. Repeated trips in one call for heat can push many integrated boards into a lockout. Lockout length (often about an hour) and behavior vary by board.
- **Flame-loss retries:** These can also end in a timed lockout. On many boards this lockout shows as 2 flashes (failure to prove or sustain flame).
- **Thermostat-caused cycling:** This never locks out. It simply repeats.

**A rollout switch trip is different.** On American Standard furnaces, rollout switches are typically manual-reset. A rollout trip normally stops the furnace and keeps it off rather than causing repeated short cycles, and it usually means flames actually rolled out of the burner area, for example from a blocked heat exchanger or flue, or a cracked heat exchanger. See the rollout warning below.

**Check the status LED** through the blower door sight glass before you cut power, since a power cycle may clear the stored code. If you see a specific flash pattern, see the related flash-code pages listed below.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

This list is ordered by how often technicians typically find each cause: airflow first, then controls, then components.

1. **Restricted airflow from a dirty filter.** This is the number one cause. The heat exchanger overheats and the high-limit switch opens, shutting the burners off after a few minutes.
2. **Closed or blocked supply registers or return grilles.** Furniture, rugs or closed vents create the same overheating. This is common when many rooms have been shut off.
3. **Thermostat problems.** Possible issues include:
   - The thermostat is mounted near a supply register, a lamp or sunlight.
   - Weak batteries.
   - A wrong cycle-rate (CPH) setting.
   - A mis-set heat anticipator on older mechanical thermostats.
4. **Dirty or failing flame sensor.** The burners light, then shut down within seconds because the board can't prove flame. It retries repeatedly and may show 8 flashes on boards that report low flame current. If the retries run out, many boards lock out and show 2 flashes.
5. **Blower problems.** A dirty blower wheel, a weak motor or capacitor, or an incorrect blower speed setting for heating causes low airflow and limit trips.
6. **Weak or failed high-limit switch.** A limit switch can open below its rated temperature even with good airflow.
7. **Oversized furnace or restrictive ductwork.** The house heats too fast, or the ducts can't move enough air. This is a design problem a technician confirms with temperature-rise and static-pressure readings.
8. **Overfiring or a cracked heat exchanger.** Gas pressure set too high causes overheating. Long-term overheating can crack the heat exchanger, which is a safety issue a pro must inspect.

### Rollout switch trips: a safety warning, not a nuisance fault

A tripped rollout switch is not a typical short-cycling cause, and it should not be treated as a worn-out switch. It usually signals real flame rollout from a blocked heat exchanger or flue, or a cracked heat exchanger. If a technician tells you the rollout switch has tripped, or the furnace shuts down and stays off:

- **Do not reset it repeatedly,** and don't open the cabinet to reach it yourself.
- **Shut the furnace off** at the service switch.
- **Call a technician** to find out why the flame rolled out before the furnace runs again.

If a CO alarm sounds, or anyone feels headache, nausea or dizziness, leave the house and call 911.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These checks are safe for a homeowner. Do not open the burner or blower compartment beyond looking through the sight glass at the LED.

1. **Replace the air filter.** Hold the old one up to a light. If you can't see light through it, it was likely the problem. Match the size printed on the frame, and note the airflow arrow direction.
2. **Open every supply register and clear every return grille.** Move furniture, rugs and curtains away from them.
3. **Check the thermostat:**
   - Replace the batteries.
   - Confirm it's set to HEAT with the fan on AUTO.
   - Make sure no register, lamp, TV or sunny window is warming it.
   - On programmable models, look for a cycles-per-hour setting. Gas forced air is usually 3 to 5 CPH, but check your thermostat manual.
4. **Check the breaker and the furnace service switch.** Make sure they're fully on, and not tripping or loose.
5. **Seat the blower door.** Confirm it is fully latched. A loose panel can interrupt operation on many models.
6. **Check the condensate drain on high-efficiency (90%+) furnaces.** Make sure the drain line and trap outlet aren't visibly kinked or overflowing.
7. **Do one reset only.** If the unit is locked out, turn power off at the switch for 30 seconds and back on, one time. If short cycling returns, stop and call a technician. Repeated resets can mask overheating that damages the heat exchanger. This power reset does not apply to a tripped rollout switch. That always needs a technician.

**Make sure your CO alarms are working.** If anyone feels headache, nausea or dizziness, leave the house and call 911.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Use this list to sanity-check a quote:

- **Read the LED code and fault history** on the control board. Some newer communicating models store a fault history.
- **Measure temperature rise** (supply air minus return air) and compare it to the range on the furnace rating plate. A rise above the range points to an airflow problem or overfiring.
- **Measure total external static pressure** to find restrictive ductwork or a clogged coil.
- **Inspect and clean the blower wheel.** Check the motor amp draw and capacitor (PSC motors), and confirm the heating speed tap or ECM airflow settings.
- **Test the high-limit switch** for continuity, and watch whether the limit opens during operation.
- **Check the rollout switches.** If one has tripped, the technician should inspect the heat exchanger and flue for blockage or cracks before resetting it, not just reset or replace the switch.
- **Measure flame-sensor microamps,** then clean or replace the sensor.
- **Check the gas manifold pressure** and adjust it to spec if the unit is overfiring.
- **Check the thermostat wiring and placement,** and verify that cycling stops when the thermostat is jumpered at the board as a test.
- **Inspect the heat exchanger,** with a camera or a combustion analysis, if overheating has been ongoing.

**Red flag:** A quote for a new furnace for "short cycling" without temperature-rise or static-pressure readings. Ask for those numbers.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 3–5 min, burners off, blower keeps running | High limit opening (overheating) | Replace filter, open all vents | Check temp rise, blower, limit switch, gas pressure |
| Flame lights then shuts off in seconds, retries | Flame sensor not proving flame | Note LED flashes, one reset only | Clean or replace flame sensor, check ground |
| Short cycles, no LED fault, house warms unevenly | Thermostat location or settings | Change batteries, adjust CPH, remove heat sources | Relocate or replace thermostat, check wiring |
| Started after closing rooms off | Restricted airflow | Reopen registers | Static pressure test if it persists |
| Weak airflow at all registers | Dirty blower or failing motor/capacitor | Replace filter | Clean blower wheel, test motor and capacitor |
| Short cycling for years, ducts small or noisy | Oversized furnace or undersized ducts | None | Load calculation, duct modification |
| Furnace shuts down and stays off; rollout switch found tripped | Possible flame rollout (blocked heat exchanger or flue, cracked heat exchanger) | Shut off furnace, do not reset repeatedly, call a technician | Inspect heat exchanger and flue, correct the cause, then reset or replace the switch |
| Soot, odor or CO alarm | Possible cracked heat exchanger | Shut off furnace, leave and call 911 if alarm sounds | Heat exchanger inspection or replacement |

**About heat exchanger warranties:** Lifetime or limited-lifetime heat exchanger coverage usually depends on the furnace having been registered and on you being the original owner. Check your paperwork before you approve the repair. On an older furnace, a technician will often recommend replacing the whole furnace instead of the heat exchanger.
