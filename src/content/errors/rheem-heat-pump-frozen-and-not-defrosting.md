## What this code means

"Frozen and not defrosting" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. In heating mode, the outdoor coil of a heat pump runs below freezing and collects frost. Rheem heat pumps clear that frost with a **defrost cycle**. The reversing valve briefly switches the system to cooling mode, the outdoor fan stops, and the indoor backup heat usually comes on so you don't get cold air. The cycle ends once the coil reaches its termination temperature or hits a time limit.

When the outdoor unit becomes solidly encased in ice, or the coil stays white and never clears, one of three things is wrong:

- **The defrost cycle isn't starting.** This points to the board, the sensor, or a relay.
- **Defrost runs but can't finish the job.** This points to the reversing valve, the refrigerant charge, or the outdoor fan.
- **Ice is forming faster than defrost can clear it.** This points to airflow problems or water dripping onto the unit.

How defrost is triggered depends on the model and board generation. Older Rheem/Ruud units use **time/temperature defrost**: a coil sensor or thermostat plus a selectable interval, typically 30, 60 or 90 minutes. Newer variable-speed and communicating models (for example, Prestige series with EcoNet) use **demand defrost**, which is driven by sensor readings. Some boards flash a diagnostic code for a sensor or defrost fault. If yours is flashing, see the separate flashing-light page. Icing by itself does not usually cause a lockout. Once the ice melts, the unit typically tries to run again, and it will refreeze if the cause is still there.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or poorly attached defrost (coil) sensor/thermostat.** If the sensor clamped to the outdoor coil tubing comes loose, fails, or reads incorrectly, the board never sees a cold enough coil to start defrost. This is one of the most common culprits on Rheem units.
2. **Defrost control board failure.** A failed relay, a bad timer circuit, or damage from a power surge can stop defrost from starting. On time/temperature boards, a wrong interval setting can also let ice build between cycles.
3. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** An undercharged system runs a colder coil, frosts faster, and defrosts poorly. Ice often starts at the bottom of the coil or appears unevenly.
4. **Reversing valve or its solenoid not shifting.** The board calls for defrost, but the valve never changes over, so the coil never warms.
5. **Outdoor fan motor or fan capacitor problems.** A fan that doesn't run reduces heat transfer and speeds up icing. A fan that fails to stop during defrost (a bad defrost relay) prevents proper melting.
6. **Restricted airflow.** On the outdoor side, this means a dirty coil, snow drifts, leaves, or shrubs. On the indoor side, it means a clogged filter or closed registers, which raises head pressure and changes how the system behaves.
7. **Site and installation issues.** These include a unit sitting on the ground without enough riser height, poor drainage under the unit, or roof runoff or a gutter dripping onto the top of the unit. All of these let meltwater refreeze into a solid block.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do not chip, scrape, or pour hot water on the ice. Do not open the outdoor unit's service panel.

- **Switch the thermostat to Emergency Heat (EM/AUX)**, or turn heating off, so the outdoor unit stops running while it's iced. This protects the compressor.
- **Check thermostat settings and batteries.** Confirm it's set to Heat with a sensible setpoint, and replace weak batteries. An erratic thermostat can short-cycle the system.
- **Replace the indoor air filter** if it's dirty, and **open any closed supply registers and return grilles.**
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Brush away loose snow, leaves and debris from the sides and top by hand or with a soft broom. Keep at least 18–24 inches of clearance around it.
- **Look for water dripping onto the unit** from gutters or the roof edge. Fixing a gutter or adding a diverter is a reasonable homeowner job.
- **Check the breakers** for both the indoor unit and outdoor unit (including the outdoor disconnect). If one has tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- After the ice has melted naturally, go back to normal Heat and **watch for a defrost cycle** within an hour or two. You should see steam, the fan stopping, and a whoosh when it starts and ends. If the unit ices up again, call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will usually:

- **Confirm the defrost method and board settings.** This includes the interval jumper on time/temperature boards and any stored fault codes on demand-defrost or communicating controls.
- **Force a test defrost.** Many Rheem defrost boards have test pins or a test mode for this. The tech then watches whether the reversing valve shifts, the outdoor fan stops, and backup heat engages.
- **Check the defrost sensor.** This means checking how it's mounted and how well it contacts the tubing, and taking resistance or temperature readings against the manufacturer's chart.
- **Measure voltage at the reversing valve solenoid** during defrost, and check whether the valve actually shifts by reading temperatures and pressures.
- **Check refrigerant charge** with gauges or probes, using superheat/subcooling per Rheem specs. If the charge is low, they should look for the leak rather than just "topping off."
- **Inspect the outdoor fan motor and capacitor,** the coil's cleanliness, and how the unit is sited and drains.

Be wary of a quote that adds refrigerant without any leak check, or one that replaces the compressor before the board, sensor and valve have been tested.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coil solid with ice, never see steam/defrost | Failed defrost sensor or board | Run emergency heat, let it thaw, call a pro | Test sensor and board, force defrost, replace failed part |
| Defrost seems to start (fan stops) but ice remains | Reversing valve or solenoid not shifting | Emergency heat; avoid running heat pump | Test solenoid voltage and coil, replace solenoid or valve |
| Ice heavy on bottom/partial coil, weak heat | Low refrigerant (leak) | Emergency heat; note when it started | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge to spec |
| Outdoor fan not spinning in heat mode | Fan motor or capacitor failure | Check outdoor breaker/disconnect once | Test and replace capacitor or motor |
| Fan keeps running during defrost | Defrost relay/board fault | Emergency heat | Diagnose and replace board |
| Ice builds fast, dirty filter or closed vents | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, open registers, clear around unit | Clean coils, check blower and duct issues |
| Thick ice cap on top, icicles from roof | Water dripping onto unit, poor drainage | Fix gutter/drip source, keep area clear | Raise unit on risers or stand, correct drainage |
