## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a single Bryant fault code. It means the system isn't removing enough heat from your home. There are three basic ways this happens:

- **The outdoor unit isn't running.** The condenser fan and compressor are off.
- **The equipment runs but doesn't move enough air.** Airflow across the indoor coil is restricted.
- **The refrigerant circuit isn't working properly.** Charge is low, there's a restriction, or the compressor is weak.

**How Bryant systems report it varies by model:**

- **Single-stage Legacy and Preferred units** usually display nothing. You just notice warm air or a thermostat that never reaches setpoint.
- **Communicating Evolution systems** may log a specific fault on the Evolution Connex control and flash a status LED on the outdoor control board. Examples include high- or low-pressure trips, outdoor coil temperature faults, and compressor faults. If your thermostat shows a code, write it down. It narrows the diagnosis considerably.

**Reset behavior:**

- Most Bryant outdoor units have a built-in anti-short-cycle delay of about five minutes. Brief waits after a thermostat change are normal.
- Pressure-switch trips on some models auto-reset. Repeated trips can cause a lockout that holds until power is cycled.

Because the likely causes run from a $10 filter to a failed compressor, the outcome depends on what a technician finds.

**If your Bryant is a heat pump** and the problem appears in heating mode, see "Bryant Heat Pump Not Heating" instead.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow.** Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat setting or power problem.** Common examples:
   - Mode set to Heat or Off, or fan set to On (which can blow uncooled air between cycles)
   - Dead batteries
   - A tripped breaker or outdoor disconnect
3. **Failed run capacitor.** This is one of the most common outdoor-unit failures, especially after hot weather. The fan and/or compressor hums but won't start.
4. **Failed or pitted contactor.** The outdoor unit gets no power even though the thermostat is calling for cooling.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** Common leak points are the evaporator coil, condenser coil, and service valves. Symptoms are weak cooling, ice on the copper lines, and long run times.
6. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil.** Grass clippings, cottonwood and dust block heat rejection. This can trip the high-pressure switch on models that have one.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil.** This is a result of low airflow or low charge, not a root cause. It blocks airflow until thawed.
8. **Condenser fan motor failure.** The compressor runs, but heat isn't rejected. Pressure and temperature climb until a safety trips.
9. **Indoor blower problem.** A failed blower motor or blower capacitor means little or no air at the vents. On ECM-equipped furnaces and fan coils, the blower may show its own code (see Related codes).
10. **Clogged condensate drain tripping a float switch.** Many installations have a float or safety switch that shuts off cooling when the drain backs up.
11. **Compressor or control board failure.** These are less common and expensive. Technicians confirm them only after ruling out the items above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Thermostat**
   - Confirm the mode is set to Cool and the setpoint is at least 3°F below room temperature.
   - Set the fan to Auto.
   - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
   - On Evolution controls, note any fault message shown.
2. **Air filter**
   - Pull the filter. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it with the same size.
3. **Breakers and disconnect**
   - Check the AC and furnace/air-handler breakers in your main panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once only**.
   - Make sure the outdoor disconnect is in the On position.
   - Make sure the indoor unit's service switch (often looks like a light switch) is on.
   - If a breaker trips again, stop and call a technician. That indicates an electrical or compressor fault.
4. **Wait out the delay**
   - After any reset or thermostat change, wait at least 5–10 minutes before deciding the outdoor unit won't start.
5. **Ice check**
   - Look for ice on the copper line at the outdoor unit or at the indoor coil area.
   - If you see ice, set the system to Off and the fan to On to thaw it.
   - Do not chip the ice off.
6. **Vents and returns**
   - Open supply registers.
   - Clear furniture and rugs from return grilles.
7. **Outdoor unit surroundings**
   - From outside the unit, clear leaves, grass and debris around it.
   - Keep about 2 feet of clearance around the sides.
   - Do not open the unit or touch the fan.
8. **Condensate**
   - Check whether the condensate pan or drain line is overflowing, or the condensate pump is full.
   - A clogged drain may have tripped a safety switch.
   - Clear the line's accessible end, or pour a cup of distilled vinegar into the access tee if your system has one.
9. **Panels**
   - Make sure the furnace or air-handler blower door is fully seated. Many units won't run with the door loose.

Call a technician if:

- The outdoor unit still won't start.
- The outdoor unit hums without the fan spinning.
- The coil re-freezes.
- A breaker trips again.

Do not try to restart a buzzing compressor or push the fan blades.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician should be able to explain what they measured. Expect these steps:

- **Confirm the thermostat call.** They verify 24V signals (Y, G) at the indoor and outdoor units. On Evolution systems, they read stored faults from the control and the outdoor board LED.
- **Test the capacitor.** They measure the run capacitor with a meter against its microfarad rating. A reading more than about 6–10% under the rating, or a bulging case, means replacement.
- **Inspect the contactor.** They check for pitting, burned contacts, and coil voltage.
- **Check the motors.** They measure condenser fan motor and compressor amperage, and check windings and grounds.
- **Measure temperature split.** They measure supply versus return air. Roughly 15–22°F is typical for a single-stage system, depending on conditions.
- **Measure refrigerant charge.** They attach gauges or probes and use superheat (fixed orifice) or subcooling (TXV, common on Bryant). They use Bryant's charging chart for your model and conditions.
  - If the charge is low, a proper visit includes leak searching (electronic detector, bubbles, or nitrogen pressure test), not just a top-off.
- **Check airflow.** They measure static pressure and check blower operation and the evaporator coil condition.
- **Inspect the condenser coil.** They check for dirt and clean it if needed.
- **Check the condensate system.** They test the float and safety switches and confirm the drain is clear.

**Red flags in a quote:**

- Recommending a new system or compressor without measurements
- Adding refrigerant repeatedly without a leak search
- Not checking the capacitor before condemning the compressor

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing runs, thermostat blank | Dead batteries, tripped breaker, service switch off | Replace batteries; check breakers once; flip service switch on | Check transformer, fuse, low-voltage wiring |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped disconnect/breaker, failed contactor, float switch tripped | Check outdoor disconnect and breaker; clear condensate drain | Test contactor, 24V signal, safety switches |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan doesn't spin | Failed run capacitor or fan motor | Turn system off; don't touch the fan | Test and replace capacitor or fan motor |
| Outdoor fan runs, air is barely cool | Low refrigerant, dirty coils, weak compressor | Replace filter; clear debris around unit | Check charge, leak search, clean coil, compressor tests |
| Ice on copper lines or indoor coil | Dirty filter, low airflow, low refrigerant | Off mode with fan On to thaw; replace filter | Airflow and charge diagnosis, leak repair |
| Weak airflow at vents | Clogged filter, blocked returns, blower issue | Replace filter; open registers and returns | Blower motor/capacitor test, duct static pressure |
| Water around indoor unit, AC shuts off | Clogged condensate drain, float switch tripped | Clear drain line; empty pan or pump | Clear drain, replace condensate pump or switch |
| Runs briefly, then shuts off repeatedly | High/low pressure trips, overheating compressor | Clean debris around unit; reset once | Diagnose pressure switch trips, fan motor, charge |
| Evolution thermostat shows fault message | Specific system fault logged | Record the code and message | Read fault history, diagnose per Bryant service data |

## Related codes

Bryant Heat Pump Not Heating covers the heating-mode counterpart if your Bryant is a heat pump.

Bryant Furnace Code 41: Blower Motor Fault Fixes and Bryant Furnace Code 44: Blower Calibration Fault Fixes apply when weak or absent indoor airflow is reported by the furnace that houses your AC blower.

Bryant Furnace Code 45: Control Circuitry Lockout Fixes is relevant if the furnace control board itself has locked out and is not powering the cooling call.

Bryant Furnace Code 13 and Bryant Furnace Code 33 may appear on furnace boards when a limit or safety circuit is open. On some installations, this includes a condensate float switch wired into that circuit.
