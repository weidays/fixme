## What this code means

Lennox **Code E110 means low line voltage** — the furnace's integrated control board is measuring incoming 120V (line) power below the minimum it needs to operate safely and reliably. The board monitors supply voltage on every call for heat, and when it drops below the acceptable threshold it posts E110 and typically will not start, or will interrupt, the ignition sequence to protect the inducer motor, blower and the board itself.

This is fundamentally an **electrical supply** fault, not a gas or flame-proving fault. The board is telling you the power reaching it is too weak — the fix lives in the wiring, breaker, feeder, or the utility supply, not in the burners or flame sensor.

On most Lennox boards E110 **auto-clears once voltage returns to range**, so you may see it appear and disappear. A code that keeps re-posting means the low-voltage condition is still present. Because exact voltage thresholds and code text vary by board revision, confirm the reading against your furnace's board legend or installation manual.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Genuine low utility voltage / brownout** — the power company is delivering below-spec voltage, common during heat waves, high grid demand, or in areas with marginal service.
2. **Loose or corroded connection at the breaker, disconnect, or furnace whip** — a poor connection creates resistance and drops voltage under load.
3. **Undersized or overloaded circuit** — the furnace shares a circuit with other large loads, or the branch wiring is too small for the run length, causing voltage sag when the inducer or blower starts.
4. **Failing breaker or service disconnect** — a weak or damaged breaker can no longer pass full voltage under load.
5. **Loose neutral or degraded feeder in the main panel** — a bigger house-wide issue that can also cause lights to flicker and other appliances to misbehave.
6. **Failing transformer or control board voltage-sensing circuit** — less common; the incoming line is fine but the board misreads it or the low-voltage transformer is faulty.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Everything inside the cabinet is technician work.

- **Check the breaker and furnace switch.** Find the furnace circuit breaker in your panel and the light-switch-style disconnect near the furnace. Reset the breaker once (fully off, then on) and confirm the switch is on.
- **Look for a whole-house pattern.** If lights dim or other appliances act up at the same time, you likely have a house-wide or utility supply issue — note this for the technician or your utility.
- **Check your thermostat.** Confirm it's set to Heat with a temperature above room temperature; replace the thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** A clogged filter won't cause E110 directly, but ruling it out keeps the diagnosis clean.
- **Confirm supply and return vents are open and unblocked.**
- **Check the condensate line** on high-efficiency models for standing water or blockage.

If E110 returns after a single breaker reset, stop and call a professional (or your utility if a whole-house voltage problem is suspected). Do **not** open the cabinet, test wiring, or repeatedly reset the unit.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will verify the power problem before touching any furnace component:

- **Measure incoming line voltage** at the furnace with a multimeter, both at rest and under load (inducer/blower running), watching for voltage sag below the board's threshold.
- **Compare against utility spec** — steady low voltage points to a utility service problem; large drops only under load point to connections or wiring.
- **Inspect and tighten connections** at the breaker, disconnect, furnace junction box, and control board terminals, checking for heat damage or corrosion.
- **Test the breaker and disconnect** under load to confirm they pass full voltage.
- **Check the panel** for a loose neutral or degraded feeder if the whole house is affected.
- **Verify the transformer and board voltage sensing** only after confirming the incoming line is actually adequate — this avoids replacing a good board.
- **Recommend a utility call** if the service voltage itself is out of spec.

A quote that jumps straight to replacing the control board without first documenting a voltage measurement deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| E110 appears, furnace won't start | Low incoming line voltage | Reset breaker once; confirm switch on | Measure line voltage at furnace under load |
| Code comes and goes with no pattern | Utility brownout / marginal service | Note if house lights dim too | Verify utility voltage; advise utility call |
| E110 only when blower/inducer starts | Loose connection or undersized circuit | None inside cabinet | Tighten/repair connections; check wire gauge |
| Whole house lights flicker + E110 | Loose neutral or degraded feeder | Call utility if widespread | Inspect main panel, neutral, feeders |
| Weak or tripping furnace breaker | Failing breaker or disconnect | Reset once, do not force | Test and replace breaker/disconnect |
| Line voltage measures fine but code persists | Faulty transformer or board sensing | None | Test transformer; replace board if confirmed |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Electrical work should be done by a licensed HVAC or electrical pro.

- **DIY breaker reset / filter / thermostat batteries:** $0–$25
- **Tighten or repair a loose furnace/whip connection:** $100–$250
- **Replace a failing furnace breaker or disconnect:** $150–$350
- **Repair a loose neutral or feeder in the main panel:** $200–$600+ (electrician)
- **Replace low-voltage transformer:** $150–$350
- **Replace integrated control board (only if confirmed faulty):** $400–$700+
- **Utility service voltage repair:** typically no charge to you if the fault is on the utility's side — call your power company

Costs vary by region, board model and labor rates. A proper voltage measurement should always come before parts replacement.
