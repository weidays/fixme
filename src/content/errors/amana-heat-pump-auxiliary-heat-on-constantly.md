## What this code means

"Auxiliary heat on constantly" is a symptom, not a numbered Amana fault code. Most thermostats show AUX or AUX HEAT whenever the backup heat is running. That backup is either the electric heat strips in your Amana air handler or a gas furnace on a dual-fuel system.

Some aux heat is normal:

- **Defrost:** During defrost, the strips run so the system doesn't blow cold air.
- **Very cold weather:** Aux runs when the outdoor temperature drops below your system's balance point.
- **Big setpoint jumps:** Aux runs when you raise the setpoint more than about 2°F at once.

It becomes a problem when aux runs for hours in mild weather, or never shuts off. That means one of three things:

1. **The heat pump can't keep up.** The compressor isn't producing enough heat.
2. **The thermostat is calling for aux** when it shouldn't.
3. **The aux circuit is stuck on.** For example, a welded heat-strip relay or sequencer energizes the strips even with no call.

The thermostat only tells you whether aux is being *called*. A stuck relay can run the strips with no AUX indicator at all.

How this behaves depends on your setup:

- **Conventional 24V thermostats:** Behavior varies with the thermostat's own staging and lockout settings.
- **Amana communicating systems (ComfortBridge/CoolCloud):** The air handler or outdoor board may log a related fault, such as an outdoor sensor or compressor issue, that explains why backup heat is being used.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat set to Emergency Heat, or set up wrong.** EM HEAT disables the compressor. On a new or replaced thermostat, a common cause is a wrong O/B reversing-valve setting or a wrong system type (set as "conventional" instead of "heat pump"). A missing or too-low aux lockout temperature can also cause it.
2. **Normal recovery or cold-weather operation mistaken for a fault.** Large setback schedules, "smart recovery," or outdoor temperatures below the balance point all legitimately bring on aux. Some smart thermostats aggressively use aux to hit a schedule on time.
3. **Restricted airflow.** A dirty filter, closed registers, or a blocked return reduces heat output and lengthens run times. That can trip time-based aux staging.
4. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** The heat pump runs but delivers lukewarm air, so the thermostat keeps adding aux. This is one of the most common mechanical causes.
5. **Outdoor unit not running, or frozen/defrost problems.** This includes a failed capacitor, contactor, outdoor fan, or compressor. If the outdoor unit is iced or stuck in defrost, the strips run continuously. These issues are covered in the separate defrost and icing guides.
6. **Stuck heat-strip sequencer or relay.** The contacts weld closed, so the strips stay energized even when the thermostat isn't calling for aux.
7. **Faulty outdoor temperature sensor or wiring fault.** This affects thermostats or communicating systems that use outdoor temperature to decide when to use aux. A shorted W2/aux wire, or a miswired W2 jumpered to the heat call, can also cause it.
8. **Reversing valve not fully shifting.** The heat pump puts out little heat in heating mode, so aux makes up the difference.
9. **Undersized heat pump or a poorly insulated home.** This is a design issue, not a failure. Aux runs a lot every cold season.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the thermostat mode.** Make sure it says HEAT (or AUTO), not EM HEAT or EMERGENCY. Switch it back to HEAT and watch for 30-60 minutes.
- **Avoid big temperature jumps.** Raise the setpoint 1-2°F at a time. If you use deep night setbacks, try a smaller setback, or turn on "heat pump" or "adaptive recovery" in the thermostat settings.
- **Check the thermostat's system type.** If you installed a new thermostat recently, confirm in its installer menu (using the manual) that it is set to "heat pump." Also confirm the O/B setting matches Amana: most Amana heat pumps energize O in cooling.
  - If you aren't sure about the wiring, stop here and call a technician.
- **Replace thermostat batteries** if your model uses them. Weak batteries can cause erratic behavior.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty.
- **Open your vents.** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Look at the outdoor unit while the thermostat calls for heat** (outside defrost). The fan should spin and you should hear the compressor running.
  - Light frost during a defrost cycle is normal.
  - Heavy ice, or a silent outdoor unit, needs a technician.
- **Check the breakers.** Make sure the outdoor unit's breaker and outdoor disconnect are on.
  - If a breaker has tripped, you may reset it once.
  - If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.
- **Make sure the air handler access panels are fully seated.**
- **Clear snow and debris** from around the outdoor unit. Keep about 2 feet of clearance.

Do not open the air handler or outdoor unit cabinet. Heat-strip circuits carry 240V at high current.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should:

- **Verify the thermostat configuration and wiring.** This includes system type, O/B, aux lockout, and staging. They should also confirm whether W2/aux is actually being called at the air handler.
- **Measure temperature split at the air handler** with the heat pump only (strips off). A healthy heat pump typically delivers air roughly 20-30°F warmer than return air in moderate weather.
- **Check refrigerant pressures and superheat/subcooling** against Amana's charging chart for your model. If the charge is low, they should leak-search rather than just "top off."
- **Test the outdoor unit:** capacitor, contactor, compressor amp draw, outdoor fan motor, and the defrost control and sensor.
- **Check the heat-strip sequencers or relays.** With no aux call, the strips should draw zero amps. If they draw current, a relay or sequencer is stuck.
- **Check the reversing valve.** They do this by checking line temperatures and pressures in heat mode.
- **On communicating systems,** pull the fault history from the ComfortBridge or CoolCloud app, or from the board's 7-segment display.
- **Evaluate the balance point and sizing** if no fault is found.

Be wary of a quote that adds refrigerant without finding the leak. Also be wary of one that replaces the compressor without documented amp, pressure, and winding tests.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat shows EM HEAT | Emergency heat selected | Switch to HEAT mode | None needed |
| AUX after every morning warm-up | Large setback or recovery | Reduce setback; enable heat-pump recovery setting | Adjust aux lockout/staging if needed |
| AUX constantly after new thermostat install | Wrong system type or O/B setting | Check installer settings with the manual | Correct wiring and configuration |
| Weak airflow, long run times | Dirty filter or blocked returns | Replace filter, open vents | Inspect blower and ducts |
| Heat pump runs but air is lukewarm | Low refrigerant charge | Replace filter, then call | Leak search, repair, recharge to spec |
| Outdoor unit silent, house warm only from strips | Failed capacitor, contactor, or compressor | Check breaker/disconnect; reset once | Electrical tests; replace failed part |
| Outdoor unit heavily iced | Defrost control or sensor failure | Clear snow; do not chip ice | Diagnose the defrost board and sensor |
| Strips hot with no AUX on thermostat; very high bills | Welded sequencer or relay | Call a pro | Amp test; replace the sequencer or relay |
| AUX runs regardless of outdoor temperature | Faulty outdoor sensor or wiring | Note your thermostat settings | Test sensor and wiring; fix any W2 short |
| Little heat, normal-sounding unit | Reversing valve not fully shifting | Call a pro | Test the valve and solenoid; repair or replace |
