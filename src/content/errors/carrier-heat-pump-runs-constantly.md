## What this code means

"Runs constantly" is not a fault code that a Carrier heat pump displays. It is a symptom, and it shows up in one of two very different ways:

1. **The system runs but can't reach the setpoint.** The unit is working but losing the race. Causes include restricted airflow, a dirty coil, low refrigerant charge or an undersized system.
2. **The system keeps running after the thermostat is satisfied (or turned off).** This is a control problem: a stuck contactor, a thermostat fault or a wiring fault.

Some long run times are normal.

- **Infinity and Greenspeed (variable-speed) models** are designed to run long, low-capacity cycles. Constant low-speed operation on these units usually means the system is working as designed.
- **Extreme temperatures:** in very hot or very cold weather, even a correctly sized single- or two-stage unit may run continuously.

Because this is a symptom rather than a stored code, there is nothing to reset. If the control board is also flashing a diagnostic code, that code takes priority. See the related Carrier heat pump pages listed below.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked returns and registers.** Reduced airflow cuts capacity, so run times get longer.
2. **Thermostat setting or fault.** Common examples:
   - The fan is set to "On" rather than "Auto," so the blower runs nonstop.
   - The setpoint is unrealistic.
   - The thermostat has weak batteries or is placed poorly (in sun or near a supply vent).
   - The thermostat is miswired or not configured for a heat pump.
3. **Dirty outdoor coil or blocked outdoor unit.** Debris, cottonwood, leaves or snow on the coil reduce heat transfer.
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** The unit runs long and output is weak. In cooling mode you may see ice on the lines. In heating mode you may notice heavy reliance on auxiliary heat.
5. **Stuck or welded contactor in the outdoor unit.** The outdoor unit keeps running even with the thermostat off. This needs prompt attention.
6. **Dirty indoor coil or a failing blower motor.** Both reduce airflow and capacity.
7. **Reversing valve or defrost issues.** The unit runs but delivers the wrong temperature or only partial capacity.
8. **Duct leakage or a system undersized for the home.** The unit never catches up on design days.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** confirm the mode (Heat or Cool) and set the fan to **Auto**. Pick a realistic setpoint, roughly 68–70°F for heat and 74–78°F for cool, then see whether the unit cycles off. Replace the batteries if the thermostat uses them.
- **Turn the thermostat to Off.** If the outdoor unit **keeps running** after a few minutes, shut it off at the outdoor disconnect or the breaker and call a technician. This points to a stuck contactor, and the compressor can be damaged if it keeps running.
- **Air filter:** replace it if it is dirty. Check the filter every 1–3 months.
- **Vents and returns:** open all supply registers and clear furniture or rugs away from the return grilles.
- **Outdoor unit:** from the outside only, clear leaves, snow and debris from around the unit, and keep about 2 feet of open space. Do not open the panels.
- **Condensate line:** make sure the drain line or pan isn't backed up. A tripped float switch can cause erratic operation.
- **Breaker:** if you turned power off, restore it **once**. If the problem returns, stop and call for service.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically work through these steps:

- **Confirm the model type.** For variable-speed Infinity or Greenspeed units, they will review the Infinity control's operating status and fault history before calling long runtime a defect.
- **Check thermostat call signals.** They verify the call signals at the air handler or furnace and at the outdoor unit. If the contactor stays closed with no 24V call present, the contactor is stuck.
- **Inspect the contactor** for pitting or welded contacts.
- **Measure temperature split and airflow.** A normal split across the indoor coil is roughly 16–22°F in cooling. They will also check static pressure.
- **Check refrigerant charge** using superheat and subcooling against Carrier's charging chart. If the charge is low, they should perform leak detection before recharging.
- **Inspect the coils.** They check the indoor and outdoor coil condition and clean them if needed.
- **Test components.** This includes the blower capacitor and motor, the reversing valve, and defrost operation.
- **Evaluate load and sizing.** If everything checks out, they may assess sizing and duct leakage.

Be wary of a quote to "add refrigerant" that doesn't mention finding the leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blower runs nonstop, outdoor unit cycles normally | Thermostat fan set to On | Set fan to Auto | Check thermostat wiring if the problem persists |
| Runs long, weak airflow at vents | Dirty filter or blocked returns | Replace filter, clear vents | Check blower and static pressure |
| Outdoor unit runs with thermostat off | Stuck or welded contactor | Shut off breaker or disconnect, call a pro | Replace contactor |
| Runs long, little temperature change, ice on lines | Low refrigerant charge | Turn off and call a pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Runs long, outdoor coil visibly matted | Dirty outdoor coil | Clear debris around the unit | Clean coil |
| Runs constantly at low speed, home comfortable | Normal variable-speed operation | None | None needed |
| Never catches up in extreme weather | Sizing or duct leakage | Keep setpoint moderate | Load calculation, duct sealing |

## Repair costs

These are typical US price ranges:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter, thermostat settings, batteries | $0–$40 |
| Service call and diagnosis | $90–$200 |
| Contactor replacement | $150–$350 |
| Thermostat replacement (installed) | $150–$500 (Infinity control: $400–$900) |
| Coil cleaning (indoor or outdoor) | $100–$400 |
| Capacitor or blower motor | $150–$900 |
| Refrigerant leak repair plus recharge | $400–$1,500+ |
| Reversing valve replacement | $800–$2,000 |
