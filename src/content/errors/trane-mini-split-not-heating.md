## What this code means

"Not heating" is a symptom, not a single error code. Your Trane mini-split turns on in Heat mode but the room doesn't warm up. The indoor unit may blow cool or room-temperature air, blow nothing, or warm only weakly.

On Trane ductless systems, a heating failure usually shows up in one of three ways:

- **The system is protecting itself.** Defrost, cold-air prevention, high-pressure protection or low-ambient limits pause heat on purpose. These pauses are normal and clear on their own.
- **An error code or blinking LED appears.** This is common with sensor, communication or inverter faults. Some codes clear once the condition goes away. Others hold until power is cycled. The exact behavior and code meaning depend on the series and its manual, so record any code you see before resetting.
- **The system runs but produces little heat.** This points to restricted airflow, low refrigerant charge or a component fault.

**How this page differs from related ones:** this page covers ductless systems specifically. Ducted Trane heat pumps are covered under Trane Heat Pump Not Heating. If your unit actively blows cold air, also see Trane Heat Pump Blowing Cold Air in Heat Mode.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Wrong mode or settings.** The unit is set to Auto, Cool, Dry or Fan, the setpoint is below room temperature, or a timer or sleep setting is limiting output. The remote batteries may also be dead.
2. **Dirty indoor filter or blocked indoor coil.** Restricted airflow lowers heat output. It can also make the indoor coil overheat and trigger high-pressure protection, which reduces or stops heating.
3. **Normal defrost or warm-up delay.** Heat stops for about 5 to 15 minutes while the outdoor coil defrosts. At startup, the indoor fan waits until the coil is warm.
4. **Outdoor temperature below the model's heating range.** Non-low-ambient models lose significant capacity in deep cold.
5. **Blocked or iced outdoor unit.** Snow, leaves, a drift or a clogged base pan can block airflow, or ice can build up when defrost isn't clearing it.
6. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Flare connections are a common leak point on ductless systems. Low charge causes weak heat over time and may trigger a protection code.
7. **Failed temperature sensor (thermistor).** A bad room, indoor coil, outdoor coil or discharge sensor can confuse the board's heating and defrost logic. This usually shows an error code.
8. **Reversing valve or coil failure.** The unit cools fine but won't switch into heat mode.
9. **Communication or inverter board fault.** A failed indoor-to-outdoor communication link, outdoor PCB or inverter board will typically show a code, and the compressor won't run.
10. **Compressor or outdoor fan motor failure.** This is less common. See the related compressor and fan pages.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the remote.** Make sure it is set to Heat (the sun icon), not Auto or Cool. Set the temperature at least 3–4°F above room temperature. Cancel any timers or sleep modes, and replace the remote batteries. On multi-zone systems, make sure no other indoor head is set to Cool, since a mode conflict can block heating.
- **Wait out a delay.** Give the system 15 minutes. If the outdoor unit is steaming or dripping, it's probably defrosting, so let it finish.
- **Clean the indoor filters.** Open the front panel, slide out the mesh filters, vacuum or rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry completely and reinstall them. Don't spray or touch the coil behind them.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove snow, leaves and debris, keeping about 2 feet clear around the unit and above it. Gently brush away loose snow. **Don't chip ice off the coil.** Make sure the unit isn't buried and that its drain area isn't frozen shut.
- **Check the power.** Look at the breaker and the outdoor disconnect. If the breaker has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Do one reset.** Turn the power off at the breaker or disconnect, wait 5 minutes, then restore it. Do this only once. If an error code or blinking LED returns, write it down.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Make sure the indoor unit isn't dripping and the drain line outlet isn't blocked or frozen.

Stop there. Panels, wiring, boards, sensors and refrigerant work are all jobs for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will typically do the following:

1. **Read the fault code or LED blink history.** They'll look it up in the service manual for your exact Trane series, since codes differ between platforms.
2. **Confirm the mode and setup.** This includes checking for multi-zone mode conflicts and DIP or jumper settings.
3. **Measure temperatures.** They'll check supply-air temperature and the temperature split, then compare them to what's expected for the current outdoor temperature.
4. **Check refrigerant pressures** using the service port in heating mode, plus superheat and subcooling where applicable. If the charge is low, they should **find and repair the leak** with an electronic detector or bubble test at the flares, rather than just topping it off.
5. **Test the thermistors** by comparing their resistance against the manufacturer's temperature/resistance chart.
6. **Check the reversing valve coil** for voltage and confirm the valve actually shifts.
7. **Check inverter output and indoor-to-outdoor communication voltage,** and verify the compressor and fan motor windings.
8. **Inspect defrost operation** and check the outdoor coil and base pan for ice.

Be cautious about a quote that recommends adding refrigerant without a leak search, or replacing a board without first checking codes and sensors.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan blows cool air, no code | Wrong mode or setpoint, or mode conflict | Set to Heat, raise the setpoint, match the zone modes | None if settings fix it |
| No airflow for a few minutes after start | Cold-air prevention (normal) | Wait 5 minutes | None |
| Heat stops, outdoor unit steams | Defrost cycle (normal) | Wait 15 minutes | Inspect defrost and sensor if it repeats very often |
| Weak heat, dusty filters | Restricted airflow | Clean the filters | Deep-clean the indoor coil and blower wheel |
| Outdoor unit packed with ice | Failed defrost or blocked drainage | Clear snow and debris only | Diagnose the defrost sensor, board or drainage |
| Weak heat that gets worse over weeks | Low refrigerant from a leak | None | Find and repair the leak, evacuate, recharge |
| Cools fine but won't heat | Reversing valve or coil | None | Test the coil and replace the valve |
| Error code or blinking LED | Sensor, communication or inverter fault | Record the code, do one power reset | Diagnose using the code and replace the part |
| Little heat in severe cold | Below the model's rated range | Use backup heat | Advise on a low-ambient model if needed |

## Repair costs

Typical US prices, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Settings, filter cleaning | $0 |
| Diagnostic visit | $90 – $200 |
| Professional coil and blower cleaning | $150 – $400 per head |
| Thermistor replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Reversing valve coil | $150 – $300 |
| Reversing valve replacement (includes refrigerant recovery and recharge) | $800 – $1,800 |
| Leak search and flare repair plus recharge | $300 – $1,000 |
| Indoor PCB | $250 – $600 |
| Outdoor or inverter board | $400 – $1,200 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350 – $800 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500 – $3,500+ |

Warranty coverage often pays for major parts but not labor. Registration status matters, so check before approving a repair. If the system is out of warranty and the compressor has failed, replacing the unit is often worth comparing on cost.
