## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. It describes a Lennox heat pump whose outdoor (condenser) fan is still while the system calls for heating or cooling. Often you'll hear the compressor humming or running with the fan stopped.

On most single- and two-stage Lennox units, the fan is a PSC or ECM motor. It is powered through the contactor and controlled by the defrost control board, which turns the fan off during defrost.

On variable-capacity models such as the XP25 and SL25XPV, the inverter control drives the fan. A failure there may also show an alert code on the outdoor control's display or on an iComfort/S30 thermostat. Check there first and look up that specific code.

Is it actually a fault? Rule out normal defrost first:

- **Normal defrost:** In heat mode, the fan stops, the compressor keeps running, the unit steams, and the fan restarts within about 2–15 minutes.
- **A real fault:** The fan stays off past defrost, stays off in cooling mode, or hums without turning.

Running the compressor without the fan risks high-pressure trips and compressor damage. That is why this is pro-level.

Defrost control fault codes vary by board. Some lock out after repeated high-pressure trips and hold until power is cycled. Others retry automatically. Check the label on your unit or ask your technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor (PSC-motor units).** This is the most common cause. The motor hums or the blade turns slowly, and it may spin if nudged. Many Lennox units use a dual capacitor shared with the compressor.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor.** This includes worn bearings, an open winding, or an overheated motor that trips its internal thermal protector. The blade may feel stiff or gritty, or the motor may stop after running a few minutes.
3. **Defrost control board fault.** The board's fan relay contacts can fail open, or the board can stay stuck in defrost logic. In that case the compressor runs and the fan never gets power.
4. **Contactor or wiring problem.** Pitted contactor contacts on the fan leg, a burned or loose wire, or rodent damage at the fan plug or board terminals.
5. **Physical obstruction.** Ice in heat mode, sticks, or debris jamming the blade. A blade loose on its shaft is also possible.
6. **ECM motor or inverter fault (variable-capacity models).** The fan motor module or the outdoor inverter control may fail to command the fan. These are diagnosed with Lennox service codes.
7. **High-pressure or temperature safety lockout.** This usually follows one of the causes above. Once the board locks out, nothing outdoors runs, including the fan.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Watch for defrost.** In heat mode, if the unit is steaming and the fan restarts within about 15 minutes, there is no problem. Don't call anyone yet.
- **Thermostat.** Confirm it's set to Heat or Cool with a setpoint that creates a real call for heating or cooling. Replace batteries if the display is weak or blank.
- **Breakers and disconnect.** Check the outdoor unit's breaker in the main panel and the disconnect box near the unit. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Air filter.** Replace it if it's dirty. A badly clogged filter in heat mode raises system pressures and can contribute to safety trips.
- **Look, don't reach.** With the system **off at the thermostat and the disconnect**, look through the top grille for sticks, leaves or heavy ice around the blade. Clear leaves and debris from around the outside of the coil, keeping about 2 feet of clearance. Never put hands or tools through the grille.
- **One reset.** If the system seems locked out, turn the power off at the disconnect for about 5 minutes, then restore it. Do this only once. If the fan still won't spin, turn the system off at the thermostat to protect the compressor, and call for service.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically work through these steps:

1. **Confirm the call.** Verify 24 VAC at the contactor coil and check the defrost board for flashing fault or lockout codes. On iComfort and variable-capacity units, read the service codes.
2. **Measure line voltage.** Check incoming 240 V at the contactor and inspect the contacts for pitting.
3. **Test the capacitor.** Measure microfarads on the fan (FAN/COM) terminals against the rated value. Readings more than about 6% low, or a bulging case, mean replacement.
4. **Check voltage to the motor.** If the board is in run mode and voltage reaches the fan leads, the motor is suspect. If no voltage reaches the fan, the board relay or wiring is suspect.
5. **Test the motor.** Measure winding resistance, check for shorts to ground, and spin the shaft by hand with power off to check the bearings. ECM motors are tested with Lennox procedures or a motor tester.
6. **Check after the repair.** Confirm the fan's rotation direction and amp draw, and verify system pressures are normal so the compressor wasn't stressed.

Red flags in a quote:

- Replacing a motor without first testing the capacitor.
- Replacing a board without first confirming voltage at the fan output.
- Recommending a new system for a fan failure on a unit that is otherwise healthy.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan off, unit steaming in heat mode, restarts within 15 min | Normal defrost | None; wait | None |
| Fan hums but won't turn; spins slowly | Weak or failed run capacitor | Turn system off at thermostat | Test and replace capacitor |
| Fan runs a few minutes then stops; motor hot | Motor bearings or thermal overload | Turn system off | Test and replace condenser fan motor |
| Compressor runs, fan never starts, no hum | Defrost board fan relay or wiring | Check breaker and disconnect; one reset | Voltage checks; repair wiring or replace board |
| Nothing outdoors runs at all | Tripped breaker, contactor, or lockout | Reset breaker once; check thermostat | Diagnose contactor, 24 V circuit, lockout cause |
| Blade blocked or iced | Debris or ice | Power off; clear debris from outside only | Remove jammed debris; check defrost operation |
| Variable-capacity unit shows alert code | ECM motor or inverter fault | Note the code; one power cycle | Lennox service-code diagnosis; replace motor or inverter |
