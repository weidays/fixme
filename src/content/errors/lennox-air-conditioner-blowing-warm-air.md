## What this code means

"Blowing warm air" is a symptom, not a numbered Lennox code. It means the indoor blower is moving air, but the air coming out is room temperature or warmer.

This page focuses on air that is actually **warm**, not just "not cold enough." Warm air usually points to one of three things:

- **The outdoor unit isn't removing heat.** The compressor or condenser fan isn't running, or isn't running correctly.
- **The system has almost no refrigerant.**
- **Warm attic air is getting into the supply ducts.**

Single-stage Lennox units (Merit and most Elite models) have no outdoor fault display. Variable-capacity Signature models with iComfort or S30/S40 thermostats may log an alert that a technician can read. Alert numbers differ by model, so check your unit's installation manual rather than relying on generic code lists.

Behavior after the fault clears also depends on the model:
- Basic units simply restart once the condition clears.
- Units with a compressor protection or low-pressure lockout may hold off for a timed delay. Some need a power cycle to reset.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor (dual capacitor).** This is the most common Lennox outdoor failure. With a bad capacitor:
   - The outdoor fan and/or compressor won't start.
   - The unit often hums, and the indoor blower keeps pushing warm air.
2. **Pitted or stuck contactor, or no 24V signal reaching it.** When the contactor doesn't pull in, the outdoor unit never starts.
3. **Tripped outdoor breaker or pulled disconnect.** The indoor blower runs on the furnace or air handler circuit, so it keeps going while the outdoor unit has no power.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** Leaks are common at evaporator coils and service valves. A low charge usually causes weak cooling and icing first, then warm air once the charge is very low.
5. **Compressor overload trip or failed compressor.** A hot, overloaded compressor shuts itself off and cycles back on later. A failed compressor won't run at all.
6. **Dirty condenser coil or failed outdoor fan motor.** Both cause high head pressure and overload trips. They usually show up as cooling that starts and then turns warm.
7. **Disconnected or torn supply duct.** This one is common in attic installs. It typically causes warm air at only some registers.
8. **Thermostat set to Heat, Fan "On," or a heat pump wiring/setting mismatch.** This happens most often after a thermostat replacement.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat.** Confirm it is set to **Cool**, the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. With the fan set to **On**, the blower runs warm air between cooling cycles. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Air filter.** Replace it if it's dirty. A badly clogged filter can freeze the coil, and after defrosting the system may blow poorly cooled air.
- **Outdoor breaker and disconnect.**
  - Check the AC breaker in your panel. Reset it **once** if it has tripped.
  - Make sure the outdoor disconnect is fully seated.
  - If the breaker trips again, stop and call a technician. Repeated trips mean a real fault (see "Breaker keeps tripping").
- **Listen at the outdoor unit while cooling is called for.**
  - **Silent:** points to power or the contactor.
  - **Humming with the fan not spinning:** turn cooling off and call a technician. Don't push the fan blades with a stick, because this is a capacitor problem.
  - **Fan running, no compressor:** a technician needs to check the capacitor, compressor, or overload.
- **Outdoor unit clearance.** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and anything within about 2 feet of the coil. Gently hosing the outside of the fins from top to bottom with the power off at the disconnect is fine. Don't pressure-wash them.
- **Registers.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate line.** Some systems have a float switch that stops the outdoor unit when the drain backs up. If the drain pan is full, clear the line or pan as described in "Leaking water."
- **Exterior panels.** Confirm the furnace or air handler panels are fully seated.

## How a technician will diagnose it

1. **Confirm the call for cooling.** The tech checks for 24V at the contactor coil (Y and C terminals) and confirms the thermostat type and settings.
2. **Check line voltage and the contactor.** They measure voltage across the contactor and inspect for pitted contacts or welded points.
3. **Test the capacitor.** With the capacitor safely discharged, they measure microfarads on both the HERM and FAN sides against the rating on the label. A reading more than about 6% low means replacement.
4. **Check the compressor.** This includes amp draw, winding resistance (C-S-R), checks for a short to ground, and whether the internal overload has tripped. A tripped overload must cool down before the compressor can be judged.
5. **Read the refrigerant charge.** On a running system the tech measures:
   - suction and liquid pressures,
   - superheat (for piston/fixed orifice systems) or subcooling (for TXV systems, which most Lennox units use),
   - temperature split across the coil (a normal split is about 16–22°F).

   If the charge is low, they should **find the leak** with an electronic detector, bubbles, or a nitrogen pressure test before recharging.
6. **Inspect ducts.** They check the supply plenum and attic runs for gaps or disconnections.
7. **Pull logged alerts (communicating systems only).** On iComfort/S30/S40 systems, they read the stored alert history.

**Red flags on a quote:**
- Recommending "add refrigerant" with no leak search.
- Condemning the compressor without testing the capacitor and windings first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor fan blowing warm | Tripped breaker, open disconnect, or no 24V signal | Check thermostat mode; reset breaker once; check disconnect | Trace low-voltage circuit; test contactor coil |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan doesn't spin | Failed run capacitor or seized fan motor | Turn cooling off and call | Test and replace capacitor or fan motor |
| Outdoor fan runs, compressor doesn't | Weak capacitor (HERM side), overload trip, or failed compressor | Turn off; clear debris around the unit | Test capacitor, windings, and amp draw |
| Cold at first, then warm after running a while | Dirty condenser, low charge, or overheating compressor | Rinse outside of coil gently; replace filter | Clean coil; check charge; find leak |
| Ice on the copper line, then warm air | Low refrigerant or airflow problem | Replace filter; set system to Off with fan On to thaw | Find and repair leak; recharge |
| Warm air in only some rooms | Disconnected or leaking duct, or closed damper | Open registers; check visible dampers | Reconnect and seal ducts |
| Warm air started after a new thermostat | Wrong settings or wiring | Check mode and equipment-type settings | Correct the wiring and configuration |
