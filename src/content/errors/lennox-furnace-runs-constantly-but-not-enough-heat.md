## What this code means

"Runs constantly but not enough heat" is not a single Lennox fault code — it's a performance complaint. Your furnace is calling for heat and firing, but it either produces too little heat or can't move that heat into the living space fast enough to satisfy the thermostat. Because it never reaches setpoint, the thermostat keeps calling and the furnace runs on and on.

On Lennox two-stage and variable-capacity furnaces (SLP99V, EL296V, and similar), this often shows up as the unit being stuck in **low fire** when it should ramp to high fire. On single-stage units (ML180, ML193), it usually points to restricted airflow, a weak flame, or an oversized heat loss (poor insulation, extreme cold, leaky ducts).

There's no lockout or auto-reset tied to this condition itself — the furnace keeps operating normally. But the underlying cause may store a related diagnostic code (limit, flame sense, or pressure switch), so a technician should read the control board's LED/display history. Note that the alphanumeric fault codes Lennox uses differ by control board generation, so a code only means what your specific board's manual says it means.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow means warm air crawls out of the registers and the furnace can't satisfy the thermostat.
2. **Thermostat fan set to ON instead of AUTO** — the blower runs nonstop, delivering room-temperature air between burner cycles and feeling like "no heat."
3. **Closed, blocked, or leaky supply/return registers and ducts** — heat is being produced but not delivered, or it's dumping into an unconditioned space.
4. **Furnace stuck in low fire / staging fault** (two-stage and variable models) — a wiring, thermostat compatibility, or control-board staging problem keeps it from ramping up.
5. **Weak or dirty flame sensor / partially clogged burners** — reduced flame current or incomplete combustion lowers heat output.
6. **Failing gas valve or low gas supply pressure** — the burner fires but under-fires, producing less BTU than rated.
7. **Limit switch cycling the burner** due to overheating from poor airflow — burner shuts off repeatedly while the blower keeps running.
8. **Undersized furnace for the home or severe heat loss** — in extreme cold, a marginal system simply can't keep up.

**A note on the heat exchanger:** a cracked or corroded heat exchanger is primarily a carbon monoxide safety failure, not a heat-output problem. It usually doesn't measurably reduce how much heat you get until the furnace is already cycling on limit or rollout. Treat it as a safety issue that is sometimes discovered while investigating poor performance — not as the explanation for a house that won't warm up.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform:

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or more than 90 days old, swap it. Filter sizes vary by furnace cabinet width, so read the size printed on the cardboard frame of the filter already in the unit rather than assuming a common size. This alone fixes a large share of "runs constantly" complaints.
- **Set the thermostat to HEAT and the fan to AUTO** (not ON). Confirm the setpoint is above room temperature.
- **Replace thermostat batteries** if your model uses them — a weak thermostat can send erratic calls.
- **Walk the house and open every supply register and return grille.** Make sure furniture, rugs, or drapes aren't blocking them. Closing too many registers starves airflow.
- **Check visible ductwork in the basement or attic** for obvious disconnected or crushed sections — but don't crawl into unsafe spaces.
- **Check the breaker** labeled for the furnace; if it has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Look at the condensate drain** (high-efficiency models) for standing water or an obvious clog that could be triggering a safety shutdown. This is a **visual check only** — do not disassemble the condensate trap or pull the inducer drain hoses, which sit in the combustion path and must be reassembled and verified by a technician.

If these don't restore normal heating, the problem is inside the cabinet and needs a technician. **If you ever smell gas, shut the furnace off, leave the house, and call your gas utility's emergency line or 911 before doing anything else.**

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified HVAC tech will typically:

- **Read the control board's fault/LED history** for staging, flame, limit, or pressure codes that point to a root cause, interpreting them against the manual for that specific board.
- **Measure temperature rise** (return vs. supply air) and compare it to the rating-plate spec; a rise that's too high means airflow restriction, too low means under-firing.
- **Verify blower operation and speed tap / ECM programming** to confirm the furnace is moving its rated CFM.
- **Confirm staging** on two-stage and variable units — checking whether high fire is being commanded and delivered.
- **Measure manifold gas pressure** and inspect the gas valve to confirm the burner is firing at its rated BTU input.
- **Measure flame current (microamps)** and inspect/clean the flame sensor and burners.
- **Inspect the heat exchanger** for cracks or corrosion, often with a camera and/or combustion analysis.
- **Static pressure testing of the duct system** to catch undersized or leaky ductwork.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow at every register | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Measure static pressure, check blower |
| Blower runs even when air is cool | Fan set to ON | Switch thermostat to AUTO | Verify blower relay/control logic |
| Some rooms cold, airflow weak | Closed/blocked/leaky ducts | Open vents, clear obstructions | Duct leakage and sizing testing |
| Furnace never ramps to high fire | Staging/control fault (2-stage) | None — call pro | Test staging, thermostat compat, board |
| Flame looks small/yellow or cycles | Weak flame / dirty burners | None — call pro | Clean flame sensor & burners, test current |
| Burner under-fires, low heat | Gas valve or low supply pressure | None — call pro | Measure manifold pressure, test valve |
| Burner cycles while blower runs | Limit tripping from low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Diagnose airflow, test limit switch |
| Soot, sharp or burnt odors | Possible combustion fault or cracked heat exchanger — **treat as a CO emergency** | Shut the furnace off at its service switch and **do not restart it**; open windows/doors to ventilate; if anyone has a headache, nausea or dizziness, or a CO alarm sounds, get everyone outside first and call 911 or the gas utility's emergency line | Combustion analysis, CO testing, inspect/replace heat exchanger |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional and model variation applies):

- **Air filter:** $15–$50 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$10 (DIY).
- **Thermostat replacement / recalibration:** $150–$400 installed.
- **Flame sensor cleaning or replacement:** $100–$275.
- **Burner cleaning / tune-up:** $100–$300.
- **Duct repair or sealing:** $350–$1,500+ depending on scope.
- **Blower motor, PSC (standard single-speed):** $400–$900 installed.
- **Blower motor, ECM / variable-speed** (as used in SLP99V, EL296V and similar): $700–$1,800 installed, since the motor and its control module often have to be replaced together.
- **Gas valve replacement:** $300–$650.
- **Control / integrated circuit board (staging faults):** $500–$1,200 — communicating and integrated Lennox boards sit at the upper end.
- **Heat exchanger replacement:** $1,500–$3,500+ installed, and often more on a variable-capacity Lennox — frequently approaching the cost of a new furnace, so get a replacement quote alongside the repair quote on older units.

A dirty filter costs you nothing but a few dollars; a cracked heat exchanger is the expensive, safety-critical worst case.

## Related issues

Lennox reports faults with alphanumeric codes that vary by control board generation, so look up any code you see in the manual for your furnace's specific control board rather than a generic list. By symptom:

- **Lennox furnace limit switch open** — airflow restriction is a shared root cause.
- **Lennox furnace weak flame signal / low flame current** — a weak flame lowers heat output.
- **Lennox furnace flame sensor problems** — dirty sensors reduce performance.
- **Lennox furnace ignition lockout** — the furnace stops trying to light after failed attempts.
- **Lennox furnace short cycling** — related airflow and limit issues.
- **Lennox furnace keeps shutting off** — overlapping airflow and safety causes.
- **Lennox furnace blower won't shut off** — if the complaint is really a fan-ON issue.
