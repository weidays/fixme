## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a **symptom**, not a Carrier fault code — most Carrier air conditioners don't display a code for this. You notice it when the compressor in the outdoor unit is humming or running but the fan blade on top isn't turning, or the whole outdoor unit is silent while the indoor blower runs.

The outdoor (condenser) fan pulls air across the coil to reject heat. Without it, the system can't cool and the compressor overheats fast. This is rated **pro** because the most common fixes involve a capacitor that stores a dangerous charge, 240-volt wiring, or motor replacement.

**Turn the system off now.** Set the thermostat to "off" (not just "cool") so the compressor stops running without the fan.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor.** The single most common cause. The capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque. When it dies, the motor hums but won't start — or spins if you nudge the blade.
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor.** Worn bearings or a shorted winding. Often smells burnt or the motor is too hot to touch.
3. **Contactor not pulling in.** The contactor is the relay that sends power to the fan and compressor. Pitted contacts or a bad coil means no power reaches the fan.
4. **Debris or ice jamming the blade.** Sticks, leaves, or a bent blade physically block rotation.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown fuses at the disconnect.** Kills power to the whole outdoor unit — nothing runs.
6. **Loose or corroded wiring.** Especially at the capacitor spade terminals or motor connections.
7. **Control board / low-voltage fault (Infinity/Performance boards).** Less common; the board isn't calling for the fan. Behavior varies by model.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without opening the electrical panel of the outdoor unit:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to "cool" and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause icing that mimics or worsens cooling problems. Replace if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop — that's an electrical fault for a pro.
- **Visible debris:** With the unit **powered off at the breaker**, look through the fan grille for leaves, sticks, or ice. Clear loose debris you can reach from outside. Do not remove panels or reach toward the motor.
- **Supply vents:** Make sure indoor registers are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate:** Check that the indoor drain pan isn't overflowing (some systems shut down on a full pan).

**Do not** open the outdoor unit's access panel, touch the capacitor, or spin the fan repeatedly to "get it going." The capacitor can hold a lethal charge even with power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A quote should reflect these steps:

1. **Confirm power** at the disconnect and breaker with a meter.
2. **Discharge and test the capacitor** with a multimeter, comparing microfarad (µF) reading to the data plate rating. A weak or zero reading confirms the capacitor.
3. **Check the contactor** — listen for it pulling in, inspect contacts for pitting, and verify 24V at the coil.
4. **Test the fan motor** — check windings for continuity/shorts, spin the blade by hand for bearing drag, and verify voltage reaching the motor.
5. **Inspect wiring** at the capacitor, contactor, and motor for loose or burnt connections.
6. On **Infinity/Performance systems**, read any stored fault data at the control board or user interface to rule out a board-level command issue.

Sanity check: if a tech says "you need a new motor" without ever testing the capacitor first, ask why — the capacitor is cheaper and far more common.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan doesn't turn | Failed run capacitor | Shut system off; don't spin blade repeatedly | Discharge & test capacitor, replace |
| Fan spins if nudged with a stick | Weak/dead capacitor | Turn off, schedule service | Replace dual/single run capacitor |
| Motor hot, burnt smell, won't run | Burned-out fan motor | Turn off at breaker | Replace condenser fan motor, verify capacitor |
| Whole outdoor unit silent | Breaker/disconnect fuse or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test fuses, contactor coil, wiring |
| Buzzing but nothing engages | Contactor not pulling in | None | Replace contactor, check 24V control |
| Blade physically stuck | Debris or bent blade | Clear visible debris (power off) | Straighten/replace blade, check bearings |
| Fan cycles oddly on Infinity system | Control board / low-voltage | Note any UI fault codes | Diagnose board and wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor replacement:** $400–$650 (more for high-efficiency ECM motors on Infinity units)
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150–$300
- **Disconnect fuse / wiring repair:** $100–$300
- **Control board (Infinity/Performance):** $400–$900+
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$180 (often credited toward the repair)

Most no-spin calls turn out to be a **capacitor or contactor** — usually under $350 total. If the unit is old and the **compressor** was damaged by running without the fan, repair costs can climb toward $1,500–$2,800, at which point replacement is worth pricing.
