## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom rather than a formal Carrier fault code. On most single-stage Carrier condensers there is no display or code — you simply notice the compressor humming or the unit running while the top fan blade sits still. On **Carrier Infinity** systems, the outdoor board and Infinity control at the thermostat may log a related fault (for example a communication or outdoor unit fault), but Carrier does not publish a single universal "fan not spinning" code across all model years.

The outdoor fan (condenser fan) pulls air across the outdoor coil to reject the heat your system removed from inside. If it stops while the compressor runs, head pressure and temperature spike quickly. **This is why you should turn the system off rather than let it keep running.**

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor.** The most common cause by far. The dual run capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque. When it weakens, the motor hums but won't start, or only spins after a manual push.
2. **Burned-out condenser fan motor.** Bearings seize or windings fail with age, heat, and moisture. The motor won't turn even with a good capacitor.
3. **Contactor not pulling in (or welded).** The contactor is the relay that sends 240V to the outdoor unit. A failed coil, pitted contacts, or a stuck contactor can starve the fan of power.
4. **Debris or physical obstruction.** A stick, ice, or bent blade jamming the fan; or the blade set screw slipping on the motor shaft.
5. **Control board fault (Infinity/Performance).** On communicating units, a failed outdoor control board can withhold the fan command.
6. **Low-voltage / 24V wiring or transformer issue.** No control signal reaches the contactor, so nothing energizes.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect.** Sometimes only the outdoor circuit is dead while the indoor blower still runs.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the system OFF at the thermostat** so the compressor isn't running without airflow.
- **Check the thermostat** is set to Cool and the setpoint is below room temperature; replace batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Check the breaker** for the outdoor unit in your main panel — reset it once if tripped. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Look at the outdoor unit** for obvious debris in the fan blade, ice, or a fallen branch. Only clear debris with the disconnect pulled and power off.
- **Check your air filter and supply vents** indoors — restricted airflow can cause icing that stalls the whole system.
- **Check the condensate drain / pan** — a full float switch can shut the system down on some setups.

Do **not** open the electrical access panel on the condenser, touch the capacitor, or spin the blade by hand with power on. The capacitor holds a dangerous charge even after the power is cut.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirms 240V** at the disconnect and at the contactor line side.
- **Checks the contactor** — verifies the 24V coil is energizing and the contacts are pulling in and not pitted or welded.
- **Tests the run capacitor** with a meter, comparing the measured microfarads (µF) against the rating printed on the can (typically within ±6%).
- **Checks the fan motor** windings for resistance/open circuit and spins the shaft to feel for seized bearings.
- **Verifies the low-voltage signal** and transformer output if the contactor isn't pulling in.
- **On Infinity/communicating units**, reads stored fault codes and checks the outdoor control board.

A good tech will tell you *which* component failed and show you the reading. Be cautious of a quote to replace the whole motor before the capacitor has been tested.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan won't start | Failed run capacitor | Turn system off; don't force it | Test & replace capacitor |
| Fan spins only after a push | Weak run capacitor | Turn off; note this for the tech | Replace capacitor |
| No sound at all from unit | No power / tripped breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Check disconnect, contactor, 24V |
| Motor won't turn, good cap | Burned-out fan motor | Turn off | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Blade jammed by debris/ice | Obstruction | Clear debris with power off | Inspect blade & shaft |
| Buzzing at contactor, no fan | Contactor coil/contacts | None | Replace contactor |
| Fan dead, indoor runs (Infinity) | Board or comm fault | Note thermostat codes | Diagnose outdoor board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor replacement:** $300 – $650 (varies with horsepower and unit access)
- **Control board (Infinity/communicating):** $400 – $900+
- **Low-voltage wiring / transformer repair:** $150 – $400
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180 (often credited toward the repair)

DIY-only cost is effectively **$0** if it's a breaker reset or clearing debris. Anything inside the electrical cabinet — especially the capacitor — is a pro job due to shock risk.
