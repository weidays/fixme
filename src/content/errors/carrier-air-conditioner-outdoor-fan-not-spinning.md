## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a Carrier fault code — it's a physical symptom. On most Carrier condensers, the compressor and the outdoor fan motor run together whenever the system calls for cooling. If the compressor hums or runs but the fan blade sits still, the unit cannot reject heat and will quickly overheat.

Infinity and some Performance systems with communicating controls may log a related fault (such as a high-pressure or discharge-temperature trip) at the indoor board, because the fan failure causes those safeties to open. Basic Comfort-series units have no display and simply run hot until a safety shuts the compressor down.

**Bottom line:** turn the system off at the thermostat until the fan issue is fixed. Continuing to run risks a compressor failure that costs several times more than the fan repair.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — The single most common cause. The dual run capacitor provides the starting torque for the fan motor. When it weakens, the motor hums but won't start, or starts only if you nudge the blade.
2. **Seized or burned-out fan motor** — Worn bearings lock the shaft, or the windings fail. The motor may be silent, hum, or trip the breaker.
3. **Failed or pitted contactor** — The contactor sends power to both the compressor and fan. Burned contacts can partially close, powering the compressor but not the fan.
4. **Debris jamming the blade** — Leaves, sticks, or a bent blade physically block rotation.
5. **Loss of power to the outdoor unit** — Tripped breaker, blown fuse in the disconnect, or a failed 24V control signal.
6. **Wiring / relay fault on the control board** — Less common; more likely on Infinity communicating systems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. **Do not open the electrical panel on the condenser** — the capacitor holds a lethal charge.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery-powered model.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause the indoor coil to freeze and trip protections; swap it if dirty.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor furnace/air-handler breaker and the outdoor AC breaker. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Look at the pull-out disconnect box near the condenser to confirm it's fully seated.
- **Visible debris:** With the unit **off at the breaker**, look through the top grille for leaves or sticks blocking the fan. Do not reach in with power on.
- **Vents:** Make sure indoor supply and return vents aren't blocked.
- **Condensate:** A full condensate pan/float switch can shut the whole system down; check for standing water at the indoor unit.

If the fan still won't spin after these checks, shut the system off and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

1. **Cut and verify power**, then safely discharge the run capacitor before touching any terminals.
2. **Test the capacitor** with a meter — a 45/5 μF cap reading well below spec confirms a bad capacitor.
3. **Check the contactor** for pitted or welded contacts and confirm 24V is reaching the coil.
4. **Test the fan motor** — measure winding resistance, check for an open thermal overload, and spin the blade to feel for seized bearings.
5. **Verify control signal**, confirming the board or thermostat is actually calling for the fan.
6. **Check amp draw** on the motor once running to catch a failing motor before it dies.

Sanity check: if a tech condemns the motor without testing the capacitor first, ask why — the capacitor is cheaper and far more common.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan sits still | Failed run capacitor | Shut off at thermostat | Test & replace capacitor |
| Fan spins if nudged, then keeps running | Weak run capacitor | Shut off, don't run it | Replace capacitor |
| Fan silent, no hum, breaker OK | Seized/burned motor | Shut off system | Test & replace fan motor |
| Blade physically blocked | Debris or bent blade | Power off, clear visible debris | Straighten/replace blade |
| Nothing runs outdoors | Tripped breaker, blown fuse, contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, fuses, wiring |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Shorted motor or wiring | Stop resetting | Diagnose short, replace parts |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts plus labor:

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC):** $350 – $600
- **Condenser fan motor (ECM / communicating):** $450 – $650+
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $75 – $175 (often credited toward the repair)

DIY capacitor swaps are possible but involve a dangerous stored charge — most homeowners should not attempt this. If your unit is under Carrier's parts warranty, the motor part may be covered, but labor usually isn't.
