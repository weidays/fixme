## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a numeric fault code — it's a physical symptom on your Carrier condenser (the outdoor unit). The large fan on top of the unit is supposed to spin whenever the compressor runs, pulling air across the condenser coil to dump heat outside.

When that fan stops but the compressor still tries to run, refrigerant pressure and temperature climb fast. Modern Carrier units may trip a high-pressure switch or internal overload to protect the compressor, so you may notice the whole unit shutting down shortly after starting.

**Turn the system off at the thermostat now.** Running the compressor with a dead fan is one of the fastest ways to kill an expensive compressor.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed dual-run capacitor** — By far the most common cause. The capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque. When it weakens or fails, the fan hums but won't spin, or won't move at all.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor** — Worn bearings or a burned winding. The motor may be seized, noisy, or dead even with a good capacitor.
3. **Contactor not pulling in** — The contactor is the relay that sends power to the fan and compressor. Pitted, stuck, or unenergized contacts can leave the fan without power.
4. **Debris or ice jamming the blade** — Sticks, leaves, or a bent blade physically blocking rotation. Occasionally the blade is seized to a rusted shaft.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — The outdoor disconnect fuse or the circuit breaker may be tripped, cutting all power to the unit.
6. **High-pressure or overload lockout** — A safety switch has shut the unit down due to a prior overheat event or dirty coil.
7. **Control board or wiring fault** — Less common; loose low-voltage wiring or a failed board on Infinity/communicating systems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. **Do not open the electrical panel on the condenser or touch the capacitor — it stores a dangerous charge even with power off.**

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. On battery models, replace the batteries.
- **Air filter:** A clogged indoor filter can cause freeze-ups and pressure trips. Replace it if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor furnace/air-handler breaker and the outdoor AC breaker. If tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Make sure the pull-out disconnect box next to the condenser is fully seated (not pulled out).
- **Visible debris:** With the system OFF, look through the top grille for leaves, sticks, or ice on the blade. Clear only what you can reach from outside — never reach in while power is on.
- **Airflow:** Make sure the condenser has 2+ feet of clearance and the coil fins aren't packed with dirt or cottonwood fluff.

If the fan still won't run after these checks, shut the system off and call a technician. Repeatedly resetting a locked-out unit can damage the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Verify 240V line power at the disconnect and 24V control voltage to the contactor.
- **Discharge and test the capacitor** with a meter, comparing measured microfarads to the rating printed on it.
- Try to spin the fan blade by hand (power off) to check for a seized motor or bearing drag.
- Do the "start-with-a-stick" or capacitor-swap test: if the fan starts once nudged, it points to a weak capacitor.
- Check the contactor for pitted contacts and confirm it's pulling in.
- Measure motor winding resistance and amp draw against the nameplate.
- Inspect the high-pressure switch and system pressures if there's a lockout.

A quote that jumps straight to "new motor" or "new compressor" without a capacitor test deserves a second opinion — the capacitor is cheap and by far the most likely culprit.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Turn unit off | Test and replace capacitor |
| Fan dead, no hum, unit silent | Tripped breaker / blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Check disconnect, wiring, fuses |
| Fan won't turn by hand, noisy | Seized/worn fan motor | Turn unit off | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Compressor runs, fan doesn't | Bad capacitor or motor | Shut off to protect compressor | Test capacitor, then motor |
| Unit starts then shuts off fast | High-pressure/overload lockout | Clear coil debris | Diagnose pressure fault, clean coil |
| Blade physically stuck | Debris or bent blade | Clear visible debris (power off) | Straighten/replace blade |
| Buzzing at contactor, no fan | Stuck/failed contactor | Turn unit off | Replace contactor |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices, including parts and labor. Your Carrier model, tonnage, and local rates affect the final number.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180
- **Dual-run capacitor:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor:** $150 – $300
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350 – $650
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $300
- **High-pressure switch:** $200 – $400
- **Coil cleaning (if pressure-related):** $100 – $250
- **Control board (Infinity/communicating):** $400 – $900+

The good news: the most common cause — a failed capacitor — is also one of the cheapest fixes. Many "fan won't spin" calls are resolved for under $350.
