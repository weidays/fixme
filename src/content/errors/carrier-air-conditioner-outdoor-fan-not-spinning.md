## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a Carrier fault code — it's a physical symptom. On most Carrier air conditioners there's no diagnostic display for this at the condenser (the outdoor unit), so you're diagnosing by observation. The outdoor fan sits on top of the condenser unit and pulls air across the coil to dump the heat your system pulled out of the house.

When that fan stalls but the compressor still tries to run, refrigerant pressure and temperature climb fast. That's why this is rated **pro**: continuing to run the system risks a compressor failure, which is the single most expensive part in the unit.

On Carrier **Infinity variable-speed** systems, the control board *can* log related faults and may shut the outdoor unit down on its own. On standard **Comfort and Performance** single-stage units, there's usually no code — just a fan that won't turn.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — The most common cause by far. The dual run capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. A bulged, leaking, or weak capacitor leaves the fan humming or dead while the compressor may still run.
2. **Seized or burned-out fan motor** — Worn bearings or a shorted winding stop the motor. Often smells burnt or the motor is very hot.
3. **Bad contactor** — The contactor is the relay that sends power to the fan and compressor. Pitted or stuck contacts can interrupt power to the fan.
4. **Obstructed or damaged fan blade** — Debris, a bent blade, or ice buildup physically blocking rotation.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — Loss of high-voltage power to the whole condenser (compressor won't run either).
6. **Control board or ECM fault (Infinity/variable-speed only)** — On models with a variable-speed outdoor fan, a board or module failure can stop the fan.
7. **Loose or corroded wiring** — A broken connection at the motor, capacitor, or contactor.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

You can do these without opening the electrical panel of the condenser:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor furnace/air handler breaker and the outdoor AC/condenser breaker or disconnect. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Air filter:** A filthy filter can cause freeze-ups and pressure problems. Replace if dirty.
- **Visible debris:** Look down through the top fan grille for obvious leaves, sticks, or a bird's nest against the blade. Turn the system OFF at the thermostat first. Do not reach in while power is live.
- **Ice on the unit:** If the outdoor coil or lines are iced, turn the system off and set the fan to ON at the thermostat to help it thaw. See our Frozen Evaporator Coil guide.
- **Condensate:** Confirm the indoor drain isn't backed up and tripping a float switch, which can shut the system down.

If the fan still won't spin after these, shut the system off and call a technician. **Do not** keep resetting the breaker or open the condenser access panel — capacitors hold a dangerous charge even with power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will work through this in order — use it to sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm power** at the disconnect and breaker with a meter.
2. **Test the run capacitor** with a capacitance meter against its rated microfarads (µF). A capacitor reading well below spec gets replaced.
3. **Check the contactor** for pitted contacts and confirm it's pulling in and passing voltage to the fan.
4. **Test the fan motor** — check windings for shorts/opens, spin the blade by hand for bearing drag, and verify voltage reaches the motor.
5. **Inspect the blade and wiring** for damage, corrosion, or loose terminals.
6. **On Infinity/variable-speed units**, read any stored faults and test the outdoor fan module/board.

A capacitor test takes minutes. If a tech immediately quotes a full motor or "new unit" without testing the capacitor first, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin; spins if nudged | Failed run capacitor | None safe (capacitor holds charge) | Test and replace capacitor |
| No power at all to outdoor unit | Tripped breaker / blown fuse / contactor | Reset breaker once | Diagnose electrical fault, replace fuse/contactor |
| Fan silent, motor hot or burnt smell | Burned-out fan motor | Shut system off | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Blade blocked by debris or ice | Obstruction | Clear visible debris (power off), thaw ice | Inspect for damage, verify motor |
| Compressor runs, fan dead | Capacitor or motor | Shut off to protect compressor | Test capacitor and motor |
| Fan won't spin, no other issue on Infinity unit | Board / fan module fault | None | Read faults, test/replace module or board |
| Blade wobbles or is bent | Damaged fan blade | Shut system off | Replace blade, check motor bearings |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included. Actual pricing varies by region and access.

- **Run capacitor:** $150 – $300
- **Contactor:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC):** $300 – $650
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Wiring repair:** $100 – $250
- **Infinity/variable-speed fan module or control board:** $400 – $900+
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180 (often credited toward the repair)

A capacitor is the cheapest and most common fix. If your unit is 12+ years old and the fan motor or board has failed, ask your tech whether a repair or full replacement is the better value.
