## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a numeric Carrier fault code — it's a physical symptom you observe at the condenser unit outside. When your Carrier AC calls for cooling, the compressor and the outdoor (condenser) fan should both start. The fan pulls air across the outdoor coil to reject the heat your system pulled out of the house.

If the fan blade sits still while the unit hums, or the whole unit is silent, the outdoor fan circuit has a problem. This matters because the compressor may still be running with no airflow over the coil — a condition that spikes head pressure and can destroy the compressor within minutes. On Infinity systems with a communicating board, you may also see a related fault or a system fault light, but many Carrier condensers have no display at all outside, so the fan is your primary indicator.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run/start capacitor** — By far the most common cause. The dual-run capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. When it weakens, the motor hums but won't spin (or spins if nudged).
2. **Burned-out condenser fan motor** — Motors fail from age, heat, and bearing wear. A dead motor won't respond even with a good capacitor.
3. **Failed contactor** — The contactor is the relay that sends 240V to the compressor and fan. Pitted or stuck contacts can leave the fan unpowered.
4. **Debris jamming the blade** — Sticks, leaves, or a bent blade physically blocking rotation.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown high-voltage fuse/disconnect** — No power reaching the unit at all.
6. **Motor bearing seizure** — The motor is electrically fine but mechanically locked.
7. **Control board or relay fault** — On some Performance and Infinity units, a board relay controls the fan; a board fault is less common but possible.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps you should attempt yourself:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery-powered model.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause the system to short-cycle or freeze; swap it if it's dirty.
- **Indoor breaker AND outdoor disconnect breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Visible debris (power OFF):** Turn the unit off at the thermostat and the outdoor disconnect. Look through the top grille for obvious sticks or leaves. Do not stick fingers or tools deep inside the unit.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure indoor supply and return vents are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate:** Check that the drain pan isn't overflowing and any float switch hasn't tripped, which can shut the system down.

Do **not** open the electrical panel on the condenser, discharge or replace a capacitor, or push the fan blade with a tool while the unit has power. Capacitors hold a dangerous charge even when power is off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech will work through this sequence — knowing it helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm voltage at the disconnect** — verifies 240V is reaching the unit.
2. **Watch the start attempt** — do the compressor and fan try to start together?
3. **Test the capacitor** with a meter for microfarad (µF) value against the rating printed on it. A weak cap is the #1 finding.
4. **Check the contactor** for pitting, chatter, and correct 24V coil pull-in.
5. **Test the fan motor windings** for continuity, shorts, and open circuits, and spin the blade by hand (power off) to check for a seized bearing.
6. **Verify the board/relay output** on communicating (Infinity) or relay-driven systems.

A good tech won't just swap the fan motor without first ruling out the far cheaper capacitor.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit hums, fan still, spins if nudged | Failed capacitor | Shut off system, don't force blade | Test and replace dual-run capacitor |
| Fan dead, compressor runs | Bad capacitor or motor | Turn off at thermostat immediately | Test cap, then motor windings |
| Whole outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker/disconnect or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test power, contactor coil and contacts |
| Fan blade physically stuck | Debris or seized bearing | Power off, clear visible debris | Free/replace blade or motor |
| Loud grinding then stop | Failing motor bearing | Shut down to prevent damage | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Buzzing at contactor, intermittent fan | Pitted/stuck contactor | None | Replace contactor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $75–$175 (often credited toward the repair)
- **Dual-run capacitor:** $150–$300
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$650+ (higher for larger tonnage, ECM motors, or difficult access)
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150–$300
- **Control board (Infinity/communicating):** $400–$900

DIY part cost alone: a capacitor is ~$15–$40 and a universal fan motor ~$80–$200 — but the labor risk with 240V and stored charge is why this repair is rated pro-level.
