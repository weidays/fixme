## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a stored fault code on most Carrier air conditioners. The outdoor unit (the condenser) has a fan that pulls air across the coil to dump heat outside. When that fan stops turning while the system is calling for cooling, the unit cannot reject heat.

Depending on your model, you may or may not get a diagnostic light. Basic Carrier Comfort and Performance condensers have no display — the fan simply won't run. Infinity variable-speed systems can log control faults at the thermostat, but the failure modes for an ECM fan differ from the standard PSC motor described here.

Because running the compressor with a dead fan risks compressor damage, this problem is rated **pro**: do your safe checks, then shut the system down and call a technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor.** The single most common cause. The capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. When it weakens or fails, the fan hums but won't turn, or won't start at all.
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor.** Bearings wear out or the windings fail. The motor may be hot, silent, or tripping the breaker.
3. **Failed contactor.** The contactor is the relay that sends power to the outdoor unit. Pitted or stuck contacts can starve the fan (and compressor) of power.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse.** No power reaching the outdoor unit at all — fan and compressor both dead.
5. **Obstructed or jammed fan blade.** Debris, ice, or a bent blade can physically stop the fan.
6. **Control board or wiring fault.** Loose or corroded connections, or a bad board on units so equipped.
7. **Thermostat not actually calling for cooling.** A dead or misprogrammed thermostat means the outdoor unit never gets the signal to start.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all safe with the unit powered normally — do **not** open the outdoor electrical panel.

- **Thermostat:** Set it to COOL and lower the setpoint 3–5°F below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Confirm the display shows a cooling call.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can freeze the indoor coil and shut the system down. Replace it if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor furnace/air-handler breaker and the outdoor AC breaker in your main panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** There's a pull-out disconnect box on the wall near the condenser. Make sure it's fully seated.
- **Visible obstruction:** With the system OFF at the thermostat, look down through the fan grille for leaves, twigs, or debris on the blade. Clear anything obvious.
- **Ice on the unit:** If the coil or lines are iced up, turn the system OFF and let it thaw. See our Frozen Evaporator Coil guide.
- **Do not** open the electrical access panel, touch the capacitor, or repeatedly reset a breaker that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

1. Confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor unit when the thermostat calls (verifies the signal side).
2. Kill power at the disconnect and safely **discharge and test the run capacitor** with a meter — comparing measured microfarads to the rating printed on the cap.
3. Test the contactor for proper pull-in and check the contacts for pitting.
4. Test the fan motor windings for resistance and check for a ground fault or open winding.
5. Spin the blade by hand (power off) to check for a seized bearing.
6. Verify incoming voltage and inspect wiring for burnt or loose connections.

A good quote names the specific failed part and its measured value — not just "the fan's bad." If they replace a motor, ask that they replace the matched capacitor too.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but doesn't spin | Failed run capacitor | Turn unit off; don't force it | Test and replace capacitor |
| Fan starts after a gentle push, then runs | Weak capacitor | Turn off; note for tech | Replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit totally dead (no fan, no compressor) | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once; check disconnect | Test contactor and power supply |
| Fan silent, motor hot to touch | Burned-out motor | Turn off system | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Blade won't turn by hand (power off) | Seized bearings or jammed blade | Clear visible debris only | Replace motor or blade |
| Compressor runs, fan doesn't | Capacitor or motor | Turn off immediately | Diagnose fan circuit |
| Breaker trips every time | Shorted motor or wiring fault | Stop resetting; call pro | Locate and repair fault |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices including parts and labor. Your area and unit age will vary.

- **Run capacitor:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor:** $150 – $300
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC):** $300 – $650
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Control board (units so equipped):** $300 – $700
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $75 – $200 (often credited toward the repair)
- **Tripped breaker or thermostat setting:** $0 DIY

If your Carrier condenser is 12–15+ years old and needs a motor plus capacitor, get a quote on total system health before investing — repair costs can approach the value of an aging unit.
