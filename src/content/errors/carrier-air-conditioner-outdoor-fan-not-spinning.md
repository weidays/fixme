## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a numbered code. It means the condenser fan on top of your Carrier outdoor unit isn't turning while the system is calling for cooling. Without that fan pulling air across the condenser coil, the system can't reject heat. Cooling drops off fast, head pressure climbs, and the compressor will usually shut down on its internal overload.

On standard Carrier condensers, the fan motor is a PSC motor. It's powered through the contactor and started by the fan side of a dual run capacitor. On some Infinity and variable-speed models, the motor is an ECM type driven by the outdoor control board. Those units may show a fault on the Infinity user interface, so check it for codes.

If you have a Carrier heat pump rather than a cooling-only AC, see the separate heat pump page. Heat pumps add defrost-control behavior that can stop the fan on purpose.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor (fan side of the dual capacitor).** This is the most common cause on PSC-motor Carrier units. Typical signs are a hum, a fan that turns slowly, or a fan that only starts if nudged. Heat accelerates capacitor failure.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor.** The bearings seize, the windings open, or the motor's internal thermal overload trips repeatedly. Often the motor is hot to the touch, or the blade feels stiff.
3. **Contactor problem.** Pitted or stuck contacts, or a failed coil, can cut power to the fan. If the fan and compressor share the same pole, both may be dead.
4. **No power or no cooling call reaching the unit.** This can be a tripped breaker, a pulled or blown outdoor disconnect, a thermostat that isn't calling for cooling, or a low-voltage (24V) control issue. In this case the compressor is usually silent too.
5. **Wiring fault.** Burned, loose, or rodent-damaged wires at the motor, capacitor, or contactor.
6. **Control board or ECM drive fault (Infinity/variable-speed models only).** The board doesn't command the fan, or the ECM module fails. Check the Infinity control for active fault codes.
7. **Obstruction or blade damage.** Debris, sticks, or a bent blade jamming the fan. This is less common, and clearing it means reaching into the unit, which is technician work.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL, with the setpoint several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Air filter:** Replace a clogged filter. This won't fix a dead fan, but a dirty filter adds strain and can cause icing that confuses the diagnosis.
- **Breakers and disconnect:** Check the AC breaker in your main panel and reset it once if tripped. Make sure the outdoor disconnect is fully seated. If the breaker trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **One power reset:** Turn the system off at the thermostat, switch off the breaker for 5 minutes, then restore power and call for cooling. Do this only once.
- **Look, don't touch:** From outside, check whether the fan is still while the compressor hums. Note any burning smell or visible debris on the top grille. Don't poke anything through the grille.
- **Condensate:** Make sure the indoor condensate line and pan aren't overflowing. A tripped float switch can kill the cooling call to the outdoor unit.
- **Infinity owners:** Note any fault messages on the user interface for the technician.

If the fan still won't spin, shut cooling off at the thermostat to protect the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

1. **Confirm the call.** The tech verifies 24V at the contactor coil and line voltage at the disconnect.
2. **Check the contactor.** They look at whether it pulls in and measure voltage across the load side to the fan.
3. **Test the capacitor.** After discharging it, they measure microfarads on the FAN and HERM terminals and compare against the rating, typically within ±6%.
4. **Check the motor.** They measure winding resistance, check for shorts to ground, and spin the blade by hand with power off to feel for bearing drag.
5. **Check ECM models.** On ECM/Infinity models, they check the board's fan command, the communication signal, and the stored fault history.
6. **Inspect the rest.** They look over the wiring, the blade, and the condenser coil condition. After the repair, they confirm operating pressures and amp draw.

A legitimate quote should name the specific failed component and the test result that proved it.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan still or slow | Weak fan-side capacitor | Turn cooling off | Test and replace dual run capacitor |
| Fan starts only when pushed | Failing capacitor or motor bearings | Turn cooling off; don't push the blade | Capacitor test; replace motor if bearings are dragging |
| Fan and compressor both silent | No power, no cooling call, or contactor | Check thermostat, breaker, disconnect; one reset | Verify 24V and line voltage; replace contactor |
| Fan runs briefly then stops, motor hot | Motor thermal overload or failing motor | Turn cooling off | Amp-draw test; replace condenser fan motor |
| Burning smell from outdoor unit | Burned wiring or motor | Shut off breaker | Repair wiring; replace damaged components |
| Infinity UI shows outdoor fault | Board or ECM drive issue | Note the code; one reset | Board and ECM diagnosis and replacement |
| Breaker trips when AC starts | Shorted motor or wiring | Don't reset more than once | Insulation and short-to-ground testing |

## Repair costs

These are typical US installed prices, including the service call:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service call / diagnostic | $75–$200 |
| Dual run capacitor | $150–$400 |
| Contactor | $150–$450 |
| PSC condenser fan motor (with blade transfer) | $400–$900 |
| ECM fan motor (Infinity/variable-speed) | $700–$1,500+ |
| Outdoor control board | $500–$1,200+ |
| Wiring repair | $150–$400 |

Expect higher prices for evening or weekend calls, and in high-cost metro areas. If the compressor overheated repeatedly while the fan was dead, ask the tech to check compressor health before you approve a large repair on an older unit.
