## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a numeric fault code on most Carrier units — it's a physical symptom you can see and hear. The outdoor unit (the condenser) has a large fan on top that blows heat out of the refrigerant. When it stops spinning while the compressor is running, heat has nowhere to go.

On **Carrier Infinity systems**, the control board may log a related fault and display it on the wall control, since these units monitor the outdoor fan more closely. On **Comfort and Performance single-stage units**, there's usually no code at all — you just notice the fan sitting still while the unit hums or the house isn't cooling.

**This is time-sensitive.** A compressor running without condenser airflow builds dangerous head pressure and heat. Turn the system off at the thermostat until it's diagnosed.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor.** The single most common cause. The capacitor gives the fan motor the torque it needs to start. When it weakens or fails, the motor hums but won't spin (or spins if nudged). Often a dual capacitor also serves the compressor, so a bad one can affect both.
2. **Bad fan motor.** Bearings seize, windings burn out, or the motor overheats and fails thermally. A motor that's hot, smells burnt, or won't turn even with a good capacitor is likely done.
3. **Contactor not pulling in.** The contactor is the relay that sends power to the fan and compressor. Pitted, stuck, or failed contacts cut power to the fan.
4. **Debris jamming the blade.** Leaves, twigs, or ice can physically block the fan. Sometimes a stick or grass has fallen through the top grille.
5. **Tripped or failed high-pressure switch (or thermal overload).** If pressure or temperature climbs too high, safeties can cut power to protect the system.
6. **Wiring or low-voltage control fault.** Corroded connections, chewed wires, or a bad thermostat signal on Infinity systems.
7. **Control board failure (Infinity/variable-speed units).** Less common, but board-driven fan control can fail on higher-end units.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the system off first.** At the thermostat, switch cooling to OFF so the compressor isn't running without airflow.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to COOL and below room temperature. For battery-powered stats, replace the batteries.
- **Check the breakers.** Look for a tripped AC breaker in your main panel and the outdoor disconnect near the condenser. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** A clogged filter contributes to freeze-ups and pressure problems that can shut things down.
- **Look for visible debris.** With the system OFF and power off at the disconnect, look through the top grille for leaves or sticks jamming the blade. Do not reach in — just look.
- **Check for ice.** If the outdoor coil or lines are iced over, shut the system off and let it thaw. See our Frozen Evaporator Coil guide.

Do **not** open the electrical panel on the condenser, touch the capacitor, or push the fan blade to "help" it run. A humming motor drawing power is a diagnostic clue for the technician, not a DIY fix.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheapest to most expensive cause:

1. **Visual and mechanical check** — spins the blade by hand (power off) to feel for seized bearings; inspects for debris and burnt smells.
2. **Capacitor test** — safely discharges and measures the capacitor with a meter against its rated microfarads (µF). Out-of-spec readings mean replacement.
3. **Contactor check** — verifies 24V at the coil and inspects contacts for pitting; checks that line voltage passes through when energized.
4. **Motor test** — checks windings for continuity and shorts, confirms the motor gets voltage but still won't run (pointing to the motor vs. the capacitor).
5. **Pressure and safety check** — reads system pressures and verifies high-pressure switch and overloads.
6. **Board/control diagnostics** — on Infinity units, reads stored faults and confirms the board is commanding the fan.

Ask the tech to show you the capacitor reading or the failed component. A quote that jumps straight to a motor or compressor without testing the capacitor first deserves a second look.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin; spins if nudged | Failed run capacitor | Shut off system; don't push the blade | Discharge & replace capacitor to spec |
| Fan dead, motor hot or smells burnt | Failed fan motor | Turn off, call pro | Test windings, replace motor |
| Nothing energizes outside; compressor also silent | Contactor not pulling in | Check breaker/disconnect | Test/replace contactor |
| Blade physically blocked | Debris or ice | Power off, remove visible debris; thaw ice | Clear jam, inspect blade & motor |
| Unit shuts down shortly after starting | High-pressure/overload trip | Replace filter, clear coil | Diagnose pressure cause, check safeties |
| Infinity control shows fault, fan won't run | Board or wiring fault | Note the code shown | Read faults, test wiring/board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor unless noted:

- **Capacitor replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor (standard PSC):** $350 – $650
- **Infinity/variable-speed fan motor:** $500 – $900+
- **Debris removal / cleaning:** $75 – $200 (often part of a service call)
- **High-pressure switch:** $150 – $350
- **Control board (Infinity units):** $400 – $800+
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180 (often applied to the repair)

Costs vary by region, model, and whether a specialty Infinity part is needed. Get an itemized quote before authorizing work.
