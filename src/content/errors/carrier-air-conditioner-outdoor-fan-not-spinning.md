## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a numeric fault code on Carrier equipment — it's a symptom you observe at the condenser (the outdoor unit). The compressor may be running, humming, or silent, but the fan blade on top of the unit stays still.

The outdoor fan's job is to pull air across the condenser coil so the system can dump the heat it collected from your house. When that fan stops, the unit loses its ability to reject heat almost immediately. High-pressure and high-temperature conditions build fast, and the compressor is at serious risk.

**Because a non-spinning fan can destroy a compressor in minutes, treat this as a pro-level problem.** Shut the system off and leave it off until the fan is fixed. Variable-speed Infinity and Greenspeed condensers use an ECM fan motor with board-level control and may log a system fault at the indoor control instead — those behave differently from the single-speed PSC motors on most Comfort and Performance units.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor.** The most common cause by far. The dual run capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start spinning. When it weakens, the fan hums but won't turn — or spins if you nudge it, which is a classic sign.
2. **Seized or burned-out fan motor.** Bearings wear out and lock up, or the windings burn open. A motor that's hot, smells scorched, or won't turn by hand is likely done.
3. **Failed contactor.** The contactor is the relay that sends 240V to the fan and compressor. Pitted or stuck contacts can prevent power from reaching the fan.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse.** No power means no fan. Sometimes only the outdoor circuit is affected.
5. **Obstructed or damaged fan blade.** Debris, ice, a bent blade, or a stick jamming the blade can physically stop rotation.
6. **Loose or corroded wiring.** A disconnected fan motor lead or corroded terminal breaks the circuit.
7. **Board or control failure (variable-speed units).** On Infinity/Greenspeed ECM systems, a bad control board or motor module can stop the fan.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. **Do not open the electrical panel on the condenser** — it holds live 240V and a charged capacitor that can shock you even with the power off.

- **Turn the system off at the thermostat.** Do this first to stop the compressor from running without the fan.
- **Check your breakers.** Look at the main panel for a tripped AC breaker (double-width, 240V). Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Check the outdoor disconnect.** Many Carrier units have a pull-out disconnect box on the wall near the condenser. Make sure it's fully seated.
- **Look for visible obstructions.** With the power off, look through the top grille for leaves, sticks, or ice against the fan blade. Do not reach inside if anything looks damaged.
- **Check your air filter and indoor vents.** A badly clogged filter or closed vents can cause freeze-ups that mimic outdoor problems — worth ruling out.
- **Check the condensate drain.** A full drain pan can trigger a safety float switch that shuts the system down; clear standing water if you can see it.

If the breaker holds and nothing is obstructing the blade but the fan still won't spin, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the steps helps you sanity-check the quote:

1. **Confirm the symptom safely** by killing power at the disconnect before opening the electrical compartment.
2. **Discharge and test the capacitor** with a meter. A capacitor reading well below its rated microfarads is the verdict — a cheap, fast fix.
3. **Test the contactor** for voltage in and out, and inspect the contacts for pitting or welding.
4. **Test the fan motor** for continuity, proper resistance, and whether the shaft turns freely by hand.
5. **Check line and low-voltage power** — the disconnect, breaker, and 24V control fuse on the board.
6. **On variable-speed units,** they'll pull fault history from the Infinity control and test the ECM motor module.

A good tech won't just swap the whole motor if a $30 capacitor is the real problem. If they quote a full motor without testing the capacitor first, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Shut off system; don't nudge the blade | Test and replace capacitor |
| Fan spins if nudged, then dies | Weak capacitor | Shut off system | Replace capacitor |
| Compressor runs, fan dead, no hum | Failed motor or open wiring | Shut off system | Test motor, replace if seized/burned |
| Nothing runs outside | Tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, control fuse, power |
| Blade physically stuck | Debris, ice, bent blade | Clear visible debris (power off) | Straighten/replace blade, check motor |
| Loud grinding then stops | Bad motor bearings | Shut off system | Replace fan motor |
| Fan cycles oddly (variable-speed) | ECM/board fault | Note any Infinity fault code | Test ECM module, board diagnostics |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$180
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC):** $350–$650+
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150–$400
- **ECM fan motor / module (Infinity/Greenspeed):** $600–$1,200+ due to specialty parts
- **Low-voltage fuse or wiring repair:** $100–$250

Prices vary by region, unit access, and whether your unit is still under Carrier's parts warranty (labor is usually not covered). Get a written diagnosis before approving a full motor swap — a capacitor is often the actual fix.
