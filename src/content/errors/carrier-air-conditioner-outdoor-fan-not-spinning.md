## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a stored fault code on most Carrier air conditioners. The outdoor unit (the condenser) has a fan that pulls air across the coil to reject heat. When that fan stops, the system can't shed heat, pressures climb, and the compressor is at risk.

You may notice the condenser is running (humming or buzzing) but the top-mounted fan blade sits still, or the whole unit is silent. On Infinity and some two-stage systems, a related fault may appear on the wall control or the outdoor board's LED — but the plain single-stage units give you no code, just a fan that won't move.

Because this condition can quickly damage the compressor, treat it as **pro-level**. Turn the system off and diagnose before running it again.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — By far the most common cause. The dual (or fan) capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque. When it weakens or fails, the fan won't start, hums, or spins slowly. The compressor often still runs.
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor** — Bearings wear out or windings fail. The motor won't turn even with a good capacitor, and may smell hot.
3. **Debris jamming the fan blade** — Sticks, leaves, ice, or a warped blade physically block rotation.
4. **Failed contactor** — The contactor is the relay that sends line voltage to the fan and compressor. Pitted or stuck contacts can leave the fan unpowered.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuse** — No power reaching the outdoor unit at all.
6. **Control board / relay failure** — More common on two-stage and Infinity units where the board controls the fan directly. Varies significantly by model.
7. **Loose or corroded wiring** — Vibration and weather can loosen fan motor leads over years.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for a homeowner. Stop here if the fix isn't obvious.

- **Thermostat:** Set to COOL and a temperature several degrees below room temp. Confirm the system is actually calling for cooling.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause icing and pressure issues that lead to shutdowns. Replace if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check the AC breaker in your main panel and reset it once if tripped. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated trips signal an electrical fault.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Confirm the pull-out disconnect box near the condenser is fully seated (some get bumped or pulled during yard work).
- **Visible debris:** With the system **off at the thermostat and breaker**, look through the top grille for leaves, sticks, or ice on the blade. Clear only what you can reach without removing panels.
- **Give it a visual, not a spin:** Do not stick a screwdriver or your hand in to start the blade with power on. If clearing debris doesn't help, call a pro.

Do **not** open the electrical panel of the condenser. The capacitor can hold a dangerous charge even with power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will work through this order — use it to sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm power** at the disconnect and contactor with a meter.
2. **Test the run capacitor** with a capacitance meter against its rated microfarads (µF). A reading well below spec confirms a bad cap.
3. **Check the contactor** for pitted contacts and proper 24V coil pull-in.
4. **Test the fan motor** — resistance across windings, checks for an open or shorted winding, and whether it spins freely by hand once discharged.
5. **Inspect wiring** for loose or heat-damaged connections at the motor and board.
6. **On Infinity/two-stage units,** read fault data from the wall control or outdoor board LED to isolate a board-driven fan control problem.

A capacitor test takes minutes; if the tech replaces a motor or board without testing the cheap capacitor first, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit hums, fan still | Failed run capacitor | None (line voltage) | Test & replace capacitor |
| Fan won't turn, no hum | Seized fan motor | None | Test windings, replace motor |
| Blade physically blocked | Debris/ice jam | Clear debris with power off | Inspect blade & bearings |
| Whole unit dead | Breaker/disconnect/contactor | Reset breaker once, seat disconnect | Test contactor & power supply |
| Fan runs intermittently | Weak capacitor or contactor | None | Replace failing component |
| Fan dead on 2-stage/Infinity unit | Control board relay | Note any wall-control fault | Read board codes, replace board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts + labor:

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $350. The most common and cheapest true fix.
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350.
- **Condenser fan motor replacement:** $400 – $650+, higher for ECM/variable-speed motors on Infinity units.
- **Control board (two-stage/Infinity):** $500 – $900+, varies widely by model and parts availability.
- **Wiring repair:** $100 – $300.
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180, often credited toward the repair.

If a compressor was run without the fan for a while, get a compressor health check — that's a far more expensive problem.
