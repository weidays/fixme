## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a lettered Carrier fault code. On most single-stage and two-stage Carrier condensers there is no diagnostic display — the outdoor unit either runs or it doesn't. On **Infinity and Greenspeed variable-speed systems**, the control board and Infinity thermostat *can* log related fault codes, so check the thermostat's service/fault menu if you have one.

The outdoor (condenser) fan pulls air across the outdoor coil to reject the heat your system pumps out of the house. When it stops while the compressor runs, pressure and temperature climb fast. This is why the severity is **pro** — continuing to run the unit risks a compressor failure that can cost thousands.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — the single most common cause. The dual run capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. When it weakens, the fan hums but won't spin, or only spins after a manual push.
2. **Burned-out condenser fan motor** — bearings seize or windings fail. Often accompanied by a burnt smell or a motor that's hot to the touch.
3. **Failed contactor** — the relay that sends power to the outdoor unit. Pitted or stuck contacts can leave the fan without power.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor disconnect or main panel breaker has tripped, killing power to the whole condenser.
5. **Obstruction or debris** — sticks, ice, or a bent blade physically jamming the fan.
6. **Control board / low-voltage fault** — a bad 24V signal, failed board, or wiring issue (more common on Infinity/variable-speed boards).
7. **Overloaded/overheated motor** — some motors have a thermal cutout that shuts the fan off until it cools, then it restarts on its own.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do these with the system **off at the thermostat** first.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is well below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause the indoor coil to freeze and the whole system to short-cycle. Replace it if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check both the main panel breaker for the AC and the outdoor disconnect box near the condenser. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Indoor vents:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate:** Check for a full condensate pan or a tripped float switch, which can shut the system down.
- **Visible obstruction:** With power OFF at the disconnect, look through the top grille for leaves, sticks, or ice on the fan blade. Clear only what you can reach without removing panels.

Do **not** remove the electrical access panel, touch the capacitor, or push the fan blade to start it while power is on. Capacitors store a dangerous charge even with power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify power** at the disconnect and confirm 24V is reaching the contactor coil.
2. **Test the contactor** — check that it's pulling in and that voltage passes through the contacts.
3. **Discharge and test the run capacitor** with a meter, comparing the reading to its rated microfarads (µF). A capacitor reading well below spec is the culprit.
4. **Test the fan motor** — check windings for continuity/resistance, verify it's not seized, and confirm it draws proper amperage.
5. **Inspect wiring and the board** for burnt terminals, loose connections, or logged faults (on Infinity systems).
6. **Confirm the fix** by measuring refrigerant pressures and temperatures after repair to ensure the compressor wasn't damaged.

If a tech quotes a compressor replacement, ask them to confirm the fan/capacitor were ruled out first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Shut off system; don't run it | Discharge and replace capacitor |
| Fan spins only after a push | Weak capacitor | Shut off system | Test µF, replace capacitor |
| No power to whole outdoor unit | Tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor, find trip cause |
| Fan dead, burnt smell, hot motor | Failed fan motor | Shut off system | Test windings, replace motor |
| Fan blade physically stuck | Debris or ice | Kill power, clear visible debris | Inspect blade, bearings, coil |
| Fan quits then restarts on its own | Motor thermal overload | Note the pattern for the tech | Test motor amperage, replace if failing |
| Infinity thermostat shows a fault | Board / low-voltage issue | Record the fault code | Diagnose board and wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Breaker reset / debris removal:** $0 DIY, or a $75–$150 service call if you have a pro out.
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$300 installed. The part itself is cheap; you're paying for safe diagnosis and handling.
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650 installed, depending on horsepower and whether a matching blade is needed.
- **Control board (Infinity/variable-speed):** $400–$900+ — these boards are expensive and model-specific.
- **Compressor damage from running without the fan:** $1,500–$3,000+, or often a full condenser replacement. This is exactly what you avoid by shutting the system off promptly.
