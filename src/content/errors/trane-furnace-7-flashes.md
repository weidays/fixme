## What this code means

Seven flashes on your Trane furnace's diagnostic LED indicates a **gas valve circuit error** — the control board has determined that the gas valve circuit is not energized correctly. That covers more than one situation: the board may command the valve and not see the circuit energize as expected, or it may see the valve circuit energized when it should not be, or be unable to confirm it can de-energize its own valve driver.

The fault lives in the **valve control circuit** — the wiring between the board and the valve, the valve's electrical coil, or the board's valve-driver output. In practice the furnace will normally not run while this code is active, but because the exact response varies from control to control, the right move is to leave the unit switched off and have a licensed technician evaluate it rather than attempting to run or bypass anything.

On many Trane and American Standard integrated furnace controls, this fault **latches until power is cycled**; some controls retry automatically — check the diagnostic label inside the blower door for your specific board. Cycling power once is the only homeowner-permitted reset. If the code returns, the fault is still present and requires a technician.

> **Note:** Flash-count legends can differ between board revisions. Confirm the meaning of 7 flashes against the diagnostic label printed inside your furnace's blower compartment door.

> **If you smell gas at any time:** leave the house, don't operate switches, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Loose, corroded, or damaged valve wiring, connectors, or harness** — backed-out terminals, chafed conductors, a damaged plug, a pinched harness, or moisture intrusion at the valve connector. Wiring and connection problems are the most common trigger for this code.
2. **Failed gas valve coil/solenoid** — the electrical coil inside the valve has opened or shorted and no longer draws current correctly.
3. **Control board valve-driver failure** — the output circuit on the board that energizes the valve has failed.
4. **Incorrect valve after a prior repair** — a wrong-voltage or mismatched replacement gas valve installed during earlier service.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should attempt. Everything else on this code lives inside the cabinet and is technician work.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to *Heat* and the setpoint is above room temperature. Replace the thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Check and replace a heavily clogged filter — while unrelated to the valve circuit directly, ruling it out helps a clean diagnosis.
- **Breaker / disconnect:** Locate the furnace breaker or the switch on the unit and cycle it **once** to attempt a single reset. Wait a few minutes and watch whether 7 flashes returns.
- **Blower door:** Look at the blower access panel from the outside and confirm it is sitting flush; you can push it gently to seat it. **Do not remove the panel or open the cabinet.** A loose panel can hold the door safety switch open and mask the real issue.
- **Vents and registers:** Confirm supply and return registers are open and unobstructed.
- **Condensate line (high-efficiency models):** Check that the drain isn't backed up or overflowing.

If 7 flashes returns after one reset, stop, leave the furnace off, and call a licensed HVAC technician. Do not repeatedly reset the unit.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will work the gas valve circuit methodically:

1. **Confirm the flash code** against the board legend and check for any secondary faults.
2. **Inspect wiring and connectors** from the board to the gas valve for corrosion, loose terminals, chafing, or moisture.
3. **Measure valve command voltage** at the valve terminals with a multimeter when the board calls for heat — verifying the board is actually sending the signal.
4. **Test the gas valve coil resistance** to determine whether the solenoid is open, shorted, or within spec.
5. **Check the board's valve-driver output** to isolate whether the fault is in the valve or the board.
6. **Verify the correct valve** is installed (right voltage and part number) if the unit was previously serviced.

This sequence lets the tech isolate wiring vs. valve vs. board — you should see those steps reflected in an honest quote.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 flashes, no ignition attempt or valve stays closed | Loose/corroded valve wiring | Cycle power once; confirm panel is seated (do not remove it) | Inspect and repair wiring and connectors |
| 7 flashes returns immediately after reset | Failed gas valve coil | None — leave the unit off and call a pro | Test coil resistance; replace gas valve |
| No response to valve command; board signal absent | Control board driver failure | None — leave the unit off and call a pro | Test driver output; replace control board |
| Fault appeared after a recent service call | Wrong/mismatched valve installed | None — leave the unit off and call a pro | Verify part number/voltage; correct valve |
| Intermittent 7 flashes in damp conditions | Moisture at connector/harness | Check for obvious condensate backup | Dry, repair, or replace affected harness |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges for this code, parts and labor combined:

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80–$180
- **Wiring or connector repair:** $150–$350 total
- **Gas valve replacement:** $400–$900+ depending on model and valve type — two-stage and modulating valves sit at the top of that range or above
- **Integrated control board replacement:** roughly $400–$1,200 installed, depending on board and market
- **Homeowner DIY reset:** $0 (single power cycle only)

Because every documented cause of this code is inside the cabinet, expect a paid service call. The good news is that a simple loose-connection repair sits at the low end. Get an itemized quote that names whether the wiring, valve, or board is being replaced, and confirm whether the price includes the part, labor, and the diagnostic fee.
