## What this code means

On most Bryant gas furnaces, **Code 14 indicates an ignition lockout** — the control board tried to light the burners a set number of times (typically three or four), failed to prove a stable flame each time, and shut the system down for safety.

Because the exact flash-code legend **varies by board revision**, always confirm Code 14 against the fault-code label printed inside your furnace's blower door. Some older or OEM-variant boards use a different numbering scheme, so the door label is the authority for your specific unit.

A lockout is the furnace doing its job: rather than keep pumping gas into a chamber that won't light, it stops. The heat stays off until the fault is cleared and the underlying cause is fixed. This is why we rate it **pro-level** — the diagnosis often involves the igniter, flame-sensing circuit, or gas train, and repeated reset attempts can be unsafe.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or failing flame sensor** — the most common cause. If the sensor can't detect the flame, the board assumes ignition failed and eventually locks out.
2. **Weak or cracked hot surface igniter** — the igniter no longer gets hot enough to light gas reliably, so the burner misses on some or all attempts.
3. **Insufficient or interrupted gas supply** — a partially closed manual gas shutoff, a tripped gas meter, or low pressure starves the burners.
4. **Dirty burners or clogged burner ports** — carbon or debris disrupts the flame so it can't be sensed consistently.
5. **Weak or failing gas valve** — the valve doesn't open fully or reliably, producing a marginal flame.
6. **Poor flame-sensor wiring or grounding** — a loose connection or bad chassis ground causes intermittent flame-detection failures.
7. **Control board fault** — least common; the board mis-reads flame current or mis-sequences ignition.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should perform. **Do not** open the gas valve manifold, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out.

- **Air filter:** A clogged filter chokes airflow and can contribute to nuisance faults. Replace it if it's dirty.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to Heat and the setpoint is above room temperature. Replace low batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check that the furnace breaker and the service switch (the light-switch-looking toggle near the furnace) are on.
- **Gas supply — visual only:** Verify other gas appliances (stove, water heater) are working. If nothing gas-powered runs, the issue is your gas supply, not the furnace. Do not adjust the furnace gas valve yourself.
- **Condensate:** On high-efficiency (condensing) models, a full or clogged condensate drain/trap can trip a pressure safety. Look for standing water or a blocked drain line.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **One reset:** You may cycle power at the breaker **once**. If Code 14 returns, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. **Read stored fault history** on the board to confirm Code 14 and see any companion codes.
2. **Watch a full ignition sequence** — inducer start, igniter glow, gas valve opening, and whether a flame is established.
3. **Measure flame-sensor microamp signal** with a meter; a reading well below spec (often under ~1–2 µA when several are expected) points to a dirty sensor or grounding problem.
4. **Test the hot surface igniter** for resistance and visually check for cracks or hot spots.
5. **Check incoming and manifold gas pressure** with a manometer against the rating-plate spec.
6. **Inspect burners** for debris, rust, or misaligned flame.
7. **Verify grounding and wiring** at the sensor, valve, and board.

If a tech quotes a board or gas valve replacement without first checking the flame sensor and igniter, ask them to justify it — those are the cheapest and most common fixes.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Igniter glows, flame lights briefly then drops | Dirty flame sensor | Replace filter; one power reset | Clean/replace sensor, measure microamps |
| Igniter glows but no flame ever appears | Gas supply or gas valve issue | Confirm other gas appliances work | Check gas pressure, test/replace valve |
| Igniter doesn't glow at all | Failed hot surface igniter | Visual check only | Test and replace igniter |
| Locks out only on cold mornings | Marginal igniter or borderline flame signal | Replace filter | Replace igniter, verify flame current |
| Weak, yellow, or uneven flame | Dirty burners | None (safety) | Clean burners and ports |
| Random intermittent lockouts | Loose wiring or bad ground | Check breaker/thermostat | Repair wiring, verify chassis ground |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined where applicable:

- **Flame sensor cleaning:** $0 DIY, or ~$80–$180 as a service call.
- **Flame sensor replacement:** ~$100–$250 installed.
- **Hot surface igniter replacement:** ~$150–$350 installed.
- **Burner cleaning/service:** ~$150–$400.
- **Gas valve replacement:** ~$300–$600 installed.
- **Control board replacement:** ~$400–$700 installed.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** ~$80–$180, often credited toward the repair.

Costs vary by region, furnace age, and whether OEM parts are required. A cleaned flame sensor is often the cheapest resolution, which is why many techs check it first.
