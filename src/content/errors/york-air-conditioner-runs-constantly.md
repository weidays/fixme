## What this code means

"Runs constantly" is a symptom, not a stored York fault code. It means the AC never reaches the thermostat setpoint and shuts off, or it keeps running when it should stop. Most standard York condensers have no diagnostic display. Some communicating and variable-speed models, such as the Affinity series paired with a compatible thermostat, may log related faults. This varies by model, so check your thermostat and outdoor control board if your system has one.

The problem falls into two groups, and they need different responses:

- **The AC is running but can't keep up.** Air comes out cool, but the house never gets down to the setpoint. Causes include a capacity loss, an airflow restriction, or a heat load bigger than the system can handle.
- **The AC won't shut off at all.** The outdoor unit runs even after the setpoint is reached, or even with the thermostat turned off. This points to a control fault and needs prompt attention to avoid compressor damage.

On two-stage and variable-speed York units, long low-speed run cycles are normal by design. Constant runtime on those units is only a concern if the house is not reaching the setpoint.

This is not a lockout condition, so there is nothing to reset. If the root cause is a frozen coil, a warm-air problem, or no cooling at all, see the related pages listed at the bottom.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Extreme outdoor heat or a heat load beyond design.** On very hot days, a properly sized system may run continuously. This is normal if the house is still staying within a few degrees of the setpoint.
2. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow.** A clogged filter reduces cooling capacity, which lengthens run times.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil.** Dirt, cottonwood fluff, and grass clippings stop the coil from releasing heat, so capacity drops.
4. **Thermostat issues.** Possible problems include:
   - Wrong settings or a setpoint that is too low
   - Fan set to ON instead of AUTO (the indoor blower runs all the time, but the compressor does not)
   - Poor placement, such as in sunlight or near a heat source
   - Low batteries
   - Inaccurate temperature sensing
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Capacity drops, and the system runs long with weak cooling.
6. **Stuck or welded contactor in the outdoor unit.** The compressor and fan keep running regardless of what the thermostat calls for.
7. **Leaky or poorly insulated ductwork.** This is especially common with ducts in a hot attic, where cooled air is lost before it reaches the rooms.
8. **Dirty evaporator coil or a weak indoor blower.** Both reduce airflow across the coil and limit heat transfer.
9. **Undersized system or a failing compressor.** The unit runs at reduced capacity. A technician confirms this with pressure readings and amp draw.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat.** Confirm it is set to COOL, the fan is on AUTO, and the setpoint is reasonable (75–78°F). Replace the batteries if it has them. Check that it is not sitting in direct sun or next to a lamp, oven, or TV.
- **Test the shutoff.** Set the thermostat to OFF and wait 5 minutes. If the outdoor unit is still running, turn off the outdoor disconnect or the AC breaker and call a technician. Don't leave the unit running uncontrolled.
- **Air filter.** Replace it if it is gray or clogged. Check it monthly in summer.
- **Vents and registers.** Open all supply registers. Clear furniture and rugs away from supply and return grilles.
- **Outdoor unit, exterior only.**
  - Clear leaves, debris, and plants so there are at least 2 feet of space around the unit.
  - You may gently rinse the outside of the fins with a garden hose at low pressure, with power off at the disconnect. Do not open the cabinet.
- **Condensate line.** Make sure the drain line is flowing and the indoor pan is not overflowing.
- **Breaker.** If you shut the unit down, restore the breaker once. If the problem returns, leave it off and call a technician.
- **Look for ice.** Check for frost on the refrigerant line at the outdoor unit. If you see ice, turn the system off and run the fan only so the coil thaws. Then call for service.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will typically:

1. **Measure the temperature split.** A healthy system usually shows about 15–22°F between return air and supply air. A low split points to low charge, a dirty coil, or compressor trouble.
2. **Check airflow.** This includes the filter, blower speed, static pressure, and the condition of the evaporator coil.
3. **Measure refrigerant performance.** The technician reads pressures, superheat, and subcooling and compares them with York's charging chart for your model. A low charge calls for a leak search.
4. **Inspect the contactor.** The technician checks whether it opens when the 24V call ends and looks for pitted or welded contacts.
5. **Check the thermostat wiring.** A short on the Y wire can keep the system called to run.
6. **Measure compressor amp draw** and compare it with the nameplate rating.
7. **Inspect the condenser coil and ductwork.** If the equipment checks out, they may suggest a load calculation (Manual J).

Be skeptical of a quote that adds refrigerant without a leak search, or that recommends full system replacement without pressure readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs all afternoon on very hot days, but the house stays close to the setpoint | Normal operation under high load | Close blinds and keep a reasonable setpoint | None needed unless performance worsens |
| Weak airflow, and the filter is dirty | Clogged filter | Replace the filter | Check the blower and static pressure if the problem persists |
| Outdoor unit runs with the thermostat OFF | Welded contactor or shorted Y wire | Shut off power at the disconnect or breaker | Replace the contactor or repair the wiring |
| Air is cool but not cold, and runtime is long | Low refrigerant charge or a leak | Turn the system off if the lines are icing | Leak search, repair, and recharge |
| Outdoor fins are matted with debris | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris and rinse the fins from outside | Chemical coil cleaning |
| Indoor blower never stops | Fan set to ON | Set the fan to AUTO | Check the fan relay or board if AUTO doesn't fix it |
| Some rooms are hot while the system runs nonstop | Duct leaks or a lack of insulation | Open the registers in those rooms | Seal and insulate the ducts |
| Runs constantly, and the temperature split is good but the house stays warm | Undersized system | None | Load calculation and sizing review |
