## What this code means

Lennox ductless mini-splits run a self-diagnostic routine on both the indoor and outdoor boards. When the routine finds a fault, the indoor unit signals it in one of three ways:

- It flashes an LED (often the operation/run or timer light) a set number of times, then pauses and repeats.
- It shows an alphanumeric code on the indoor display.
- On many outdoor units, it flashes an LED on the outdoor control board that only a technician can see with the cover off.

**A blinking light is not one fault. It is a signal that a fault code is stored.** Lennox's ductless lines may be built on different OEM platforms depending on the series and production year, so the same flash count can mean different things on different units. Don't rely on a flash-code chart written for another model. Use the manual for your exact model number.

Not every blink is a fault. On many models the operation LED blinks during heat start-up or a defrost cycle, and that is normal. If the unit resumes heating on its own, nothing is wrong.

If the unit has actually stopped working, count the flashes and write down any displayed code before you do anything else. That information, plus your model number from the indoor unit's side label, is what a technician needs.

Typical behavior:

- The unit stops cooling or heating, or runs in a limited mode.
- Many protective faults retry automatically after a delay.
- Sensor, communication and board faults generally hold until power is cycled.
- A fault that keeps coming back after a reset points to a real hardware or refrigerant problem, not a glitch.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

This ranking reflects general experience across ductless systems, not a Lennox-published ranking. Communication, sensor and power faults are typically the most frequent causes, and inverter or compressor faults are less common:

1. **Indoor-to-outdoor communication error.** This is typically the most common blinking fault on ductless systems. Causes include loose, miswired or damaged interconnect wiring between the units, a power interruption, or a failing indoor or outdoor board. It often shows up right after installation, a storm, or wiring work.
2. **Temperature sensor (thermistor) fault.** An indoor room sensor, indoor coil sensor, or outdoor coil, ambient or discharge sensor has gone open or shorted, or its connector has come loose.
3. **Power supply or voltage problem.** Low or unstable supply voltage, a brownout, or a tripped or partially failed breaker can trigger protective shutdown and a blinking code.
4. **Protective shutdown from airflow or pressure conditions.** Examples include coil freeze protection, high-temperature or high-pressure protection in heat mode, or overload from a badly clogged filter or blocked outdoor coil. Some of these clear automatically when conditions return to normal.
5. **Indoor fan motor fault.** The control board detects that the blower motor is not reaching the commanded speed.
6. **Inverter, IPM or compressor drive fault.** This covers an overcurrent or IPM protection trip, a compressor that will not start, or a failed outdoor inverter board. It is less common but the most expensive cause.
7. **Refrigerant-related protection.** Low charge from a leak can trigger high discharge temperature or low-pressure protection on models that monitor it.
8. **Indoor float switch / condensate pump fault.** This applies only to models or installs that use a condensate pump or float switch, such as some ceiling cassettes and units with add-on pumps. A full pan or drain backup stops operation.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Rule out normal operation.** If the light is blinking during heat start-up or defrost, wait a few minutes. On many Lennox units this is normal and the unit will resume heating on its own.
- **Record the pattern.** Count the flashes between pauses, note which LED and what color, and write down any displayed code. Take a short phone video.
- **Check the remote.** Replace the remote batteries and confirm the mode (Cool, Heat, Auto) and set temperature are correct. A dead remote does not cause a fault code, but it can be mistaken for one.
- **Inspect the filters.** Open the indoor unit's front grille, the user-access part only. Slide out the mesh filters and rinse them if they are dusty. Let them dry fully, then reinstall.
- **Check the breaker and disconnect.** Look for a tripped breaker on the mini-split circuit and confirm the outdoor disconnect is on.
- **Perform ONE power reset.** Turn off the breaker for about five minutes, then restore power. If the blinking returns, do not reset again. Call a technician.
- **Clear around the units.** Remove leaves, snow or debris from around the outdoor unit, keeping about 2 feet clear. Make sure curtains and furniture are not blocking the indoor unit's airflow.
- **Check condensate.** Look for water dripping from the indoor unit or a clogged drain outlet outside. If your system has a condensate pump, listen for it running. Do not open the pump or the unit to clear it beyond the visible drain end.
- **Confirm the panels are seated.** Make sure the front grille is closed and properly latched.

Do not open the outdoor unit or the indoor unit's electrical cover. Mini-split inverter boards hold dangerous capacitor voltage for several minutes after power is removed.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified technician should:

1. **Read the code.** They will match the flash count or display code to the service manual for your exact model. On many units they will also read the outdoor board's LED pattern, and some can use the remote's check or diagnostic function.
2. **Verify power.** Expect them to measure line voltage at the outdoor unit and check the breaker and disconnect for heat damage or loose terminals.
3. **Test communication.** They will check the interconnect wiring terminals, confirm correct terminal-to-terminal wiring, and measure the signal line voltage. They should also look for nicked or water-damaged cable along the lineset.
4. **Test sensors.** They will unplug and measure each thermistor's resistance against the manufacturer's temperature-resistance chart.
5. **Check motors.** They will verify indoor fan motor feedback and the outdoor fan operation.
6. **Evaluate the inverter and compressor.** This includes checking compressor winding resistance, insulation to ground, IPM or inverter board diagnostics, and DC bus voltage.
7. **Check refrigerant only if indicated.** If sensor or pressure data point to undercharge, they will connect gauges and leak-search before adding refrigerant. Topping off without finding the leak is a red flag.

A credible quote names the specific code, the component that failed, and how it was tested. Be wary of a recommendation to "replace the board" without measurements, or to replace the whole system over a sensor or wiring fault.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blinking right after power outage or install | Communication error | One power reset | Check interconnect wiring, terminals, signal voltage, boards |
| Blinking, indoor fan runs, no cooling or heating | Sensor fault or outdoor protection | Clean filters, one reset | Test thermistors, read outdoor board LED |
| Blinking in heat mode on mild days, unit shuts down | High-pressure/overload protection, often from restricted indoor airflow | Clean filters, clear furniture or curtains from the indoor unit | Check fan motors, coil, charge |
| Operation LED blinks during heat start-up or in cold weather, then heating resumes | Normal start-up or defrost behavior on many models, not a fault | None; wait for heating to resume | Only needed if the unit stays stopped and keeps blinking |
| Blinking with ice on indoor coil or lines | Freeze protection, low airflow or low charge | Clean filters, switch to Fan only to thaw | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Blinking, indoor fan does not spin | Indoor fan motor fault | One reset | Test motor feedback, replace motor or board |
| Blinking, outdoor unit clicks but compressor won't run | Inverter/IPM or compressor fault | Check breaker; no repeated resets | Inverter board and compressor tests |
| Blinking with water leaking from indoor unit | Float switch / drain backup | Clear visible drain outlet | Clean pan, drain, pump; check float switch |
| Blinking returns within minutes of reset | Persistent hardware fault | Stop resetting, call pro | Full code-based diagnosis |

## Repair costs

Typical US prices, including parts and labor:

| Item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic visit | $100 – $250 |
| Interconnect wiring repair or re-termination | $150 – $400 (more if the cable must be re-run through walls) |
| Thermistor/sensor replacement | $150 – $400 |
| Indoor fan motor | $300 – $700 |
| Indoor control board | $300 – $800 |
| Outdoor inverter/control board | $600 – $1,500 |
| Refrigerant leak repair and recharge | $400 – $1,500, depending on leak location and refrigerant type |
| Condensate pump or drain service | $150 – $450 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500 – $2,500+ per outdoor unit; replacing the condenser may be more sensible on older units |

Check your warranty before approving work. Lennox mini-split parts coverage varies by product line and by whether the unit was registered, so read the Lennox warranty certificate for your specific series. Many inverter and board faults may fall under parts coverage. Labor coverage is usually separate.
