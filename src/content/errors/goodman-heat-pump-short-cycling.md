## What this code means

Short cycling means your Goodman heat pump starts, runs only a few minutes, and shuts off. It then restarts again soon after, over and over. Most single-stage Goodman heat pumps do not display a code for this. You notice it by sound and by uneven temperatures in the house.

On two-stage and communicating models (ComfortBridge or ComfortNet), the outdoor control may log a related fault, such as a high- or low-pressure switch trip or a discharge temperature fault. Check the board LED or the thermostat's fault history.

**Restart delays are normal, but repeated trips are not:**
- Most Goodman outdoor controls include an anti-short-cycle timer, typically about 3 to 5 minutes. A pause before restart is expected.
- Pressure switch trips usually auto-reset at first.
- After several trips in a single call for heat or cooling, many boards enter a lockout. A hard lockout holds until power is cycled.
- Behavior varies by model, so consult the unit's wiring diagram or manual.

This page covers short cycling on a heat pump in heating or cooling mode. Furnace burner short cycling is a different issue with its own page.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

Causes follow Goodman's general troubleshooting order: thermostat and airflow first, then electrical, then refrigerant system.

1. **Thermostat problems.** Possible causes include:
   - Weak batteries.
   - Poor location, such as near a supply register, in sunlight, or near a heat source.
   - A cycle rate (CPH) setting that is too high.
   - A thermostat not configured for heat pump operation.
2. **Restricted indoor airflow.** A clogged filter, closed or blocked registers, or a dirty indoor coil starves the system of air. This drives head pressure up in heat mode, or low suction and freezing in cooling, and can trip the pressure switches.
3. **Dirty or blocked outdoor coil.** Debris, leaves, or snow around the outdoor unit can cause high-pressure trips in cooling mode.
4. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** This can trip the low-pressure switch, which then resets and the unit restarts. The result is the classic on-off pattern.
5. **Failing pressure switch or loss-of-charge switch.** A switch that opens at the wrong pressure stops the compressor even when pressures are normal.
6. **Electrical faults.** A weak run capacitor, pitted contactor, or loose wiring can cause the compressor to overheat and trip its internal overload.
7. **Outdoor fan problems.** A fan that is not running in cooling mode causes high-pressure trips. This is covered in detail on the outdoor fan page.
8. **Oversized equipment.** The system satisfies the thermostat too fast, which is most noticeable on single-stage units in mild weather.
9. **Control board or sensor faults.** These include a faulty defrost board or a failed coil or ambient sensor. On communicating models, configuration errors are also possible.
10. **Refrigerant overcharge or a metering device problem.** An overcharge or a TXV or piston fault causes abnormal pressures that trip the switches.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Check the thermostat.**
   - Replace the batteries.
   - Confirm the system is set to the correct mode, such as Heat or Cool rather than Emergency Heat.
   - Confirm the thermostat is configured for a heat pump, not a gas furnace.
   - If there is a cycles-per-hour setting, a typical heat pump value is 2 to 3 CPH.
   - Make sure the thermostat is not near a supply register, a lamp, or direct sun.
2. **Replace the air filter.** A dirty filter is the most common cheap fix.
3. **Open the registers and clear the returns.** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles are not blocked by furniture or rugs.
4. **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove leaves, snow, and debris, keeping about 2 feet of clearance. Do this from outside the unit only. Do not open the panels.
5. **Check the condensate drain.** In cooling, a full float switch can cut the system on and off. Make sure the drain line or pan is not backed up.
6. **Do one reset if needed.** If the unit seems locked out, turn the system off at the thermostat. Switch off the breaker or outdoor disconnect for about 5 minutes, then restore power. Do this only once. If short cycling returns, stop and call a technician.
7. **Check the panels.** Confirm the indoor unit's access panels are fully seated.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Reads fault codes or flash history on the outdoor control and, on communicating systems, the thermostat or ComfortBridge app.
- Checks the thermostat wiring and settings, and confirms what is ending each cycle (the thermostat, a safety switch, or an overload).
- Measures static pressure and airflow, and inspects the indoor and outdoor coils.
- Attaches gauges or digital probes and measures superheat and subcooling to check the charge. If the charge is low, they should leak-search rather than just top it off.
- Tests the high-pressure, low-pressure, and loss-of-charge switches.
- Tests the capacitor, contactor, compressor amp draw, and wiring.
- Checks the outdoor fan motor and its capacitor.
- Checks the defrost board and sensors.
- Evaluates whether the equipment is the right size for the home.

**When reviewing a quote, push back if:**
- It recommends adding refrigerant without a leak check.
- It recommends replacing the compressor before testing the capacitor, contactor, and pressures.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2–5 min, stops, restarts after a pause | Thermostat location, batteries, or settings | Replace batteries, check mode, CPH, and heat pump setup | Relocate or reconfigure the thermostat, check wiring |
| Short cycles with weak airflow at registers | Dirty filter or blocked returns | Replace the filter, open the registers | Clean the indoor coil, check the blower and static pressure |
| Short cycles in cooling on hot days | Dirty outdoor coil or fan problem | Clear debris around the outdoor unit | Clean the coil, test the fan motor and capacitor |
| Short cycles, low output, ice on lines | Low refrigerant charge | Replace the filter and call for service | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Board logs pressure trips, then locks out | Pressure switch trips or failed switch | One power reset only | Test the switches and diagnose the pressures |
| Compressor clicks off hot and hums on restart | Weak capacitor or overload trip | None. Call for service | Test and replace the capacitor or contactor, check amp draw |
| Short cycles only in mild weather, temps fine | Oversized single-stage unit | Adjust the thermostat cycle rate | Load calculation, consider two-stage or variable-speed equipment |
| Shuts off when the drain pan fills (cooling) | Clogged condensate line or tripped float switch | Clear the visible drain line or pan | Clear the trap and verify the float switch |
