## What this code means

"Runs constantly" is a symptom, not a fault code. Trane heat pumps don't display a specific code for it. It shows up in one of two ways, and they point to different problems:

1. **The unit runs nonstop and the house doesn't reach the setpoint.** This is a capacity problem: airflow, refrigerant charge, coil condition, sizing or weather.
2. **The unit keeps running after the thermostat is satisfied or turned off.** This is a control problem. Most often the outdoor contactor is stuck closed, or a thermostat wire is shorted.

Some long runs are by design. Trane two-stage units (such as XL18i and XR17) spend most of their time on low stage. Variable-speed TruComfort units (XV18, XV20i) aim for near-continuous low-speed operation. On those models, judge the system by comfort and power use, not by how long it runs.

There is no lockout or reset tied to this symptom. A stuck contactor will not clear itself. The unit keeps running until power is removed at the disconnect or breaker.

If your issue is the electric backup heat staying on, see Trane Heat Pump Aux Heat On Constantly. If the indoor fan alone won't stop, see the blower page listed under Related codes.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or restricted airflow.** This reduces heat transfer and stretches cycles in both heating and cooling. It is the most common cause and the easiest fix.
2. **Thermostat setting or placement.** Common examples:
   - The fan is set to On instead of Auto. Only the indoor fan runs nonstop in this case.
   - An extreme setpoint.
   - The thermostat sits in sun or near a supply register or drafty spot.
   - Weak batteries or a miscalibrated sensor.
3. **Extreme outdoor temperatures.** A heat pump's heating output drops as it gets colder outside. Below the balance point (often 25–35°F), long or constant runs are expected.
4. **Dirty outdoor or indoor coil.** Debris, cottonwood or grass on the outdoor coil, or a dirty indoor evaporator coil, cuts capacity.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** The unit runs long and still falls short. You may see a partly frosted coil in cooling mode, or weak heat in heating mode.
6. **Stuck or welded contactor.** The compressor and outdoor fan keep running with no call from the thermostat. This is common on older single- and two-stage units.
7. **Shorted or miswired thermostat cable.** A damaged low-voltage wire, often chewed or pinched, can energize Y (the compressor call) continuously.
8. **Reversing valve problems.** A valve stuck in the wrong position, or leaking between positions, lowers output. The unit then runs without reaching the setpoint.
9. **Failing compressor or weak run capacitor.** The compressor runs but pumps poorly, so capacity is low.
10. **Undersized system or ductwork problems.** Leaky, undersized or disconnected ducts can cause it, as can a home that has been added onto since installation.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Confirm the mode (Heat or Cool) and a reasonable setpoint. Set the fan to **Auto**. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or shows a low-battery icon. If the thermostat is in direct sun or near a register, note that for your technician.
- **Test the control:** Set the thermostat to Off and wait 5–10 minutes.
  - If the outdoor unit keeps running, shut it off at the outdoor disconnect or the breaker.
  - Leave it off and call a technician. Don't keep restoring power.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it is dirty or more than 1–3 months old. Make sure the airflow arrow points toward the unit.
- **Vents and returns:** Open the supply registers and clear furniture or rugs off the return grilles.
- **Outdoor unit:** From outside the unit, without opening any panel:
  - Clear leaves, snow and debris from around it.
  - Keep about 2 feet of clearance.
  - Gently hose off loose surface debris from the outside of the coil, with power off at the disconnect.
- **Condensate line:** In cooling season, make sure the drain is flowing and the pan isn't full. A clogged drain can trip a float switch, which causes other symptoms.
- **Panels:** Confirm the indoor and outdoor access panels are seated.
- **Compare with the weather:** Constant running on a 10°F night or a 100°F afternoon may simply mean it's working at full capacity.

Anything beyond this, including opening the cabinet, the contactor, the capacitor, wiring or refrigerant, is technician work.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically:

1. **Confirm the complaint type.** They will check whether the system won't shut off, or runs without satisfying the setpoint.
2. **Check the control signal.** They will verify whether 24V is present on Y (and O/B) at the outdoor unit when the thermostat isn't calling.
   - Voltage present with no call means a thermostat or wiring fault.
   - No voltage, yet the compressor still runs, means a welded contactor.
3. **Inspect the contactor** for pitted, welded contacts or insect debris.
4. **Measure airflow:** static pressure, blower speed settings and the temperature split across the indoor coil.
5. **Check refrigerant performance:** superheat and subcooling compared with Trane's charging chart for the specific model. If the charge is low, they should do a leak search before recharging.
6. **Inspect and clean the coils,** indoor and outdoor.
7. **Check the reversing valve** by comparing line temperatures in heat and cool modes.
8. **Check compressor amp draw and the capacitor** against the nameplate and rated microfarad values.
9. **Evaluate sizing and ducts** if everything else checks out. This may include a Manual J load calculation or a duct leakage test.

Be wary of a quote to "add refrigerant" with no leak search, or a recommendation to replace the system before the basic tests above have been done.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indoor fan never stops, outdoor unit cycles normally | Fan set to On | Set the fan to Auto | Blower relay or control check if Auto doesn't fix it |
| Runs nonstop, weak airflow at the registers | Dirty filter or blocked returns | Replace the filter, clear the returns | Static pressure test, blower or duct correction |
| Outdoor unit runs with the thermostat Off | Welded contactor or shorted Y wire | Shut off the disconnect or breaker and leave it off | Replace the contactor, repair the wiring |
| Runs long, house a few degrees short, outdoor coil matted | Dirty outdoor coil | Clear debris, gently rinse the outside surface with power off | Chemical coil cleaning |
| Runs long, poor heating and cooling, frost patches in cooling mode | Low refrigerant from a leak | Replace the filter, then call | Leak search, repair, evacuation and recharge |
| Constant running only in very cold or hot weather | Normal capacity limit or undersizing | Moderate the setpoint, check the filter | Load calculation, balance point and aux heat settings |
| Weak output in one mode, odd line temperatures | Reversing valve fault | None | Test or replace the reversing valve |
| Runs constantly, high power bills, compressor hot or noisy | Weak compressor or failing capacitor | None | Amp draw and capacitor test, then replace the part |
| Erratic runs, thermostat in sun or near a vent | Thermostat placement or sensor | Replace the batteries, note the location | Relocate or replace the thermostat |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Air filter | $10–$40 (DIY) |
| Thermostat batteries | $5–$10 (DIY) |
| Service call and diagnosis | $90–$200 |
| Contactor replacement | $150–$350 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Thermostat wire repair | $100–$300 |
| Thermostat replacement | $150–$500 installed; Trane communicating thermostats cost more |
| Coil cleaning (outdoor or indoor) | $100–$400 |
| Refrigerant leak search | $200–$500 |
| Leak repair plus recharge | $400–$1,500+, depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A, or R-454B on newer units) |
| Reversing valve replacement | $900–$2,000 |
| Indoor or outdoor coil replacement | $1,200–$2,800 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800–$3,500+ |
| New system (if undersized or failing) | $6,000–$15,000+ |

Check your Trane registered limited warranty. Registered units often carry a 10-year parts warranty, and some compressors carry 12 years. Labor is usually not covered unless you bought an extended plan.
