## What this code means

"Won't turn on" is not a stored fault code. It means your Bryant air conditioner shows no response to a cooling call. The indoor blower doesn't start, the outdoor unit stays silent, and often the thermostat screen is blank too.

On a Bryant split system, the indoor furnace or air handler supplies the 24-volt control power. That power runs the thermostat and signals the outdoor condenser to start. So when the whole system is dead, the problem is almost always one of three things:

- Lost line power to the indoor unit
- An open safety switch, such as a condensate float switch
- A failure in the 24V control circuit

Some situations are covered on other pages:

- **Indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit doesn't:** see Compressor not running or Outdoor fan not spinning.
- **Breaker trips when the unit starts:** see Breaker keeps tripping.

**Resetting:** There is no lockout to clear for this symptom. If power was the cause, the system resumes once power returns, after the roughly 5-minute compressor delay. A tripped float switch resets on its own once the water drains, but the cause of the clog still needs fixing.

**Evolution systems:** The Evolution Connex control may show a "communication fault" or similar message. Note the exact text before you call.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat problem.** The thermostat may have dead batteries, be set to Heat or Off, or have a setpoint above room temperature. A schedule or hold may also be overriding your setting.
2. **Tripped breaker or switched-off service switch.** This can be the indoor furnace or air handler breaker, or the light-style service switch on or near the furnace. Either one kills 24V control power to the entire system.
3. **Condensate float or overflow switch tripped.** A clogged drain fills the pan, and the switch cuts the 24V signal to prevent a water leak. This is very common in summer.
4. **Blower door safety switch open.** The furnace or air handler access panel isn't seated, so the interlock switch removes power.
5. **Blown low-voltage fuse on the control board.** This is usually a 3A or 5A automotive-style fuse. It typically blows from a shorted thermostat wire, often outdoors where wires get chewed or rubbed through.
6. **Failed 24V transformer.**
7. **Control board failure.** On Evolution systems, this can also be a communication failure between the thermostat and the indoor unit.
8. **Damaged thermostat wiring.** Breaks or shorts can occur between the thermostat, the indoor unit and the condenser.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat.** Replace the batteries if the screen is blank or dim. Set the mode to **Cool**, the fan to **Auto**, and the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature. Cancel any hold or vacation mode. Then wait 5 minutes for the compressor delay.
- **Breakers.** Check the panel for tripped breakers marked furnace, air handler, AC or condenser. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and see Breaker keeps tripping.
- **Furnace service switch.** Make sure the wall switch near the furnace or air handler is on. These are often mistaken for a light switch and turned off.
- **Outdoor disconnect.** Confirm the outdoor disconnect is in the ON position, or the pull-out block is fully inserted. Don't open the box beyond that.
- **Blower access panel.** Make sure the front panel of the furnace or air handler is fully seated so the door switch closes. Press it firmly into place without removing any screws inside.
- **Condensate drain.** Look for water in the drain pan or the secondary pan, or around the float switch. If the drain line has an accessible cleanout or vent tee, you can clear it with a wet/dry vacuum at the outdoor end of the line. Once the water drains, the float switch should reset itself.
- **Air filter.** Replace a clogged filter. A blocked filter won't usually stop startup by itself, but it often comes with a frozen coil and overflowing drains.
- **One reset.** Turn the thermostat to Off. Switch off the indoor and outdoor breakers for 5 minutes, restore power, then call for cooling. Do this only once.

If the system still won't start, call a technician. Don't open the control compartment or touch the fuses or wiring yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically work through these steps:

1. **Check line voltage.** Verify 120V at the furnace or air handler and 240V at the condenser.
2. **Read the board LED.** Check the status light on the indoor control board and compare its flash code to the chart on the door label. A steady light means normal standby. No light means there's no power to the board, or the fuse or transformer has failed.
3. **Test the 24V circuit.** Measure transformer output across R and C (about 24–28VAC), then check the low-voltage fuse.
4. **Look for a short if the fuse is blown.** Isolate the thermostat wires and the outdoor Y/C pair to find the short before replacing the fuse. Simply swapping the fuse without finding the short is a red flag.
5. **Check safety switches.** Confirm the float switch and door switch are closed.
6. **Jumper at the board.** Briefly jumper R to Y and G at the board to separate a thermostat or wiring fault from a board fault.
7. **On Evolution systems,** check ABCD communication wiring voltages and review the fault history in the control's service menu.
8. **Clear the drain.** If the float switch had tripped, flush the drain and confirm the trap and slope are correct.

Expect a diagnostic fee first. A quote for a new board should come with a confirmed 24V input and a failed output. It shouldn't be based on a guess.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat screen blank | Dead batteries or no 24V | Replace batteries, check breaker and service switch | Test transformer, fuse, R/C wiring |
| Screen normal, nothing runs | Float switch tripped or blown fuse | Check drain pan for water, clear drain line | Replace fuse, find short, clear drain |
| Water in pan or near furnace, unit dead | Clogged condensate drain | Vacuum drain line at outdoor end | Flush drain, correct trap or slope |
| Dead after filter change or service | Blower panel not seated | Reseat access panel firmly | Check or replace door switch |
| Board LED off, breaker on | Failed transformer or board | One power reset | Replace transformer or control board |
| Fuse keeps blowing | Shorted thermostat or outdoor wire | None | Locate and repair wiring short |
| Evolution shows communication fault | ABCD wiring or control issue | Note message, one power reset | Diagnose comm wiring, control or board |
| Breaker trips immediately | Electrical short | Reset once only | See Breaker keeps tripping |
