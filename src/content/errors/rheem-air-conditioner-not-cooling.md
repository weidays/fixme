## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a stored fault code. It means the system is running, or trying to run, without lowering the indoor temperature. Most Rheem single-stage condensers have no diagnostic display, so the cause has to be traced by symptom.

Some two-stage and variable-speed Rheem models report faults as LED flash codes on the outdoor control board or on an EcoNet thermostat. Examples include high or low pressure lockouts and compressor protection trips.

On units with lockout logic, the behavior varies by board:
- Some boards retry automatically after a delay.
- Some hold a hard lockout after repeated pressure trips until power is cycled.

Check the label inside your outdoor unit's service panel, or the installation manual, for your model's exact behavior.

This page covers central air conditioners. If you have a heat pump, see the separate heat pump not-cooling page.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow.** Restricted airflow drops cooling capacity and can freeze the indoor coil. This is the most common cause and the cheapest fix.
2. **Thermostat settings or power.** Causes include a thermostat set to Heat or Fan only, dead batteries, or a setpoint above room temperature.
3. **Tripped breaker or outdoor disconnect.** The indoor blower runs on a separate circuit, so the house moves air but nothing cools it.
4. **Failed run capacitor.** This is the most common electrical failure on Rheem condensers. The compressor or fan hums but won't start, or runs and overheats.
5. **Failed or pitted contactor.** The outdoor unit won't engage even though the thermostat is calling for cooling.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil.** It usually results from low airflow or low refrigerant. Airflow stops and ice may be visible on the refrigerant lines.
7. **Refrigerant leak.** Rheem evaporator coils, like many brands' coils, have had documented formicary corrosion leaks. On models with pressure switches, a leak may trigger a low-pressure lockout.
8. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil.** It causes high head pressure, poor cooling and possible high-pressure trips on hot days.
9. **Condensate overflow safety switch tripped.** A clogged drain trips a float switch that shuts off cooling.
10. **Outdoor fan motor failure.** The compressor runs but overheats and cycles off on internal overload.
11. **Compressor failure.** This is the least common cause but the most expensive one, and it is usually confirmed only after the items above are ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set it to Cool, with the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature and the fan on Auto. Replace the batteries if the display is blank or dim.
- **Air filter:** Pull it out. If it's gray or clogged, or you can't see light through it, replace it.
- **Breakers:** Check the main panel for both the furnace/air handler and the AC (usually a double-pole breaker). Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Make sure the disconnect box next to the condenser hasn't been pulled or switched off.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look without touching anything. Is the fan spinning? Clear leaves, grass clippings and debris to about 2 feet around the unit. Gently hose the outside of the fins from the top down, with the power off at the disconnect.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper line or the indoor unit, set the thermostat to Off and the fan to On until it fully thaws. Don't chip at the ice.
- **Vents and registers:** Open supply registers and clear blocked return grilles.
- **Condensate line:** If the drain pan is full or the line is clogged, clear the end of the line with a wet/dry vac. A cup of white vinegar poured into the access tee can help. This may reset a tripped float switch.
- **Panels:** Make sure the blower compartment door is fully seated. Many units won't run with it loose.
- **One reset:** If your outdoor board shows a lockout, turn the power off at the breaker for 5 minutes, then restore it. Do this one time only. If it locks out again, call a technician.

Do not open the condenser's electrical panel, touch capacitors (they hold a charge even with power off), or attempt any refrigerant work.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically:

1. **Confirm the call.** They verify 24V at the outdoor contactor coil and confirm the thermostat is calling for cooling.
2. **Test line voltage** at the disconnect and contactor, and inspect the contactor points for pitting.
3. **Test the run capacitor** with a meter in microfarads (µF). They compare the reading against its rating, usually within ±6%.
4. **Measure amperage** on the compressor and fan motor and compare it against the nameplate.
5. **Attach gauges or digital probes.** They check suction and liquid pressures, superheat (fixed-orifice systems) or subcooling (TXV systems, common on Rheem). These are compared to the charging chart on the unit.
6. **Measure temperature split** across the indoor coil. A normal split is roughly 15–20°F.
7. **Check airflow:** static pressure, blower speed and filter, and evaporator coil cleanliness.
8. **Read board flash codes** on two-stage or variable-speed models, if equipped.
9. **Leak-search** with an electronic detector, bubbles or a nitrogen pressure test if the charge is low.

**Red flags in a quote:**
- "Just needs a top-off" with no leak search.
- A compressor replacement quoted without amp or winding tests.
- Refrigerant added without measuring superheat or subcooling.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing runs indoors or out | Thermostat, batteries, breaker | Check settings, replace batteries, reset breaker once | Low-voltage wiring, transformer, fuse on control board |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Outdoor breaker or disconnect, contactor, capacitor | Check outdoor breaker and disconnect | Test and replace contactor or capacitor |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan doesn't spin | Failed capacitor or fan motor | Turn off power at the disconnect, then call | Replace capacitor or fan motor |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Dirty filter, low airflow, low refrigerant | Replace filter, run fan only to thaw, open vents | Check charge, leak search, blower check |
| Runs constantly, air only slightly cool | Low refrigerant, dirty coils | Replace filter, rinse outdoor coil | Leak search, coil cleaning, charge correction |
| Cooling stops, water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate, tripped float switch | Clear condensate line | Clear trap, replace pump or switch |
| Works mornings, quits on hot afternoons | Dirty condenser, weak capacitor, high-pressure trip | Clear debris, rinse coil | Test capacitor, check pressures and fan |
| Outdoor board flashing a code (some models) | Pressure or compressor protection lockout | One power-cycle reset | Interpret code, fix root cause |
| Breaker trips when compressor starts | Shorted or grounded compressor, bad capacitor | Do not keep resetting | Compressor winding tests, hard-start kit or replacement |
