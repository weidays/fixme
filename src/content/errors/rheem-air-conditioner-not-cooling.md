## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on Rheem or Ruud air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your system may run and blow air, but that air isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. Because central AC has no single "not cooling" light, this page covers the full range of causes, from the trivial (dirty filter) to the serious (failed compressor or refrigerant leak).

If your Rheem **heat pump** is showing a flashing fault light instead, that's a different diagnostic path — see Related codes below.

On EcoNet communicating systems, a genuine hardware fault may show a lettered/numbered code on the thermostat or the outdoor control board. If you have an actual displayed code, look that up specifically rather than treating it as a generic "not cooling" issue.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. A dirty filter starves the system for airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil into a block of ice.
2. **Dirty condenser coil (outdoor unit)** — grass, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection so the system can't dump heat outside.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuse at the outdoor unit** — the indoor blower runs on a separate circuit, so you get airflow with no cooling.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks the coil until it thaws.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a common, inexpensive part. The compressor or condenser fan won't start, so the outdoor unit hums or does nothing.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or burns out.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — you never "use up" refrigerant; low charge always means a leak. Symptoms include weak cooling and ice on the copper lines.
8. **Thermostat problem or wiring fault** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a broken thermostat wire.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome, often the end result of chronic overheating or electrical faults.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do not open the sealed refrigerant system or bypass any safety switch.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. Try lowering it 3–4 degrees to force a call for cooling.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clean filter often restores cooling within a day.
- **Breakers:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker on both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) circuits. Reset **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for the fan spinning when cooling is called. Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the unit and gently rinse the outer coil fins with a garden hose (power off first).
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain:** A full drain pan or clogged line can trigger a float switch that shuts off cooling. Check for standing water and clear the drain line with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor termination.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper refrigerant lines, turn the system to OFF (fan can stay ON) and let it thaw fully — often several hours — before running again.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheapest and most likely to most expensive:

1. **Verifies the complaint** — measures supply and return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F).
2. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, blower operation, and evaporator coil for dirt or ice.
3. **Inspects electricals** — tests the run capacitor with a meter, checks the contactor for pitting, verifies incoming voltage.
4. **Attaches refrigerant gauges** — reads suction and liquid pressures against superheat/subcooling targets for your specific model. Abnormal readings point to charge or airflow problems.
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, they use an electronic detector or dye to find the leak rather than just recharging.
6. **Compressor test** — checks amp draw and winding resistance if the compressor won't start or trips the breaker.

Ask to see the temperature split and pressure readings. A quote for a "recharge" without a leak search is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blows air but it's not cold | Dirty filter or dirty condenser coil | Replace filter; rinse outdoor coil | Deep coil clean, airflow check |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker, bad contactor or capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace capacitor or contactor |
| Ice on coil or copper lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw fully, replace filter | Leak search, recharge, airflow fix |
| Weak cooling on hot days only | Undercharge or dirty condenser | Clean outdoor unit | Verify charge with gauges |
| Outdoor fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Replace capacitor |
| House never reaches setpoint | Undersized charge, duct leaks, failing compressor | Check thermostat & filter | Full pressure and compressor diagnosis |
| No cooling, water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain / float switch | Clear drain line with wet/dry vac | Clear drain, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 pro
- **Professional coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$300
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$2,000 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,800
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800–$2,500 (often worth weighing against full system replacement on older units)

Systems still under Rheem's parts warranty may cover the part cost, leaving you to pay labor only. Have your model and serial number ready to check coverage.
