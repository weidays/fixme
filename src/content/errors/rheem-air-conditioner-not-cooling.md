## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific Rheem fault code — your air conditioner is running (or trying to) but the house isn't getting cold. On Rheem and Ruud central systems this can mean the outdoor condenser won't start, the indoor blower is moving warm air, or the system is running constantly without lowering the temperature.

Because there's no single blinking light for "not cooling," the cause has to be narrowed down by symptom. Some Rheem outdoor units with control boards will flash diagnostic codes for pressure or capacitor faults, but many single-stage RA-series condensers have no board and no display — the diagnosis is entirely mechanical and electrical.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. A clogged filter starves the system, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the coil.
2. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure. A weak or blown capacitor stops the compressor and/or condenser fan from starting; you may hear humming.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant. Ice on the copper lines means the system can't transfer heat until it thaws.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak or no cooling and often ice. Refrigerant doesn't get "used up," so low charge always means a leak.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so cooling drops on hot days.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the condenser burns or sticks, leaving the outdoor unit dead or intermittent.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor disconnect or panel breaker cut power.
8. **Bad thermostat or dead batteries** — no cooling call is being sent.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — compressor runs but the fan doesn't, causing overheating and shutdown.
10. **Failed compressor** — the least common but most expensive; the unit hums, trips the breaker, or does nothing.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop at anything that requires opening the sealed system or electrical panels beyond the breaker.

- **Thermostat:** Set to COOL, temperature several degrees below room temp, fan on AUTO. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Do this first — it fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Breaker:** Check the panel for a tripped AC breaker. Flip it fully OFF, then ON once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting it.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** There's a pull-out switch in a box near the outdoor unit. Make sure it's fully seated.
- **Supply and return vents:** Confirm they're open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate:** A clogged drain can trigger a float switch that shuts cooling off. Look for standing water at the indoor unit; a wet/dry vac on the drain line can clear a simple clog.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper lines, turn the system to OFF and the fan to ON to thaw it (can take a few hours). If it re-freezes, you have an airflow or refrigerant problem for a tech.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirms the symptom** — checks thermostat call, indoor and outdoor operation, and measures supply/return air temperature split (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and evaporator coil for dirt or ice.
3. **Tests electrical components** — measures the capacitor's microfarad rating, checks the contactor, and verifies voltage at the condenser.
4. **Reads refrigerant pressures** — connects gauges to measure suction and head pressure and calculates superheat/subcooling. This tells them if the charge is low and often points to a leak.
5. **Leak search if charge is low** — uses an electronic detector or dye to find the leak before recharging (topping off without repair is bad practice).
6. **Checks the compressor and fan motor** — for amp draw, windings, and mechanical failure.

A good tech won't just add refrigerant and leave. Ask what the measured pressures and temperature split were.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air from vents, filter dirty | Clogged filter / low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Clean coil, check blower |
| Outdoor unit humming, won't start | Failed run/start capacitor | None (capacitors hold charge) | Test & replace capacitor |
| Ice on coil or copper lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find leak, recharge, verify charge |
| Weak cooling on hot days | Dirty condenser coil | Gently rinse outdoor coil with hose | Deep coil clean, pressure check |
| Outdoor unit totally dead | Breaker, disconnect, contactor | Reset breaker once, seat disconnect | Test/replace contactor |
| Compressor runs, fan doesn't spin | Failed condenser fan motor | None | Replace fan motor & capacitor |
| No cooling call at all | Thermostat / dead batteries | Replace batteries, check settings | Replace thermostat, check wiring |
| Unit hums then trips breaker | Failed compressor | None — stop resetting | Compressor test / replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included where noted:

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** under $10 (DIY)
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650 installed
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+; on an older unit, replacing the whole system is often the better value
- **Service/diagnostic call:** $80–$200, often credited toward the repair

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (older R-410A systems vs newer refrigerants), and whether the part is under Rheem's warranty. Rheem parts warranties often cover the component but not labor.
