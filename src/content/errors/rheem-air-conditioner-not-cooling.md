## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you notice: the Rheem AC is running (or trying to), but the air coming from your vents is warm, weak, or your home never reaches the thermostat setpoint. Depending on your model, you may see no code at all, a flashing light on the control board, or a comfort-control error at a Rheem EcoNet thermostat.

Because "not cooling" covers everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, the goal here is to help you separate the safe, homeowner-fixable causes from the sealed-system and high-voltage repairs that legally and safely require a licensed HVAC tech.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant. Ice on the coil or line set blocks cooling entirely.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums, clicks, or won't start. Very common on units 5+ years old.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — gradual loss of cooling, warm air, possible hissing or icing. Requires leak repair and recharge.
5. **Dirty condenser coil (outdoor unit)** — grass, dust, and debris block heat rejection, so the system can't cool efficiently.
6. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse to the outdoor unit** — indoor blower runs but outdoor unit is silent.
7. **Failed contactor or control board** — the outdoor unit won't energize even with a cooling call.
8. **Bad thermostat or wiring / EcoNet communication fault** — no cooling call reaches the equipment.
9. **Failed compressor** — the least common but most expensive; unit runs but produces no cold air.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop at anything that requires opening the sealed refrigerant system or touching high-voltage components.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and check. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **AUTO**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — that's a fault, not a nuisance trip.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the exterior coil fins with a garden hose (power off) — do not use a pressure washer.
- **Condensate drain:** If the indoor unit sits over a drain pan and the pan is full, a clogged drain may have tripped a float safety switch. You can clear a visible clog, but don't disable the switch.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** and the fan to **ON** for a few hours to thaw it, then restart. If it re-freezes, you have an airflow or refrigerant problem — call a tech.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Rheem tech will work in a logical order so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is typically 15–22°F).
2. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower, and coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Test electrical** — measure capacitor microfarads, check the contactor, and confirm voltage at the outdoor unit.
4. **Gauge the refrigerant** — connect manifold gauges, read pressures and superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct. Low readings point to a leak.
5. **Leak search** — if refrigerant is low, use an electronic detector or dye to find the leak before recharging.
6. **Compressor test** — check amp draw and windings if the compressor won't start or run.
7. **Board / thermostat check** — verify the cooling call, board diagnostics, and any EcoNet communication faults.

Ask to see the temperature split and refrigerant readings. A reputable tech shows their numbers rather than just declaring "you're low on Freon."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak airflow | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower & duct if it persists |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw with fan on, replace filter | Find airflow restriction or refrigerant leak |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, capacitor, or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test capacitor, contactor, voltage |
| Compressor hums but won't start | Failed run/start capacitor | None (high voltage) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Gradual loss of cooling over weeks | Refrigerant leak | None (sealed system) | Locate leak, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor coil packed with debris | Dirty condenser | Clear debris, gentle hose rinse | Professional coil cleaning |
| No cooling call at all | Thermostat or EcoNet fault | Check batteries/settings | Diagnose wiring, board, or comms |
| Unit runs constantly, never cools | Failed compressor | None | Compressor test / replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Prices vary by region, unit age, and warranty status.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$250
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 (more for EcoNet smart controls)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs. R-32)
- **Control board:** $300–$650
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,200+ (often worth comparing against a new system if the unit is 12+ years old)

If your Rheem AC is under its factory parts warranty (typically 10 years with registration), the part may be covered — you'd pay labor and refrigerant only. Keep your registration and model/serial info handy.
