## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a numbered fault code on most Amana heat pumps. It's a symptom: the compressor may hum or run, but the large fan on top of the outdoor unit stays still. That fan pulls air across the outdoor coil. In cooling mode it rejects heat. In heating mode it absorbs heat from outdoor air.

On a heat pump, two things make this symptom different from a straight AC:

- **Defrost is normal.** In heating mode, the defrost control on conventional Amana heat pumps stops the outdoor fan on purpose during defrost. A short stop with steam rising off the unit is expected. A fan that won't run at all, in either mode, is a fault.
- **The defrost board controls the fan.** On conventional units, the fan's power usually passes through a relay on the defrost control board. A failed board can kill the fan while everything else works.

On Amana inverter models (AVZC and similar), the fan is a variable-speed ECM motor driven by the inverter control board. That board usually flashes or displays a fault code. Your installation or service manual lists what each code means for your specific model.

This symptom does not reset on its own if a component has failed. If the compressor overheats from lack of airflow, its internal overload trips and resets only after it cools. That can look like intermittent operation. It is damaging the compressor, so don't keep running the unit.

If you have a central air conditioner rather than a heat pump, see our page Amana AC Outdoor Fan Not Spinning: Causes & Fixes. This page covers what's distinct to heat pumps.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor (conventional units).** This is the most common cause. A weak fan capacitor, or the fan section of a dual-run capacitor, leaves the motor humming without starting, or starting only when nudged. Capacitors degrade fastest in heat.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor.** The bearings seize or the windings open. Common signs are a motor that's hot to the touch with no rotation, or a shaft that's stiff when the power is off.
3. **Defrost control board fault (conventional heat pumps).** The fan relay on the board fails open, so the fan never gets power. A board stuck in defrost also keeps the fan off. Our separate page on being stuck in defrost covers that case.
4. **Inverter control board or ECM fan fault (inverter models).** The board shuts down or never powers the fan and logs a diagnostic code. Codes and procedures vary by model.
5. **Wiring or contactor problems.** These include burned or loose fan leads, a pitted contactor, or wires chewed by rodents.
6. **Physical obstruction.** Ice buildup around the fan blade in winter, or debris such as sticks or leaves jamming the blade.
7. **Thermostat or power issue.** The unit isn't actually being called to run, or a breaker or disconnect is off. In that case, the compressor won't be running either.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Confirm it's not defrost.** In heat mode, watch for up to 10–15 minutes. If you see steam or melting frost and the fan then restarts, the system is working normally.
- **Check the thermostat.** Make sure it's set to HEAT or COOL with a setpoint that calls for operation. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Check the breaker and outdoor disconnect.** Look for a tripped breaker and reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a technician. See our page Amana AC Breaker Keeps Tripping: Causes, Fixes & Costs.
- **Look at the unit from outside the fan guard only.** Check for sticks, leaves, or heavy ice visibly blocking the blade. Turn power off at the breaker and the disconnect before removing loose debris from the top grille. Never reach through the guard.
- **Reduce load on the heat pump.** If the fan is confirmed dead, set the thermostat to EM HEAT, if equipped, or turn the system off to protect the compressor.
- **Check the air filter.** A clogged filter won't stop the outdoor fan, but replacing it rules out airflow problems before the service visit.

Do not open the electrical panel, touch the capacitor, or spin the blade by hand on a live unit.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- They'll verify the thermostat call and confirm 24 V at the contactor. They'll also check whether the compressor is running.
- They'll **discharge the capacitor and test its microfarads** against the rating printed on the label. A reading more than about 6% below rating usually means replacement.
- They'll **spin the fan shaft with the power off** to check the bearings, and **check the motor windings** for open circuits or a short to ground.
- On conventional heat pumps, they'll **check the defrost board's fan output** (often the OF1/OF2 relay terminals) in both normal and defrost modes. They may also run the board's test or speed-up pins.
- On inverter models, they'll **read the control board's fault code** and follow Amana's diagnostic chart for the fan motor and board.
- They'll inspect the contactor and wiring for burned or loose connections.
- They'll check whether the compressor overheated, and confirm normal pressures after the repair.

A sound quote names the specific failed part and explains how it was tested. "Replaced fan motor and capacitor" is common and reasonable when the motor has failed, because a new motor usually comes with a new capacitor.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums, doesn't turn; compressor runs | Weak run capacitor | Shut the system off or use EM HEAT | Test and replace the capacitor |
| Fan stops briefly in winter with steam | Normal defrost cycle | Wait 10–15 minutes | None needed |
| Fan silent, motor hot, shaft stiff | Seized fan motor | Shut the system off | Replace the motor, usually with a new capacitor |
| Fan dead in heat and cool; contactor pulled in | Defrost board fan relay | Check breaker and thermostat | Test the board outputs; replace the board |
| Inverter unit shows fault code, fan off | ECM fan or inverter board | Note the code; reset power once | Diagnose per Amana's code chart |
| Fan blocked by ice or debris | Obstruction | Power off, clear visible debris from outside | Inspect drainage and the defrost cause |
| Nothing outdoors runs | No call or no power | Check thermostat, batteries, breaker | Check the low-voltage circuit and contactor |
