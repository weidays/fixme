## What this code means

"Not responding to remote" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. Your York mini-split's indoor unit isn't acting on commands from the handheld infrared (IR) remote. Signs include:

- no beep,
- no change on the indicator lights, or
- the unit ignores mode and temperature changes.

The fault sits somewhere along the command path:

1. The remote sends an IR signal.
2. The receiver window on the indoor unit picks it up.
3. The indoor control board acts on the command.

The problem can be anywhere along that chain, or in the power feeding the board.

There is no stored lockout that auto-resets. If the board has frozen, one power cycle may clear it. A failed receiver or board will not clear on its own.

If the indicator lights are blinking a pattern, the unit is reporting a fault rather than ignoring you. See the Blinking Light Error page instead.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Weak or dead remote batteries.** This is the most common cause by far. A low remote display or a range that keeps shrinking are typical signs.
2. **Remote set wrong or locked.** Some York remotes have a child/key lock, or show °C instead of °F, or are left in a mode that looks like no response. Which features exist depends on the remote model.
3. **Signal path blocked or interfered with.** Possible culprits:
   - you're too far away or at a steep angle,
   - furniture or curtains are in the way,
   - the receiver window is dirty,
   - bright sunlight hits the receiver, or
   - certain LED or fluorescent lights are nearby.
4. **Faulty remote.** It may have been dropped, have water damage, or have corroded battery contacts.
5. **Indoor unit has no power or its board has frozen.** Check for a tripped breaker, a turned-off disconnect, or a brownout that left the board unresponsive.
6. **Failed IR receiver or display board on the indoor unit.**
7. **Failed indoor main control board, or a wiring/connector fault** between the display/receiver board and the main board.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the remote batteries** with fresh alkaline AAAs. Check that they're seated with the right polarity and that the contacts look clean.
- **Check the remote's display.** Look for a lock icon and the mode setting. Your owner's manual explains how to unlock the remote and reset it to defaults for your model.
- **Test the remote with a phone camera.** Press a button while looking at the remote's tip through the camera. A flicker means the remote is transmitting.
- **Get closer.** Stand within about 15 feet, point straight at the indoor unit, and make sure nothing is in the way. Shade the unit from direct sun and turn off nearby LED or fluorescent lights to test.
- **Wipe the receiver window** on the front of the indoor unit with a soft, dry cloth. Don't open the unit.
- **Check power.** Confirm the mini-split breaker is on and the outdoor disconnect is in the on position. Look for any indicator light on the indoor unit.
- **Do one power cycle.** Turn the mini-split breaker off for about five minutes, then turn it back on. Do this once only.
- **Try the manual/emergency button.** This is usually behind the front panel lift-up. If the unit runs from the button but not the remote, the remote or the receiver is at fault. Use it only as your manual describes.
- **Check the filters.** Lift the front panel per the manual. This won't fix a remote problem, but it's worth doing while you're there.

Stop at this point. Don't open the indoor unit's cover or electrical box.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Confirm the remote transmits, using a camera or an IR tester. They may try a known-good matching remote.
- Check supply voltage at the indoor unit, and the communication between the indoor and outdoor units.
- Inspect the display/receiver board and its ribbon or connector to the main board for looseness, corrosion or damage.
- Test the receiver's output and the board's low-voltage supply with a meter, following York service documentation for that model.
- Read any stored fault codes. Then replace the receiver/display board or the main control board only after confirming the failure.
- If a wired wall controller or Wi-Fi module is installed, check whether it overrides or conflicts with IR control. This varies by setup.

A good quote names which part has failed and how it was confirmed. Be wary of a recommendation to replace the main board before the remote and receiver have been ruled out.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remote screen blank or dim | Dead batteries | Replace the batteries | None needed |
| Lock icon on the remote | Child/key lock on | Unlock per the manual | None needed |
| No flash on the phone camera with new batteries | Faulty remote | Buy a model-matched remote | Program or verify a compatible remote |
| Works up close only, or in the dark only | Blocked or interfered signal, dirty window | Clear the line of sight, wipe the window, shade from sun | Check receiver sensitivity |
| Unit runs from the manual button but ignores the remote | Failed receiver/display board | Use the manual button temporarily | Test and replace the receiver/display board |
| No lights, no response at all | No power or a frozen board | Check the breaker and do one power cycle | Check supply, wiring and the main board |
| Ignores both the remote and the manual button | Main control board fault | Turn power off and call a technician | Diagnose and replace the main board |
