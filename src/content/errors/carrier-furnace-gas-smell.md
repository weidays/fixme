## Emergency: what to do right now

**If you smell gas, leaving the building is the first and only action. Do not troubleshoot, do not shut anything off inside, and do not read the rest of this page until you are outside.**

1. **Leave the house immediately.** Take everyone and all pets with you. Do not stop to investigate the smell or find the source.
2. **Touch nothing electrical on your way out.** No light switches, no phone calls or texts indoors, no thermostat, no appliances, no garage door opener, no matches or lighters. A spark can ignite accumulated gas.
3. **Do not try to shut the furnace off at the wall switch.** That standard light-switch-style switch can arc, and it is usually in the same room where gas is collecting. Leave it alone.
4. **Do not hunt for the manual gas shutoff valve inside the house.** Searching for and operating an indoor valve keeps you in a gas-filled space. Leave that to the utility or fire department.
5. **Leave doors open behind you** if they are already on your way out. Do not go back inside or detour to open windows.
6. **From outside — a neighbor's home or well away from the building — call your gas utility's emergency line or 911.** The utility will dispatch a technician, usually at no charge for the safety visit.
7. **Shut off gas at the exterior meter only if the utility tells you to** and only at the outside meter valve. Otherwise, wait for responders.
8. **Do not go back inside** until the utility or fire department says it is safe.
9. **Do not restart the furnace yourself** after the area is cleared. Have the furnace inspected first (see below).

Routine checks — filter, thermostat, breaker, vents — mean nothing while gas is present. They come only after the area has been declared safe and the furnace has been inspected.

## What this code means

A **gas smell is not a Carrier diagnostic fault code** — it is a physical warning sign that unburned natural gas or propane is escaping somewhere in or around your furnace. The LED control board reports electrical and combustion faults (for example, ignition lockout or ignition proving failure), but it will **not** flash a code that means "gas leak." You detect this one with your nose.

**A note on code numbers:** Carrier's two-digit LED status codes vary by control board generation, so the same number can mean different things — or not exist at all — on another furnace. Infinity-controlled furnaces report faults through the Infinity wall control, with a different display and numbering. Always check the status-code label printed inside the blower door of your specific furnace rather than assuming a number from another model applies.

Utilities add a chemical called **mercaptan** to natural gas and propane, giving it a strong rotten-egg or sulfur smell. If you notice that odor near your Carrier furnace, in the utility closet, basement, or through the vents, **treat it as an emergency regardless of whether the furnace is running or showing any code.**

Gas leaks can come from a failed gas valve, a loose union or fitting, a cracked heat exchanger passing unburned gas, or a supply line problem. Some of these will cause the furnace to lock out; others let gas escape silently. Do not wait for the furnace to tell you.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Loose or failing gas fitting/union at the furnace.** Vibration and age can loosen threaded connections, letting small amounts of gas escape at joints near the valve or manifold.
2. **Failed or leaking gas valve.** The Carrier gas valve can fail internally or at its seals, allowing gas to seep even when the furnace is off.
3. **Unburned gas from a failed ignition sequence.** If the igniter or flame sensor fails, gas may release into the burner box before shutting off, producing a brief odor. This often pairs with an ignition lockout or ignition proving fault.
4. **Cracked or corroded heat exchanger.** A compromised heat exchanger can leak combustion byproducts and, less commonly, unburned gas — also a carbon monoxide risk.
5. **Supply-line leak upstream of the furnace.** The leak may not be in the furnace at all but in the black iron or flex line feeding it. This is the gas utility's and a licensed plumber's territory.
6. **Nearby non-furnace source.** A water heater, stove, or dryer sharing the space can be the actual source. The utility will identify which appliance is leaking.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Once the area is declared safe, a licensed HVAC technician or the utility will:

- **Use a combustible-gas detector** to pinpoint the leak location along the valve, manifold, unions, and supply line.
- **Apply leak-detection solution** to fittings and watch for bubbles to confirm the exact joint.
- **Test the gas valve** for internal leakage and proper shutoff, replacing it if the seals or body have failed.
- **Inspect the heat exchanger** with a camera or by combustion analysis for cracks that leak gas or carbon monoxide.
- **Check gas pressure** at the manifold with a manometer against Carrier's rated inlet and outlet specs.
- **Verify the ignition sequence** (igniter, flame sensor, timing) if unburned gas from a failed startup was the source — this may also clear a related ignition or flame-proving fault.
- **Perform a combustion analysis** and CO test before returning the furnace to service.

A quote should identify a specific failed component. Be cautious of any recommendation to simply "reset and monitor" a confirmed gas leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | Homeowner action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rotten-egg smell near furnace | Loose fitting or failing gas valve | Leave immediately, call utility/911 from outside | Locate leak with gas detector, tighten or replace valve/fitting |
| Brief gas whiff at startup, then furnace locks out | Failed ignition, unburned gas release | Leave and call the utility; after the area is cleared, have the ignition system inspected before the furnace is restarted — do not restart it yourself | Inspect and replace igniter/flame sensor, verify ignition timing, read stored fault codes |
| Persistent faint sulfur odor, furnace runs | Small supply-line or union leak | Leave immediately, call utility from outside | Pressure-test line, replace leaking section |
| Gas smell plus soot or headaches/nausea | Cracked heat exchanger, combustion issue | Evacuate, call 911 — possible CO | Camera-inspect heat exchanger, CO test, replace exchanger or furnace |
| Smell strongest at another appliance | Water heater/stove leak, not furnace | Leave immediately, call utility from outside | Utility isolates and repairs correct appliance |

## Repair costs

Costs depend entirely on where the leak is. The gas utility's emergency safety visit is **usually free**, but the repair afterward is on you.

| Repair | Typical US cost (parts + labor) |
|---|---|
| Utility emergency safety shutoff/inspection | Often $0 |
| Tighten/re-seal a loose gas fitting | $75 – $200 |
| Gas valve replacement | $300 – $600+ |
| Flame sensor cleaning/replacement | $80 – $250 |
| Hot-surface igniter replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Supply-line repair (licensed plumber) | $200 – $700+ |
| Heat exchanger replacement | $1,500 – $3,500+ (often favors furnace replacement) |
| Full furnace replacement | $3,500 – $7,500+ |

If a heat exchanger crack is the cause and the furnace is older, replacing the whole unit is often more cost-effective than the exchanger alone. Get a written diagnosis and a second opinion for any high-dollar quote.

## Related codes

Code numbers below follow one common Carrier LED control board generation. Your furnace may use different numbering — or an Infinity wall control — so confirm against the label inside your blower door.

- **Carrier Furnace Code 14: Ignition Lockout Causes & Fixes** — when repeated failed ignition attempts can release unburned gas.
- **Carrier Furnace Code 34: Ignition Proving Failure & Fixes** — flame not detected after gas is released.
- **Carrier Furnace Code 22: Abnormal Flame-Proving Signal** — flame-sensing faults tied to combustion.
- **Carrier Furnace Code 21: Gas Heating Lockout — Causes & Fixes** — the control will not permit gas heat; Carrier documents this as typically flame sensed with the gas valve de-energized, or a gas valve relay stuck closed.
- **Carrier Furnace Keeps Shutting Off: Causes, Fixes & Costs** — safety shutdowns that may accompany combustion problems.
