## What this code means

"Runs constantly" is a symptom, not a fault code. A Bryant AC does not display a code for it. It usually means one of two things:

1. **The system can't reach the setpoint.** It runs because it is short on capacity, airflow or refrigerant.
2. **The system can't shut off.** The compressor and outdoor fan keep running after the thermostat is satisfied, or even when the thermostat is set to OFF. This points to a control or electrical fault.

Bryant two-stage and variable-speed units (Evolution and many Preferred models) are designed to run long, low-speed cycles for efficiency and humidity control. On those models, a long run time is only a problem if the house isn't getting cool. There is no reset that clears this condition. It continues until the underlying cause is fixed.

This page covers continuous running. If the unit also blows warm air, see the "Blowing warm air" and "Not cooling" pages. If it cycles on and off rapidly, see "Short cycling."

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Extreme outdoor heat or a setpoint that is too low.** A system sized for a 95°F design day will run nonstop above that temperature. It will also run nonstop when the thermostat is set to 68°F or lower.
2. **Dirty air filter or restricted airflow.** Reduced airflow lowers cooling capacity and can lead to a frozen coil.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil.** Cottonwood, grass clippings or dirt on the coil cut heat rejection, so the unit runs longer.
4. **Thermostat issues.** Possible problems include:
   - The fan is set to ON, which is mistaken for cooling.
   - The thermostat is poorly located, such as in sun or near a heat source.
   - The batteries are weak.
   - The thermostat is failing or miscalibrated.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** Cooling capacity drops and the unit never satisfies the thermostat.
6. **Stuck or welded contactor in the outdoor unit.** The compressor and fan keep running even with the thermostat off.
7. **Shorted thermostat wire.** A damaged cable, often chewed or pinched, can keep the cooling call energized.
8. **Leaky ductwork or poor attic insulation.** Cooled air is lost before it reaches the rooms.
9. **Undersized system or a failing compressor.** The unit runs but can't keep up even when everything else checks out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the thermostat.**
  - Confirm it is set to COOL with the fan on AUTO, not ON.
  - Try a setpoint of 74–78°F.
  - Replace the batteries if it has them.
  - Make sure it isn't in direct sun or near a lamp or oven.
- **Replace the air filter** if it looks gray or clogged. Hold it up to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it.
- **Open supply registers and clear return grilles.** Furniture, rugs and closed vents all restrict airflow.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from outside.** Clear leaves, weeds and debris to at least 2 feet around it. Do not open the cabinet.
- **Check the condensate line.** Look for a full drain pan or a tripped float switch, and clear the drain line only at the accessible outside end.
- **Set the thermostat to OFF and wait 5 minutes.** If the outdoor unit is still running, turn it off at the outdoor disconnect or the breaker and call a technician. A compressor that runs without a cooling call can damage itself.
- **Look for ice.** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines, switch to fan-only and let it thaw (see "Frozen evaporator coil").

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirms the thermostat call.** Checks for 24V on the Y terminal at the outdoor unit with the thermostat satisfied. Voltage present means a thermostat or wiring fault.
- **Inspects the contactor.** Looks for pitted or welded contacts and checks for a stuck plunger.
- **Measures temperature split.** A return-to-supply difference of about 15–20°F is typical.
- **Checks airflow.** Measures static pressure and inspects the blower and the evaporator coil.
- **Takes refrigerant readings.** Records superheat and subcooling against the Bryant charging chart on the unit. If the charge is low, they should look for the leak, not just add refrigerant.
- **Checks the outdoor coil and compressor.** Inspects coil cleanliness, compressor amp draw and the capacitor.
- **Reviews the user interface on Evolution systems.** Checks the fault history and the staging behavior.
- **Evaluates the house if the equipment checks out.** This may include ducts, insulation, or a load calculation (Manual J) to check sizing.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs all afternoon on very hot days and the house is cool-ish | Heat load beyond the design temperature | Raise the setpoint and close the blinds | None needed, or a load calculation |
| Runs nonstop and airflow is weak | Dirty filter or blocked returns | Replace the filter and open the vents | Clean the coil, check the blower |
| Outdoor unit runs with the thermostat off | Welded contactor or shorted wire | Shut off the disconnect or breaker | Replace the contactor, repair the wiring |
| Runs constantly with weak cooling and ice on the lines | Low refrigerant or airflow problem | Switch to fan-only to thaw, change the filter | Leak search, repair and recharge |
| Outdoor coil is matted with debris | Poor heat rejection | Clear debris from outside the unit | Chemical coil cleaning |
| The fan never stops but there's no cooling | Thermostat fan set to ON | Set the fan to AUTO | Check the thermostat wiring if it persists |
| Long low-speed cycles on an Evolution unit, house comfortable | Normal variable-speed operation | No action | None |
