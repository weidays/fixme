## What this code means

"Blowing cold air" is a symptom, not a single Rheem fault code — but your furnace will usually report the real cause on the control board. Depending on the board generation, that fault may appear as a **flash code on a diagnostic LED or as a two-character alphanumeric display**, so check what your board actually shows before assuming a flash count. The common thread is that the blower is moving air that isn't being heated.

On Rheem gas furnaces this happens in a few documented patterns:

- The blower runs continuously because the thermostat fan is set to ON, so you feel unheated air between burns (normal operation, wrong setting).
- The burners ignite but the furnace fails to **prove flame**, so the board shuts the gas valve while the blower keeps running to purge the cabinet.
- A **high-limit switch** has tripped from overheating, locking out the burners but allowing the blower to cool the heat exchanger.
- The control board has entered a soft lockout and is only running the blower.

Whether the fault **auto-resets or holds** depends on the board generation and the specific condition. Flame-proving faults on many Rheem boards retry several times, then lock out for about one hour (auto-reset) or until power is cycled. A hard limit lockout typically holds until the condition clears and power is cycled. Check your door-panel diagnostic label for the exact code — it varies by series.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat fan set to ON (not AUTO).** The single most common reason for "cold air." The blower runs 24/7 and delivers room-temperature air between heating cycles. No fault at all.
2. **Dirty or failing flame sensor.** Burners light, but the sensor can't confirm flame within a few seconds, so the gas valve closes and the blower purges. Very common on older Rheem units.
3. **Tripped high-limit switch from restricted airflow.** A clogged filter or blocked returns cause overheating; the limit cuts the burners but the blower keeps running to shed heat.
4. **Failed igniter or no ignition.** If the hot-surface igniter is cracked or weak, the burners never light but the blower may still cycle. (See the related "Won't ignite" page for this specific path.)
5. **Control board soft lockout.** After repeated failed trials, the Rheem board stops firing the burners and may run only the inducer/blower. Reflected by a specific flash pattern or display code.
6. **Gas valve or wiring fault.** Less common; the board calls for heat but the valve never opens. Diagnosed by a technician.
7. **Condensate blockage on high-efficiency models (R95/R96/R97).** A backed-up condensate trap can trip a pressure safety and prevent ignition on condensing units.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

**If you smell gas or any burning odor, stop: leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside — do not perform these checks and do not reset the unit.**

Otherwise, these are the only steps a homeowner should perform:

- **Set the thermostat fan to AUTO** and heat mode a few degrees above room temperature. If warm air returns on the next cycle, the fix was simply the fan setting.
- **Replace the air filter** if it's dirty. A clogged filter is the leading cause of limit-trip overheating.
- **Replace thermostat batteries** (if battery-powered) and confirm the display reads correctly.
- **Check your breaker** and the furnace service switch (the light-switch-looking toggle near the unit). Reset a tripped breaker once.
- **Walk the house and open all supply and return vents.** Blocked registers starve airflow and trip the limit.
- **On high-efficiency condensing models, check the condensate drain** for standing water or an obvious clog; clear a visible blockage at the drain exit.
- **One reset only:** cycle power at the switch or breaker a single time. If cold air returns, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.

Do **not** open the burner compartment, touch the flame sensor, igniter, wiring, or any safety switch. That's technician territory.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Read the control-board fault code — flash pattern or two-character display — and cross-reference it to your model's label.
- Watch a full heat cycle: inducer start, igniter glow, burner ignition, and flame-proving.
- Measure **flame-sense microamps** with a meter and clean or replace the flame sensor if signal is weak.
- Check and reset the **high-limit and rollout switches**, then find the airflow or heat-exchanger reason they tripped.
- Test the **hot-surface igniter** resistance and the **gas valve** for correct operation.
- Inspect the **pressure switch and condensate trap** on condensing models.
- Verify blower speed, temperature rise, and static pressure to confirm airflow is within Rheem's spec.

A good quote names the specific part that failed and the reason — not just "replace the board."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool air all the time, even between cycles | Fan set to ON | Switch thermostat fan to AUTO | None if resolved |
| Burners light then shut off, blower blows cold | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None — call pro | Clean or replace flame sensor; test microamps |
| Cold air, furnace shuts down, warm cabinet | Tripped high-limit from low airflow | Replace filter; open vents | Find airflow cause; test/reset limit switch |
| Igniter glows, no flame, blower runs | Failed igniter or gas valve | One reset only | Test igniter and gas valve; replace as needed |
| Repeated cold air, specific fault code shown | Control board soft lockout | One reset; note the code | Diagnose per code; replace board if faulty |
| Condensing model, no ignition, water pooling | Blocked condensate / pressure switch | Clear visible drain clog | Clear trap; test pressure switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor:

- **Thermostat set to AUTO / filter swap:** $0–$40 DIY.
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** $80–$180 service call.
- **Flame sensor replacement:** $150–$300.
- **Hot-surface igniter replacement:** $150–$350.
- **High-limit or rollout switch replacement:** $150–$400 (plus fixing the root airflow cause).
- **Pressure switch / condensate repair (condensing models):** $150–$400.
- **Gas valve replacement:** $400–$900+, including the combustion and safety checks required afterward.
- **Control board replacement:** $400–$1,200 installed. The board alone is often $200–$500 before labor and diagnostic, and boards for condensing models (R95/R96/R97) generally cost more than R801/R802 boards.

Most "blowing cold air" calls turn out to be a dirty flame sensor or a thermostat setting — the cheapest outcomes. Get the fault code before approving a board swap.
