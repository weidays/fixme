## What this code means

"Breaker keeps tripping" is not a Lennox fault code. It means a circuit breaker in your home's electrical panel is opening to protect the wiring from too much current or a short circuit. The heat pump's control board doesn't create this condition. It simply loses power when the breaker opens.

Some Lennox units will show something useful when power comes back. Communicating systems with an iComfort or S30 thermostat, and inverter models, may log a code in their alert history. On most standard heat pumps, though, a trip just looks like the system going dead.

A breaker does not reset itself. It stays off until someone switches it back on.

When the trip happens matters:

- **Instantly when you reset it, or as soon as the system calls for heat or cooling:** this points to a dead short, such as a grounded compressor, shorted wiring or a failed motor.
- **After several minutes or hours of running:** this points to overload, such as a struggling compressor, failing capacitor, loose connections or heat strips.

This page covers only breaker trips. If your system has power but behaves strangely, see the related pages listed at the end.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor.** The compressor or outdoor fan motor can't start and draws locked-rotor current. This is the most common cause on single-stage and two-stage units. A locked rotor from a bad capacitor often trips the compressor's internal overload before the breaker, so you may first notice humming and the unit shutting off on its own. Capacitors still rank first because they fail far more often than compressors short to ground, and repeated locked-rotor starts can trip a breaker.
2. **Compressor problem: hard start, locked rotor, or a winding shorted to ground.** A grounded compressor usually trips the breaker instantly, rather than after a few failed starts. Lennox's outdoor unit manuals have technicians check the compressor windings and insulation to ground when there are overcurrent complaints.
3. **Electric auxiliary or emergency heat strip fault.** A shorted element, failed heater relay or sequencer, or burned terminals can cause it. This trips the air handler circuit, usually only in heating weather.
4. **Loose, burned or corroded wiring connections.** Check points include the contactor, the disconnect, the breaker lug and the heat strip terminals. High resistance creates heat and nuisance trips.
5. **Pitted or welded contactor, or a shorted outdoor fan motor.** A failing fan motor also overheats the unit, which raises compressor current.
6. **Worn, weak or wrong-size breaker.** Breakers fatigue over years of heavy loads. The correct breaker size is the "Max Fuse/Breaker" value on the unit nameplate.
7. **High head pressure overload.** This comes from a badly dirty outdoor coil, blocked airflow, or a refrigerant overcharge or non-condensables. It usually trips the compressor's internal overload first, but it can trip a marginal breaker.
8. **Water or rodent damage in the disconnect or the low-voltage and line-voltage wiring.**

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Find out which breaker trips.** Note whether it is the outdoor unit or the air handler and heat strips, and whether it happens in heating, cooling or both. Write down when it trips: instantly, at startup, or after running.
- **Reset the breaker once.** Switch it fully off, then back on. If it trips again, keep it off and call a technician. Do not keep resetting it.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** A clogged filter overheats the heat strips and strains the system.
- **Check the thermostat.** If it is set to Emergency Heat when you don't need it, switch it back to normal Heat. Replace the batteries if it uses them.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from outside the cabinet.** Clear leaves, snow and debris at least 2 feet around it, and make sure the top fan discharge isn't blocked. Do not open the panels.
- **Open supply vents and registers** and make sure they aren't blocked.
- **If you see smoke, visible arcing or fire, leave the house and call 911.** Do not try to reset anything or investigate first.
- **Switch the breaker off, keep it off, and call a technician** if you notice any of these:
  - a burning smell
  - scorch marks on the panel or the disconnect
  - a warm breaker
- **Call a technician** if the outdoor unit hums but won't start.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- They will confirm which circuit trips and compare the breaker and wire size to the nameplate's MCA and Max Fuse/Breaker ratings.
- They will check the breaker itself, its bus connection, and the disconnect for heat damage.
- They will megohm or ohm-test the compressor windings (common, start, run) and test for a short to ground.
- They will test the run capacitor, or dual capacitor, against its microfarad (µF) rating.
- They will measure amp draw on the compressor, outdoor fan motor and each heat strip element, then compare it to the rated load amps and locked-rotor amps on the nameplate.
- They will inspect the contactor, the heat strip sequencers or relays, and all lugs for pitting or burn marks.
- On inverter models, they will read the inverter drive's fault history.
- If the compressor amps are high but the windings test fine, they will check pressures, the coil condition and the refrigerant charge.

A good quote names the specific failed part and the measurement behind it, such as "compressor grounded" or "capacitor reads 30 µF on a 45 µF rating." Be wary of a quote that just says "replace the unit."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breaker trips instantly when reset | Grounded compressor or shorted wiring | Keep the breaker off | Test windings to ground, inspect wiring |
| Outdoor unit hums, then shuts off or the breaker trips | Failed capacitor or locked compressor | Reset once only, then call | Replace the capacitor, add a hard-start kit, or assess the compressor |
| Air handler breaker trips in cold weather | Heat strip element, sequencer or terminal fault | Replace the filter, avoid Emergency Heat | Test the strips, replace the element, sequencer or wiring |
| Trips after long run times on hot days | High head pressure or a weak breaker | Clear debris around the outdoor unit | Clean the coil, check the charge, test or replace the breaker |
| Breaker or panel feels warm, burning smell, scorch marks | Loose or burned connection | Switch the breaker off and keep it off; if there is smoke, arcing or fire, leave and call 911 | Repair lugs, replace the breaker or disconnect |
| Outdoor fan not spinning before the trip | Fan motor or fan capacitor | None beyond clearing debris | Replace the motor or capacitor |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges and include parts and labor:

- **Service call and diagnosis:** $90–$200
- **Run capacitor (single or dual):** $150–$400
- **Hard-start kit:** $200–$450
- **Contactor:** $150–$400
- **Circuit breaker replacement (by an electrician or HVAC tech):** $150–$350
- **Disconnect box replacement:** $200–$450
- **Wiring or lug repair:** $100–$400
- **Heat strip element or sequencer:** $200–$700
- **Outdoor fan motor:** $350–$800
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800–$3,500. The parts warranty may cover the compressor, often 10 years on registered Lennox units. Terms vary by series and registration date, so check your model's warranty certificate. Labor and refrigerant usually are not covered. On older systems, replacing the outdoor unit is often the better value.
