## What this code means

"Fan runs but no cold air" is not a fault code. It is a symptom. Your Bryant indoor blower moves air through the vents, but that air is room temperature or warm, so the refrigeration side is not doing its job.

The most common reason is that the outdoor condensing unit is not running at all, or only partly running. Sometimes the outdoor fan spins but the compressor does not.

This page covers that "blower on, outdoor side off or not compressing" situation. Separate pages cover the other versions of this problem:

- **Weak but somewhat cool air:** see Bryant AC Not Cooling.
- **Visible ice on the indoor coil:** see Frozen Evaporator Coil.
- **Compressor hums but the outdoor fan blade is still:** see Outdoor Fan Not Spinning.

How the system behaves depends on the series:

- **Single-stage Legacy and Preferred units** have no self-diagnostics on the outdoor unit.
- **Evolution communicating systems** may show a fault on the Evolution Connex thermostat or flash a code on the outdoor board.
- **Compressor thermal overload trips** reset on their own once the compressor cools.
- **Control-board lockouts** on communicating units may hold until power is cycled.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Outdoor power interrupted.** The breaker has tripped, the outdoor disconnect is pulled or its fuses are blown, or the indoor/outdoor service switch is off.
2. **Failed run capacitor.** This is the most common component failure on Bryant condensers. The compressor and/or fan cannot start, the unit may hum, and the compressor trips on overload.
3. **Failed or pitted contactor.** The contactor does not pull in, so the outdoor unit gets no power even though the thermostat is calling for cooling.
4. **Thermostat or low-voltage control issue.** Possible problems include:
   - The thermostat is set to Fan On instead of Auto/Cool.
   - Dead batteries.
   - A broken Y (cooling) wire.
   - A blown 3-amp fuse on the indoor control board, often caused by a chafed thermostat wire.
5. **Safety switch open.** Two common examples:
   - A float switch has tripped because of a clogged condensate drain, which cuts the cooling call.
   - A high- or low-pressure switch has opened on units equipped with one.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** The compressor may run but produce little cooling. It may also short-cycle or stop on the low-pressure switch.
7. **Compressor failure or tripped internal overload.** Possible problems include a seized compressor, a grounded or open winding, or repeated overload trips caused by a dirty condenser coil or a bad capacitor.
8. **Communicating control fault (Evolution units only).** A communication loss between the thermostat and the outdoor unit, or a board fault, will be logged in the thermostat's service menu.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Check the thermostat.**
   - Set it to **Cool** and the fan to **Auto**.
   - Lower the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature.
   - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
   - On Evolution thermostats, note any alert message to give the technician.
2. **Check the breakers.** Find the AC/condenser breaker (usually a double-pole 240V breaker) and the furnace or air handler breaker. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a pro.
3. **Check the outdoor disconnect box.** Confirm the pull-out block or lever is fully in the ON position. Do not open the box beyond that.
4. **Replace a dirty air filter.** Severe restriction can lead to freezing and poor cooling.
5. **Check the condensate drain.** If the drain pan is full or a float switch has tripped, clear the drain line at its outdoor end or access port with a wet/dry vac. Cooling may resume once the switch resets.
6. **Seat the panels.** Make sure the indoor blower door is fully seated, because a loose door can break the circuit.
7. **Listen and look outside, and stop the system if something is wrong.** With cooling called for, watch the outdoor unit from a safe distance. If it hums or buzzes without running, or is silent, set the thermostat to **Off** to protect the compressor.
8. **Allow one reset.** If you suspect an overload trip, leave the system off for 30–60 minutes and try it once. Do not keep cycling it.

Leave anything inside the outdoor unit or the furnace cabinet to a technician. That includes capacitors, contactors, fuses, wiring and refrigerant. Capacitors hold a dangerous charge even when the power is off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent technician will typically work through these steps:

- **Confirm the cooling call.** They check for 24V across Y and C at the outdoor unit, then trace back to the indoor board, its 3A fuse and the thermostat wiring if voltage is missing.
- **Check line voltage.** They confirm 240V at the disconnect and at the contactor's line side, and test the disconnect fuses.
- **Inspect the contactor.** They check that it pulls in, measure voltage across its contacts, and look for pitting, burning or insect damage.
- **Test the capacitor.** They discharge it, then measure microfarads against the rating printed on it. A reading more than about 6% low means replacement, as does a bulging case.
- **Check the compressor.** They measure winding resistance (common, run and start) and test for ground. They also judge whether a hot compressor is simply sitting on its internal overload.
- **Check refrigerant charge** (once the unit runs). They use gauges and measure superheat or subcooling against Bryant's charging chart. If the charge is low, they should recommend finding the leak, not just topping it off.
- **Pull fault history** on Evolution units from the thermostat service menu or the outdoor board LEDs.

A quote that jumps straight to a new compressor or a full system replacement, with no capacitor test and no winding readings, deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outdoor unit completely silent | Tripped breaker, disconnect off, no 24V signal | Check the thermostat mode, reset the breaker once, confirm the disconnect is on | Test the disconnect fuses, contactor coil, 24V circuit and indoor board fuse |
| Outdoor unit hums or buzzes, nothing turns | Failed run capacitor or stuck compressor | Turn the thermostat Off | Test and replace the capacitor; check compressor windings and add a hard-start kit if appropriate |
| Outdoor fan spins, compressor silent | Bad capacitor (compressor side), overload trip, failed compressor | Turn Off, wait 30–60 min, try once | Test the capacitor and compressor windings, check the condenser coil |
| Unit runs, air only slightly cool | Low refrigerant, dirty coil, restriction | Replace the filter, check that vents are open | Gauge check, leak search, repair and recharge |
| Cooling stops, water near the indoor unit | Float switch tripped by a clogged drain | Clear the condensate line | Clean the drain pan and trap, verify the switch |
| Starts, then shuts off within minutes | Pressure switch trip, overheating compressor | Turn Off, replace the filter | Check charge, condenser fan and coil; read pressure switch status |
| Evolution thermostat shows an alert | Communication or board fault | Record the message, cycle power once | Pull fault history, check the data wiring and board |

## Repair costs

These are typical US installed prices, including the service call:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service/diagnostic call | $75–$200 |
| Breaker or thermostat fix (DIY) | $0–$10 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $120–$350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Indoor board fuse or thermostat wire repair | $100–$300 |
| Condensate drain clearing / float switch | $100–$250 |
| Hard-start kit | $150–$350 |
| Refrigerant leak search | $200–$600 |
| Leak repair plus recharge (R-410A or R-454B) | $400–$1,500+, depending on the leak location and the amount of refrigerant |
| Outdoor control board (Evolution) | $400–$900 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500–$3,000+ (lower if the parts warranty still covers it) |
| Condenser replacement | $3,500–$7,500+ |

Bryant's standard parts warranty is often 10 years when the unit is registered. Check your registration before approving a compressor or board replacement, because labor is usually not covered.
