## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on most American Standard air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your outdoor unit or air handler may be running, but the air coming from your vents is warm, weak, or barely cooler than room temperature.

On communicating **AccuLink**-equipped systems (many Gold and Platinum series units), the thermostat may display a numeric fault or a plain-language alert instead of just failing silently. Older single-stage units give you no code at all — you diagnose by symptom. Because behavior varies by model and control board, treat the steps below as a general roadmap rather than an exact match for every unit.

A "not cooling" complaint almost always traces back to one of three areas: **airflow**, **refrigerant**, or **the outdoor unit's electrical/mechanical components**.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — Restricts airflow, drops cooling output, and can freeze the coil. The single most common cause.
2. **Thermostat problem** — Dead batteries, wrong mode (set to "fan" or "heat"), or a miswired/failed thermostat.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — The outdoor unit or air handler has lost power on one circuit.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — A very common failure. The compressor and/or fan motor can't start, so the outdoor unit hums but doesn't cool.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — The outdoor unit can't reject heat, killing efficiency.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — Causes weak cooling and often a frozen indoor coil. Requires a licensed pro.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — Result of low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks cooling entirely.
8. **Failed contactor or condenser fan motor** — Outdoor unit doesn't energize or the fan won't spin.
9. **Failed compressor** — The most expensive outcome; the unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for a homeowner. If none fix it, call a licensed HVAC tech.

- **Check the air filter.** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of weak cooling and frozen coils.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **AUTO**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Check the breakers.** Look for a tripped breaker for both the indoor air handler and the outdoor condenser. Flip it fully OFF, then ON. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting it.
- **Check your vents and registers.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture, rugs, or closed doors.
- **Check the condensate drain.** A clogged drain can trigger a safety float switch that shuts the system down. Clear standing water from the drain pan if you can safely reach it.
- **Look for ice.** If the indoor coil or the copper lines at the outdoor unit are frosted, switch the system to **fan-only** for a few hours to thaw it before running cooling again.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verifies the complaint** and checks thermostat settings and call-for-cool signal (24V at the contactor).
2. **Inspects airflow** — filter, blower operation, and coil condition.
3. **Measures electrical values** — capacitor microfarads against the rating stamped on it, contactor operation, and voltage/amp draw on the compressor and fan.
4. **Reads AccuLink fault history** on communicating systems via the thermostat or a service tool.
5. **Attaches gauges** to read suction and head pressures, then calculates **superheat and subcooling** to judge refrigerant charge. This is where low refrigerant and leaks are confirmed.
6. **Leak-searches** with an electronic detector or dye if the charge is low — a proper repair finds and fixes the leak rather than just topping off.
7. **Tests the compressor** for shorted, open, or grounded windings if it won't start.

A tech quoting a "recharge" without mentioning leak detection is cutting a corner — refrigerant doesn't get used up; if it's low, it leaked out.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm air, indoor coil iced | Dirty filter or low airflow | Replace filter, thaw on fan-only | Verify blower, static pressure |
| Warm air, ice on outdoor lines | Low refrigerant / leak | Thaw system, then call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Bad fan motor or capacitor | Shut off, call pro | Replace fan motor/capacitor |
| Nothing runs outside | Tripped breaker/contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor & 24V signal |
| Thermostat blank/wrong mode | Dead batteries, wrong setting | Replace batteries, set to COOL | Rewire/replace thermostat |
| System shut off, water in pan | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain, empty pan | Clear line, test float switch |
| Runs constantly, no cooling | Failed compressor | None — call pro | Compressor test/replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included where noted:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** under $10 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$300
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type (older R-22 systems cost far more)
- **Coil replacement (evaporator/condenser):** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ — often the point where replacing the whole system makes more sense

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type, and whether your unit is still under American Standard's warranty (registered systems often carry 10-year parts coverage — labor is usually separate).

## Related codes

- **AC freezing up / iced coil** — closely related airflow and refrigerant issue.
- **Outdoor unit won't turn on** — contactor, capacitor, or breaker faults.
- **AccuLink communication fault** — control board or wiring issue on communicating Gold/Platinum systems.
- **AC short cycling** — often overlaps with capacitor, thermostat, or refrigerant problems.
- **Blower not running** — an air-handler airflow fault that mimics "not cooling."

*This page is general guidance. Diagnostics and part numbers vary by model and control board — always confirm against your unit's service literature or a licensed technician.*
