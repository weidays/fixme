## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a lit fault code on most American Standard systems — it's the symptom you notice: the system runs but your home won't reach the thermostat setpoint, or the air from the vents isn't cold. On communicating **AccuLink** systems, the thermostat may display a specific alert or error number that points to the real fault; on standard 24V systems there's no display, so you diagnose by behavior.

The underlying problem falls into one of three buckets: **airflow** (filters, coils, blower), **electrical** (capacitor, contactor, breaker, control board), or **refrigerant/compressor** (leaks, low charge, failed compressor). This page walks each in order of likelihood.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return** — restricts airflow, freezes the evaporator coil, and kills cooling.
2. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuse** feeding the outdoor unit — indoor blower runs, outdoor unit is dead.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or condenser fan hums but won't start.
4. **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil** — traps heat, cripples cooling capacity, especially in heat.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — gradual loss of cooling, possible ice on the lines. Requires a pro.
6. **Bad contactor** — pitted or stuck contacts prevent the outdoor unit from energizing.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; blows warm despite running.
8. **Thermostat problem or dead batteries** — wrong mode, wrong setpoint, or no call for cooling.
9. **Control board / AccuLink communication fault** — on communicating systems, a lost link can stop cooling.
10. **Failed compressor** — least common but most expensive; unit runs but never cools.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for a homeowner. Stop if a check requires opening the sealed electrical or refrigerant systems.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on AUTO. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check your panel for a tripped breaker labeled for the AC or air handler. Reset it **once**. If it trips again immediately, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for leaves, grass clippings, or debris packed against the condenser fins and clear them. Gently straighten bent fins with a fin comb if needed.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trigger a safety float switch that shuts the system down. Check for standing water in the drain pan and clear the drain line if you're comfortable doing so.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper refrigerant lines or coil, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** for a few hours to thaw it, then call a technician — icing points to airflow or refrigerant issues.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheap to expensive, and their steps should match your quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply and return air temperature; a proper split is roughly 15–20°F.
2. **Checks airflow** — filter, coil, blower operation.
3. **Confirms 24V call for cooling** at the outdoor unit and inspects the **contactor** for pitting or stuck contacts.
4. **Tests the capacitor** with a meter against its rated microfarads — a weak cap is a top find.
5. **Reads AccuLink diagnostics** on communicating systems for board or sensor faults.
6. **Measures refrigerant pressures** with gauges and checks superheat/subcooling to judge charge and hunt leaks.
7. **Tests the compressor** electrically if it won't start after cap and contactor check out.

If a quote jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without checking the filter, capacitor, and pressures, ask questions.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blower runs, air not cold | Dirty filter / frozen coil | Replace filter, thaw coil with fan ON | Diagnose airflow or low charge |
| Indoor runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker / blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Replace fuse, find cause of trip |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call a pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Weak cooling, warm outdoor coil | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris, rinse gently | Deep coil cleaning |
| Gradual loss of cooling, iced lines | Low refrigerant leak | Turn off, thaw | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Nothing happens on cooling call | Bad contactor / thermostat | Check tstat mode & batteries | Replace contactor or tstat |
| Alert on AccuLink thermostat | Communication / board fault | Note the code, cycle power once | Diagnose board/wiring |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Failing compressor | None — call a pro | Test and replace compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 pro
- **Capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Control board / AccuLink module:** $400–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+, often making replacement of an older system worth considering

Prices vary by region, system age, and refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older R-22 units cost more to recharge).
