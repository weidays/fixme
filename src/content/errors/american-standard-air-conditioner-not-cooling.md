## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom homeowners report most often. Your American Standard air conditioner may be running (the outdoor unit hums, the indoor blower moves air) but the air coming from your vents is warm or only mildly cool, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

On systems with a communicating control board (many American Standard Gold and Platinum series with the AccuLink / ComfortLink platform), the thermostat may display a fault or a specific alert instead of a warm-air complaint. On older single-stage Silver-series units there's usually no code at all — just poor performance. Because the diagnostic path depends on your specific board and compressor type, treat the steps below as a general framework and confirm specifics against your unit's service label.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant. A frozen coil blows warm air because ice blocks heat transfer.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the outdoor unit hums but the fan or compressor won't start. Very common and relatively cheap.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system runs constantly but can't cool. Requires a certified tech; topping off without repair is illegal and temporary.
5. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection.
6. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — indoor unit runs, outdoor unit is dead.
7. **Failed condenser fan motor** — unit overheats and shuts down on high pressure.
8. **Contactor or capacitor relay failure** — the outdoor unit never energizes.
9. **Thermostat problem or wiring fault** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a broken low-voltage wire.
10. **Failed compressor** — least common but most expensive; unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all safe for a homeowner. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass safety switches, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting off.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker feeding the air handler and the outdoor unit. Reset **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser and gently hose off the exterior fins (power off first). Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan; drain tablets help prevent clogs.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or refrigerant lines, turn the system to OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw fully before running again.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician follows a repeatable sequence — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Confirms the complaint** and checks the thermostat call for cooling and the mode.
2. **Measures airflow and filter/coil condition** at the air handler.
3. **Checks electrical:** voltage at the contactor, capacitor microfarad reading (measured, not guessed), and amp draw on the fan and compressor.
4. **Attaches gauges to read refrigerant pressures** and measures the temperature split across the coil (typically 15–22°F on a healthy system). This tells them if refrigerant is low.
5. **If refrigerant is low, performs a leak search** — a legitimate tech won't just add refrigerant without finding the leak.
6. **On communicating systems,** reads any fault history stored on the board.

Be cautious if a tech proposes a refrigerant recharge without any leak test, or a compressor replacement without confirming the capacitor and contactor first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, dirty filter | Restricted airflow | Replace filter | Verify airflow, clean coils |
| Ice on indoor coil/lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw fully | Find cause; leak test |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan won't spin | Failed capacitor | None — power risk | Test & replace capacitor |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant / leak | None | Leak search & repair, recharge |
| Outdoor unit dead, indoor runs | Tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Warm air, dirty outdoor coil | Blocked heat rejection | Hose off fins | Chemical coil cleaning |
| No cooling, water near air handler | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear drain, tablets | Clear drain, test float switch |
| Loud outdoor unit, then shutdown | Bad fan motor / high pressure | None | Replace condenser fan motor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$700
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250
- **Coil cleaning (condenser or evaporator):** $150–$500
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 (higher if it's an evaporator coil leak)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,200
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth weighing against a full system replacement on older units)

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs R-22 legacy systems), and whether your unit is under warranty. American Standard offers registered-warranty coverage on parts for many models — check your registration before paying out of pocket.

## Related codes

- **AC freezing up / iced coil** — closely related low-airflow or low-refrigerant issue.
- **Outdoor fan not spinning** — usually the capacitor or fan motor.
- **AccuLink / ComfortLink communication fault** — thermostat-to-board errors on Gold and Platinum series.
- **Compressor won't start / hard-start** — capacitor, contactor, or compressor windings.
- **High head pressure lockout** — dirty coil or failed condenser fan on units with pressure switches.
