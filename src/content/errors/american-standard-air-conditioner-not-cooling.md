## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your American Standard air conditioner runs but the air coming from your vents is warm, weak, or barely there. On newer American Standard systems with an AccuLink/ComfortLink II thermostat, the thermostat may display a specific fault or alert rather than a number; on older 24V systems there's no display at all, just poor performance.

Because "not cooling" can stem from anything from a $20 filter to a $1,800 compressor, this page walks you through the safe checks you can do yourself and how a technician should diagnose the rest — so you can sanity-check any quote you get.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil. The single most common cause.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so no cold air reaches you.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a failed thermostat that isn't calling for cooling.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the outdoor fan or compressor hums but won't start, or starts and stops.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
6. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is caked with dirt/grass, so it can't reject heat.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling and can freeze the coil. This is a repair, not a top-off.
8. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the compressor doesn't engage.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive outcome; the outdoor unit runs the fan but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should attempt. Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of weak cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. If it has batteries, replace them.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler and the outdoor unit. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure vents aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers, and that return grilles are clear.
- **Frozen coil / ice:** If you see ice on the refrigerant line or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF and set the fan to ON to let it thaw for a few hours before running again.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan; a wet/dry vac on the outdoor drain line end can clear a simple clog.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser so it can breathe. Do not spray water into the electrical components.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work methodically — this list helps you tell a thorough diagnosis from a guess:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** at the thermostat and confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor contactor.
2. **Check line power** and measure amp draw on the compressor and condenser fan.
3. **Test the capacitor** with a meter — a weak or bulging capacitor is a common, cheap fix.
4. **Inspect the contactor** for pitting or failure to pull in.
5. **Measure airflow and check the evaporator coil** for ice or dirt; inspect the blower.
6. **Attach gauges to read refrigerant pressures** and calculate superheat/subcooling. Low charge points to a leak — they should locate it, not just add refrigerant.
7. **On AccuLink/ComfortLink II systems,** connect to the control to read stored fault history.
8. **Assess the compressor** last, since it's the costliest component. A good tech confirms it's truly failed before quoting a replacement.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace the filter | Check duct/blower if new filter doesn't fix it |
| Ice on lines or coil | Frozen evaporator | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Find low airflow or low-charge cause |
| No air at all | Thermostat/battery, breaker | Replace batteries, reset breaker once | Test thermostat and low-voltage circuit |
| Outdoor fan hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — has stored charge | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker/fuse/contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, transformer, wiring |
| Runs but no cooling, warm outdoor air | Low refrigerant / compressor | None | Leak search, repair, recharge or compressor |
| Water pooling, unit shuts off | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain line | Clear drain, test float switch |
| Outdoor coil dirty/hot | Blocked condenser | Clear debris, gently rinse fins | Deep coil cleaning |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 (more for AccuLink/ComfortLink II)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,800+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,800+ (often worth comparing against a new unit if out of warranty)

Note: American Standard's registered limited warranty may cover parts like the compressor for eligible units — check your registration before paying for parts.

## Related codes

- **AccuLink/ComfortLink II thermostat alerts** — model-specific fault messages for airflow, refrigerant, and communication faults
- **Outdoor unit not turning on** — capacitor, contactor, or breaker issues
- **AC frozen / ice on lines** — airflow and refrigerant charge problems
- **AC short cycling** — capacitor, thermostat, or overcharge/undercharge

*If your thermostat shows a specific alert code, look up that exact code — behavior and displayed messages vary by control board and thermostat generation.*
