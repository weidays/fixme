## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific fault code. Your American Standard air conditioner may be running (fan spinning, thermostat calling) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches setpoint. On systems with a communicating **AccuLink** thermostat, you may also see an on-screen alert or a flashing diagnostic LED on the outdoor control board.

Because American Standard builds single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed (Platinum series) equipment, the exact behavior and any board LED codes vary by model. This page covers the general causes that apply across the lineup, and flags where diagnosis depends on your specific unit.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return airflow** — the single most common cause of weak or no cooling.
2. **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil** — grass, leaves, and dirt insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; the coil turns to a block of ice and airflow drops to nearly nothing.
4. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a wiring/communication fault (common on AccuLink setups).
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums or won't start. Very common failure part.
6. **Bad contactor** — the outdoor unit doesn't energize at all.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system cools weakly, then not at all, and often freezes up. Requires EPA-certified repair.
8. **Failed compressor** — the outdoor unit runs the fan but the compressor is silent, tripping, or drawing high amps.
9. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor disconnect or panel breaker.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should perform:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on AUTO. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A dirty filter alone can stop cooling and freeze the coil.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed dampers aren't blocking airflow. Open all supply registers.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker (indoor air handler/furnace **and** outdoor AC). Reset **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Outdoor unit:** Visually check the condenser coil for grass clippings, cottonwood, or leaves. Clear away debris and cut back plants at least 2 feet on all sides. Never spray a live unit's electronics — clean gently with the power off.
- **Condensate drain:** If the indoor drain pan is full or overflowing, a float switch may have shut the system off. Clear standing water and check that the drain line isn't clogged.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** but leave the fan on to thaw it (can take several hours). Ice means a real problem — get it diagnosed.

Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch. Those steps require a licensed technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Confirm the complaint** and verify thermostat call and mode.
2. **Check airflow** — filter, blower operation, and static pressure.
3. **Measure electrical** — line voltage, capacitor microfarads, contactor pull-in, and compressor/fan amp draw against nameplate.
4. **Read board diagnostics** — on communicating (AccuLink) systems they'll pull fault history from the control; on standard units they'll interpret LED flash codes if equipped.
5. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and take temperatures to calculate **superheat and subcooling** — this tells them whether refrigerant charge is correct without guessing.
6. **Leak search** if the charge is low (electronic detector or dye), because just "topping off" without finding the leak is bad practice.
7. **Compressor test** — resistance, amp draw, and start behavior if the compressor is suspect.

Be wary of any quote to "add a pound of Freon" without a leak search, or to replace the compressor without electrical and pressure readings first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check blower & static pressure |
| Ice on copper lines/coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn to OFF, run fan to thaw | Find airflow issue or leak, recharge |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Failed run capacitor | None | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit totally dead | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Cools weakly then quits | Refrigerant leak | None | Leak search + EPA-certified recharge |
| Fan spins, compressor silent/humming | Failed compressor or hard start | None | Amp/electrical test, compressor eval |
| No response at thermostat | Dead batteries / comms fault | Replace batteries | Diagnose control wiring / AccuLink |
| Indoor drain pan full, unit off | Clogged condensate line | Clear standing water | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement on older units)
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90–$200, often applied to the repair

Costs vary by region, refrigerant type (older R-410A vs. newer refrigerants), and whether your unit is under American Standard's parts warranty.

## Related codes

- **AC frozen / iced-up coil** — closely related; often the same root cause as not cooling.
- **AC not turning on** — when the unit is completely dead rather than running warm.
- **AccuLink communication fault** — on Platinum series communicating systems.
- **Outdoor fan not spinning** — a specific subset that points to capacitor or motor failure.
