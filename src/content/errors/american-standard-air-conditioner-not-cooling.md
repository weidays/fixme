## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific error code — it's the symptom you notice when your American Standard air conditioner is running (or trying to) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

American Standard equipment ranges from basic single-stage **Silver series** condensers to communicating **Platinum series** variable-speed systems. On Platinum systems paired with an **AccuLink** or **comfort control** thermostat, actual fault codes may be stored and displayed. On most Silver and Gold single-stage units there is **no fault code at all** — you diagnose by symptom and measurement. Because of that, this page walks the symptom, not a code number.

If you smell burning, see smoke, or hear a loud repeated buzzing/tripping from the outdoor unit, shut the system off at the breaker and call a technician before doing anything else.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so no cold air reaches the vents.
3. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — undercharge means poor or no cooling. This is a repair job, not a top-off.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common outdoor-unit failure; the compressor or fan hums but won't start.
5. **Bad contactor** — the relay that sends power to the compressor pits or sticks, so the outdoor unit won't energize.
6. **Failed condenser fan motor** — outdoor fan doesn't spin, unit overheats and shuts down on high pressure.
7. **Thermostat problem or dead batteries** — wrong mode, wrong setpoint, or a comms fault on communicating systems.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — power to the condenser or air handler is cut.
9. **Dirty condenser coil** — outdoor coil caked with dirt/cottonwood can't reject heat, killing capacity.
10. **Failed compressor** — least common but most expensive; the unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or handle wiring inside the panels.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and look at it against a light. If it's gray or clogged, replace it. This alone fixes a huge share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor air handler/furnace breaker and the outdoor condenser breaker. Reset **once** if tripped. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Frozen coil check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and set the fan to **On** for a few hours to thaw. Don't chip the ice.
- **Condensate:** A full drain pan or clogged condensate line can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Look for standing water near the indoor unit.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass and debris away from the condenser and gently rinse the outside of the coil with a garden hose (power off).

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech confirms the root cause with measurements — not guesswork. Use this to sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** and check thermostat mode, setpoint and call for cooling.
2. **Read stored codes** on communicating Platinum/AccuLink systems; on single-stage units, they go straight to measurements.
3. **Check airflow and the indoor coil** for ice or restriction.
4. **Measure voltage** at the contactor and test the **capacitor** with a meter (a swollen or out-of-spec cap is an easy find).
5. **Check the contactor and condenser fan motor** operation.
6. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and measure **superheat/subcooling** and temperature split to judge charge — and, if low, perform a **leak search** rather than just adding refrigerant.
7. **Test the compressor** windings and amp draw if everything upstream checks out.

Be wary of any quote that adds refrigerant without mentioning a leak search, or that recommends a compressor replacement without capacitor, contactor and charge tests first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/no airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Check blower, coil, ductwork |
| Ice on coil or lines | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, thaw with fan on | Find cause, repair leak/airflow |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Bad capacitor or fan motor | None | Test and replace cap/motor |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None | Replace capacitor, verify start |
| Outdoor unit dead, no power | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Runs constantly, poor cooling | Low refrigerant / dirty coil | Rinse outdoor coil | Leak search, charge, clean coil |
| No cold air, running fine | Failed compressor | None | Compressor/system replacement |
| Blank thermostat | Dead batteries or comms fault | Replace batteries | Diagnose control wiring |
| Water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate, float trip | Clear visible drain | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Regional rates and system size vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY).
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400.
- **Contactor:** $150–$350.
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$700.
- **Condensate line clearing:** $75–$250.
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,800; often the point where whole-system replacement makes more sense on older units.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $75–$200, often credited toward the repair.

## Related codes

- **AC frozen coil / ice on lines** — closely tied to low airflow and low refrigerant.
- **AC won't turn on** — power, contactor and thermostat faults.
- **AC short cycling** — capacitor, overcharge or control issues.
- **AccuLink / comfort control communication fault** — on Platinum communicating systems, blocks normal cooling operation.
- **High-pressure lockout** — dirty condenser coil or failed fan motor causing shutdown.
