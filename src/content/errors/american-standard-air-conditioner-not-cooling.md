## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom rather than a specific fault code on American Standard systems. Your air conditioner may be running, blowing air, or even showing no error at all — but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

On older American Standard units with a standard 24V thermostat, there is usually no on-board diagnostic display, so you're troubleshooting by symptom. On newer communicating systems using the **AccuLink** control platform (paired with a compatible communicating thermostat), you may see a specific alert or fault message on the thermostat screen — the exact wording and codes vary by board and thermostat firmware, so read what your screen actually shows rather than assuming.

This is rated **pro** because the most common lasting causes — refrigerant loss, compressor and electrical faults — require licensed tools and EPA certification to fix legally and safely.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, can freeze the indoor coil and choke cooling. Most common and cheapest.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — caused by low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, dust and cottonwood clog the fins so the unit can't reject heat.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a weak or bulged capacitor stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — refrigerant doesn't get "used up," so low charge always means a leak somewhere.
6. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit can pit or stick, cutting power to the compressor.
7. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a wiring/communication fault (especially on AccuLink systems).
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
9. **Failed compressor** — the least common but most expensive; the unit hums but won't cool.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for a homeowner. Stop at anything requiring the panel to come off the electrical or refrigerant components.

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it takes them and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breaker** — check the electrical panel and the outdoor disconnect. If a breaker is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting it.
- **Vents and registers** — make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain** — if there's a full drip pan or a float switch has shut the system down, clearing a clogged drain line can restore operation.
- **Outdoor unit** — clear leaves, grass clippings and debris from around the condenser and gently rinse loose dirt off the exterior fins with a garden hose (power off first). Check for ice on the lines — if the unit is iced over, turn the system to **FAN ONLY** for a few hours to thaw before calling.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician follows a logical path — this list helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm the complaint** and check thermostat call for cooling and airflow at the vents.
2. **Inspect the filter and indoor coil** for restriction or ice.
3. **Measure temperature split** (return vs. supply air) — a healthy system drops roughly 15–20°F.
4. **Check outdoor power** at the disconnect and contactor, and test the **capacitor** with a meter (microfarad reading).
5. **Inspect the contactor** for pitting and confirm the compressor and fan energize.
6. **Attach gauges and measure refrigerant pressures and superheat/subcooling** — this tells them if the charge is correct.
7. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — electronic detector, dye, or soap on fittings.
8. On AccuLink systems, they'll read fault history from the thermostat/control board.

Be cautious of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search, or to "replace the compressor" without ruling out the capacitor and contactor first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check coil for resulting ice |
| Ice on indoor coil or lines | Low airflow or low charge | Turn to FAN, thaw fully | Diagnose airflow vs. refrigerant |
| Outdoor unit runs, air not cold | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse fins, clear debris | Deep coil cleaning, pressure check |
| Outdoor fan/compressor won't start, humming | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Runs constantly, never cools enough | Low refrigerant (leak) | None | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor unit dead, indoor blows | Tripped breaker/contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Blank/wrong thermostat, no call | Thermostat/batteries | Replace batteries, set to COOL | Repair wiring/communication fault |
| Loud hum, no cooling, coils clean | Failed compressor | None | Compressor or system replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 professional
- **Capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 (communicating AccuLink thermostats cost more)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ — often the point where replacing the whole outdoor unit makes more sense, especially on older R-22 systems

Diagnostic/service call fees typically run $75–$200 and are often credited toward the repair.

## Related codes

- **AC frozen / iced over** — airflow or refrigerant problem, closely tied to "not cooling."
- **Outdoor unit not turning on** — power, contactor or capacitor faults.
- **AC blowing warm air** — overlapping cause list; check filter and refrigerant.
- **AccuLink communication fault** — thermostat-to-board communication loss on communicating systems (fault wording varies by model).
- **Short cycling** — unit starts and stops rapidly; can accompany capacitor or thermostat issues.
