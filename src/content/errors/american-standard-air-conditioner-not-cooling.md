## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific fault code. American Standard air conditioners generally do not flash numeric error codes at the outdoor unit; most fault information lives in the indoor control board, the communicating thermostat (like the AccuLink or Nexia-enabled models), or a variable-speed inverter board on Platinum-series units.

When your American Standard AC runs but the house won't cool, the system is failing somewhere in the chain: the thermostat calls for cooling, the indoor blower moves air, the outdoor condenser and compressor reject heat, and refrigerant carries heat outside. A breakdown at any point produces the same complaint — warm or weak airflow. Because the causes range from a $15 filter to a $2,500 compressor, and diagnosis usually requires gauges and meters, this is rated a **pro** repair once the safe checks below are exhausted.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setpoint, or a communication fault on AccuLink/Nexia systems.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler loses power while the thermostat still shows a call.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan won't start; you may hear humming or clicking.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces or eliminates cooling; requires EPA-certified repair.
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or burns out.
8. **Condensate safety switch tripped** — a full drain pan shuts the system down on many installs.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor or blower motor** — no airflow across a coil means no heat transfer.
10. **Failed compressor** — the least common but most expensive; the system runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do not open the electrical compartments, touch refrigerant lines, or bypass any switch.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the single most common cause.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it uses them.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler or outdoor unit. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs; closed returns starve the system.
- **Outdoor unit clearance:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser so it can breathe. Never spray high-pressure water into the fins.
- **Condensate drain:** If your system has a float switch and it tripped, a clogged drain line is likely. If you can see standing water in the drain pan, the unit may have shut itself off intentionally — call a pro to clear the line safely.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant line or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and run the **fan only** to thaw it. Do not run cooling until it's fully thawed.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech typically works the chain in order so you can sanity-check the invoice:

1. **Confirms the call and power** — verifies the thermostat is sending a cooling signal and that 24V control power and line voltage are present.
2. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, blower operation, and coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Reads the outdoor unit** — measures capacitor microfarads, tests the contactor, and checks that the compressor and condenser fan start and draw normal amperage.
4. **Puts gauges on the system** — measures suction and liquid pressures and superheat/subcooling to determine if refrigerant charge is correct or if there's a leak.
5. **Leak search if low** — uses electronic detectors or dye; a proper repair fixes the leak before recharging, not just "topping off."
6. **Checks controls/board** — on communicating Platinum or Gold systems, reads fault history from the thermostat or inverter board.

Be cautious of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without a documented pressure reading and leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Verify airflow, inspect coil |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries / no power | Replace batteries, check breaker | Test 24V transformer & wiring |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor & line voltage |
| Humming, fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — leave sealed panel closed | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on lines/coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Fan-only to thaw, replace filter | Find leak, correct charge |
| Runs constantly, no cooling | Low refrigerant / compressor | None | Gauge readings, leak search |
| System shut off, water in pan | Clogged condensate drain | Do not bypass float switch | Clear drain line safely |
| Loud outdoor unit, no cold air | Failed compressor | None | Test windings, quote replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Regional labor rates and communicating vs. conventional equipment affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $10 – $40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 – $15 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor:** $150 – $300
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100 – $250
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350 – $700
- **Blower motor (ECM/variable-speed):** $500 – $1,200
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300 – $1,500+ depending on leak location
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500 – $2,500+ (often worth comparing against system replacement if out of warranty)

Note: American Standard equipment often carries a registered parts warranty. If your unit is under 10 years old and was registered, the part may be free — but labor and refrigerant usually are not.
