## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific numeric fault code — it's the symptom most American Standard air conditioner owners describe when the system runs but the house won't reach setpoint, or when the outdoor unit won't start at all. American Standard's higher-end AccuLink (communicating) systems paired with a compatible thermostat may display a text alert or store a fault in the control board, while older non-communicating single-stage units give no code at all.

Because "not cooling" can stem from anything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, this page walks you from the cheapest, safest checks up to the pro-only repairs so you can sanity-check any quote you get.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — restricts return air, freezes the indoor coil, and kills cooling capacity.
2. **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil** — grass, cottonwood and dirt insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or fan hums but won't spin.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — long cooling cycles, warm air, ice on the lineset or coil.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
7. **Condenser fan motor failure** — outdoor fan won't spin, unit overheats and shuts off on high pressure.
8. **Thermostat, wiring, or communication fault** — especially on AccuLink systems that lose the link between components.
9. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from airflow or refrigerant problems; blows lukewarm air.
10. **Compressor failure** — the costliest cause, usually last on the list.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or panels off:

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to COOL and lower the setpoint 3–5°F below room temp. Confirm it's not in HEAT, FAN-ONLY, or a schedule/hold. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor AC breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs, and the return grille is clear.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear grass, leaves and debris away from the condenser and gently rinse the outside fins with a garden hose (power off first). Keep at least two feet of clearance.
- **Condensate:** If your unit has a float switch or the drain is clogged, a full drain pan can shut cooling down. Check for standing water near the indoor unit.

Do **not** remove electrical panels, touch the capacitor, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch. Those are pro jobs.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician follows a logical path — knowing it helps you spot a shortcut or upsell:

1. **Verifies the complaint** and checks thermostat call, filter, and airflow.
2. **Measures voltage** at the disconnect and contactor, and tests the capacitor with a meter (a weak capacitor often reads well below its rated µF).
3. **Checks the contactor** for pitting and proper pull-in.
4. **Confirms the condenser fan and compressor** attempt to start, listening for hum or overload trips.
5. **Puts gauges on the system** to read suction and head pressures, then calculates superheat/subcooling to judge the refrigerant charge — this is where a leak shows up.
6. **Inspects both coils** for dirt or ice.
7. **On AccuLink systems,** reads stored faults and confirms communication between the thermostat, air handler and outdoor board.

A trustworthy quote names the specific failed part and the reading that proves it — not just "needs refrigerant."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm airflow, house won't cool | Dirty filter or blocked vents | Replace filter, open vents | Check airflow, static pressure |
| Outdoor unit silent, no fan or compressor | Tripped breaker or bad contactor/capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, capacitor, wiring |
| Compressor hums but won't start | Failed run/start capacitor | None — pro job | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor fan won't spin, unit shuts off | Condenser fan motor or capacitor | Power off, clear debris | Replace fan motor/capacitor |
| Ice on lineset or indoor coil | Low refrigerant or airflow restriction | Turn off AC, run fan to thaw | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Long cycles, warm air, no ice | Low refrigerant / dirty condenser | Rinse outdoor coil | Leak search, charge, coil clean |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries or low-voltage fuse | Replace batteries | Check 24V transformer, fuse, wiring |
| System runs, breaker trips repeatedly | Compressor or electrical fault | Stop resetting; call pro | Amp-draw test, compressor diagnosis |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250 as part of a service call.
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400 installed.
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650 installed.
- **Low-voltage fuse / transformer:** $75–$250.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type.
- **TXV / metering device:** $500–$900.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against a new system on older units).

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older R-22 systems), and whether the part is under American Standard's parts warranty — which covers parts but usually not labor.

## Related codes

- **AccuLink communication fault** — thermostat loses link with the outdoor or indoor unit.
- **Outdoor unit locked out on high/low pressure** — often points to airflow, coil or refrigerant problems.
- **Frozen evaporator coil** — closely tied to "not cooling" and low airflow.
- **Blower not running (indoor)** — no airflow means no cooling even if the outdoor unit works.
- **Compressor overload trip** — the last-resort electrical protection when the compressor struggles.

If your system shows a specific numeric or text fault on an AccuLink thermostat, note it exactly before calling — it speeds up diagnosis and helps you verify the technician's findings.
