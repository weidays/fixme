## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom homeowners describe when an American Standard air conditioner runs but the home never reaches the set temperature, or the air from the vents isn't cold. On American Standard systems with AccuLink or ComfortLink controls, an actual fault may be logged and readable at a compatible thermostat, but many single- and two-stage systems use simple 24V controls that give no code at all.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a clogged filter to a failed compressor, the goal is to narrow it down: is the outdoor unit running? Is air moving indoors? Is the air cold at all, or just weak? The answers point to very different repairs and costs.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause of weak cooling; restricts airflow and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), or a miswired/failed thermostat, especially after a recent replacement.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — dirt, grass and cottonwood block heat rejection, so the system runs but can't cool effectively.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common outdoor failure; the compressor or fan hums but won't start, or trips the breaker.
5. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper line or no cold air.
6. **Low refrigerant / leak** — undercharge from a slow leak reduces capacity. Requires EPA-certified repair.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit won't run at all.
8. **Failed contactor or control board** — the outdoor unit gets no start signal.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive outcome; the unit runs the fan but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening the sealed refrigerant system or bypassing a safety.

- **Filter:** Pull and inspect the air filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it and give the system time to recover.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit clearance:** Clear leaves, grass and debris from around the condenser and gently rinse the exterior fins with a garden hose (power off).
- **Condensate drain:** Check for standing water at the indoor unit. A full drain pan or clogged line can trigger a safety float switch and stop cooling. Clearing a visible clog is fine; drain-line tablets help prevent it.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or coil, turn the system to **Fan only** and let it thaw fully before restarting. Persistent icing means call a tech.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirms the complaint** and checks thermostat call, mode and wiring.
2. **Measures airflow and static pressure**, inspects filter and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Checks electrical:** capacitor microfarad reading, contactor condition, and voltage/amp draw at the compressor and fan.
4. **Reads controls:** on AccuLink/ComfortLink systems, pulls stored fault data; on standard systems, verifies 24V signals.
5. **Takes refrigerant readings:** connects gauges to measure pressures, then calculates superheat and subcooling to determine if the charge is correct and whether there's a leak.
6. **Inspects the condenser coil** for airflow and cleanliness.
7. **Tests the compressor** for windings, start ability and mechanical condition if all else checks out.

A good tech should show you the measured readings — capacitor value, subcooling, amp draw — not just quote a part.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter, let system recover | Check coil/blower if unresolved |
| Nothing cold, wrong setting | Thermostat mode/batteries | Set to Cool/Auto, replace batteries | Test/replace thermostat, verify wiring |
| Outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker/fuse | Reset breaker once | Find cause of trip; replace fuse/contactor |
| Fan hums, won't spin | Failed capacitor | None — high voltage | Test and replace run capacitor |
| Ice on coil/lines | Low airflow or low charge | Fan-only to thaw, replace filter | Diagnose airflow vs refrigerant leak |
| Runs constantly, poor cooling | Dirty condenser or low charge | Rinse outdoor fins, hose off debris | Clean coil, check charge, find leak |
| Water/full drain pan | Clogged condensate line | Clear visible clog, add tablets | Clear line, service float switch |
| Fan runs, zero cooling | Compressor failure | None | Test compressor, quote repair/replace |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts and labor):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$450.
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300.
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$250.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500, depending on leak location.
- **Control board (AccuLink/ComfortLink):** $400–$900.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+, often making replacement of an older unit worth considering.

Prices vary by region, unit stage (single, two-stage, variable-speed), and whether the system is still under American Standard's warranty — registered units often carry longer parts coverage.

## Related codes

- **AC freezing up / iced coil** — closely related airflow and refrigerant issue.
- **Outdoor unit won't start** — capacitor, contactor or breaker faults.
- **AC short cycling** — overheating, dirty coil or control fault.
- **AccuLink / ComfortLink communication faults** — control-level errors on variable-speed systems.
- **Blower not running** — indoor airflow loss that mimics "not cooling."
