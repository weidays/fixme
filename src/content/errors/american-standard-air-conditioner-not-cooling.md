## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code — it's the symptom you'll see most often on an American Standard air conditioner: the system runs (or tries to) but your home never reaches setpoint, or the air from the vents is warm.

On basic Silver and Gold series systems with a conventional thermostat, there is no diagnostic display, so you're troubleshooting behavior directly. On Platinum series systems paired with an **AccuLink™ / ComfortLink™ II** communicating thermostat, the thermostat may show an alert or fault message that points to a specific subsystem. **What the thermostat reports varies by model and firmware**, so treat any on-screen message as a starting clue, not a final diagnosis.

The root cause falls into one of three buckets: **airflow** (filter, coil, blower), **electrical** (breaker, capacitor, contactor, control board), or **refrigerant** (a leak or restriction). This page walks each one.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return** — the single most common reason for weak or no cooling. Restricted airflow can also freeze the indoor coil into a block of ice.
2. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuse** at the outdoor unit — the indoor blower runs but the condenser doesn't.
3. **Failed run capacitor** — a bulged or dead capacitor stops the compressor and/or fan motor from starting. Very common on units 6+ years old.
4. **Burned or stuck contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit fails to close, so the condenser never energizes.
5. **Frozen indoor coil (evaporator)** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines and no cold air.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes gradual loss of cooling and often coil icing. Requires a certified tech; **never** just "top it off."
7. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — mats down heat rejection, so the system runs constantly but can't cool.
8. **Thermostat fault or dead batteries** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a wiring/communication issue (especially on communicating ComfortLink systems).
9. **Failed compressor or fan motor** — the outdoor unit hums, trips the breaker, or the fan won't spin.
10. **Control/communication board fault** — less common; more likely on Platinum communicating systems where the thermostat and outdoor board must talk.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe. Stop at anything requiring you to open sealed compartments, touch capacitors, or handle refrigerant.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the fan to **AUTO**, and setpoint several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers in your panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Make sure the pull-out disconnect box near the condenser is fully seated (it can be bumped loose by landscaping).
- **Vents and registers:** Confirm supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain:** If cooling stopped and you see a full drain pan or a tripped float switch, the drain line may be clogged. You can clear a visible clog and pour drain tablets in the pan.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper lines, turn the system to **FAN ONLY** to thaw it (can take a few hours), then replace the filter. Persistent icing means call a pro — it usually signals low refrigerant.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm the symptom and check airflow** — filter, blower operation, and static pressure.
2. **Verify the call for cooling** reaches the outdoor unit (24V at the contactor). On communicating systems they'll check the thermostat-to-board link.
3. **Measure the capacitor** with a meter and inspect the contactor for pitting or welding.
4. **Test the compressor and fan motor** for proper amp draw and start.
5. **Take refrigerant readings** — superheat and subcooling — to determine if the charge is correct or if there's a leak. If low, they'll perform a **leak search** (dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen pressure test).
6. **Inspect the coils** indoor and out for cleanliness and ice.

A good tech shows you the measured numbers (capacitor microfarads, pressures, amps) rather than just guessing. If someone wants to add refrigerant without finding the leak, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter / blocked return | Replace filter, open vents | Check blower, clean coil |
| Indoor blower runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuse | Reset breaker once, seat disconnect | Find electrical fault, test contactor |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — capacitor holds charge | Discharge and replace capacitor |
| Condenser never energizes | Burned/stuck contactor | None | Replace contactor, verify 24V signal |
| Ice on coil or copper lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Set to FAN, thaw, replace filter | Leak search, recharge, coil check |
| Gradual loss of cooling over weeks | Refrigerant leak | None — EPA regulated | Locate/repair leak, evacuate, recharge |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Dirty condenser coil | Gently rinse outdoor coil with hose | Deep coil clean, check charge |
| Nothing responds at all | Dead thermostat / no power | Replace batteries, check breaker | Test transformer, wiring, board |
| Outdoor fan won't spin | Failed fan motor or capacitor | None | Replace motor/capacitor |
| Breaker trips instantly on start | Shorted compressor | Leave breaker off | Test compressor, likely replace |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY, free labor).
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY).
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 for a service call.
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400 installed.
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$300.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs older R-22 is far pricier).
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650 installed.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500 — often the point where replacing the whole outdoor unit or system makes more sense, especially on older units.
- **Control/communication board (Platinum systems):** $400–$900.

Get itemized quotes. If a system is 12+ years old and needs a compressor or major refrigerant work, ask about full replacement cost before committing.

## Related codes

- **Frozen evaporator coil** — the icing pattern that often accompanies "not cooling."
- **AccuLink / ComfortLink II communication faults** — thermostat-to-board errors on Platinum systems.
- **Outdoor unit not running** — the electrical subset of this symptom.
- **AC short cycling** — related but distinct: the system starts and stops rapidly instead of failing to cool.
- **Condensate float switch tripped** — a safety cutoff that stops cooling when the drain backs up.
