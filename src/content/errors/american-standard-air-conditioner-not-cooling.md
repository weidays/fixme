## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on most American Standard air conditioners — it's the symptom you're seeing: the system runs (or tries to run) but your home never reaches the thermostat setpoint. Because American Standard ACs don't have their own display, the diagnostics live in the matched indoor furnace/air handler board and your thermostat (often an American Standard AccuLink or a third-party 24V stat).

Depending on your model and control board, "not cooling" can mean the outdoor unit never starts, it starts but the air is barely cool, or the coil freezes and airflow drops off. The cause could be as simple as a clogged filter or as serious as a failed compressor. This page walks you through the likely causes, what you can safely check, and how a technician will confirm the problem so you can sanity-check any quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (heat vs cool), or a wiring/communication fault, especially with AccuLink stats.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power or no 24V call.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor and/or condenser fan won't start; you may hear a hum or clicking.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is caked with dirt/cottonwood, so it can't reject heat.
6. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, ice on the lineset, or the coil freezing over.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — a result of low airflow or low charge; blows warm until it thaws.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — compressor overheats and shuts down on its internal overload.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; often the last thing confirmed after other causes are ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do — no panels, no refrigerant, no electrical bypassing:

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If it's gray and clogged, replace it. This is the single most common cause of weak or lost cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered and the display is dim or blank.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs. Closing too many vents can freeze the coil.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear grass, leaves, and debris from around the condenser and gently rinse visible dirt off the fins with a garden hose (unit off). Keep at least two feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain:** If you see water pooling or a full drain pan, a clogged condensate line can trigger a safety float switch that stops cooling. You can often clear the visible drain outlet, but a full-line clog needs a pro.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lineset or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and run the **fan only** for a few hours to thaw it, then replace the filter before restarting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech should work through this in a logical order, and your invoice should reflect it:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** — confirms the thermostat is sending 24V (Y) to the outdoor unit.
2. **Check airflow and filter** — inspects the filter, blower, and evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Test electrical components** — measures the capacitor's microfarads, checks the contactor for pitting, and confirms line voltage at the condenser.
4. **Inspect the condenser coil and fan** — verifies the fan runs and the coil is clean enough to reject heat.
5. **Connect gauges (refrigerant side)** — reads suction and liquid pressures and takes superheat/subcooling to judge the charge. Abnormal readings point to a leak or restriction.
6. **Leak search if charge is low** — uses an electronic detector or dye; a proper repair fixes the leak, not just tops off refrigerant.
7. **Compressor test** — checks amp draw, windings, and start behavior only after simpler causes are ruled out.

If a tech jumps straight to "you need refrigerant" or "you need a compressor" without checking the filter, capacitor, and pressures, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower/coil |
| Blank/dim thermostat | Dead batteries | Replace batteries | Check stat wiring/24V |
| Outdoor unit dead, breaker tripped | Tripped breaker | Reset once | Find the fault causing trips |
| Humming, fan not spinning | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor runs | Bad contactor | None — call pro | Replace contactor |
| Ice on lineset/coil | Low airflow or low charge | Fan-only thaw, new filter | Find cause, check charge |
| Air slightly cool, long run times | Low refrigerant/leak | None — call pro | Leak search & repair, recharge |
| Loud outdoor unit, then shuts off | Failing fan or compressor | None — call pro | Test motor/compressor |
| Water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Clear visible outlet | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor, for a matched American Standard system:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser fan motor:** $400–$700 installed
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250
- **Coil cleaning (deep):** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,800
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500 (often worth weighing against a full system replacement on older units)

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs newer blends), and whether your unit is still under American Standard's parts warranty — registered systems often carry 10-year parts coverage, which can dramatically cut the bill.

## Related codes

- **American Standard AC freezing up / iced coil** — closely tied to airflow and low-charge issues above.
- **American Standard outdoor unit not turning on** — capacitor, contactor, or breaker faults.
- **AccuLink communication fault / "Wait" on thermostat** — control/communication problems on Platinum series systems.
- **Condensate float switch / safety lockout** — drain-related cooling loss.
- **Furnace/air handler blower not running** — no indoor airflow means no cooling delivery even if the outdoor unit works.
