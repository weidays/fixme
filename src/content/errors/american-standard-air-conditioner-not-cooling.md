## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you're seeing: the system is on but your home stays warm, or air from the vents isn't cold. On American Standard equipment this can happen with or without a stored fault.

Systems with the AccuLink communicating control (paired with a Gold or Platinum thermostat) may display a plain-language alert or a fault flag. Traditional 24V American Standard systems usually give no message at all — you just notice the house won't hold temperature. Because the exact reporting depends on your control type and model year, treat "not cooling" as a starting point, not a diagnosis.

The severity here is **pro**: several likely causes (refrigerant, compressor, electrical faults) require a licensed technician and, in some cases, EPA certification to handle legally and safely.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — chokes airflow across the indoor coil, drops capacity, and can freeze the coil.
2. **Thermostat setting or fault** — wrong mode, high setpoint, dead batteries, or a communication error on AccuLink systems.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so nothing feels cold.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system runs but can't remove heat. Always a leak, never "normal use."
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the outdoor fan or compressor won't start or start reliably.
6. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit fails or sticks.
7. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — caked with dirt, grass, or cottonwood, so the unit can't reject heat.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
9. **Failing compressor** — the "heart" of the system; runs but doesn't pump, or won't run at all.
10. **Clogged condensate drain triggering a safety float switch** — shuts cooling off to prevent water damage.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. If none fix it, call a pro.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and look. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This is the single most common cause.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor and outdoor AC breakers in your panel. If one is tripped, switch it fully off then on **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the outside fins with a garden hose (never a pressure washer) if they look dirty. Power the unit off at the breaker first.
- **Condensate drain:** If you see a full drain pan or standing water near the indoor unit, a clogged drain may have tripped a float switch. You can clear a visible clog with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor drain outlet; a persistent clog needs a pro.
- **Frozen coil / ice:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and set the fan to **On** to thaw it (can take several hours). If it re-freezes, you have a deeper problem — call a technician.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass any safety switch, or remove electrical covers on the outdoor unit.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a repeatable process — use this to sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verifies the complaint** and checks thermostat mode, setpoint, and (on AccuLink) any stored faults or communication errors.
2. **Confirms airflow** — filter, blower operation, and return/supply temperatures. They'll measure the temperature split across the indoor coil (typically ~15–20°F when healthy).
3. **Checks power and controls** — voltage at the disconnect, contactor operation, and the run capacitor's microfarad rating against spec.
4. **Inspects both coils** for dirt and the evaporator for ice.
5. **Takes refrigerant readings** — pressures and superheat/subcooling — to judge charge and detect a possible leak. If low, they should **find the leak**, not just top it off.
6. **Tests the compressor** — amp draw, windings, and start behavior.
7. **Presents findings** with a specific part or repair, not a vague "it needs Freon."

Be cautious of any quote to "just add refrigerant" with no leak search — that's a temporary fix you'll pay for again.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, indoor fan running | Dirty filter or low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Check blower, static pressure, coil |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Find airflow/refrigerant root cause |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor runs | Breaker, contactor, or capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test capacitor, contactor, power |
| Outdoor fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Replace capacitor, verify amp draw |
| House won't cool on hot days only | Low refrigerant / undercharge | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged condensate drain | Vac out visible clog | Clear drain, test float switch |
| Unit runs constantly, never cools | Failing compressor or bad charge | None — call pro | Compressor test, system evaluation |
| AccuLink thermostat shows error | Communication fault | Restart at thermostat | Check wiring, board, addressing |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Your price varies by region and unit age.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs. legacy R-22 is far pricier)
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to full system replacement on older units)
- **Control/communication board (AccuLink):** $400–$1,000

If your unit is over 12–15 years old and needs a compressor or major refrigerant work, ask your tech to price a full replacement for comparison.

## Related codes

- **Frozen evaporator coil** — closely tied to the "not cooling" symptom via low airflow or low charge.
- **AccuLink communication fault** — on Gold/Platinum communicating systems, can stop cooling with a thermostat alert.
- **Outdoor unit won't start** — capacitor, contactor, or breaker faults that overlap with "not cooling."
- **AC short cycling** — runs briefly then stops; often points to charge, airflow, or control issues.
- **Condensate overflow / float switch trip** — safety shutoff that mimics a "not cooling" complaint.
