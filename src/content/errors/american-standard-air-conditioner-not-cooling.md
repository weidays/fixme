## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's a symptom your American Standard air conditioner is displaying by running without lowering your home's temperature, or by not running at all when there's a cooling demand. On communicating **AccuLink** systems (Platinum series with an 850/1050 control or Trane/American Standard link thermostat), you may see an actual fault or error code on the thermostat that points to a specific subsystem. On older 24V systems there's no display — you diagnose by behavior.

The underlying issue is almost always one of four things: **airflow** (filter/coil/blower), **refrigerant charge**, **electrical components** (capacitor, contactor, control board), or the **compressor** itself. This page walks through each so you can tell a fair diagnosis from an upsell.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — restricts airflow across the indoor coil, drops capacity, and can freeze the coil. Most common and cheapest.
2. **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil** — grass, cottonwood, and dirt insulate the coil so it can't reject heat. Very common in summer.
3. **Thermostat setting or wiring issue** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a miswired/failing thermostat (especially after a DIY swap on AccuLink systems, which need a compatible communicating stat).
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a cheap part that stops the compressor and/or fan motor from starting. The fan may hum but not spin.
5. **Failed contactor** — the relay that sends power to the outdoor unit; pitted or stuck contacts stop the condenser from running.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling, long run times, and often ice on the lines. Requires a licensed tech; adding refrigerant without finding the leak is a temporary and improper fix.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; the unit runs but blows warm.
8. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; unit hums, trips the breaker, or won't start.
9. **Control board / AccuLink communication fault** — the indoor and outdoor units can't talk, so the compressor never gets a call.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do not open the electrical panels on the outdoor unit or touch the capacitor — it stores a dangerous charge.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Recheck cooling after an hour.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, fan on **Auto**, and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — never repeatedly reset.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for grass clippings, leaves, or debris smothering the coil. Turn the disconnect off and gently rinse from the outside with a garden hose (low pressure) if it's clearly dirty. Keep 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Clear standing water from the pan and check the drain line exit for a clog.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and set the fan to **On** to thaw it (can take a few hours), then replace the filter. If it re-freezes, that's a refrigerant or airflow problem for a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should:

- Confirm the thermostat call and check for AccuLink fault codes on communicating systems.
- Measure the **temperature split** across the indoor coil (typically 15–20°F drop between return and supply air).
- Check filter and blower operation, and inspect the evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
- Test the **capacitor** (microfarad reading vs. rating) and inspect the **contactor** for pitting.
- Put gauges on the system to read **suction and liquid pressures** and calculate superheat/subcooling — this reveals low charge, overcharge, or restrictions.
- If low on refrigerant, perform a **leak search** (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test) rather than just topping off.
- Verify compressor amp draw and confirm it starts under load.

Ask for the actual pressure and capacitor readings. A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace compressor" without these measurements deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit runs, warm air from vents | Dirty filter or low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Check blower, coil, temp split |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cooling | Fouled condenser coil | Rinse coil, clear debris | Deep coil clean, pressure check |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor blows | Contactor or capacitor failure | Check/reset breaker once | Test and replace contactor/capacitor |
| Fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — leave power off | Replace capacitor |
| Ice on refrigerant lines | Low charge or low airflow | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Leak search, recharge |
| Long run times, weak cooling | Low refrigerant (leak) | None | Locate leak, repair, recharge |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Compressor or wiring fault | Stop resetting, call pro | Amp test, compressor diagnosis |
| Thermostat blank/unresponsive | Dead batteries or control fault | Replace batteries | Check board, AccuLink comms |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your area and unit age will vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 (communicating AccuLink stats cost more)
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to full system replacement on older units)
- **Control board (AccuLink):** $400–$900

If the unit is over 12–15 years old and needs a compressor or coil, get a replacement quote too — the repair may not be worth it.

## Related codes

- **Frozen evaporator coil** — often the reason a running unit blows warm.
- **AccuLink communication error** — indoor/outdoor units can't link on Platinum communicating systems.
- **Compressor lockout / short cycling** — repeated safety trips that stop cooling.
- **Outdoor fan motor failure** — condenser can't reject heat, mimicking "not cooling."
