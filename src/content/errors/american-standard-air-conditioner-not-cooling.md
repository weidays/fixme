## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on most American Standard air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your system may run, blow air, and still leave your home warm, or it may not turn on at all. Because American Standard condensers are built by Trane, the underlying causes and diagnostics are nearly identical across both brands.

If your system uses the **AccuLink communicating control** (Platinum series with a Nexia/AccuLink thermostat), the thermostat may display a specific numeric fault or a "system malfunction" message that points to the real problem. Single-stage Silver and Gold units with a standard 24V thermostat won't show a code — you'll just notice warm air. Behavior varies by board and thermostat, so check your thermostat display before assuming there's no diagnostic info available.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the most common cause of weak or no cooling; restricted airflow can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so the unit runs but doesn't cool.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a wiring/communication fault (common on AccuLink setups).
4. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power, indoor blower may still run.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a bulging or dead capacitor stops the compressor or condenser fan; a very common, affordable repair.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling capacity and can freeze the coil; always indicates a leak that needs repair.
7. **Dirty condenser coil** — a caked outdoor coil can't reject heat, so the home never cools.
8. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the compressor doesn't engage.
9. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; the unit runs but produces no cold air, often the most expensive repair.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner:

- **Air filter** — pull it out and hold it to light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the #1 fix.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Swap in fresh batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Breakers** — check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated resets can damage equipment.
- **Vents and registers** — make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit** — clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser so it can breathe. Gently rinse the fins with a garden hose (power off first).
- **Condensate drain** — if the indoor unit shut off, a full drain pan or clogged line may have tripped a safety float. Clear standing water and check the drain line.
- **Ice on the coil or lineset** — if you see ice, turn the system OFF and set the fan to ON to thaw it. Running a frozen unit makes things worse. Once thawed, replace the filter and restart; if it re-freezes, call a technician.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. Verify thermostat call and 24V control voltage at the indoor and outdoor units.
2. Check the contactor and run capacitor with a meter (a weak capacitor is a frequent, quick find).
3. Measure amp draw on the compressor and condenser fan motor.
4. Inspect the evaporator and condenser coils for ice, dirt, and airflow restriction.
5. Connect refrigerant gauges to read **superheat and subcooling** — the definitive test for a refrigerant charge or leak problem.
6. On AccuLink/communicating systems, pull fault history from the thermostat and control board.
7. If low on charge, perform a leak search (electronic sniffer, dye, or nitrogen pressure test) rather than just topping off.

If a quote jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search, ask why — topping off a leaking system is a temporary and often illegal shortcut.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air from vents, weak flow | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and ductwork |
| Ice on indoor coil or copper line | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Check charge, find leak, verify airflow |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Bad capacitor or contactor | Check breaker only | Test and replace capacitor/contactor |
| Indoor blower runs, no outdoor unit | Tripped breaker or blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Diagnose short, replace fuse |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries or wiring fault | Replace batteries | Check 24V wiring and control board |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant or bad compressor | None (call pro) | Leak search, charge, or compressor test |
| Warm air, outdoor coil caked in dirt | Dirty condenser | Rinse fins with hose | Deep coil cleaning |
| AccuLink shows system malfunction | Communication or board fault | Note the message | Read fault history, replace board/wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts and labor; varies by region and model):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $0 DIY – $150 service call
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Coil cleaning (evaporator/condenser):** $150–$450
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (more if the evaporator coil is leaking)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ — on older units, often worth comparing to a full system replacement.

If your system is out of warranty and needs a compressor or coil, get a replacement quote too — American Standard registered systems may still carry a compressor or parts warranty, so check your paperwork first.
