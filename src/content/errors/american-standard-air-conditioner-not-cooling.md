## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific fault code. Your American Standard air conditioner may be running, blowing air, and drawing power, but the air coming from your vents isn't cold — or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

Unlike a furnace, most American Standard single-stage and two-stage outdoor units don't flash blinking error codes at the condenser. Variable-speed and communicating systems (paired with an AccuLink / Nexia-compatible thermostat) *can* display fault messages at the thermostat, so if you have one of those, check the thermostat screen for a specific alert first.

Because cooling depends on airflow, refrigerant charge, electrical components, and the compressor all working together, "not cooling" has a wide range of causes — from a $15 filter to a failed compressor.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow can freeze the indoor coil, which then blows warm or no air.
2. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — a coil caked in dust, grass, or cottonwood can't reject heat, so the system runs but barely cools.
3. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode/setting, dead batteries, or a wiring/communication fault.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure that prevents the compressor or condenser fan motor from starting; you may hear a hum or clicking.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — no power to the outdoor unit.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling, ice on the copper lines, and long run times. Always indicates a leak, not "used up" refrigerant.
7. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit is worn or pitted.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from airflow or refrigerant issues; blows warm air until it thaws.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — the outdoor fan doesn't spin, the unit overheats and shuts off on high pressure.
10. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; the unit runs but doesn't move refrigerant.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all safe for a homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening a sealed electrical panel or the refrigerant system.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Do this first.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the temperature is set below room temperature. Set the fan to AUTO. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor AC breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse visible dirt off the outside of the coil fins with a garden hose (power off). Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper refrigerant lines or the indoor coil, turn the system to OFF and set the fan to ON to thaw it (can take several hours). Recurring ice means a real fault — call a pro.
- **Condensate drain:** If a full drain pan or a clogged line has tripped a safety float switch, the system may not cool. Check for standing water and clear a visible clog; drain tablets help prevent recurrence.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will confirm the problem systematically. Use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower, and coils for restriction or ice.
3. **Electrical checks** — test the capacitor with a meter, inspect the contactor, and check the low-voltage fuse and control voltage.
4. **Gauge the refrigerant** — connect manifold gauges to read pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling. Abnormal readings point to charge or airflow issues.
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, they should find the leak (electronic detector, dye, or bubble test) before adding refrigerant. Adding refrigerant without fixing the leak is a red flag.
6. **Component testing** — verify condenser fan motor and compressor amperage draw and operation.
7. **Communicating systems** — read fault history at the AccuLink/Nexia thermostat or via service tools.

Be wary of any tech who reaches for the refrigerant gauges before checking the filter, coils, and capacitor.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak flow | Dirty filter / frozen coil | Replace filter; thaw coil (fan ON, cool OFF) | Diagnose airflow or charge cause |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Failed run capacitor | None (electrical) | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Ice on copper lines | Low refrigerant / airflow | Thaw; replace filter | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low charge / dirty condenser | Rinse coil, replace filter | Gauge system, leak search |
| Humming, then shuts off | Weak capacitor / compressor | None | Test capacitor and compressor |
| Thermostat blank | Dead batteries / power loss | Replace batteries; check breaker | Trace low-voltage fault |
| Water/float switch tripped | Clogged condensate drain | Clear line, add tablets | Clear drain, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Actual price depends on your region and system type.

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $0 DIY – $150 pro
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$300
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$650
- **Coil cleaning (professional):** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ (depends on leak location; a leaking evaporator coil is at the high end)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement, especially out of warranty)

American Standard systems carry a registered limited warranty (commonly 10 years on parts, longer on the compressor for some models) if registered within 60 days of install — check your warranty before paying for major parts.

## Related codes

- **AC frozen / iced up** — evaporator coil freezing from airflow or refrigerant problems.
- **AC not turning on** — no power, thermostat, or contactor issues.
- **Outdoor fan not spinning** — capacitor or condenser fan motor failure.
- **Thermostat communication fault (AccuLink/Nexia)** — on variable-speed communicating systems.
- **Compressor short-cycling** — repeated on/off from electrical or pressure faults.
