## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's a symptom. On most American Standard split-system air conditioners, the outdoor unit either runs without producing cold air, short-cycles, or won't start at all. The indoor air handler may blow, but the air coming from the vents stays warm or only mildly cool.

On **AccuLink communicating systems** (paired with a compatible ComfortLink or AccuLink thermostat), the system may display an actual diagnostic fault or alert at the thermostat. On **standard single-stage units**, there is no display — you diagnose by symptom, not by a code. Which behavior you get depends on your specific model and thermostat pairing, so we cover both below.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. A clogged filter starves the system, can freeze the indoor coil, and kills cooling.
2. **Tripped breaker or condensate safety switch** — a full drain pan or a tripped float switch shuts the system down to prevent water damage.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, dust, and debris insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common electrical failure; the compressor or fan hums, struggles, or won't start.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling, ice on the copper lines, and long run times. Requires a certified tech.
6. **Failed condenser fan motor** — the compressor runs hot and shuts off on internal overload if the fan isn't moving air.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — usually a downstream effect of low airflow or low refrigerant.
8. **Failed compressor or contactor** — the outdoor unit won't engage at all, or trips the breaker.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for any homeowner. Do not go beyond them.

- **Air filter** — pull it. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to COOL, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on AUTO. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker** — check both the indoor and outdoor breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers** — make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit** — clear leaves, grass, and debris from around and on top of the condenser. Keep at least two feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain** — if you see water pooling near the indoor unit or the system stopped after a wet spell, the drain may be clogged and the safety switch tripped. Cleaning tablets and clearing the line is DIY-friendly; anything electrical is not.
- **Coil check** — if you see ice on the copper refrigerant lines or the indoor coil, turn the system to OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw for a few hours before restarting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the workflow helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint and airflow** — check filter, blower operation, and temperature split across the coil (typically 15–20°F on a healthy system).
2. **Check electrical** — test the capacitor with a meter, inspect the contactor for pitting, and confirm the compressor and fan motor draw correct amperage.
3. **Inspect the condenser coil and fan** — clean if dirty, confirm the fan spins freely and starts on demand.
4. **Read pressures** — connect gauges to check refrigerant charge. Low pressures point to a leak; the tech should then perform a **leak search** (electronic sniffer, dye, or nitrogen), not just add refrigerant.
5. **On AccuLink systems** — pull stored fault history from the thermostat to see what tripped.

A good tech finds the *root cause*. Be cautious of anyone who wants to "add a couple pounds of Freon" without searching for a leak — that's a temporary and non-compliant fix.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, filter is clean | Low refrigerant or failed compressor | None — call a pro | Leak search, recharge, or compressor test |
| Very weak airflow, coil iced | Dirty filter / frozen coil | Replace filter, thaw coil (fan ON) | Restore airflow, check charge |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor blows | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, test control circuit |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed condenser fan motor | Clear debris only | Replace fan motor / capacitor |
| System stopped after rain, water pooling | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear drain, use tablets | Clear line, verify pump/switch |
| Hot air, high energy bills, long runs | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse coil gently, clear debris | Deep coil cleaning, charge check |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10 – $40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $200 professional
- **Run capacitor:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350 – $650
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150 – $400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500 – $1,500 (higher if the coil must be replaced)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900 – $2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800 – $2,500+ (on an older unit, replacing the whole system is often more economical)

Prices vary by region, model, and refrigerant type. Systems using older R-22 refrigerant cost significantly more to recharge and may be worth replacing.

## Related codes

- **American Standard AC freezing up / iced coil** — airflow and refrigerant problems that overlap with no-cooling.
- **American Standard AC not turning on** — no power or control-circuit faults.
- **AccuLink / ComfortLink communication faults** — thermostat-displayed errors on communicating systems.
- **American Standard AC short cycling** — related capacitor, charge, or airflow issues.
