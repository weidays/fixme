## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a fault code. Most American Standard air conditioners — including standard 24V models and communicating **AccuLink** systems — don't display a literal "not cooling" error. Instead, you'll notice warm air from the vents, an outdoor unit that won't start, or a system that runs constantly without lowering the house temperature.

On AccuLink communicating systems, the thermostat may show a specific fault or a communication message that points to the real problem. On standard non-communicating units, there is no display, so diagnosis relies on physical inspection and meter readings.

Because the underlying cause can be anything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, this page walks you through the safe checks first, then explains how a pro narrows it down — so you can sanity-check any quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat set wrong or failing** — set to "fan on" instead of "cool," wrong temperature, or dead batteries.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit or air handler loses power.
4. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; blocks cooling until it thaws.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan won't start; you may hear humming.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, ice on lines, and long run times. Requires a licensed tech.
7. **Dirty condenser coil (outdoor)** — the unit can't reject heat, so cooling fades on hot days.
8. **Failed contactor or condenser fan motor** — outdoor unit hums or stays silent.
9. **Clogged condensate drain / safety float switch** — on some setups a full drain pan shuts the system down.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the outdoor unit runs but produces no cold air.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all homeowner-safe. Stop if anything requires opening the sealed refrigerant system, the compressor compartment, or bypassing a safety switch.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the #1 cause of poor cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the target temperature is below room temperature, and the fan is on **AUTO**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor air handler and the outdoor unit breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the exterior fins with a garden hose (unit off).
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** and the fan to **ON** to thaw it (can take a few hours). Recurring ice means a real problem — call a pro.
- **Condensate drain:** If your system has a drain pan that's full, or a float switch that's shutting the unit off, clearing the drain line may restore operation.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech follows a logical sequence so they don't guess. A good quote reflects this process:

1. **Confirm the complaint** and check thermostat call for cooling with a meter.
2. **Verify power** at the disconnect, contactor, and control board (or AccuLink comm status).
3. **Read the capacitor** with a meter — a weak or dead capacitor is a common, cheap fix.
4. **Check airflow** — filter, blower operation, and coil condition; a frozen coil is inspected after thawing.
5. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and read suction/liquid pressures and superheat/subcooling to judge charge. Low readings point to a leak.
6. **Leak search** with an electronic detector or dye if refrigerant is low — topping off without finding the leak is bad practice.
7. **Test the compressor and fan motor** — amperage draw, windings, and start components.
8. On **AccuLink** systems, they'll read live data and any stored faults from the thermostat and control board.

Be cautious of any quote that jumps straight to "you need a new compressor" or "just add refrigerant" without gauge readings and a leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vents blow warm, fan runs | Thermostat setting or low refrigerant | Confirm COOL mode & temp | Gauge pressures, leak search |
| Outdoor unit silent | Breaker, contactor, or capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor or capacitor |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Replace dual-run capacitor |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Set to OFF/fan ON to thaw; change filter | Find airflow or charge fault |
| Weak cooling on hot days | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse fins with hose (off) | Deep coil cleaning |
| Long run times, house won't cool | Low refrigerant / dirty coils | Change filter, clean condenser | Charge check, leak repair |
| System shuts off randomly | Full drain pan / float switch | Clear condensate drain | Repair drain, verify float |
| Runs but no cold air at all | Failed compressor | None — call pro | Compressor test/replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included. Prices vary by region and by whether your system is a communicating AccuLink unit (specialized parts cost more).

- **Air filter:** $15–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$450
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $400–$700
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Coil cleaning (deep):** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often makes replacing an older unit worth considering)
- **AccuLink control board / thermostat:** $400–$900

If your unit is under 8 years old, check your American Standard registered limited warranty — the compressor and major parts may be covered, though labor usually isn't.
