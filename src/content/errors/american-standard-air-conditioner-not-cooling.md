## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific fault code on most American Standard air conditioners. The unit may run continuously without lowering the temperature, blow room-temperature air, or short-cycle. On variable-speed AccuComfort systems paired with a communicating thermostat, you may instead see a specific error code or fault message on the thermostat display — those point to a defined component and should be read exactly as shown.

Because "not cooling" covers everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, the goal below is to separate the cheap, safe things you can rule out yourself from the sealed-system and electrical work that requires a licensed pro.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling output, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode (not on COOL), dead batteries, or a setpoint above room temperature.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; the coil ices over and blocks cooling.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan won't start; you may hear a hum or clicking.
6. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is packed with debris, so it can't reject heat.
7. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit fails, so the compressor never energizes.
8. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced capacity, warm air, possible coil freeze. Requires a certified tech.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cooling.
10. **Control board or inverter fault (variable-speed models)** — usually shows a specific thermostat code.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting down.

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is at least a few degrees below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery model and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breaker** — check the electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers** — make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain** — a clogged drain can trigger a safety float switch that shuts the system off. Look for standing water in the drain pan; drain tablets help prevent future clogs.
- **Outdoor unit** — clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser and gently rinse the fins with a garden hose (power off first).

If the indoor coil or outdoor lines are covered in ice, turn the system to OFF (leave the fan running to help it thaw) and call a technician — running a frozen system can damage the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work through this sequence, which helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm the complaint** — verify airflow, thermostat call, and whether the outdoor unit energizes.
2. **Check electrical** — measure voltage at the contactor, test the run capacitor, and inspect the contactor contacts.
3. **Read any thermostat/board codes** — on AccuComfort/communicating systems, the fault code narrows it down fast.
4. **Measure refrigerant pressures** — using gauges on the service ports to check charge and look for signs of a leak or restriction.
5. **Test the compressor** — check windings and amp draw to determine if it's electrically failed or seized.
6. **Leak search** — if charge is low, locate the leak with electronic detectors or dye rather than just adding refrigerant.

Be cautious of any tech who tops off refrigerant without searching for the leak — that's a temporary fix that will recur.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and ductwork |
| Indoor unit runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor, check wiring |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Replace capacitor |
| Ice on coil or refrigerant line | Low refrigerant or low airflow | Set to OFF, let it thaw | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Warm air, no ice, correct charge | Failing compressor | None — call pro | Test and replace compressor |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries or wiring | Replace batteries | Check thermostat wiring/board |
| Fault code on smart thermostat | Board/inverter fault | Note the exact code | Diagnose per code, replace board |
| Water near indoor unit, system off | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain, add tablets | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US prices including parts and labor. Your area and system size affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $0–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $0–$10 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150–$300 pro
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$300
- **Contactor:** $150–$400
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$1,800+ (often worth comparing against full system replacement on older units)
- **Control/inverter board (variable-speed models):** $600–$1,500

Note: R-22 systems cost significantly more to recharge because the refrigerant is phased out. If your unit uses R-22 and needs major work, ask about replacement instead.

## Related codes

- **AC frozen coil / iced lines** — closely tied to airflow and refrigerant issues above.
- **Outdoor unit not turning on** — overlaps with breaker, contactor, and capacitor faults.
- **AccuComfort communicating thermostat fault codes** — model-specific; read the exact code on the display.
- **Condensate float switch shutoff** — a safety trip that can present as "not cooling."
