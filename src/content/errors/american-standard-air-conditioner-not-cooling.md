## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific fault code. Your American Standard system is either running without producing cold air, blowing warm air, or short-cycling and never reaching the thermostat setpoint. Many American Standard systems don't display a numeric error for this — instead, communicating (AccuLink) systems may show a status message at the thermostat, while older 24V systems just fail to cool with no code at all.

The underlying issue is almost always one of three things: **airflow restriction**, **refrigerant charge problems (from a leak)**, or an **electrical/component failure** in the outdoor condensing unit. Because refrigerant work and electrical diagnosis require certification and tools, this is rated **pro** — you can rule out the easy stuff safely, but the fix usually needs a licensed technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return** — the single most common cause of weak or no cooling; restricts airflow and can freeze the coil.
2. **Frozen indoor evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer and warm air blows.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — outdoor fan or compressor won't start; you may hear a hum but no fan.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — gradual loss of cooling capacity; never a "top-off" fix.
5. **Tripped condensate float switch** — a full drain pan shuts down cooling to prevent water damage.
6. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — buildup keeps the unit from rejecting heat.
7. **Thermostat problem or wiring fault** — dead batteries, wrong setting, or a broken 24V signal.
8. **Contactor failure** — worn contacts stop the outdoor unit from energizing.
9. **Compressor failure** — least common but most expensive; unit hums, trips breaker, or does nothing.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for a homeowner. Stop here if the fix requires opening the sealed refrigerant system or the electrical panel of the condenser.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the temperature is below room temperature. If it's a battery model with a blank or dim screen, replace the batteries.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for leaves, grass, or debris packed against the coil fins. Gently rinse the exterior with a garden hose (power off) if it's clearly dirty.
- **Condensate drain / float switch:** If there's a small switch on the drain pan or line and it's tripped, a clogged drain is likely. You can clear a visible clog with a wet/dry vac on the drain line outlet, but leave internal work to a tech.
- **Check for ice:** If you see frost or ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to FAN ONLY and let it thaw for several hours before running cooling again.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A quote should reflect actual measurement, not guessing. A competent tech will:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** at the thermostat and confirm 24V signal (or communicating status) reaching the equipment.
2. **Measure airflow** and inspect the filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Check electrical components** — capacitor microfarad reading, contactor condition, and voltage/amp draw at the compressor and fan motor.
4. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and read suction/liquid pressures and temperatures to calculate superheat and subcooling. This tells them if the charge is correct or if there's a leak.
5. **Perform a leak search** (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test) if refrigerant is low — a good tech finds the leak rather than just adding gas.
6. **Inspect the condenser coil** and outdoor fan operation.

If a tech proposes adding refrigerant without doing a leak search, ask why — a low charge always means it leaked out somewhere.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but isn't cold | Dirty filter, low airflow, or low refrigerant | Replace filter, check vents | Measure airflow, check charge/leak |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Set to FAN, thaw, replace filter | Diagnose airflow vs. charge, repair leak |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan won't spin | Failed capacitor or motor | None — power off | Test/replace capacitor or fan motor |
| Outdoor unit totally dead | Breaker, contactor, or control fault | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, board, wiring |
| Cooling stops, water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain / float switch | Vac out drain line, clear clog | Clear drain, clean pan, test switch |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Compressor or electrical short | Stop resetting, power off | Diagnose compressor/electrical fault |
| Warm air, thermostat blank | Dead batteries or wiring | Replace batteries | Trace 24V wiring, test transformer |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain cleaning / clog clear:** $0 DIY – $250 pro
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Outdoor fan motor:** $400–$700 installed
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair:** $400–$1,500 depending on leak location; coil leaks are the high end
- **Refrigerant recharge (after repair):** $150–$600+ depending on system size and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often better to weigh against full-system replacement; check your American Standard warranty first, as many carry a 10-year registered parts warranty)

## Related codes

- **AC frozen / iced-up coil** — often the visible result of "not cooling."
- **Outdoor fan not running** — capacitor or motor fault subset of this problem.
- **AC short-cycling** — related airflow or charge issue.
- **Condensate overflow / float switch tripped** — common cooling shutdown cause.
- **Communicating (AccuLink) thermostat "no communication"** — control-side fault on Platinum/variable systems that can present as no cooling.
