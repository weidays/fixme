## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the most common symptom homeowners report for American Standard air conditioners. It means the system is either not producing cold air at all, or the air coming from your vents isn't cold enough to reach your thermostat setpoint.

On most American Standard split systems (Platinum, Gold, and Silver series), the outdoor condenser and the indoor evaporator coil work together. If either side fails, or if refrigerant, airflow, or electrical power is compromised, you'll feel warm or weak air. Some variable-speed and communicating systems will display a specific fault or error code on the wall control (AccuLink / Nexia thermostats) — if yours does, note that code, as it narrows the diagnosis considerably.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a $0 filter swap to a failed compressor, this page ranks the likely causes and shows you what's safe to check versus what needs a licensed pro.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the coil, and kills cooling capacity. The single most common cause.
2. **Thermostat set wrong or dead batteries** — set to "off"/"fan only," wrong program, or dead batteries on battery-powered models.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit or air handler has lost power.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks cooling entirely.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor and/or fan won't start.
6. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so cooling fades, especially in hot weather.
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
8. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces or eliminates cooling; requires leak repair, not just a recharge.
9. **Clogged condensate drain / float switch tripped** — a safety switch may shut the system down to prevent overflow.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive failure; the outdoor unit hums or trips the breaker but won't cool.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything looks damaged, smells burnt, or is beyond these steps.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Do this first.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to "Cool," the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on "Auto." Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler and outdoor unit. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed registers aren't blocking airflow in occupied rooms.
- **Outdoor unit:** Visually check that the condenser isn't buried in leaves, grass clippings, or debris. Gently rinse the exterior fins with a garden hose (power off) if they're dirty.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or coil, turn the system to "Off" and the fan to "On" for a few hours to thaw, then replace the filter. Persistent freezing means a real problem — call a pro.
- **Condensate drain:** If your unit has a drain pan that's overflowing, a float switch may have shut it down. Clearing a visibly clogged drain line can restore operation.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician follows a logical sequence — you can sanity-check any quote against it:

1. **Confirms the symptom** and checks thermostat call for cooling and 24V control voltage.
2. **Verifies power** to the air handler and outdoor unit; inspects breakers, disconnect, and fuses.
3. **Checks the capacitor and contactor** with a meter — reads microfarads on the capacitor and looks for pitted or welded contacts.
4. **Measures airflow and inspects the coil** for ice or dirt; a frozen coil points to airflow or charge problems.
5. **Attaches refrigerant gauges** to read pressures and calculates superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct — this requires EPA certification.
6. **Leak-searches** with electronic detectors or dye if the charge is low, because low charge means a leak.
7. **Tests the compressor** for proper amp draw, windings, and start behavior if the unit won't cool despite good power and charge.
8. **Reads any stored fault codes** on communicating (AccuLink) systems via the wall control.

Expect a diagnostic fee of roughly $80–$180, often credited toward the repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check duct/blower if it recurs |
| No air at all, unit dead | Tripped breaker / no power | Reset breaker once | Diagnose electrical fault |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low charge | Thaw with fan on, new filter | Find cause: airflow or leak |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cooling | Failed capacitor or low charge | None | Test/replace capacitor, check charge |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Bad capacitor or contactor | None | Replace capacitor/contactor |
| Cooling fades in heat | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse fins with hose (power off) | Deep coil cleaning |
| Water near indoor unit, system off | Clogged drain / float switch | Clear visible clog | Clear line, test float switch |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Electrical fault or locked compressor | Leave breaker off | Diagnose compressor/wiring |
| No cool air, no code, good power | Low refrigerant leak | None | Leak search + recharge |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location
- **Fan motor (outdoor):** $400–$700
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to a new unit if out of warranty)
- **Diagnostic fee:** $80–$180 (frequently credited toward repair)

American Standard registered systems may carry a 10-year parts limited warranty — check your registration, as covered parts can dramatically lower a compressor or coil repair.

## Related codes

- **AC freezing up / ice on lines** — airflow and refrigerant issues
- **Outdoor unit not turning on** — capacitor, contactor, or power faults
- **AC blowing warm air** — the same symptom set covered here
- **Thermostat not calling for cool** — control-side faults on AccuLink / Nexia systems
- **Condensate float switch open** — drain and safety-switch shutdowns

If your communicating thermostat shows a specific numbered fault code, look that code up directly — it will point to the exact subsystem faster than the general "not cooling" symptom.
