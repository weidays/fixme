## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your American Standard air conditioner runs but the house won't reach the setpoint, or the air from the vents feels warm. On systems with a communicating thermostat (like the AccuLink platform), you may see a specific error or status message, but many American Standard units use simpler controls that show nothing on a display.

The condenser (outdoor unit), the evaporator coil (indoor, at the furnace or air handler), the thermostat and the refrigerant charge all have to work together to move heat out of your home. When one link breaks, you get warm air, weak airflow, or a unit that runs constantly without cooling. This page walks through the likely causes in order and helps you sanity-check any repair quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — chokes airflow across the indoor coil, drops capacity and can freeze the coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so the unit blows warm.
3. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat when the fins are packed with dirt, grass or cottonwood.
4. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a wiring/communication fault (especially on AccuLink systems).
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a common cause of a compressor or condenser fan that hums but won't start.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling capacity; requires EPA-certified diagnosis and repair.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
8. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the condenser isn't closing.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor or blower motor** — no airflow over one of the coils.
10. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass safety switches, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting off.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the single most common cooling problem.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for tripped breakers to both the indoor and outdoor units. Reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the outside fins with a garden hose (power off first). Never bend the fins or use a pressure washer.
- **Condensate drain:** If the indoor unit has a full drain pan or a float switch has tripped, a clogged condensate line can shut cooling down. You can pour drain tablets or clear a visible clog, but don't disable the float switch.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF and set the fan to ON to thaw it (can take a few hours), then replace the filter. If it re-freezes, call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified technician will confirm the fault instead of guessing, so a good quote should reference actual measurements:

- **Airflow and filter check**, then inspect the evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
- **Thermostat and control voltage check** — verifying 24V call signals reach the outdoor unit (and communication on AccuLink models).
- **Electrical tests** — measuring capacitor microfarads, testing the contactor, and checking amp draw on the compressor and fan motors.
- **Refrigerant readings** — gauges on the service ports to measure suction/head pressure, plus superheat and subcooling to confirm charge. Low charge points to a leak.
- **Leak search** — with an electronic detector or dye if the charge is low. A reputable tech finds and fixes the leak rather than just topping off.
- **Compressor test** — checking for a locked or shorted compressor if everything else is ruled out.

Ask for the actual capacitor reading, pressure numbers, or subcooling values. A quote for "add refrigerant" with no mention of a leak search is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak airflow | Dirty filter or frozen coil | Replace filter, thaw coil with fan on | Find why coil froze; check airflow/charge |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor blows warm | Tripped breaker or blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Diagnose short, replace disconnect fuse |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — leave it off | Test and replace capacitor |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant / dirty condenser | Clean condenser coil, replace filter | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Fan motor or capacitor failure | Power off, don't spin by hand | Replace fan motor or capacitor |
| Clicks but doesn't run | Failed contactor | None | Replace contactor |
| No cooling, thermostat blank | Dead batteries or control fault | Replace batteries | Diagnose 24V/comm wiring |
| Ice on lines, then no cooling | Low airflow or low charge | Thaw, replace filter | Check charge and airflow |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor. Your actual price varies by region, model and whether you're under warranty.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$200
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $130–$300
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900 (ECM/variable-speed motors cost more)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often close to the cost of a new condenser)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 (communicating AccuLink thermostats cost more)

If your unit is under 10 years old, check whether the compressor or coil is still covered by American Standard's registered parts warranty — that can dramatically cut the cost.
