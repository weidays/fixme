## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific error code — it's the symptom you'll describe when your American Standard air conditioner runs but the house stays warm, or the system won't run at all. American Standard systems with an AccuLink communicating control may show a fault or status message at the thermostat, while conventional 24V setups give you no code at all — just warm air.

Because the underlying causes range from a dirty filter (free) to a dead compressor (thousands), this page helps you sort out what you can safely check yourself versus what needs a licensed HVAC tech with gauges and EPA certification.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, which can freeze the indoor coil and stop cooling.
2. **Thermostat settings or wiring** — set to "fan" instead of "cool," wrong temperature, dead batteries, or a communication fault on AccuLink systems.
3. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat when packed with dirt, grass, or cottonwood.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler has lost power.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the fan or compressor hums but won't start; a very common and affordable failure.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that sends power to the compressor is pitted or stuck.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines.
8. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system runs constantly but never cools; requires a certified tech.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; the outdoor unit won't cool no matter what.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything is uncertain.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter at the air handler or return grille. If it's gray and clogged, replace it and give the system time to recover.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the target temp is below room temperature, and (for battery models) swap in fresh batteries.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel and the disconnect box next to the outdoor unit. If a breaker is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Outdoor unit:** Visually confirm it's clear of leaves, grass clippings, and debris. Gently hose off the exterior fins with the power off — never poke inside.
- **Condensate:** If a safety float switch is triggered by a full drain pan, the system may shut off. Check for standing water and clear the drain line if you can reach it.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines, turn the system to **Off** and set the fan to **On** to let it thaw. Do not run cooling on a frozen coil.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Confirms the complaint** — verifies airflow, thermostat call, and whether the outdoor unit engages.
2. **Checks power and controls** — measures voltage at the disconnect, tests the contactor and capacitor with a meter.
3. **Reads AccuLink data (if applicable)** — communicating systems store fault history the tech can pull up.
4. **Measures temperature split** — compares return vs. supply air; a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F.
5. **Connects refrigerant gauges** — checks pressures and superheat/subcooling to spot low charge or a failing compressor.
6. **Leak search** — if charge is low, uses electronic detector or dye rather than just topping off.
7. **Inspects the compressor** — tests windings and amp draw if all else checks out.

Be wary of any tech who wants to "just add refrigerant" without finding the leak — that's a temporary and non-compliant fix.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check coil for freeze damage |
| Nothing runs at all | Tripped breaker / dead thermostat | Reset breaker once, swap batteries | Trace power loss, test transformer |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — pro repair | Test and replace run capacitor |
| Ice on refrigerant lines | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Restore airflow; check refrigerant/leak |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant (leak) | None | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor coil packed with debris | Dirty condenser | Hose off exterior, power off | Deep coil clean, verify pressures |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Shorted compressor/wiring | Stop resetting | Diagnose short, test compressor |
| No cooling, AccuLink shows fault | Communication/component fault | Note the message | Pull fault data, replace component |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Communicating AccuLink parts and premium models run higher.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $75–$200 (or DIY with tablets)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **AccuLink control board:** $400–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement)

Diagnostic service call: typically $80–$180, often credited toward the repair.

## Related codes

- **AccuLink communication faults** — appear on Platinum-series communicating thermostats when a component drops offline.
- **Frozen coil / low airflow** — overlaps heavily with "not cooling" symptoms.
- **Outdoor unit not turning on** — often the same capacitor, contactor, or breaker causes.
- **Short cycling** — related root causes (dirty coil, low charge) that also degrade cooling.

Behavior and displayed messages vary by whether your American Standard system uses AccuLink communicating controls or a conventional 24V thermostat — check your model's manual to confirm which applies to you.
