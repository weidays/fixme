## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your American Standard air conditioner runs but your home doesn't reach the set temperature, or the air from the vents feels warm or only mildly cool.

On communicating American Standard systems (AccuLink / AccuComfort platforms with the Gold 824 or Platinum 1050 control), the thermostat may display an actual fault or error number, or show the system as "calling" without cooling. On standard 24V systems, there's no display code at all — you simply have a system that won't cool. Because "not cooling" spans everything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, the underlying cause matters far more than the symptom.

This page is marked **pro** severity because several of the most common causes (refrigerant issues, electrical component failure, compressor problems) legally or safely require a licensed HVAC technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause of weak or no cooling. Restricted airflow can also freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so no cold air reaches the vents.
3. **Failed run capacitor** — a bulging or dead capacitor stops the compressor and/or condenser fan from starting. Very common in hot climates.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces or eliminates cooling capacity. Requires leak repair and recharge by a pro.
5. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor relay that powers the unit; pitted or stuck contacts stop the compressor from running.
6. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked with dirt, grass, or cottonwood, so the unit can't reject heat.
7. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a communication fault on AccuLink systems.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor disconnect or panel breaker off.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cooling, or won't start at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe and may save a service call:

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the #1 fix.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to COOL, the temperature is below room temp, and the fan is on AUTO. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers** — check both the indoor air-handler breaker and the outdoor AC breaker in your main panel. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents** — make sure they're open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain** — a clogged drain can trip a safety float switch and shut the system off. You can clear a visibly clogged drain line and empty a full pan.
- **Frozen coil** — if you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper lines outside, turn the thermostat to OFF (or fan-only) and let it thaw fully — often several hours — before restarting.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or add refrigerant yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should work through this order so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — confirm airflow, thermostat call, and whether the outdoor unit is running.
2. **Check airflow and filter** — inspect for a frozen coil and restricted return.
3. **Electrical measurements** — test the capacitor microfarads, inspect the contactor, and check voltage/amp draw on the compressor and fan motor.
4. **Refrigerant readings** — connect gauges to read suction and liquid pressures, then calculate superheat/subcooling to judge charge. Abnormal readings point to a leak or restriction.
5. **Leak search** — if low, they'll use an electronic detector or dye rather than just topping off.
6. **Compressor test** — check windings and start behavior if the compressor won't run.
7. **Communicating systems** — on AccuLink/AccuComfort units, they'll pull fault history from the control board to confirm.

A good quote names the failed part and the measured reason for it. Be cautious of anyone who wants to "add a pound of refrigerant" without a leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm airflow, dirty filter | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Verify airflow, check coil |
| Ice on coil or copper lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw fully | Find airflow/refrigerant cause |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Low refrigerant / leak | None | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Nothing runs outside | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Warm air, filthy outdoor coil | Dirty condenser | Gently rinse (power off) | Deep coil cleaning |
| Thermostat blank or offline | Dead batteries / comm fault | Replace batteries | Diagnose control/comm wiring |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Compressor failure | None | Test and replace compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location
- **Condenser fan motor:** $400–$700
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,500+ (often makes older units worth replacing)
- **Full system replacement:** $5,000–$12,000+

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older R-22 systems cost far more to charge), and whether your unit is under American Standard's registered parts warranty — check your warranty before authorizing part replacement.

## Related codes

- **American Standard AC freezing up** — airflow and refrigerant faults behind a frozen coil.
- **American Standard AC short cycling** — rapid on/off from thermostat, capacitor, or charge issues.
- **AccuLink communication fault** — thermostat-to-board comm errors on Gold 824 / Platinum 1050 systems.
- **American Standard AC blowing warm air** — closely related overlap with low refrigerant and electrical faults.
