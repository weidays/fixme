## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on American Standard equipment — it's the symptom you're seeing: the system runs (or tries to) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house won't reach the thermostat setpoint. On systems with a communicating thermostat (like the AccuLink / Gold 824 or Platinum controls), you may see a warning or a fault icon, but the cooling problem itself can come from either the outdoor condenser, the indoor air handler, the refrigerant charge, or the controls tying them together.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a $10 filter to a failed compressor, this page ranks the likely causes and shows which you can safely check versus what needs a licensed HVAC tech. Refrigerant work is legally restricted to certified professionals, so this is marked **pro**.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat settings or wiring** — set to "fan on" instead of "cool," wrong program, dead batteries, or a lost communication link on 800-series controls.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power, indoor blower runs and blows warm air.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or fan hums but won't start, or the condenser fan doesn't spin.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so cooling fades in hot weather.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines.
7. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak** — never "normal" loss; means a leak needs repair and recharge.
8. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome, often on older units.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for any homeowner:

- **Air filter:** Pull it. If it's gray or clogged, replace it with a clean pleated filter. This alone fixes many "not cooling" and frozen-coil calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. If your thermostat screen is blank, that's a power/fuse issue.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker (both the indoor and outdoor circuits). Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated resets on a shorting unit are unsafe.
- **Outdoor unit airflow:** Make sure the condenser isn't buried in leaves, grass clippings, or overgrown shrubs. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Vents and registers:** Confirm supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate:** If you have a clogged drain or a full pan, a safety float switch may be shutting the system off. Check the pan; a wet-dry vac on the drain line outlet can clear a light clog.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the lines, turn the system to **Off** and the fan to **On** to thaw it (can take several hours), then start with the filter.

Do **not** open the outdoor unit's electrical box, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will work top-down so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F).
2. **Check power and controls** — confirms 24V at the contactor, tests the low-voltage fuse, and on 800-series communicating systems reads any fault data.
3. **Test the capacitor and contactor** — with a meter; a weak capacitor reads below its rated microfarads.
4. **Inspect the condenser coil and fan** — cleans if dirty, confirms the fan motor spins freely.
5. **Attach gauges** and read pressures/superheat/subcooling — this reveals low charge, a restriction, or a failing compressor. Low charge triggers a **leak search** (electronic detector or dye), not just a top-off.
6. **Check the compressor** — measures amp draw and windings if start components are good but it won't run.

If a tech quotes refrigerant without mentioning a leak search, ask why — repeatedly recharging a leaking system is wasteful and against best practice.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, blower runs | Thermostat set wrong / dirty filter | Set to Cool, replace filter | Verify controls, check charge |
| Outdoor unit dead, indoor runs | Tripped breaker / blown fuse / contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace fuse or contactor, find cause |
| Condenser hums, fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — power hazard | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on copper lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Off + fan to thaw, change filter | Diagnose airflow vs. leak, recharge |
| Cooling fades on hottest days | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse exterior gently with hose | Deep coil clean, check pressures |
| Runs constantly, weak cooling | Low refrigerant (leak) | None | Leak search, repair, evacuate, recharge |
| Nothing runs, thermostat blank | Low-voltage fuse / power | Check batteries and breaker | Trace short, replace 3A fuse |
| Loud hum then trips breaker | Failing compressor | None | Amp/winding test, compressor quote |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries/replacement:** $0–$300 depending on model.
- **Low-voltage (control board) fuse:** $75–$150.
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$350.
- **Contactor:** $150–$300.
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300.
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair + recharge:** $500–$1,600, higher if a coil must be replaced.
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500+.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often close to the cost of a new condenser — ask about replacement vs. repair, and check your warranty).

American Standard registered units often carry a strong parts warranty; have your model/serial handy, as covered parts can cut these costs significantly.

## Related codes

- **AC not turning on at all** — power, contactor, or control-board faults.
- **AC freezing up / frozen coil** — airflow and refrigerant issues.
- **AccuLink / 800-series communication fault** — thermostat-to-unit link errors on communicating systems.
- **Outdoor fan not running** — capacitor or fan motor failure.
- **AC short cycling** — thermostat, airflow, or refrigerant-related shutdowns.
