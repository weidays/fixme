## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's a symptom. Your American Standard air conditioner may be running (fan spinning, thermostat calling for cool) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the setpoint.

On units with a standard control board, there's often no error displayed at all — the system simply can't produce or move cold air. On communicating **AccuLink** systems (paired with an AccuLink/Gold or Platinum thermostat), you may see a fault or alert message on the thermostat, which helps narrow the cause. Because the display behavior varies by system generation, don't assume a blank thermostat means nothing is wrong.

The underlying problem is almost always one of three things: not enough airflow, not enough refrigerant, or an electrical/component failure at the outdoor unit.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, causing weak cooling and eventually a frozen evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat setting or fault** — set to "fan on" instead of "cool," wrong programming, or dead batteries on battery-powered models.
3. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat when the fins are packed with dirt, grass, or cottonwood.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling capacity and can freeze the indoor coil. Requires a pro; it's never a "top-off."
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a common failure; the compressor or condenser fan won't start or struggles.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that sends power to the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
7. **Condenser fan motor failure** — outdoor fan not spinning leads to overheating and shutdown.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — often a downstream symptom of low airflow or low refrigerant.
9. **Compressor failure** — the worst case; the unit hums or trips the breaker but won't cool.
10. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor disconnect or panel breaker has opened.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. If none resolve it, stop and call a technician.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, do not keep resetting — call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit airflow:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance on all sides.
- **Condensate drain:** If your indoor unit has a float switch and the drain is clogged, the system may shut down. Check for standing water in the pan.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and set the fan to **On** to thaw it (can take several hours), then check the filter.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, remove electrical panels on the outdoor unit, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work through this sequence — use it to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** and check thermostat operation and the call for cooling.
2. **Measure temperature split** across the indoor coil (return vs. supply air) — a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F.
3. **Inspect airflow** — filter, blower, and coil condition.
4. **Check electrical at the outdoor unit** — capacitor microfarad reading, contactor condition, voltage to the compressor and fan.
5. **Read refrigerant pressures** with gauges and measure superheat/subcooling to confirm the charge. Low readings point to a leak, which they should locate before recharging.
6. **On AccuLink systems,** connect to the control board to pull stored fault data.
7. **Test the compressor and fan motor** windings and amp draw if a component failure is suspected.

Be cautious of any tech who recommends "just adding refrigerant" without leak testing — refrigerant doesn't get used up, so low charge means a leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm air, indoor coil iced | Dirty filter or low airflow | Replace filter, thaw coil, open vents | Check blower, clean coil |
| Ice on refrigerant lines | Low refrigerant or airflow | Turn off, thaw, check filter | Leak search, recharge |
| Outdoor unit fan spinning, no cooling | Low refrigerant or bad capacitor | Clear debris around unit | Test capacitor, check charge |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor running | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, test power |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Failed capacitor or compressor | None — call a pro | Test/replace capacitor or compressor |
| Fan won't spin, unit hot | Condenser fan motor failure | Turn off to prevent damage | Replace fan motor |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Short in compressor or wiring | Stop resetting | Isolate and repair fault |
| No response, blank thermostat | Dead batteries or control fault | Replace batteries | Test control board/wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against full system replacement on older units)

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older R-22 units cost more), and whether the part is under American Standard's warranty. Always ask whether your unit is still under parts warranty before approving major repairs.
