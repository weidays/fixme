## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's a symptom. Your American Standard air conditioner is either running without producing cold air, short-cycling, or not turning on at all. On communicating **AccuLink / Platinum** systems, you may see an actual error on the thermostat or indoor board; on standard 24V systems there's no code at all, just warm air.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a $15 filter to a failed compressor, the goal here is to help you rule out the cheap, safe stuff first — then understand what a technician should be checking so you can sanity-check any quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, ices the indoor coil, and kills cooling capacity. The single most common cause.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a communication fault on AccuLink systems.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser has its own breaker; if it trips, the indoor blower still runs and blows warm air.
4. **Dirty condenser coil** — outdoor coil caked with dirt, grass, or cottonwood can't reject heat.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a common failure that stops the compressor and/or condenser fan from starting.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling, ice on the lines, and eventually a locked-out compressor. Requires a pro.
7. **Failed condenser fan motor** — the outdoor fan won't spin, causing overheating and shutdown.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; the whole coil turns to ice.
9. **Contactor or control board failure** — the outdoor contactor won't pull in, or (on communicating units) a board fault.
10. **Compressor failure** — the worst case; often preceded by hard starts, breaker trips, or humming without cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for any homeowner — no tools required beyond a screwdriver:

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Then run the system 30 minutes and recheck.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. If the screen is blank, that's a power/wiring issue.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the **outdoor unit** (often a separate double-pole breaker) and the indoor air handler. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated resets on a shorted unit are dangerous.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Some homes have a pull-out disconnect box near the condenser. Make sure it's fully seated.
- **Supply and return vents:** Confirm they're open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate line:** A clogged condensate drain can trip a float switch and shut cooling down. Check for standing water in the drain pan and confirm the outdoor drain line is dripping when running.
- **Outdoor unit airflow:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser and gently rinse the outside of the coil with a garden hose (power off).

If cooling doesn't return after these checks, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should follow a logical sequence — if a quote skips straight to "you need refrigerant" without measurements, be skeptical:

1. **Confirm the complaint** and check thermostat call for cooling (24V signal or AccuLink status).
2. **Measure the temperature split** across the indoor coil (return vs. supply air) — a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F.
3. **Inspect airflow** — filter, blower, and evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
4. **Check outdoor electrical** — voltage at the contactor, capacitor test with a multimeter, and confirm the fan and compressor draw proper amperage.
5. **Connect refrigerant gauges** to read suction and head pressures, then calculate **superheat and subcooling** to judge the charge.
6. **Leak search** if the charge is low — refrigerant doesn't get "used up," so low charge always means a leak.
7. **On AccuLink systems**, read fault history from the thermostat/board.

Ask for the measured numbers (temp split, capacitor microfarads, subcooling). Legitimate diagnoses come with data.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indoor blower runs, air is warm | Outdoor breaker tripped / dirty filter | Reset breaker once, replace filter | Diagnose why breaker trips; check electrical |
| Ice on refrigerant lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Replace filter, turn off AC to thaw | Find/repair leak, recharge, check airflow |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed capacitor or fan motor | None — power off | Test/replace capacitor or condenser fan motor |
| Humming outside, no cooling | Bad capacitor or failing compressor | Power off, don't repeat resets | Test capacitor; assess compressor |
| Weak cooling, long run times | Dirty condenser coil / low charge | Rinse outdoor coil, replace filter | Clean coil, verify charge/subcooling |
| Nothing runs, thermostat blank | Dead batteries / control power | Replace batteries, check breaker | Trace 24V fault, check board/transformer |
| Water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate line, float trip | Clear standing water, check drain | Clear line, service pan/pump |
| Thermostat shows AccuLink error | Communication/board fault | Note the code | Read fault, inspect wiring/board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor; varies by region and system):

- **Air filter:** $0–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Condensate line clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$450
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (more if the leak is in the coil)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500+
- **Control/communicating board:** $400–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth weighing against replacing the unit)

If your system is under 10 years old, check for an American Standard **parts warranty** — many components are covered, though labor usually isn't.

## Related codes

- **AccuLink communication fault** — thermostat can't talk to the indoor/outdoor unit.
- **AC frozen coil / iced lines** — airflow or charge problem.
- **AC short-cycling** — turns on and off rapidly; often capacitor, charge, or control related.
- **Outdoor fan won't start** — capacitor or fan motor.
- **Compressor won't start / hard start** — capacitor, start components, or failing compressor.
