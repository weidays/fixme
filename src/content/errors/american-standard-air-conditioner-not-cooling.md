## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's the symptom you're experiencing. Your American Standard air conditioner may be running, blowing air, or completely silent, but the indoor temperature won't come down.

On systems with a basic thermostat, there is no error code at all: you just get warm air. On systems paired with an American Standard AccuLink or comfort control, the equipment may log a specific fault (communication error, low-pressure lockout, sensor fault) that a technician can read at the control or outdoor board. If you have one of these thermostats, note any code or alert it shows — it saves diagnostic time.

Because "not cooling" can be caused by anything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, this page is rated **pro**: a few checks are safe for homeowners, but the common real causes (refrigerant, electrical, compressor) require a licensed HVAC technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — restricts return air, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil solid.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; the system runs but pushes little or no cold air.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a failed thermostat not calling for cooling.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power, so the compressor never runs.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — very common; the compressor or fan hums but won't start, or the outdoor fan won't spin.
6. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so nothing energizes.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling that gets worse over time; always indicates a leak, never "used up" charge.
8. **Failed condenser fan motor** — outdoor unit overheats and shuts down on high-pressure protection.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive cause; unit trips or won't cool at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should attempt:

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause and the cheapest fix.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the fan to **AUTO**, and the setpoint several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor AC breakers in your panel. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris away from the condenser so it can breathe. Gently rinse the outside fins with a garden hose (power off).
- **Condensate drain:** If your indoor unit has a float switch and the drain is clogged, cooling can shut off. You can clear a visible clog with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor drain end and add pan tablets.
- **Check for ice:** If you see frost or ice on the indoor coil or the copper lines, **turn the system to OFF and set the fan to ON** to thaw it (can take several hours). Do not run cooling on an iced coil — call a pro to find why it froze.

If these don't restore cooling, the remaining causes involve refrigerant or electrical parts and require a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence — knowing it helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Reads any stored codes** on an AccuLink/comfort control and confirms the thermostat is calling for cooling.
2. **Verifies power and control voltage** — checks the breaker, contactor, and 24V signal reaching the outdoor unit.
3. **Tests the run capacitor** with a meter — a cheap, common failure they should always check.
4. **Checks the contactor** for pitting and proper pull-in.
5. **Confirms the compressor and condenser fan** actually start and draw normal amperage.
6. **Connects refrigerant gauges** to read pressures and measures superheat/subcooling to judge the charge.
7. **Inspects the coil and airflow** for freezing, and searches for leaks if the charge is low (dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen).

Be cautious of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without measuring pressures and testing the capacitor and contactor first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter / blocked vents | Replace filter, open vents | Verify airflow, check blower |
| Indoor coil/lines iced over | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn cooling OFF, fan ON to thaw | Find cause; check charge & coil |
| Fan blows but air isn't cold | Low refrigerant leak | None — call pro | Leak search, seal, recharge |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor, wiring |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Bad fan motor or capacitor | None — call pro | Replace motor/capacitor |
| No cooling, unit trips repeatedly | Compressor or electrical fault | Stop resetting; call pro | Diagnose compressor/electrical |
| Cooling stops, water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain / float switch | Clear drain, add tablets | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor; varies by region and model):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$15 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150–$250 pro
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $400–$700
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 (more for AccuLink comfort controls)
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ — often the point where full-unit replacement is worth comparing

Note: older American Standard units using R-22 refrigerant cost significantly more to recharge, which can push you toward replacement. Newer units use R-410A or R-454B.

## Related codes

- **AC blowing warm air** — overlapping symptom, usually refrigerant or electrical.
- **AC frozen evaporator coil** — a specific cause of "not cooling."
- **AC won't turn on** — no power vs. no cooling.
- **AccuLink communication fault** — thermostat-to-equipment error on communicating systems.
- **Condensate float switch open** — safety shutoff that stops cooling when the drain backs up.
