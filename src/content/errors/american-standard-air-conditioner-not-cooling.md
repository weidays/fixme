## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single blink code — it's the symptom you notice when your American Standard air conditioner runs but the house won't reach the setpoint, or the outdoor unit won't start at all. On communicating **AccuLink** systems paired with a Gold or Platinum thermostat, the control may log a specific fault (for example a temperature-sensor or high-pressure fault) that points you to the cause. On older single-stage **Silver series** units there is usually no code at all — you diagnose by symptom.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a $10 filter to a $2,500 compressor, this page walks you through the safe checks first, then what a technician should do, so you can sanity-check any quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (Heat vs Cool), or bad wiring/programming.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the outdoor fan or compressor hums but won't start.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — condenser gets no power.
6. **Dirty condenser coil** — outdoor coil caked with dirt/cottonwood can't reject heat.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — weak cooling, ice on the line, longer run times.
8. **Failed contactor or capacitor in the disconnect** — no signal reaching the compressor.
9. **Tripped high- or low-pressure safety switch** — often secondary to a dirty coil or leak.
10. **Failed compressor** — the least common but most expensive outcome.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the #1 cause of weak or no cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler and condenser. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit airflow:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser so it can breathe.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant line or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** but leave the fan running to thaw it (can take several hours). Then replace the filter before restarting.
- **Condensate:** A full drain pan or clogged condensate line can trip a safety float switch and shut cooling down. Check for standing water near the air handler.

If cooling doesn't return after these steps, the fault is inside the sealed system or electrical components — that's a pro job.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech should work in this order, and you can listen for these steps in their explanation:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** — confirm 24V signal reaches the contactor when the thermostat calls.
2. **Check power and controls** — voltage at the disconnect, contactor pull-in, and capacitor test with a meter (a weak capacitor reads below its rated microfarads).
3. **Inspect airflow** — filter, blower operation, and coil for ice or dirt.
4. **Measure refrigerant charge** — connect gauges, read suction/liquid pressures, and calculate superheat/subcooling. Low readings mean a leak, not just "add a pound."
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, they should find the leak with electronic detector or dye, not just top it off.
6. **Check safeties** — high/low pressure switches and any AccuLink fault codes stored at the thermostat.
7. **Compressor test** — winding resistance and amp draw if all else checks out.

Be cautious of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without a leak search or electrical diagnosis.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and duct |
| Outdoor fan spins, no cold air | Failed capacitor / compressor won't start | None — check breaker only | Test and replace capacitor or contactor |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find cause; charge/repair leak |
| Nothing runs outdoors | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Runs constantly, never cools enough | Low refrigerant leak or dirty coil | Clean around condenser | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Thermostat blank | Dead batteries or wiring | Replace batteries | Trace 24V/control wiring |
| Cooling stops, water near air handler | Clogged condensate / float switch | Check for standing water | Clear drain line, test float |
| Cools then shuts down repeatedly | Tripped pressure safety | Stop resetting; call pro | Diagnose pressure fault source |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10 – $40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/start capacitor:** $120 – $250
- **Contactor:** $150 – $300
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100 – $250
- **Condensate line clearing / float switch:** $100 – $250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600 – $1,600 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000 – $2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500 – $2,800+ (often worth comparing to a new condenser if out of warranty)

American Standard registered systems often carry a 10-year parts limited warranty — check your registration before paying for a major part, as it can cut costs significantly.

## Related codes

- **AC frozen coil / iced line** — a common cause of no cooling
- **Outdoor unit won't start** — capacitor/contactor faults
- **AccuLink communication fault** — communicating thermostat can't reach the outdoor board
- **Condensate float switch tripped** — safety shutdown from a full drain pan
- **High-pressure lockout** — often tied to a dirty condenser coil or overcharge
