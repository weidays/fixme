## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's a symptom. Your American Standard air conditioner may be running, blowing air, or completely silent, but the air coming from your vents isn't cold enough (or isn't cold at all). On systems with a communicating thermostat (AccuLink / Trane Link enabled), you may see a temperature-difference or comfort alert rather than a numeric code.

Because American Standard builds several tiers — single-stage (Silver), two-stage (Gold), and variable-speed (Platinum with AccuComfort) — the exact behavior and any on-board diagnostics vary. A basic single-stage condenser has no display at all, so troubleshooting is largely mechanical and electrical. Communicating systems may store fault history in the thermostat or control board, which a technician can read.

The goal of this page is to help you rule out the safe, simple causes before you pay for a service call, and to help you judge whether a quote you've been given is reasonable.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops efficiency, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat exchange and cool air stops.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or condenser fan can't start, so the outdoor unit hums or does nothing.
4. **Low refrigerant charge (leak)** — cooling weakens gradually; often paired with ice or hissing. Requires a licensed tech.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is caked with dirt/grass, so it can't reject heat.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so the condenser never energizes.
7. **Condenser fan motor failure** — the outdoor fan doesn't spin, the unit overheats and shuts down on high-pressure protection.
8. **Thermostat or control/communication fault** — wrong settings, dead batteries, or a communication error on Link systems.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive; the unit runs but can't compress refrigerant, or won't start at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Set the fan to AUTO, not ON.
- **Batteries:** If your thermostat is battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank, replace them.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of weak cooling and frozen coils.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Frozen coil / ice:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF and let it fully thaw (a few hours) before running again. Persistent icing means a real problem — get it diagnosed.
- **Condensate:** If your unit has a float switch and the drain is clogged, cooling may stop. Clear a visible, accessible drain line only.

Do **not** open the condenser electrical panel, touch the capacitor, or add refrigerant. Those are pro tasks.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified technician will typically:

1. Confirm the complaint and check thermostat settings and call for cooling.
2. Measure the **temperature split** across the indoor coil (return vs. supply air — a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
3. Inspect the air filter and evaporator coil for dirt or ice.
4. Test the **capacitor** with a meter (microfarad reading vs. the rated value).
5. Check the **contactor** for pitting and confirm 24V control voltage and line voltage.
6. Verify the **condenser fan** and compressor amp draws against nameplate values.
7. Connect gauges to read **refrigerant pressures and superheat/subcooling** — this reveals low charge, a leak, or overcharge. (Charge should only be adjusted after a leak search on American Standard sealed systems.)
8. On communicating Platinum/Gold Link systems, read stored fault codes from the thermostat or control board.

If a quote skips the pressure/electrical measurements and jumps straight to "you need refrigerant" or "you need a new compressor," ask for the readings that support it.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but isn't cold | Dirty filter / frozen coil | Replace filter; thaw coil | Check airflow, coil, charge |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — leave it off | Test & replace capacitor |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit dead | Bad contactor or breaker | Reset breaker once | Test contactor & control voltage |
| Ice on refrigerant lines | Low airflow or low charge | Replace filter, thaw, then observe | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Weak cooling, gets worse over weeks | Refrigerant leak | None | Leak search, seal, recharge |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Fan motor or capacitor | Shut unit off to prevent overheat | Replace fan motor/capacitor |
| No cool, thermostat blank | Dead batteries / comms fault | Replace batteries | Diagnose control/wiring |
| Runs constantly, never satisfies | Undersized charge / dirty condenser | Clean debris around unit | Clean coil, verify charge |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor, varies by region and model tier):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$200.
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400.
- **Contactor:** $150–$350.
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650.
- **Coil cleaning (condenser/evaporator):** $150–$450.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location; an evaporator coil leak is at the high end).
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$1,800+ — often worth weighing against replacing the outdoor unit, especially on older systems.

Variable-speed Platinum/AccuComfort units use proprietary boards and inverter-driven compressors, which cost more to diagnose and repair than single-stage Silver models. Check whether your compressor is still under American Standard's registered parts warranty (typically 10 years when registered) — that can dramatically cut the bill, though labor is usually not covered.
