## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a fault code — American Standard air conditioners are largely mechanical outdoor units controlled by your thermostat and, in variable-speed models, a control board. The unit runs (or tries to) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches setpoint.

Because the outdoor unit itself rarely displays diagnostic codes, most troubleshooting happens at the thermostat, the indoor air handler or furnace, and the outdoor condenser. Higher-end Platinum series systems with communicating thermostats (like the AccuLink platform) *may* show a fault or communication message on the thermostat screen — behavior varies by model and thermostat pairing, so check your specific thermostat manual.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a scheduling/setpoint issue.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the single most common "real" cause of gradual cooling loss.
5. **Frozen evaporator coil** — a downstream result of low airflow or low refrigerant.
6. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the fan or compressor hums but won't start.
7. **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil** — can't reject heat, so cooling collapses on hot days.
8. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that starts the outdoor unit is burnt or stuck.
9. **Compressor failure** — the worst case; the unit can't move refrigerant at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here — do not open the electrical panel of the unit or touch refrigerant lines.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the temperature is below room temp. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of poor cooling.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker feeding the AC. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated tripping signals a real fault.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance on all sides.
- **Condensate drain:** If you see water pooling near the indoor unit, the drain may be clogged — a full float switch can shut the system down.
- **Check for ice:** If you see frost or ice on the refrigerant lines or coil, turn the AC OFF and run the fan only to let it thaw. Do not run cooling on a frozen system.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A quote should reflect actual measurement, not guessing. A competent tech will:

- Verify thermostat call and confirm 24V control signal reaches the outdoor unit.
- Measure voltage at the contactor and test the capacitor with a meter.
- Check the condenser coil and blower for airflow restrictions.
- Connect gauges to read suction and liquid pressures, then calculate **superheat and subcooling** — this is how they confirm a refrigerant charge issue versus airflow.
- If refrigerant is low, perform a **leak search** (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test) — a legitimate shop won't just "top off."
- Test compressor windings and amp draw if a compressor failure is suspected.

If a tech proposes adding refrigerant without looking for a leak, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air from vents is warm | Outdoor unit not running / no power | Reset breaker once; check thermostat mode | Test contactor, capacitor, control wiring |
| Weak airflow, house won't cool | Dirty filter or frozen coil | Replace filter; run fan to thaw ice | Diagnose airflow and refrigerant charge |
| Ice on refrigerant lines | Low refrigerant or low airflow | Turn off cooling; thaw with fan | Find/repair leak, recharge to spec |
| Outdoor fan hums but won't spin | Failed capacitor | None — electrical | Replace run capacitor |
| Outdoor unit runs but no cold air | Low refrigerant / bad compressor | None | Leak test, charge, or compressor test |
| Water near indoor unit, system off | Clogged condensate drain / float switch | Clear drain if accessible | Clear line, test float switch |
| Cooling fades on hottest days | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse coil gently with hose | Deep coil cleaning, charge check |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **TXV / metering device:** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against full-unit replacement)

Note: Systems using older R-410A refrigerant are seeing rising refrigerant prices as the industry transitions; ask your tech which refrigerant your unit uses, as it affects recharge cost significantly.

## Related codes

- **American Standard AC frozen coil** — the downstream effect of the airflow and refrigerant issues above.
- **American Standard AC blower not running** — indoor airflow failures that mimic "not cooling."
- **AccuLink communication fault** — on communicating Platinum systems, a thermostat-to-unit comms error can stop cooling; check your thermostat manual for the specific message.
- **Condensate float switch tripped** — a full drain pan shuts the system down as a protective measure.
