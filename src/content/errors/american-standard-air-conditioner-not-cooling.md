## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your American Standard air conditioner runs but the house won't reach the setpoint, or the air from the vents is warm. On communicating systems with an AccuLink or Gold/Platinum control thermostat, you may also see a fault or error message on the display, but many conventional single-stage American Standard units give no code at all — they simply stop cooling.

The root cause falls into one of three buckets: **airflow problems** (dirty filter, blocked coil, weak blower), **electrical problems** (tripped breaker, bad capacitor, failed contactor or control board), or **refrigerant problems** (a leak that has lowered the charge). The right fix — and the cost — depends heavily on which bucket you're in, which is why this is rated a pro-level issue once the safe homeowner checks are done.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, can freeze the indoor coil, and is the single most common cause of weak or no cooling.
2. **Tripped breaker or disconnect for the outdoor unit** — if only the outdoor condenser lost power, the indoor blower keeps running and blows warm air.
3. **Thermostat issue** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs. "auto"), or a miswired/failing communicating thermostat.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan motor won't start; you may hear a hum or clicking at the outdoor unit.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — a coil caked with dirt and cottonwood can't reject heat, so cooling capacity drops sharply.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil.
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so the condenser never starts.
8. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes long run times, warm air, and often a frozen coil. Requires leak repair, not just a "top-off."
9. **Failed compressor or control/AccuLink board** — the most expensive outcome, usually after other symptoms are ignored.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should attempt. Stop and call a pro if none of them resolves it.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to **Cool**, fan to **Auto**, and lower the setpoint 5°F. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers/disconnect:** Check the electrical panel for the indoor and outdoor circuits. If a breaker is clearly tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop — call a technician.
- **Outdoor unit:** Make sure the pull-out disconnect near the condenser is fully seated. Clear leaves, grass, and debris away so air can flow (aim for 2 ft of clearance).
- **Vents and registers:** Confirm supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and set the fan to **On** to let it thaw for a few hours before calling — running it frozen makes things worse.
- **Condensate drain:** If your indoor unit has a safety float switch and the drain is clogged, cooling can shut off. You can pour a cup of water through the drain and check for a wet overflow pan.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech works from cheapest to most expensive, and you can sanity-check their quote against this sequence:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures the temperature split across the indoor coil (typically 16–22°F on a healthy system) and confirms the thermostat is calling for cooling.
2. **Check power and controls** — tests voltage at the disconnect, the contactor, and (on communicating models) reads any AccuLink fault codes at the control board.
3. **Test the capacitor and contactor** — a meter reading on the run capacitor (compared to its rated microfarads) quickly confirms a weak cap; the contactor is inspected for pitting.
4. **Inspect airflow** — filter, blower operation, and condenser coil condition.
5. **Gauge the refrigerant** — connects manifold gauges to read pressures and superheat/subcooling. Low readings point to a leak, which they should then locate (electronic sniffer, dye, or bubbles).
6. **Compressor check** — measures amp draw and windings if the compressor won't start or is overheating.

A good tech tells you *what* they found (a specific reading), not just "you need refrigerant." Beware of any quote to add refrigerant without mentioning a leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, house won't cool | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check blower, ductwork |
| Fan blows warm, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker / dead capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test capacitor, contactor, wiring |
| Outdoor fan runs, but no cold air | Failed compressor or low charge | None | Gauge refrigerant, test compressor |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find leak, recharge, check blower |
| Long run times, never reaches setpoint | Dirty condenser coil / low charge | Rinse coil gently, clear debris | Measure subcooling, clean coil |
| Humming at condenser, no start | Bad run capacitor | None | Replace capacitor |
| System clicks then shuts off | Failed contactor or control board | None | Replace contactor / board |
| No cooling, water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain, float trip | Clear drain, flush | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices including labor; your area and warranty status will move these.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$300
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$650
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (more if the coil must be replaced)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,000+
- **Blower motor (variable-speed/ECM):** $600–$1,200
- **AccuLink / control board:** $400–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often the point where replacing the unit makes more sense)

If your American Standard system is under 10 years old, check the parts warranty — many components are covered, so you may pay labor only.

## Related codes

- **Frozen evaporator coil** — often the root cause behind a "not cooling" complaint.
- **AC not turning on** — no fan or compressor at all, usually power or contactor related.
- **Outdoor unit running but not cooling** — points to refrigerant or compressor problems.
- **AccuLink communication fault** — on Gold/Platinum communicating systems, a control-board or wiring fault can stop cooling entirely.
- **Condensate overflow / float switch trip** — a wet-safety shutdown that mimics "not cooling."
