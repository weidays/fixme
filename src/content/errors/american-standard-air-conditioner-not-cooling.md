## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a numeric fault code on American Standard air conditioners — it's a symptom. Your outdoor unit and indoor air handler may be running, but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

American Standard's higher-end systems (Platinum and Gold series paired with an AccuLink or Nexia-compatible thermostat) may show a text message or a numbered fault on the thermostat display when a component faults. Basic single-stage systems on a conventional 24V thermostat give you no message at all — you just notice warm air. Because the exact readout varies by control board and thermostat, use this page to narrow the cause, then confirm with the specific message your system shows.

Cooling depends on three things working together: airflow across the indoor coil, a proper refrigerant charge, and the compressor moving that refrigerant. A failure in any one produces "not cooling."

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, can ice the indoor coil, kills cooling.
2. **Thermostat setting or wiring issue** — set to "heat" or "fan," dead batteries, or a lost connection to the outdoor unit.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan won't start; you may hear a hum or clicking.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is packed with dirt/grass and can't reject heat.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks all cooling.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — cooling weakens over weeks, coil may freeze. Requires a licensed tech.
8. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit fails to close.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cold.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it out and look at it. If it's gray and clogged, replace it. This is the single most common fix.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker (indoor air handler and outdoor unit often have separate breakers). Reset **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure vents aren't closed, blocked by furniture, or covered by rugs. Closing too many vents starves the system.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for leaves, grass clippings, or debris packed against the coil fins. Gently clear obvious blockage with the power off.
- **Condensate drain:** If the indoor unit sits over a full drain pan, or a safety float switch has cut the system off, the drain may be clogged. You can clear a visible clog and empty a full pan.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw for several hours before running again.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech will:

1. Verify thermostat call and confirm 24V signal reaching the outdoor unit.
2. Check incoming voltage, the contactor, and test the run capacitor with a meter.
3. Inspect and, if needed, clean the condenser and evaporator coils.
4. Attach gauges to read suction and liquid pressures and take temperature readings (superheat/subcooling) to judge the refrigerant charge.
5. If charge is low, perform a leak search (dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen pressure test) — a low charge always means a leak.
6. Test compressor windings and amp draw to confirm whether the compressor is healthy.

A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search is a red flag — refrigerant doesn't get used up, it leaks.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower, ductwork |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on coil or refrigerant line | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find airflow/refrigerant cause |
| Cooling slowly weakened over weeks | Refrigerant leak | None | Leak search and repair, recharge |
| Outdoor coil caked with dirt | Dirty condenser | Rinse gently, power off | Deep coil clean |
| Runs constantly, never cold | Failed compressor | None | Test and replace compressor |
| No display / dead thermostat | Dead batteries or wiring | Replace batteries | Check thermostat wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,800 (often worth comparing against full-system replacement on older units)

Note: R-22 systems cost far more to recharge than newer R-410A units due to R-22's phase-out. If your American Standard unit is 12+ years old and needs a compressor or coil, get a replacement quote too.

## Related codes

- **AC freezing up / iced coil** — usually low airflow or low refrigerant.
- **AC blowing warm air** — same family of causes covered here.
- **Outdoor unit not turning on** — capacitor, contactor, or breaker faults.
- **AccuLink / Nexia thermostat communication fault** — on Platinum and Gold variable-speed systems, a lost link between thermostat and outdoor board can stop cooling.

Because message text varies by control board and thermostat model, match any on-screen code to your American Standard system's manual before ordering parts.
