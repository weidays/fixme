## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the most common complaint homeowners bring to a service call. Your American Standard air conditioner may be running (indoor blower and/or outdoor fan spinning) but producing warm or barely-cool air, or it may not start at all.

American Standard split systems (4A7, 4A6 condensers) and packaged units (TTA, TTV) rely on a sealed refrigerant loop, a compressor, and matched indoor coil to move heat outside. When any link in that chain underperforms, you feel it as poor cooling. On variable-speed and communicating systems (Platinum series with AccuLink), the thermostat may show a fault or "cooling demand not met" message; single-stage units give no code at all — you just get warm air.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a miswired/failed thermostat.
3. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan hums but won't start; extremely common on units 8+ years old.
4. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil caked with dust, grass, or cottonwood can't reject heat.
5. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer.
6. **Low refrigerant / leak** — an undercharged system cools weakly; refrigerant doesn't get "used up," so low charge means a leak.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor disconnect fuse or panel breaker.
8. **Failed contactor** — pitted or stuck contactor won't energize the outdoor unit.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; often the last suspect after others are ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here — do not open the electrical compartment or touch the capacitor.

- **Filter:** Pull the return-air or furnace filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it and let the system run.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery model. Set the fan to AUTO.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler and the condenser. Reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents:** Make sure supply registers are open and returns aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a safety float switch and shut off cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan; a wet/dry vac on the outdoor drain line often clears it.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris at least 2 feet around the condenser. If the coil is dirty, gently rinse it with a garden hose (power off).
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or coil, turn the system to OFF and set the fan to ON to thaw it — running it iced up can damage the compressor.

Never reset a unit repeatedly if it keeps tripping, and never bypass a float switch or safety control.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical path, and knowing it helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply and return air temperature; a healthy split usually shows a 16–22°F drop across the coil.
2. **Check airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Test electrical** — measures voltage at the disconnect, tests the run capacitor with a meter (a weak cap reads well below its rated µF), and inspects the contactor.
4. **Gauge the refrigerant** — connects manifold gauges, checks pressures, and measures superheat/subcooling. Low subcooling with low pressure points to a leak.
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, uses an electronic detector or dye to find where it's leaking (they should always locate the leak, not just top off).
6. **Compressor test** — checks amp draw, windings, and whether it's starting. On two-stage/variable units, they'll read the outdoor control board for stored faults.

Be wary of any tech who wants to "add refrigerant" without leak-searching — that's a temporary patch on a sealed system.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, indoor fan runs | Outdoor unit off / bad capacitor | Check breaker, filter | Test/replace capacitor, contactor |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw with fan ON, replace filter | Find airflow restriction or leak |
| Outdoor fan runs, compressor hums | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Replace dual run capacitor |
| Weak cooling, long run times | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Rinse coil, replace filter | Gauge pressures, clean, repair leak |
| Nothing turns on | Tripped breaker or blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Replace disconnect fuse, find cause |
| Cools then shuts off | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear drain line | Repair drain, test float switch |
| No cooling, high amp draw | Failing compressor | None — call pro | Compressor diagnosis/replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 professional
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$300 installed
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **TXV / metering device:** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$1,800+ (often worth weighing against a new unit if out of warranty)

American Standard registered systems often carry a 10-year parts warranty — if you're the original owner, ask whether the failed part is covered before approving replacement; you'll typically still pay labor.

## Related codes

- **American Standard AccuLink / communicating fault messages** — variable-speed systems report cooling errors on the thermostat.
- **AC freezing up** — related low-airflow and low-charge causes.
- **Outdoor fan not spinning** — a common capacitor-related overlap.
- **AC blowing warm air** — same symptom cluster on single-stage units.

*Single-stage American Standard units (Silver series) give no electronic codes — diagnosis is by measurement. Platinum and communicating systems store faults on the outdoor board and thermostat, so behavior varies by model.*
