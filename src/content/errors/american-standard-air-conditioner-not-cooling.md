## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single flashing fault code — it's the symptom homeowners report when an American Standard air conditioner runs but the house stays warm, or the system won't start a cooling cycle at all. It covers a wide range of underlying conditions, from a clogged filter to a dead compressor.

On systems with an American Standard **AccuLink communicating control** (common on Platinum and some Gold series), the thermostat or control board may display a diagnostic message or fault number that points to a specific subsystem. On standard 24V setups, there's no code — you're diagnosing purely by behavior. Because the exact readout varies by model and board, treat any on-screen message as a starting clue, not a final answer.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause of weak or no cooling. Starves the indoor coil and can freeze it.
2. **Thermostat settings or dead batteries** — set to the wrong mode, wrong temperature, or a battery-powered thermostat with a dying battery.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler has lost power while the other half still runs.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure. The compressor or condenser fan won't start; you may hear humming or clicking.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is caked with dirt/grass, so the system can't reject heat.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling capacity, ice on the lines, or a hissing sound. Requires a certified tech.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — often downstream of low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks all cooling.
8. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit doesn't pull in.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor or blower motor** — no airflow across a coil.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome, sometimes the end result of ignored earlier problems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the electrical panels on the outdoor unit, touch the capacitor, or handle refrigerant lines.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. If it's battery-powered, install fresh AA batteries.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for tripped breakers serving the AC and air handler. Reset **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — don't keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture, rugs, or closed doors.
- **Condensate drain:** If your system has a safety float switch and the drain is clogged, it may shut down cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan and clear the visible drain line if you can reach it.
- **Outdoor unit:** Visually check that the condenser isn't buried in leaves, grass clippings, or debris. Gently clear obstructions — power off first — but don't remove any panels.
- **Ice:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system OFF (fan can stay ON) and let it thaw fully. Running a frozen system makes things worse.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician follows a logical sequence — knowing it helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint and airflow** — checks filter, blower operation, and return/supply temperatures.
2. **Confirm power and controls** — tests 24V signal, checks the contactor pulls in, and reads any AccuLink fault data on communicating systems.
3. **Test the capacitor** — measures microfarads against the rating printed on it; a weak cap is a cheap, common fix.
4. **Measure electrical draw** — checks compressor and fan motor amperage against nameplate.
5. **Take refrigerant readings** — connects gauges, measures superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct. Low readings mean a leak search.
6. **Inspect coils and components** — condenser coil cleanliness, evaporator condition, fan motors, and compressor health.

Be cautious of any tech who wants to "just add refrigerant" without discussing a leak — that's a temporary patch, not a repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check blower, ductwork |
| System won't turn on at all | Dead thermostat battery / tripped breaker | Replace batteries, reset breaker once | Test control wiring and transformer |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on lines/coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find cause, check charge |
| Runs constantly, never cools enough | Low refrigerant (leak) | None — call pro | Leak search and repair, recharge |
| Outdoor fan spinning, indoor warm | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris around unit | Deep-clean coil |
| Loud buzz then nothing | Failed contactor | None — call pro | Replace contactor |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Compressor or electrical short | Stop resetting — call pro | Diagnose short/compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 installed
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against system replacement, especially on older units)

Communicating AccuLink systems can carry higher diagnostic labor because of specialized controls, so a slightly higher service fee isn't necessarily a red flag.

## Related codes

- **AC frozen / iced coil** — often the same root causes (airflow or refrigerant).
- **AC blowing warm air** — closely related symptom set.
- **AccuLink communication fault** — control-board or wiring issue on communicating systems.
- **Outdoor unit not turning on** — capacitor, contactor, or power-loss overlap.
- **Short cycling** — frequent on/off tied to airflow, refrigerant, or control faults.
