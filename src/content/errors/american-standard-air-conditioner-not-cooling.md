## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your American Standard air conditioner runs but the house won't get cold, or the system won't turn on at all. Unlike a furnace flash code, most American Standard/Trane condensers don't display a numeric error for cooling loss, though systems paired with an **AccuLink (ComfortLink II) thermostat** may log alerts or communication faults.

Because this covers everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, the goal below is to help you separate a quick DIY fix from a repair that genuinely needs a pro — and to sanity-check any quote you get.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setpoint, or an AccuLink communication fault between the thermostat and equipment.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler loses power.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — ice on the indoor coil blocks airflow; usually secondary to airflow restriction or low refrigerant.
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the fan or compressor hums or won't start. Very common in hot climates.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or burns out.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — weak cooling, long run times, ice on the lines. Refrigerant does not "run out" normally — low charge means a leak.
8. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is caked with grass, dust or cottonwood, so it can't reject heat.
9. **Condensate safety switch tripped** — a clogged drain trips a float switch that shuts down cooling.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; the unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here if the problem isn't resolved — anything electrical inside the units or involving refrigerant is a pro job.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery-powered model.
- **Breaker:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker for the air handler and the outdoor unit. Reset **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated trips signal a fault.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass and debris from around the condenser and gently rinse the exterior fins with a garden hose (power off). Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain:** If you see water pooling near the air handler or the unit shut off, the drain may be clogged. You can clear a visible clog at the drain line, but a tripped internal float switch means the drain needs proper service.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or coil, turn the system to **Off** (fan can stay on) and let it thaw fully before running again. If it re-freezes, call a pro.

Do **not** open the electrical compartments, add refrigerant, bypass safety switches, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work methodically — this is how you can tell a good diagnosis from a guess:

1. **Verify the complaint** and check thermostat call, mode and wiring.
2. **Confirm power** at the disconnect, breaker and low-voltage transformer/fuse.
3. **Measure airflow** and inspect filter, blower and evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
4. **Test the capacitor** with a meter (microfarad reading vs. rating) and inspect the contactor.
5. **Take temperature split** across the indoor coil (return vs. supply air, typically 15-20°F on a healthy system).
6. **Attach gauges** to read suction/head pressures and calculate **superheat/subcooling** to judge refrigerant charge — the key test for a leak.
7. **Inspect the compressor** electrically (windings, amp draw) if pressures suggest it.

A tech who quotes a compressor or refrigerant without gauge readings and a temperature split is guessing — ask to see the numbers.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/no airflow at vents | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check blower, ductwork |
| Ice on coil or lines | Airflow restriction or low charge | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find leak, recharge, check airflow |
| Outdoor unit silent | Breaker, contactor or capacitor | Reset breaker once, check batteries | Test/replace contactor or capacitor |
| Outdoor fan hums, won't spin | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Replace run capacitor |
| Runs constantly, warm air | Low refrigerant (leak) | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Unit shut off, water near air handler | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear visible drain clog | Clear drain, verify float switch |
| Warm air, outdoor coil filthy | Dirty condenser | Rinse fins (power off) | Deep coil cleaning |
| Runs but no cooling, high amps | Failed compressor | None — call pro | Compressor replacement/quote |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Regional rates and warranty status affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $10 – $40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75 – $250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150 – $400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300 – $1,500 (varies widely by leak location and refrigerant type — R-410A vs. older R-22 is far more expensive)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $600 – $2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500 – $2,500+ (often worth comparing to a full system replacement on older units)

Ask whether the failed part is covered under American Standard's registered parts warranty — many carry a 10-year limited warranty on the compressor and coil if registered, though labor is usually not covered.

## Related codes

- **AccuLink / ComfortLink II communication fault** — thermostat can't reach the equipment; a common cause of "no cooling" on communicating systems.
- **Frozen evaporator coil** — often shows up alongside low-refrigerant symptoms.
- **Furnace/air handler blower faults** — no airflow can mimic a cooling failure.
- **Condensate overflow / float switch trip** — safety shutdown that stops cooling with no numeric code.
