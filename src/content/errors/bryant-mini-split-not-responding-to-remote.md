## What this code means

"Not responding to remote" is not a fault code stored by the unit. It is a symptom: you press a button and the indoor unit does nothing. There is no beep, no display change and no louver movement.

On Bryant ductless systems, the handheld remote sends an infrared (IR) signal to a receiver window on the indoor unit's display panel. The indoor control board then acts on that command. The problem can sit anywhere along that chain:

- the remote itself
- the line of sight between the remote and the unit
- the receiver/display board
- the indoor control board
- power to the indoor unit

Because nothing is latched, there is nothing to "reset" in the usual sense. Once the signal path or power is restored, the unit responds normally. One exception: if the board has also locked out on a real fault (blinking lights), that fault behaves according to its own rules. Many Bryant ductless faults clear only after power is cycled.

Some Bryant installations use a wired wall controller or a Wi-Fi/communicating interface instead of, or in addition to, the IR remote. In those setups, the IR remote may be intentionally disabled or overridden. Ask your installer before assuming a fault.

### Why this page is rated "pro"

The most likely causes (batteries, remote settings, line of sight and a tripped breaker) are things you can check yourself, and you should start there. This page is rated "pro" because if those checks don't restore control, every remaining cause sits inside the indoor unit's cabinet or in the system's wiring and configuration: the receiver/display board, its connector, the main control board, or controller settings. That work needs a technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dead or weak remote batteries.** This is by far the most common cause. The remote's LCD may still show text even when the batteries are too weak to drive the IR LED.
2. **Remote fault or settings.** Possible issues include:
   - a dropped or water-damaged remote
   - stuck buttons
   - a child or key lock enabled on the remote
   - a remote that is not the correct type for the indoor unit
3. **Blocked or obstructed signal path.** Causes include furniture, curtains, or a dirty or obscured receiver window. Bright sunlight or some LED/fluorescent lighting aimed at the receiver can also interfere with IR reception.
4. **No power, or a power glitch, at the indoor unit.** Possible causes:
   - a tripped breaker
   - an outdoor disconnect switched off (indoor units are often powered from the outdoor unit)
   - a board that needs a power cycle after a surge
5. **Unit locked out on a separate fault.** The board may ignore normal commands while displaying a blinking-light error.
6. **Wired controller or control-mode conflict.** On systems with a wired or Wi-Fi controller, the IR remote may be disabled by configuration.
7. **Failed IR receiver/display board.** This is a component inside the indoor unit, often caused by moisture or a surge.
8. **Failed indoor control (main) board, or a loose display-board connector.** This is less common and confirmed by a technician.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the remote batteries** with fresh alkaline cells. Remove them for 30 seconds before reinstalling so the remote resets.
- **Do the phone-camera test** described in the FAQ to see whether the remote emits IR.
- **Check the remote's settings.** Look for a lock icon and consult your owner manual for the unlock button combination, which varies by remote model.
- **Clear the line of sight.** Stand within normal room distance, per your owner manual, point directly at the receiver window and remove obstructions. Gently wipe the indoor unit's front display area with a dry, soft cloth. Close blinds if direct sun hits the receiver.
- **Check power.**
  - Is the indoor display lit?
  - Check the breaker. If it is tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and see the Breaker Keeps Tripping page.
  - Confirm the outdoor disconnect is on. Only flip the handle; do not open the box.
- **Power-cycle the system once.** Turn off the breaker, wait 5 minutes, then turn it back on. Do this one time only. If lights blink afterward, note the pattern for your technician.
- **Try the manual/emergency button** on the indoor unit, located under the front panel or near the display (varies by model). If the unit runs from the button but not the remote, the indoor unit has power and the problem is likely the remote or receiver.
- **Check the filters while the front panel is lifted.** Lifting the hinged front panel to reach the filters is homeowner-safe on most wall units. Clogged filters will not stop remote response, but service them anyway.

Do not remove the indoor unit's cover, display board or any wiring.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent technician will typically:

1. Verify the remote output with a camera or IR tester, then try a known-good matching remote.
2. Confirm supply voltage at the outdoor and indoor units, and the indoor-to-outdoor communication wiring.
3. Read any stored or flashing fault codes per the Bryant service manual for your series.
4. Check whether a wired controller, Wi-Fi interface or configuration setting has disabled IR control.
5. Inspect the receiver/display board, its ribbon or connector to the main board, and look for moisture or corrosion.
6. Swap or test the display board before condemning the main control board. Display boards are cheaper and fail more often.

A quote that jumps straight to a main board replacement without first checking the remote, power and the display board deserves a second look.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remote screen blank or dim | Dead batteries | Replace batteries | None |
| Remote screen works, no IR flash on camera | Failed remote | Buy a matching replacement remote | Confirm compatibility if unsure |
| Remote flashes, unit ignores it, display lit | Signal blockage or failed receiver | Clear the path, wipe the window, close blinds | Test/replace the display/receiver board |
| Unit dark, no response at all | Power off or tripped breaker | Check breaker and disconnect, one reset | Check voltage, wiring, fuses, board |
| Lights blinking, ignores commands | Separate fault lockout | One power cycle, note the blink pattern | Diagnose the fault code |
| Manual button works, remote never does | Receiver, connector or remote mismatch | Try a correct-model remote | Check the display connector and controller config |
| Stopped after a storm or surge | Damaged receiver or main board | One power cycle | Test and replace the affected board |
| Wall controller present, remote ignored | IR disabled by configuration | Use the wall controller, check the manual | Reconfigure control settings |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges and vary by region and model:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Batteries | $2–$10 |
| Replacement Bryant-matched remote | $30–$90 (universal remotes $15–$40, with compatibility not guaranteed) |

| Indoor main control board replacement | $350–$900 installed (diagnosis and model-specific part availability affect price) |
