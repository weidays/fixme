## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single blinking code on Goodman furnaces — it's a **behavior**. Your furnace starts a heat call, runs briefly, then shuts down well before the thermostat is satisfied, and repeats this over and over. On many Goodman boards you may see a diagnostic LED flash pattern for a related fault (open limit, pressure switch, or flame loss), but the pattern varies by model and board revision, so always check the label inside your blower door for the legend that matches your unit.

The core problem is that something is interrupting a normal, complete heating cycle. The most common trigger is the furnace **overheating and tripping its high-limit switch** because of restricted airflow — but it can also be a thermostat, flame-sensing, or venting issue.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, heat exchanger overheats, high-limit trips and cuts the burner. By far the most common cause.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or a crushed return duct starve airflow, same overheating result.
3. **Dirty flame sensor** — a weak flame signal makes the board drop the burner shortly after ignition, then it retries. Common on units 3+ years old.
4. **Failing high-limit switch** — a limit that trips prematurely or has drifted out of spec shuts the burner off early.
5. **Thermostat problems** — poor location (near a supply vent), a short-cycle/anticipator setting issue, or wiring faults cause rapid on/off.
6. **Oversized furnace** — a unit too large for the home heats the space fast, satisfies the thermostat, and cycles frequently by design.
7. **Blocked flue or pressure switch fault** — restricted venting or a stuck pressure switch interrupts the sequence early.
8. **Control board fault** — less common, but a failing board can misread inputs and terminate cycles.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe and often solve the problem:

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray or you can't see light through it, swap it. Check monthly during heating season.
- **Check your thermostat.** Make sure it's set to Heat, the temperature is above room temp, and — if battery powered — replace the batteries. Confirm it isn't mounted next to a supply vent or in direct sun.
- **Open and unblock vents.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and not blocked by furniture, rugs or closed doors.
- **Check the breaker.** A furnace on a tripping breaker will cycle. Reset it once; if it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Look for obvious flue or intake blockages** at exterior termination points (nests, debris, snow) — from outside only.
- **Check the condensate drain** on high-efficiency (condensing) models. A clogged drain or full trap can trip a safety and interrupt cycles.

Do **not** bypass any safety switch, adjust the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will:

- Measure **temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the rating plate spec — high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- Inspect and test the **flame sensor**, measuring microamp signal.
- Test the **high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and correct trip points.
- Check **static pressure** in the ductwork to find restrictions (undersized returns, dirty coil, closed dampers).
- Verify **pressure switch** operation and inspect the **flue/inducer** for blockage.
- Read the **control board LED codes** and check thermostat wiring and anticipator/cycle-rate settings.
- On condensing units, confirm the **condensate drain and trap** are clear.

If a tech quotes a board or heat exchanger replacement without first checking airflow, temperature rise and the flame sensor, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2-5 min, shuts off, repeats | Dirty filter / restricted airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, check static pressure |
| Burner lights then dies quickly, retries | Dirty/failing flame sensor | Replace filter first | Clean or replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycles even with clean filter | Failing high-limit switch | Confirm filter and vents are clear | Test and replace limit switch |
| Rapid on/off, thermostat clicks | Thermostat location/setting/wiring | Replace batteries, relocate check | Correct cycle rate/anticipator, fix wiring |
| Heats fast then off, comfortable but frequent | Oversized furnace | None | Assess sizing, consider staging/modulation |
| Cycles with inducer running oddly | Blocked flue / pressure switch | Clear exterior vent blockage | Test pressure switch, inspect flue/inducer |
| Cycles on high-efficiency model | Clogged condensate drain | Clear/flush drain and trap | Verify drain, replace switch if faulty |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional variation applies):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 DIY.
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** $80–$150; **replacement:** $150–$300.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$350.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350.
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Inducer motor (if flue restriction traces to it):** $400–$650.
- **Control board:** $350–$650.
- **Duct/airflow corrections (adding return, sealing):** $300–$1,500+ depending on scope.
- **Cracked heat exchanger** (found during diagnosis, not typically a short-cycle cause but sometimes uncovered): $1,000–$2,500, or often justifies furnace replacement.

Many short-cycling calls end at the **$10 filter or a $100–$150 flame sensor**. Diagnostic/service fees typically run $90–$180.
