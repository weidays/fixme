## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single Goodman fault code — it's a behavior. Your furnace lights, runs briefly, then shuts down and repeats the process every few minutes without ever reaching the temperature you set. Depending on your model and control board, you may or may not see a flashing LED diagnostic code through the sight window on the control panel.

Short cycling almost always means the furnace is protecting itself. A safety device — most often the high-limit switch — is sensing something wrong (usually overheating) and cutting power to the burners before damage occurs. It can also be caused by the flame sensor losing sight of the flame, a miswired or oversized thermostat, or a failing control board.

Because short cycling can stem from an overheating heat exchanger, and because chronic cycling accelerates wear on expensive parts, this is a **pro-level** problem once the basic checks below come up clean.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — The single most common cause. Restricted airflow lets the heat exchanger overheat, tripping the high-limit switch. Cheapest and easiest to rule out.
2. **Dirty or failing flame sensor** — If the sensor can't confirm flame, the board shuts the gas valve within seconds, then retries. This produces rapid, repeated ignition attempts.
3. **Blocked or closed vents/returns** — Too many closed registers or an undersized return duct starves the blower for air, mimicking a dirty filter.
4. **Failing high-limit or rollout switch** — A weak or faulty limit switch may open prematurely even when temperatures are safe.
5. **Thermostat problems** — Poor location (near a supply vent), weak batteries, loose wiring, or an oversized/miswired thermostat can cut cycles short.
6. **Oversized furnace** — A furnace too large for the home heats the air fast and satisfies the thermostat quickly, causing frequent short runs. This is a design issue, not a defect.
7. **Failing inducer motor or blocked flue/pressure switch** — If the pressure switch opens mid-cycle, the board aborts the cycle.
8. **Failing control board** — Less common, but a degraded board can misread inputs and terminate cycles early.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should perform. Do **not** bypass safety switches, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a furnace that keeps locking out.

- **Filter:** Pull the air filter. If it's gray, clogged, or you can't see light through it, replace it and run the furnace. This resolves a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Make sure it's set to **Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Check that it isn't mounted in direct sunlight or near a supply vent.
- **Vents and returns:** Walk the house and open any closed or blocked supply registers and return grilles. Move furniture or rugs off return vents.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (not tripped or half-thrown). Flip it fully off, then on, once.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency models):** If your Goodman is a condensing furnace, check that the condensate drain line and trap aren't clogged. A backed-up trap can trip the pressure switch and abort cycles.

If short cycling continues after clean filter, good thermostat, open vents, and clear condensate — stop and call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence, so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Reads the diagnostic LED code** (if your board provides one) through the sight glass and cross-references the label inside the furnace door.
2. **Measures temperature rise** across the heat exchanger and compares it to the range printed on the data plate. A high rise points to airflow restriction; a low rise points elsewhere.
3. **Tests the flame sensor** with a microamp meter — a healthy signal is typically a few microamps. A weak reading means cleaning or replacement.
4. **Checks the high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and correct opening temperature.
5. **Inspects the blower** for a dirty wheel, failing capacitor, or wrong speed tap.
6. **Verifies the pressure switch and inducer** operate correctly and the flue is clear.
7. **Confirms thermostat wiring and anticipator/cycle settings.**

Ask the tech to show you the temperature-rise reading and the flame-sensor microamp value. Those two numbers explain most short-cycling diagnoses.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs ~5 min then shuts off, filter is dirty | Overheating from restricted airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temperature rise, verify limit switch |
| Burners light then die in seconds, retries | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None (sensor cleaning is a pro task) | Clean or replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycles fast with cold rooms | Oversized furnace or bad thermostat | Check thermostat location/batteries | Evaluate sizing, adjust cycle rate/wiring |
| Shuts off with a click mid-cycle | High-limit or rollout switch tripping | Confirm airflow is clear | Test switches, find overheat root cause |
| Inducer runs, burners never stay lit | Pressure switch / clogged flue or drain | Clear condensate trap (condensing models) | Inspect flue, inducer, pressure switch |
| Random early shutoffs, no pattern | Failing control board | None | Diagnose and replace control board |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US pricing including parts and labor. Your area and model affect the total.

- **Air filter (DIY):** $10 – $40
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80 – $250
- **High-limit or rollout switch:** $150 – $400
- **Pressure switch:** $150 – $400
- **Thermostat replacement:** $130 – $400
- **Inducer motor:** $300 – $600
- **Control board:** $350 – $600
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80 – $180 (often applied to the repair)

If a tech finds a **cracked heat exchanger** while investigating chronic overheating, repair is not advised — replacement of the exchanger or the furnace is the safe path, and costs climb significantly.
