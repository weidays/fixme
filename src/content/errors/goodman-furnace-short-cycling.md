## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a specific numbered fault code — it's a behavior. Your Goodman furnace starts a heat call, runs briefly, then shuts down before the thermostat is satisfied, and repeats this over and over. On Goodman boards with a diagnostic LED, you may see it paired with a limit-open flash code or a flame-sense fault, but the furnace can also short cycle with no stored code at all.

The pattern matters. A furnace that runs 2–5 minutes then stops on a **safety limit** is protecting itself from overheating. A furnace that lights, loses flame signal, and drops out is a **flame-sense or gas** problem. A furnace that satisfies the thermostat too quickly may simply be **oversized** or reading a bad thermostat. Each points to a different repair, which is why this is rated a pro-level diagnosis.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts return airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and the high-limit switch shuts the burners off. By far the most common cause.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — a coated sensor rod stops proving flame after a few seconds, so the board closes the gas valve mid-cycle. Often looks like short cycling with an ignition/flame fault flash.
3. **Blocked airflow elsewhere** — closed or blocked supply/return vents, a collapsed flex duct, or an undersized duct system cause the same overheating as a dirty filter.
4. **Failing high-limit switch** — a weak or out-of-spec limit trips early even with adequate airflow.
5. **Thermostat problems** — dead batteries, poor location (near a supply register), loose wiring, or a short-cycle setting that's too aggressive.
6. **Flue/pressure switch or inducer issues** — a marginal inducer or partially blocked flue causes the pressure switch to open mid-cycle and drop the furnace out.
7. **Oversized furnace** — heats the space fast and satisfies the thermostat quickly; more common with older or mismatched replacement equipment.
8. **Failing control (ignition) board** — intermittent board faults can trigger unexplained shutdowns. This is a diagnosis of exclusion, made after ruling out the above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the gas valve, bypass any limit or safety switch, or repeatedly reset a furnace that keeps locking out.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat batteries:** Replace them if your model uses them, even if the screen still lights.
- **Thermostat location/settings:** Make sure it isn't in direct sun, next to a register, or set to a very short cycle rate. Confirm it's set to Heat with a setpoint above room temp.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house and open any closed registers. Keep furniture and rugs off return grilles. Blocked airflow overheats the furnace.
- **Breaker:** If the furnace tripped its breaker, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Condensate (96% units):** On high-efficiency Goodman furnaces, a clogged condensate drain or full trap can open a safety and cause shutdowns. Check for standing water; clear an obvious clog if you can safely reach it.

If none of these fix it, the furnace needs a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will watch a full cycle and read the board's LED flash code first. Then, typically:

- **Measure temperature rise** across the heat exchanger and compare it to the rating-plate spec. High rise confirms an airflow/overheating cause.
- **Check static pressure** to find duct or filter restriction.
- **Inspect and clean the flame sensor**, then measure the **microamp flame signal**. Low microamps means a weak or dirty sensor.
- **Test the high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and correct trip points.
- **Verify the inducer and pressure switch** — measuring inducer draft and pressure-switch closure to rule out flue blockage.
- **Confirm gas pressure** and burner operation (with proper tools).
- **Check the control board and wiring** last, only after other causes are eliminated.

Ask which of these they actually measured. A quote to replace a control board without a temperature-rise or flame-signal reading is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2–5 min, blower keeps going after burners stop | Overheating on high-limit from dirty filter/airflow | Replace filter, open all vents | Measure temp rise, check static pressure, test limit |
| Lights, then flame drops out after a few seconds | Dirty or failing flame sensor | Replace filter; note flash code | Clean/replace sensor, read microamp signal |
| Cycles with no burner light, inducer runs | Pressure switch opening / flue or inducer issue | Check for blocked flue termination | Test inducer draft, pressure switch, clear flue |
| Very short heat, satisfies thermostat fast | Oversized furnace or thermostat location | Move nothing that blocks tstat; check placement | Verify sizing, adjust cycle rate, relocate tstat |
| Random shutdowns, no consistent pattern | Marginal control board or loose wiring | Reset breaker once only | Inspect harness, test board, replace if faulted |
| Standing water near 96% furnace | Clogged condensate drain/trap | Clear visible clog, empty trap | Clear drain, test condensate safety switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $8–$30 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** $0 DIY, or included in a $90–$180 service visit.
- **Flame sensor replacement:** $80–$180 installed.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Condensate drain clearing / safety switch (96% units):** $100–$300.
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$600 installed.
- **Control (ignition) board:** $350–$650 installed.
- **Duct or airflow correction:** varies widely; a diagnostic visit ($90–$180) usually applies to the repair.

Diagnostic fees are often credited toward the repair if you proceed with the same company.
