## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a numbered fault code on Goodman furnaces — it's a **behavior**: the furnace fires up, runs for a short time (often 1–5 minutes), shuts off, then restarts again much sooner than a normal heating cycle should.

A properly working furnace should run long enough to satisfy your thermostat and then rest for a while. When it cycles rapidly, something is either overheating the unit, interrupting the flame signal, or telling the control board the demand is met when it isn't.

On Goodman furnaces with an integrated furnace control (IFC) board, short cycling often shows up alongside a flashing LED diagnostic code — for example a limit-open or flame-sense code. **The exact blink pattern varies by board revision**, so check the label inside your furnace's blower door for the legend that matches your model.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the most common cause. Restricted airflow lets the heat exchanger overheat, tripping the high-limit switch and shutting the burner off early.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — same effect as a dirty filter; the furnace can't move enough air and overheats.
3. **Dirty flame sensor** — a fouled sensor loses the flame signal seconds after ignition, so the board shuts the gas valve and re-tries, causing rapid cycling.
4. **Failing high-limit switch** — a weak or intermittent limit switch can trip prematurely even with adequate airflow.
5. **Thermostat problems** — bad placement (near a supply vent or heat source), low batteries, or loose/miswired wiring can cause false satisfied signals.
6. **Blocked flue or restricted exhaust** — a pressure switch that opens mid-cycle will shut the furnace down repeatedly.
7. **Oversized furnace** — a unit too large for the home heats too fast, satisfies the thermostat quickly, then re-fires often. This is a design issue, not a broken part.
8. **Failing control board (IFC)** — less common, but a bad board can misread sensors and cycle the unit erratically.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. **Do not** bypass safety switches, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a locked-out unit.

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, swap it. This single step fixes a large share of short-cycling cases.
- **Check your vents.** Walk the house and make sure supply and return registers are open and not blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed doors. Keep at least 80% of registers open.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Make sure it isn't in direct sun or above a supply vent, which can trick it into cycling.
- **Check the breaker.** Confirm the furnace breaker hasn't tripped and isn't loose in the panel.
- **Look at the condensate drain (high-efficiency models).** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and interrupt operation. If you see standing water in the drain pan, that's a clue for your technician.
- **Listen and observe.** Note whether it shuts off during ignition, shortly after the burners light, or well into the cycle — that timing helps a tech pinpoint the cause.

If cleaning the filter and clearing vents doesn't fix it, stop and call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will confirm the cause before quoting a repair. Expect them to:

- **Read the IFC board's diagnostic LED** and match the blink code to the label.
- **Measure temperature rise** across the heat exchanger and compare it to the rating plate spec — high rise points to airflow restriction or overheating.
- **Test the flame sensor's microamp signal** with a meter; a weak signal means cleaning or replacement.
- **Check the high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and proper trip points.
- **Verify the pressure switch and inducer draft**, and inspect the flue for blockages.
- **Inspect blower operation and speed taps**, plus filter and coil condition, to rule out airflow issues.
- **Check thermostat wiring and anticipator/cycle settings.**
- **Evaluate sizing** if the unit is otherwise healthy but cycles fast — a load calculation may reveal it's oversized.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shuts off after a few minutes of running, restarts | Overheating from dirty filter / blocked vents | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, check blower & limit |
| Burners light, then shut off within seconds, retry | Dirty or failing flame sensor | None (safe to inspect only) | Clean or replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycles even with clean filter and open vents | Weak high-limit switch | None | Test and replace limit switch |
| Rapid on/off with erratic timing | Thermostat placement/batteries/wiring | Replace batteries, relocate if in sun | Repair wiring, adjust cycle rate |
| Cycles with inducer running, pressure switch clicks | Blocked flue / condensate drain | Clear standing water in pan | Clear flue, replace pressure switch |
| Heats house fast, then re-fires often | Oversized furnace | None | Load calculation, staging/airflow tuning |
| No consistent pattern, mixed fault codes | Failing control board | None | Diagnose and replace IFC board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor). Prices vary by region and contractor.

- **Air filter replacement:** $0–$40 DIY.
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** $80–$180 service call. **Replacement:** $120–$300.
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Flue / condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250.
- **Thermostat repair or replacement:** $100–$350 depending on model.
- **Blower motor issues (if airflow-related):** $300–$800.
- **Integrated furnace control (IFC) board replacement:** $350–$700+.
- **Correcting an oversized furnace:** varies widely; a proper fix may mean airflow/staging changes or, rarely, replacement — get a load calculation first.

If a contractor quotes a board or blower motor without first checking the filter, flame sensor, and temperature rise, get a second opinion.
