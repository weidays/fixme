## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single Goodman fault code — it's a behavior. Your furnace starts a heating cycle, runs briefly, then shuts down, and repeats the whole sequence far more often than it should. A normal Goodman furnace runs several minutes per cycle; a short-cycling unit may fire and quit every 30 seconds to a few minutes.

On Goodman furnaces with an integrated furnace control (IFC) board, this pattern often shows up alongside a blinking diagnostic LED behind the blower door. Codes such as **limit switch open**, **flame sensed but lost**, or **failure to prove flame** frequently accompany short cycling. The exact flash pattern and code label **vary by board revision** — always match the LED flashes to the legend printed on the inside of the furnace door or in your model's manual.

Short cycling is a symptom of an underlying safety trip or control problem. It's Goodman's controls doing their job — shutting the burner down when something isn't right — not the furnace "acting up" randomly.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter / restricted airflow** — The most common cause. A clogged filter starves the heat exchanger of airflow, it overheats, and the high-limit switch trips the burner off. It cools, re-fires, and repeats.
2. **Dirty or failing flame sensor** — The sensor can't prove flame within a few seconds, so the board closes the gas valve. Burner lights, then dies almost immediately, then retries.
3. **Overheating from blocked vents/registers** — Too many closed or blocked supply registers create the same overheating trip as a dirty filter.
4. **Faulty or misplaced thermostat** — A thermostat in direct sunlight, near a supply vent, with dying batteries, or with loose wiring can send erratic on/off signals.
5. **Blocked condensate drain (high-efficiency models)** — On condensing Goodman furnaces (90%+ AFUE), a plugged drain trips the pressure switch, shutting the unit down mid-cycle.
6. **Failing high-limit or rollout switch** — A switch that's weak or intermittently open trips prematurely even with adequate airflow.
7. **Cracked/dirty flue or inducer/pressure switch issue** — Improper venting or a weak inducer motor prevents the pressure switch from proving draft, cutting cycles short.
8. **Oversized furnace** — An oversized unit heats the space too fast, satisfies the thermostat quickly, and cycles frequently. This is a design issue, not a fault.
9. **Failing integrated control board or gas valve** — Least common but possible; erratic board behavior or a sticking valve causes inconsistent cycling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should perform. Do **not** open the burner compartment while gas is flowing, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter and hold it to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone resolves a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Confirm it's set to **Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Make sure it isn't in direct sun or above a vent.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house and open any closed registers. Move furniture, rugs, or drapes blocking returns. Restricted airflow is a top cause of overheating trips.
- **Breaker:** Check that the furnace breaker is fully on. If it keeps tripping, stop and call a pro — that's an electrical fault, not a DIY fix.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency units):** Look for standing water in the drain pan or a backed-up drain line. A plugged drain trips the pressure switch. You can pour drain tablets or gently clear an accessible line, but leave internal tubing to a technician.
- **Diagnostic LED:** Note the flash pattern through the sight glass. Write it down and match it to the door legend — it helps any technician you call.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work methodically, not just swap parts. Expect them to:

1. **Read the LED fault code** and confirm the flash pattern against the board legend for your specific model.
2. **Check static pressure and airflow** — measuring temperature rise across the heat exchanger to confirm whether overheating is tripping the limit.
3. **Test the flame sensor** — cleaning it and measuring microamp flame signal (a healthy Goodman sensor typically reads several microamps; a weak reading means it's fouled or failing).
4. **Test the high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and proper trip behavior.
5. **Test the pressure switch and inducer** — verifying draft and checking for blocked flue or condensate on condensing units.
6. **Inspect the gas valve and manifold pressure** to rule out a fuel-delivery problem.
7. **Verify thermostat wiring and heat-anticipator/cycle settings.**
8. **Confirm the board's ignition sequence** end-to-end.

If a quote jumps straight to "you need a new control board" without airflow and flame-sensor testing first, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2-5 min, shuts off, restarts | Dirty filter / overheating limit trip | Replace filter, open all vents | Measure temp rise, test limit switch |
| Fires then dies in 3-10 seconds | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None (behind burner panel) | Clean or replace sensor, test microamps |
| Erratic on/off, wrong temps | Thermostat fault or placement | New batteries, relocate thermostat | Check wiring, replace thermostat |
| Cuts out mid-cycle, water in pan | Blocked condensate / pressure switch | Clear accessible drain, add tablets | Clear drain trap, test pressure switch |
| Cycles fast in mild weather only | Oversized furnace | None | Confirm sizing, adjust staging |
| Blower runs, burner won't stay lit | Weak inducer / flue / gas valve | None | Test inducer, flue draft, manifold pressure |
| LED flashes limit or rollout code | Limit/rollout switch trip | Check filter & vents first | Test and replace switch |
| No consistent pattern, random cycling | Failing control board | None | Diagnose and replace IFC board |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US pricing including parts and labor. Your area, model, and contractor rates will vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80–$250
- **High-limit or rollout switch:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$400
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $300–$650
- **Gas valve replacement:** $300–$600
- **Integrated control (IFC) board replacement:** $400–$700+
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$200 (often credited toward the repair)

If the furnace is oversized, there's no cheap "part" fix — the long-term answer is proper sizing at replacement, though staging adjustments may help.
