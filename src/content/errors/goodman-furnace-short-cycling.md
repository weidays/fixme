## What this code means

Short cycling isn't a single fault code — it's a behavior: your Goodman furnace starts a heating cycle, runs briefly, shuts down, then starts again long before the room reaches the thermostat setpoint. On most Goodman gas furnaces the control board will flash a diagnostic LED code (visible through the sight glass on the blower door) that hints at *why* the burners keep dropping out.

Because the exact codes and behavior vary by control board (single-stage, two-stage, GMVC/GMEC/GC/GM series all differ), always read the LED flash pattern against the label inside your blower compartment door. Common culprits behind short cycling include restricted airflow, a dirty flame sensor, a tripping high-limit switch, or a furnace that's simply oversized for the home.

Short cycling wastes fuel, wears out the igniter and gas valve, and can crack a heat exchanger over time — so it's worth diagnosing rather than ignoring.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the most common cause. Restricted airflow overheats the heat exchanger and trips the high-limit switch, cutting the burners early.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — a carbon-fouled sensor fails to detect flame after ignition, so the board shuts the gas valve within seconds and retries.
3. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or blocked returns starve airflow, causing the same overheating as a dirty filter.
4. **Overheating high-limit trip** — a failing blower motor, seized wheel, or duct restriction lets temperatures climb past the limit switch setpoint.
5. **Thermostat problems** — wrong location (near a heat source), weak batteries, loose wiring, or an anticipator/cycle-rate setting that's too aggressive.
6. **Flue/venting or pressure switch issues** — a partially blocked flue or a marginal pressure switch can cause the board to abort cycles.
7. **Oversized furnace** — a unit too large for the home heats the air fast, satisfies the stat, then repeats. This is a design issue, not a broken part.
8. **Control board or gas valve fault** — least common, but a failing board or intermittent valve can produce erratic cycling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here and call a pro for anything else.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many short-cycling cases.
- **Thermostat:** Set it clearly above room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Make sure it's set to Heat, not Auto-changeover confusion.
- **Registers and returns:** Walk the house and open closed supply vents. Clear furniture, rugs or filters blocking return grilles.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (flip it off, then firmly back on once).
- **Blower door / sight glass:** Make sure the blower access panel is fully closed — the door safety switch must be engaged for the furnace to run. Note any LED flash code through the sight glass.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency models):** On a 90%+ Goodman, a full or clogged condensate line/pump can trip a safety and cause cycling. If the pump reservoir is full, empty it and check the drain isn't clogged.

Do **not** bypass any safety switch, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech should work through the airflow-first logic, roughly in this order:

- Read the LED flash code and cross-check it against the board's legend.
- Verify filter, blower wheel cleanliness and blower motor amperage; measure temperature rise across the furnace and compare it to the rating-plate spec.
- Inspect and clean/test the **flame sensor** microamp signal.
- Check the **high-limit switch** operation and look for duct restrictions or closed dampers.
- Test the **pressure switch** and inspect the flue/inducer for blockage.
- Check thermostat wiring, cycle-rate/anticipator settings and heat-anticipation.
- Only after airflow and safeties check out will they suspect the **control board or gas valve**.

If a quote jumps straight to "new control board" or "new furnace" without a temperature-rise reading or flame-sensor test, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2–5 min then shuts off, restarts | Dirty filter / airflow restriction | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, check blower |
| Flame lights then dies in seconds | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None (safe cleaning is tech work) | Clean or replace flame sensor |
| Cycling with high-limit trips | Overheating, weak blower | Replace filter, clear returns | Test limit switch, blower motor |
| Blower runs but no heat between cycles | Pressure switch / flue issue | Check condensate line | Inspect inducer, flue, pressure switch |
| Cycles randomly, no clear pattern | Thermostat / wiring | New batteries, check location | Test wiring, cycle-rate settings |
| Fast even/comfortable cycles | Oversized furnace | None | Recommend staging or right-sizing |
| Erratic ignition, LED board fault | Control board / gas valve | Read flash code | Replace board or valve |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries / minor thermostat fix:** $0–$30 DIY; $150–$400 for a replacement smart thermostat installed.
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $100–$250.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$350.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$400.
- **Blower motor:** $400–$800+ (higher for ECM/variable-speed motors).
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$600.
- **Control board:** $350–$650.
- **Gas valve:** $300–$650.
- **Oversized furnace (right-sizing/replacement):** major project, $4,000+ — get a load calculation first.

Diagnostic fees typically run $80–$180 and are often applied toward the repair.
