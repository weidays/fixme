## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single Goodman fault code — it is a **behavior**: the furnace fires up, then shuts down after a very short run, and repeats this over and over. On Goodman furnaces with an integrated ignition control board, you may or may not see a flashing LED code depending on the cause. Overheating faults, flame-sensing dropout, and pressure-switch problems often produce their own diagnostic flashes, while a mismatched thermostat or oversized furnace can short cycle with **no code at all**.

The key point: short cycling means the burner run is being cut off early and often. That is either because a safety device is doing its job (good) or because a control, sensor, or airflow problem is triggering it falsely (needs a fix).

Because behavior and flash codes vary by board revision, check the diagnostic label inside your furnace's blower door for the code legend that matches your exact model.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and the high-limit switch cuts the burners early. The most common and cheapest cause.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — a carbon-coated sensor fails to prove flame a few seconds after ignition, so the board drops the gas valve, then retries. Produces a rapid fire-and-die pattern.
3. **Blocked airflow beyond the filter** — closed/blocked supply registers, a dirty blower wheel, or an undersized/collapsed return duct, all causing high-limit trips.
4. **Overheating high-limit switch trips** — the limit itself may be weak, or airflow issues are tripping it. Repeated limit trips are a leading short-cycle cause.
5. **Pressure switch / flue or condensate problem** — a blocked flue, clogged condensate trap/drain, or a marginal inducer causes the pressure switch to open mid-cycle and shut the furnace down.
6. **Faulty flame sensor wiring or grounding** — a poor ground makes flame sensing unreliable and intermittent.
7. **Thermostat problem or bad location** — a thermostat in a drafty or hot spot (near a vent or lamp) satisfies too quickly. Loose wiring or a failing thermostat can also cut demand early.
8. **Oversized furnace** — a furnace too large for the home heats the thermostat area fast and shuts off before the whole house is comfortable. A design issue, not a repair.
9. **Failing control board** — less common, but a bad board can misread inputs and cycle erratically.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** bypass any safety switch, open the gas valve manifold, or repeatedly reset a furnace that keeps locking out.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If it is gray or you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many short-cycling calls.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house. Open any closed registers and remove furniture, rugs, or boxes blocking supply or return grilles. Restricted airflow overheats the furnace.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. If it's battery-powered, install fresh batteries. Make sure it isn't mounted where a vent, sunlight, or a lamp is falsely warming it.
- **Breaker:** Check the furnace breaker isn't tripped or partially thrown. Reset it **once** if needed.
- **Condensate drain (high-efficiency models):** If your furnace has a PVC condensate line, check for a full or overflowing trap or a clogged drain. Standing water can open the pressure switch. Clear a visible clog and keep the line draining freely.
- **Listen and time it:** Note how long the burner runs before it shuts off (seconds vs. minutes) and whether the inducer/blower keep running. This helps your technician.

If it still short cycles after a clean filter and open vents, stop and call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Goodman tech works through this in order — use it to sanity-check any quote:

- **Reads the LED flash code** at the control board and cross-references the door legend for the exact model.
- **Measures temperature rise** across the furnace and compares it to the rating-plate spec; a rise that's too high confirms airflow restriction/overheating.
- **Inspects and cleans the flame sensor**, then measures **microamp flame current** with a meter — low microamps confirm a weak or dirty sensor.
- **Checks the high-limit switch** for proper operation and verifies it isn't tripping due to airflow.
- **Tests the pressure switch and inducer**, checks the flue and condensate drain for blockage, and measures inducer draft.
- **Inspects the blower wheel** for dirt buildup and confirms proper blower speed/tap setting.
- **Verifies thermostat wiring** and checks for a mismatched anticipator or a poorly located thermostat.
- **Checks the control board** inputs/outputs only after ruling out the cheaper causes above.

Be cautious if a tech jumps straight to "you need a new board" without measuring temperature rise, flame microamps, or checking the filter and airflow first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burner runs a few minutes then quits, repeats | Dirty filter / restricted airflow overheating limit | Replace filter, open all vents | Measure temp rise, clean blower, test high-limit |
| Fires then dies within ~5–10 seconds | Dirty flame sensor / poor ground | Replace filter, then call pro | Clean sensor, measure microamps, fix ground |
| Shuts off with inducer running, then retries | Pressure switch opening / blocked flue or drain | Clear visible condensate clog | Test pressure switch, clear flue, check inducer |
| Cycles far more than heating demand needs | Thermostat location or oversized furnace | Move nothing hot near thermostat, check batteries | Verify wiring, assess sizing |
| Erratic cycling with odd LED codes | Failing control board | None | Test board inputs/outputs, replace if confirmed |
| Repeated lockouts between cycles | Ignition or safety fault | One reset only | Full ignition-system diagnosis |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Simple parts vary little by region; labor and diagnostic fees drive most of the difference.

- **Air filter replacement:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90–$180
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80–$250 installed
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150–$350
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$350
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $300–$600
- **Blower wheel/motor cleaning or service:** $150–$450
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Control board replacement:** $350–$650 installed
- **Gas valve replacement:** $300–$650 installed

If your furnace is oversized (short cycling with no fault found), correcting that means a right-sized replacement or duct/airflow redesign — a much larger project best quoted after a load calculation.
