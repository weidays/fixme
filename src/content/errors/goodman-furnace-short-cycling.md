## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single blinking error code — it's a behavior: your Goodman furnace fires up, runs for a short time (often 1–5 minutes, sometimes only seconds), shuts off, then starts again far too soon. A healthy furnace usually runs several minutes per cycle and completes a handful of cycles per hour, not dozens.

On Goodman furnaces with an integrated ignition control board, short cycling is the board doing its job: it's cutting the burners because a safety switch tripped, it lost the flame signal, or it received a thermostat "satisfied" signal too early. The LED on the control board may flash a diagnostic pattern (commonly limit-switch or flame-loss codes) — check the legend on the blower-door label or panel for your specific model, since the flash codes differ between single-stage, two-stage and modulating boards.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter / restricted airflow** — the most common cause. Overheating trips the high-limit switch, which shuts off the burners.
2. **Dirty or failing flame sensor** — the flame lights but the board can't "see" it, so it cuts gas within seconds and retries.
3. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — same overheating result as a dirty filter.
4. **Thermostat problems** — bad location (near a supply register), dying batteries, or short-cycle/anticipator settings causing premature shutoff.
5. **Tripping high-limit switch** — from airflow restriction, a failing blower motor, or a genuinely bad limit switch.
6. **Flue/exhaust or pressure-switch faults** — a partially blocked flue, condensate backup, or weak inducer can drop out the pressure switch mid-cycle.
7. **Oversized furnace** — an incorrectly sized unit satisfies the thermostat quickly and cycles often; this is a design issue, not a broken part.
8. **Control board or gas valve fault** — least common, but a failing board or intermittent valve can cause erratic cycling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do — no panels off the burner compartment, no bypassing safeties:

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Make sure it's set to "Heat" and the temperature is called for. Check it isn't mounted in direct sun or next to a supply vent.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house and confirm registers aren't closed, blocked by furniture, or covered by rugs. Restricting airflow causes overheating.
- **Breaker:** If the furnace has lost power intermittently, confirm the breaker isn't half-tripped. Reset it **once** — do not repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency models):** Check for a full condensate pan or clogged drain line. A tripped float switch will stop the furnace. Clear standing water only if you can safely reach the pan/drain.

If these don't fix it, stop here — the remaining causes involve the burner compartment, gas, or electronics and belong to a licensed tech.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will:

- Read the **control board LED flash code** and cross-reference it to Goodman's legend for your model.
- Measure **temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the nameplate spec — high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- Inspect and clean or replace the **flame sensor**, then check the **microamp flame signal** on the board.
- Test the **high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and confirm they aren't tripping prematurely.
- Verify **inducer draft and pressure-switch operation**, and inspect the flue for blockage.
- Check **blower motor amperage and capacitor**, since a weak blower reduces airflow.
- Evaluate **gas pressure and valve operation**, and confirm the furnace is correctly sized for the home (Manual J) if oversizing is suspected.

You can sanity-check a quote: if a tech blames the control board without first ruling out the filter, flame sensor, and limit switch, ask them to show the flash code and their airflow/flame-signal readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes, blower keeps going, then re-fires | High-limit trip from airflow restriction | Replace filter, open all vents | Test limit switch, check blower amps/capacitor, measure temp rise |
| Lights then shuts off in 3–7 seconds, retries | Dirty flame sensor / weak ground | Replace filter only | Clean/replace flame sensor, verify microamp signal and ground |
| Fires, then quits mid-cycle repeatedly | Pressure switch dropping out / flue or condensate issue | Check/clear condensate drain | Test inducer, pressure switch, inspect flue for blockage |
| Cycles fast even in mild weather, house rooms uneven | Oversized furnace or bad thermostat location | Move nothing near thermostat | Verify sizing, adjust cycle rate, relocate thermostat |
| Erratic, no clear pattern, odd flash code | Control board or gas valve fault | Note the flash code for the tech | Diagnose board/valve, replace if confirmed |
| Loses power intermittently | Loose wiring / half-tripped breaker | Reset breaker once | Trace wiring, check connections and transformer |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor; regional and after-hours rates vary):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $150–$300.
- **High-limit or rollout switch:** $150–$400.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350.
- **Inducer motor:** $350–$550.
- **Blower motor / capacitor:** $100–$250 (capacitor) up to $400–$700 (ECM/PSC motor).
- **Integrated control board:** $400–$650.
- **Gas valve:** $400–$600.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$180, often credited toward the repair.

Oversizing is the exception — there's no cheap part fix; it's a sizing/ductwork issue that may only be fully solved at replacement time. A tech can sometimes soften the symptom by adjusting the cycle rate or firing stage.
