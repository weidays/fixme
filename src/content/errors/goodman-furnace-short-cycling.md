## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single Goodman fault code — it's a behavior. Your furnace starts a heat cycle, runs briefly, then shuts down, and repeats far more often than normal. On most Goodman integrated control boards you'll often see a diagnostic LED flashing a specific fault (for example limit-switch open or flame-loss codes) that explains *why* the cycle keeps ending early.

Short cycling almost always means the furnace is either **overheating and tripping a safety** or **losing the flame signal** and shutting down for safety. Both are the control board doing its job — the real problem is upstream. Because it involves gas ignition, flame sensing, and safety limits, we rate this **pro** for the actual repair, though several of the most common causes are homeowner-fixable.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts return airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and the high-limit switch shuts the burners off early. By far the most common cause.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or blocked returns starve airflow, same overheating result as a dirty filter.
3. **Dirty or failing flame sensor** — the board can't confirm flame, drops out, and re-tries, producing rapid on/off cycles. Common on older Goodman units.
4. **Faulty or tripping high-limit switch** — either responding correctly to real overheating, or failing and tripping prematurely.
5. **Oversized furnace** — heats the space too fast, satisfies the thermostat, and shuts off before a full cycle. Common in wrongly sized replacements; not a repair, a design issue.
6. **Thermostat problems** — bad location (near a vent), loose wiring, or a failing thermostat causing rapid demand changes.
7. **Blocked flue or condensate issues (90%+ high-efficiency models)** — a clogged condensate drain trips the pressure/float switch; blocked intake/exhaust trips the pressure switch, ending cycles.
8. **Failing inducer motor or pressure switch** — the board won't proceed or drops out mid-cycle.
9. **Control board fault** — misreading sensors or mis-timing cycles. Least common; diagnose everything else first.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, dusty, or more than 90 days old, swap it. This resolves a large share of short-cycling calls and costs a few dollars.
- **Check your thermostat.** Set it to Heat, a few degrees above room temp. If it's battery-powered, install fresh batteries. Make sure it isn't in direct sunlight or above a supply vent.
- **Open blocked vents.** Confirm supply registers aren't closed and returns aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or drapes. Closing too many rooms starves airflow.
- **Check the breaker.** A furnace breaker that's tripped or in a middle position should be flipped fully off, then on — once.
- **Inspect the condensate drain (high-efficiency models).** If you see standing water in the drain pan or near the furnace, the drain may be clogged and tripping a float switch. Clearing a visible clog or emptying the pan is fine; condensate tablets help prevent recurrence.
- **Look and listen.** Note whether the burners light and then quit, or never fully light. That detail helps your technician.

Do **not** bypass or jump out any limit or pressure switch, don't open the gas valve or burner assembly, and don't repeatedly reset a furnace that keeps locking out.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Reads the diagnostic LED / fault history** on the control board to see what's ending each cycle (limit open, flame loss, pressure switch, etc.).
- **Measures temperature rise** across the heat exchanger and compares it to the rating-plate spec — high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- **Checks airflow**: filter, blower wheel cleanliness, blower speed tap/settings, and duct restrictions.
- **Tests the flame sensor** with a microamp meter; cleans or replaces it if the flame signal is weak.
- **Verifies the high-limit switch** operation and looks for a genuine overheat vs. a failed switch.
- **Inspects inducer, pressure switch, flue and condensate path** on high-efficiency models.
- **Confirms gas pressure and burner operation.**
- **Evaluates sizing/cycle rate** if everything tests good — an oversized unit that satisfies the stat too fast is a design problem, not a broken part.

If a quote jumps straight to "replace the control board" without any temp-rise reading, filter/flame-sensor check, or fault-code readout, ask for that diagnostic data first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes, shuts off, repeats; filter is dirty | Overheating on high limit from airflow restriction | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, check blower speed |
| Burners light then flame drops seconds later | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None (pro test) | Clean or replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycles even with clean filter and open vents | Failing high-limit switch or blower issue | Confirm filter is new | Test limit switch, inspect blower wheel/motor |
| Heats room fast, short even cycles, no faults | Oversized furnace | None | Assess sizing, blower staging, thermostat settings |
| Water near furnace, cycles end early (90%+ units) | Clogged condensate / tripped float switch | Clear visible clog, add tablets | Clear drain line, test float/pressure switch |
| Inducer runs oddly or cycles mid-run | Pressure switch or inducer fault | None | Test pressure switch, inspect inducer & flue |
| Rapid cycling tied to thermostat | Bad location, wiring, or thermostat | New batteries, relocate away from vents | Test/replace thermostat, check wiring |

## Repair costs

- **Air filter:** $5–$40 (DIY).
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $0 DIY; $75–$200 for a pro service if lines are heavily clogged.
- **Flame sensor clean/replace:** $80–$250, mostly labor and diagnostics; the part itself is cheap.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$400 installed.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$400 installed.
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$650 installed.
- **Control board:** $400–$650 installed, depending on model and board revision.
- **Oversizing fix:** no cheap repair — options range from blower/thermostat tuning to eventual right-sized replacement; get a load calculation before spending big.

Prices vary by region and by Goodman model series. Get an itemized quote that lists the diagnosed cause, not just a part swap.
