## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a specific flashing error code on your Goodman furnace — it's a behavior. The furnace fires up, runs for a short time (often under two to three minutes), shuts off, and then restarts again soon after. Instead of steady, comfortable heat cycles, you get rapid on/off surges.

On Goodman furnaces with an integrated furnace control (IFC) board, short cycling is usually the *symptom* of a protective shutdown — the board or a safety switch is turning off the burners early to prevent damage. Depending on the model, you may or may not see a diagnostic LED flash (single, two-stage, and modulating boards flash differently). If a specific fault repeats until lockout, see our **Goodman Furnace Lockout** page instead.

Short cycling wastes fuel, wears out the igniter and blower motor, and can crack the heat exchanger over time. It should be diagnosed rather than ignored.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, causing the furnace to overheat and trip the high-limit switch early. By far the most common cause.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers reduce airflow, mimicking a dirty filter.
3. **Dirty or misaligned flame sensor** — the board fails to confirm flame, drops the burners, retries, and cycles. Common on Goodman units after a few seasons.
4. **Overheating from a failing blower motor or dirty blower wheel** — insufficient air movement trips the limit switch.
5. **Faulty or marginal high-limit switch** — a weak limit switch opens at too low a temperature and shuts the burners off prematurely.
6. **Thermostat problems** — poor location (near a vent), low batteries, loose wiring, or an aggressive anticipator/cycle-rate setting.
7. **Clogged condensate drain (96% high-efficiency models)** — a full condensate trap or bad pressure switch reading can cause repeated shutdowns.
8. **Oversized furnace** — an over-large unit satisfies the thermostat too quickly, then re-fires; often shows up on install or after a mismatched replacement.
9. **Control board (IFC) fault or gas valve issue** — less common, diagnosed after ruling out the above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe. Do **not** bypass any safety switch, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a locked-out unit.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter and hold it to light. If you can't see through it, replace it with the correct size (printed on the frame). Retest.
- **Thermostat:** If it's battery-powered, install fresh batteries. Make sure it's set to **Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Confirm it isn't mounted in direct sunlight or above a supply vent.
- **Breaker:** Check that the furnace breaker is fully on and hasn't tripped. Flip it fully off, then on, once.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house and open any closed registers. Make sure furniture or rugs aren't blocking returns.
- **Condensate (96% models):** Look for standing water near the furnace or a full condensate pump reservoir. If the pump is full or the drain line is obviously clogged, clearing it may restore normal operation.

If short cycling continues after fresh filter, good thermostat power, and open vents, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will:

1. **Measure temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the rating plate range. High rise points to airflow restriction; low rise can indicate other issues.
2. **Check static pressure** to confirm airflow through the ductwork, coil, and filter.
3. **Inspect and clean or test the flame sensor** with a microamp meter to confirm a stable flame signal.
4. **Read the control board LED flash codes** and check the sequence of operation against the model's diagram.
5. **Test the high-limit and rollout switches** for correct open/close behavior.
6. **Verify blower motor amperage** and inspect the blower wheel for dirt buildup.
7. **On 96% models, test the pressure switch(es) and inspect the condensate trap/drain.**
8. **Assess sizing and duct capacity** if no component is at fault — the unit may simply be oversized.

Ask to see the temperature-rise and microamp readings — those numbers justify the diagnosis.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 1-2 min, shuts off, restarts; hot air register | Dirty filter / restricted airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise & static pressure |
| Burners light then drop out repeatedly | Dirty/misaligned flame sensor | None (fragile part) | Clean or replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycles worse on cold days | Oversized unit or marginal limit | Confirm vents open | Test high-limit, evaluate sizing |
| Water near furnace, cycling (96% model) | Clogged condensate/pressure switch | Empty pump, clear obvious clog | Clear trap, test pressure switch |
| Random cycling, thermostat display flickers | Thermostat/wiring/batteries | Replace batteries, check location | Verify 24V, wiring, cycle rate |
| Blower runs weak, unit overheats | Dirty blower wheel / failing motor | None | Clean wheel, test motor amps |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional and diagnostic fees vary):

- **Air filter (DIY):** $10 – $40
- **Thermostat batteries / replacement:** $0 – $250
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80 – $220
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Pressure switch (96% models):** $150 – $350
- **Condensate pump / drain clearing:** $100 – $300
- **Blower motor cleaning:** $150 – $300; **motor replacement:** $400 – $700+
- **Control board (IFC) replacement:** $350 – $650
- **Gas valve replacement:** $400 – $700
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90 – $180 (often credited toward the repair)

If the fix is a filter or vents, expect $0 beyond materials. Board, motor, or gas valve work is where a pro-level bill lands.
