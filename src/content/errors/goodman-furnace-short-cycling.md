## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single flash code — it's a behavior. Your Goodman furnace lights the burners, runs briefly, shuts off, and then restarts a short time later, over and over. A normal heating cycle should run roughly 7 to 15 minutes.

Short cycling almost always means a **safety device is doing its job** — most often the high-limit switch shutting the burner down because the heat exchanger is getting too hot. It can also come from ignition problems, a miscommunicating thermostat, or a furnace that's simply oversized for the home.

On most Goodman boards, repeated safety trips will eventually cause a **lockout** with a specific LED flash pattern. Check the diagnostic label inside the blower door and count the flashes on the control board — the meaning of each pattern varies by model and board revision, so use *your* furnace's label.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts return airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and the high-limit switch cuts the burner. Cheapest and most common cause.
2. **Blocked airflow beyond the filter** — closed or covered supply/return vents, a collapsed flex duct, or a dirty blower wheel starving the system of air.
3. **Dirty flame sensor** — the board can't confirm flame, drops out the gas valve, and re-tries, producing rapid on/off firing.
4. **Overheating from a failing blower motor or capacitor** — the blower runs slow or not at all, so heat builds and trips the limit.
5. **Faulty high-limit switch** — a limit that trips early or is out of spec shuts the burner even when airflow is fine.
6. **Thermostat issues** — dead batteries, loose wiring, or a thermostat mounted near a supply register or in direct sun.
7. **Flue/venting or pressure-switch problems** — a partially blocked flue or borderline inducer draft can cause intermittent shutdowns.
8. **Oversized furnace** — if the furnace is too large for the home, it satisfies the thermostat fast and cycles frequently. This is a design issue, not a broken part.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. **Do not** bypass the limit switch, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many short-cycling cases.
- **Thermostat batteries:** Swap in fresh batteries if your model uses them. A low battery can cause erratic cycling.
- **Thermostat location:** Make sure it isn't in direct sunlight, above a supply vent, or near a lamp/appliance that reads false heat.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on. Don't repeatedly cycle it to "reset" a locked-out unit.
- **Supply and return vents:** Open all registers and clear furniture, rugs, or boxes blocking returns. Closing too many vents raises static pressure and causes overheating.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency models):** On 90%+ units, check that the condensate drain isn't clogged and the trap isn't full — a backed-up drain can trip a pressure or float switch and interrupt cycles.

If short cycling continues after a fresh filter and open vents, it's time for a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works the problem in order and can explain each step:

1. **Reads the LED flash code** on the control board and cross-references your furnace's diagnostic label.
2. **Measures temperature rise** across the furnace (return vs. supply air) and compares it to the rating-plate range — high rise points to airflow restriction.
3. **Checks static pressure** to find hidden duct, filter, or blower restrictions.
4. **Inspects and cleans the flame sensor**, then reads the microamp signal to confirm reliable flame sensing.
5. **Tests the blower motor and capacitor** and inspects the blower wheel for dirt buildup.
6. **Tests the high-limit and rollout switches** for correct operation and spec.
7. **Checks the inducer, pressure switch, and flue** for draft and blockage.
8. If everything checks out mechanically, **evaluates furnace sizing** against the home's load.

If a quote jumps straight to "replace the control board" without a filter, airflow, and flame-sensor check first, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fires, runs 2–4 min, shuts off, repeats | Dirty filter / restricted airflow | Replace filter, open all vents | Measure temp rise & static pressure; clean blower |
| Burner lights then drops out in seconds | Dirty flame sensor | None (safe to clean only if comfortable) | Clean/replace sensor, read microamps |
| Blower barely spins or won't start | Bad blower motor/capacitor | Confirm breaker on | Test & replace motor or capacitor |
| Rapid cycling with erratic thermostat display | Dead batteries / bad thermostat | Replace batteries | Replace/relocate thermostat, check wiring |
| Cycles fine then trips randomly | Faulty high-limit switch | None | Test limit, replace if out of spec |
| Short cycles on humid or cold days (90%+ unit) | Clogged condensate/pressure switch | Check & clear drain trap | Clear flue, test inducer & pressure switch |
| Cycles frequently but heats fine, all checks pass | Oversized furnace | None | Load calculation; discuss options |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Actual prices vary by region and contractor.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80–$250
- **Blower capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900 (higher for ECM variable-speed motors)
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$400
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$600
- **Control board:** $350–$650
- **Blower wheel cleaning / duct correction:** $150–$500
- **Manual J load calculation (sizing):** $150–$400

If the fix is an oversized furnace, no single part will solve it — a right-sized replacement is the real remedy, and any honest tech will say so.
