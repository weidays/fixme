## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single Goodman fault code — it is a **behavior** where your furnace turns on and off much more frequently than it should, often running for only 30 seconds to a few minutes before shutting down and restarting.

Goodman's integrated furnace control (IFC) board does not usually flash one dedicated "short cycling" code. Instead, you may see the diagnostic LED flashing codes tied to the underlying cause — such as a limit switch open, a failed flame sense, or a pressure switch fault. The exact code and flash pattern **varies by board generation and by model series**, so check the diagnostic label inside your furnace's blower door for your specific unit.

Short cycling is a symptom, not a diagnosis. The goal is to find *why* the furnace keeps stopping.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the most common cause. Restricted airflow makes the heat exchanger overheat, tripping the high-limit switch and shutting off the burners.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — a carbon-coated flame rod fails to prove flame within a few seconds, so the board closes the gas valve and retries. This produces a rapid ignite-then-shutdown pattern.
3. **Blocked or closed vents / undersized ductwork** — closed registers, crushed ducts, or a dirty blower wheel reduce airflow and cause limit trips.
4. **Overheating heat exchanger / limit switch cycling** — from any airflow restriction; if the switch is failing it may open early even with good airflow.
5. **Flue / pressure switch issues** — a blocked exhaust, condensate backup in the drain, or a marginal inducer causes the pressure switch to open mid-cycle.
6. **Thermostat problems** — poor location (near a supply vent), loose wiring, or a failing thermostat that "cycles" the call for heat.
7. **Oversized furnace** — an oversized unit heats the space so fast it satisfies the thermostat and shuts off, then restarts constantly. Common in retrofitted homes.
8. **Failing control board** — less common, but a faulty IFC can misread inputs or drop the heat call.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. **Do not** bypass any safety switch, open the gas valve manually, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many short-cycling cases.
- **Thermostat** — set it clearly above room temperature to confirm a steady heat call. Replace batteries if it uses them. Check that it isn't mounted near a supply vent or in direct sun.
- **Breaker** — confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (flip fully off, then on).
- **Supply and return vents** — walk the house and open any closed registers; make sure furniture, rugs, or boxes aren't blocking returns.
- **Condensate drain** (high-efficiency 90%+ models) — look for a clogged or overflowing condensate line/pan. A backup can trip the pressure switch.
- **Around the furnace** — make sure nothing is blocking the intake or exhaust termination outside.

If a fresh filter and open vents don't resolve it, call a licensed technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech should:

1. **Read the diagnostic LED** flash code on the IFC board and note it.
2. **Measure temperature rise** (return vs. supply air) and compare to the rating plate. High rise confirms an airflow problem.
3. **Check static pressure** to quantify duct/filter restriction.
4. **Clean and test the flame sensor**, measuring microamp signal, and inspect the igniter.
5. **Test the limit and pressure switches** and inspect the inducer and flue path.
6. **Inspect the heat exchanger** for cracks or blockage.
7. **Verify furnace sizing** against the home's heat load if oversizing is suspected.

If a quote jumps straight to "replace the control board" or "new furnace" without checking the filter, airflow, and flame sensor first, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 5–10 min then shuts off, restarts | Overheating from dirty filter/restricted airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, clean blower, check ducts |
| Ignites, burns ~5 sec, shuts off, retries | Dirty flame sensor | None (leave for pro) | Clean/replace flame sensor, check microamps |
| Cycles rapidly, LED shows limit code | High-limit switch tripping | Replace filter, open registers | Test limit switch, inspect heat exchanger |
| Won't finish cycle, pressure switch code | Blocked flue or condensate backup | Clear condensate drain, check exterior vent | Inspect inducer, flue, pressure switch |
| Cycles regardless of airflow | Oversized furnace or bad thermostat | Replace thermostat batteries | Load calc, thermostat/board diagnosis |
| Random shutdowns, erratic behavior | Failing control board | None | Test and replace IFC board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional variation applies):

- **Air filter** — $10–$40 DIY. No labor if you do it yourself.
- **Flame sensor cleaning** — $80–$150 as part of a service call; **replacement** $150–$300.
- **High-limit switch** — $150–$350 replaced.
- **Pressure switch** — $150–$350 replaced.
- **Inducer motor** — $300–$600 replaced.
- **Blower motor cleaning/replacement** — $150 cleaning to $600+ for a replacement.
- **Control board (IFC)** — $350–$600 replaced.
- **Ductwork correction / added returns** — $300–$1,500+ depending on scope.
- **Furnace replacement (oversizing)** — $4,000–$8,000+ and only after a load calculation confirms it.

Many short-cycling calls end up being a $10 filter or a flame sensor cleaning under a standard diagnostic fee ($80–$180).
