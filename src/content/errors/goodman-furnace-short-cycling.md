## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single blink code on a Goodman board — it's a behavior: your furnace turns on and off far more frequently than it should, often before the room ever reaches the thermostat setpoint. A healthy furnace typically runs for several minutes per cycle. Short cycling means it fires for seconds to a couple of minutes, shuts down, then restarts.

On Goodman furnaces this behavior is almost always the control board doing its job — shutting off the burners when a safety switch trips (like the high-limit), when flame isn't confirmed, or when it receives an on/off signal from a faulty thermostat. In other words, short cycling is a **symptom of another problem**, not the problem itself.

Depending on your model, the control board's diagnostic LED may flash a code that points to the underlying cause (open limit, flame sense failure, pressure switch, etc.). **The exact LED codes vary by board and series**, so check the label inside your blower door for the legend that matches your unit.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — Restricted airflow overheats the heat exchanger, tripping the high-limit switch. Most common and cheapest cause.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — The sensor can't confirm flame, so the board cuts gas within seconds of ignition, then retries. Causes rapid firing/shutoff.
3. **Thermostat problems** — Bad location (near a vent or heat source), miswiring, dead batteries, or "cycles per hour" set too high can cause frequent on/off signals.
4. **Blocked or closed vents/returns** — Too many closed registers or blocked returns starve the blower of airflow and cause overheating.
5. **Failing high-limit switch or overheating from a weak blower motor** — If airflow is fine but the limit still trips, the switch itself or the blower may be failing.
6. **Blocked flue/exhaust or pressure switch faults** — On 90%+ condensing models, a clogged condensate drain, blocked flue, or failing inducer can trip the pressure switch mid-cycle.
7. **Oversized furnace** — A furnace too large for the home heats fast and shuts off repeatedly. Common in poorly matched installs; not a repair, but a design issue.
8. **Control board fault** — Less common, but a failing integrated ignition control can misread inputs and cycle the unit.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the gas valve, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Check the setpoint. If your thermostat has a "cycles per hour" or "cph" setting, make sure it's at the recommended gas-furnace value (often the lowest, around 1–2 cph).
- **Thermostat location:** Make sure it isn't in direct sunlight, near a supply vent, or above a lamp/appliance that fools it into shutting off early.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (not tripped or partially seated).
- **Vents and returns:** Open closed supply registers and clear anything blocking return-air grilles. Don't run with too many rooms closed off.
- **Condensate drain (90%+ models):** If you see standing water near the unit or a clogged drain line, a blocked condensate line can trip the pressure switch. Clearing a visible clog is homeowner-safe; anything internal is a technician job.

If short cycling continues after clean filter, good batteries, and open vents — it's time for a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech should verify the actual cause rather than swapping parts. Expect them to:

1. **Read the board's diagnostic LED** for the stored fault code and cross-reference it to your specific Goodman model.
2. **Check temperature rise** across the heat exchanger with a thermometer at supply and return — high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
3. **Test the flame sensor** microamp signal; a weak or dropping signal explains rapid ignition/shutoff.
4. **Inspect and test safety switches** — high-limit, rollout, and pressure switches — measuring continuity and whether they're tripping legitimately or failing.
5. **Verify blower operation** — amp draw, wheel condition, and airflow (CFM).
6. **On 90%+ units, check the inducer, flue, and condensate path** for restrictions affecting the pressure switch.
7. **Confirm thermostat wiring and settings** and rule out an oversizing/design issue.

A good quote names a specific cause. Be cautious of anyone recommending a control board or heat exchanger replacement without first ruling out the filter, flame sensor, and airflow.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes, shuts off, restarts | Overheating / high-limit trip from dirty filter | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, test limit switch & blower |
| Fires up then shuts off in seconds | Dirty flame sensor | None safe beyond filter | Clean or replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycles on/off erratically, no clear pattern | Thermostat fault or bad location | Replace batteries, check cph setting, relocate | Verify wiring, replace thermostat if needed |
| Short cycles with water near unit (90%+) | Clogged condensate / pressure switch trip | Clear visible drain clog | Clear internal drain, test pressure switch & inducer |
| Heats fast then quits, whole house | Oversized furnace | Confirm filter is clean first | Evaluate sizing / airflow; may need design fix |
| Cycling with LED fault code | Board reading a fault, or board failing | Note the LED pattern | Diagnose per code, replace control board if faulty |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US prices including labor. Your area and model affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries / settings:** $0–$15 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** often folded into a service call, roughly $80–$200.
- **Flame sensor replacement:** $80–$250.
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $100–$300.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $100–$300.
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250.
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $300–$600+.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900+ depending on ECM vs. PSC.
- **Integrated control board replacement:** $350–$700+.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $75–$200, often credited toward the repair.

A furnace that's genuinely oversized isn't a "repair" — correcting it may mean modifications or, eventually, a properly sized replacement, which is a much larger conversation.
