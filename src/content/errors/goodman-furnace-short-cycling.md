## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a specific Goodman flash code — it's a behavior. Your furnace starts a heating cycle, runs for a short time (anywhere from a few seconds to a couple of minutes), then shuts down, only to try again shortly after. On boards with diagnostic LEDs, you may see flash codes for **open limit**, **flame lost**, **pressure switch open**, or the unit may simply cycle without ever posting a hard lockout.

Short cycling almost always means the furnace is either **overheating and protecting itself**, **failing to confirm safe operation** (flame, airflow, or venting), or **getting confused signals from the thermostat**. On Goodman two-stage and modulating models (GMVC, GCVC), staging logic can make normal cycling look abnormal, so it's worth confirming what "normal" is for your model in the installation manual.

If burners light then shut off within seconds repeatedly, and the unit finally goes into a hard lockout, see the **Goodman Furnace Lockout** page.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, heat builds up, and the high-limit switch trips, cutting the burners early. Most common cause by far.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — the board can't confirm flame, so it drops the burners within seconds and retries.
3. **Blocked or restricted airflow** — closed/blocked supply registers, undersized return, or a dirty blower wheel cause the same overheating as a dirty filter.
4. **Faulty high-limit switch** — a limit that trips prematurely (or a genuinely overheating exchanger) forces repeated shutdowns.
5. **Pressure switch / venting issues** — a clogged condensate drain, blocked flue, or failing inducer trips the pressure switch mid-cycle.
6. **Thermostat problems** — wrong location (near a vent), miswiring, dead batteries, or an oversized furnace cycling too fast for the space.
7. **Flame rollout or rollout switch trip** — if you smell burning, stop and see the **Goodman Furnace Flame Rollout** page immediately.
8. **Failing control board or gas valve** — least common; diagnosed after ruling out the above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, dusty, or older than 60–90 days, swap it. This alone fixes a large share of short-cycling cases.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace batteries if it uses them. Make sure it's set to **Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Confirm it isn't mounted in direct sun or above a supply vent.
- **Check the breaker.** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (not tripped or half-way).
- **Open and unblock vents.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and unobstructed by furniture or rugs.
- **Check the condensate drain (high-efficiency models).** A full or clogged drain line can trip the pressure switch. If you see standing water, clear the drain and consider condensate tablets.
- **Do NOT** bypass the limit or pressure switches, repeatedly reset a locked-out unit, or open the gas valve. If you smell gas, leave and call your gas utility's emergency line or 911.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will:

- Read the **flash code** on the control board and check fault history.
- Measure **temperature rise** across the heat exchanger and compare it to the rating plate — high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- Inspect and clean or test the **flame sensor** (microamp reading).
- Test the **high-limit switch** and **pressure switch** with a meter, and check the **inducer motor** and flue for blockage.
- Inspect the **blower wheel** for dirt buildup and verify blower speed/staging.
- Check **gas pressure** at the valve and confirm the **thermostat wiring and heat anticipator/cycle rate**.
- On modulating/two-stage units, verify **staging behavior** against spec before condemning any part.

If a quote jumps straight to "replace the control board" without a temperature-rise or microamp reading, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes, shuts off, restarts | Dirty filter / airflow restriction | Replace filter, open vents | Clean blower, check temp rise |
| Burners light then die in seconds | Dirty flame sensor | Replace/swap filter first | Clean or replace flame sensor, check microamps |
| Cycles with pressure-switch flash code | Clogged drain, flue, or inducer | Clear condensate drain | Test inducer, clear flue, replace pressure switch |
| Cycles with open-limit flash code | Overheating / bad limit switch | Replace filter, open registers | Test limit, inspect heat exchanger |
| Rapid cycling, no fault code | Thermostat location/wiring or oversized unit | Move nothing; check batteries/setpoint | Verify wiring, cycle rate, staging |
| Burning smell with cycling | Possible flame rollout | Shut down, ventilate | See Flame Rollout page — do not run |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional variation applies):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** often folded into a service call; sensor part $15–$40 if replaced.
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90–$180.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$350 replaced.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350 replaced.
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$200.
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$600 replaced.
- **Control board:** $350–$600 replaced.
- **Gas valve:** $300–$600 replaced.

Most short-cycling calls resolve at the low end once airflow and the flame sensor are addressed. Board and gas valve replacements are the exception, not the rule.
