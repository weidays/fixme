## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a specific Goodman fault code — it's a behavior. Your furnace starts a heating cycle, runs for only a short time (seconds to a couple of minutes), shuts down, then restarts soon after. Depending on the model and board, you may see a flashing diagnostic LED indicating a limit trip, a failed flame sense, or a pressure switch fault, or you may see no code at all if the thermostat is cycling the unit.

Short cycling matters because each start puts stress on the igniter, gas valve and blower, and because the underlying cause — often overheating — can degrade the heat exchanger over time. On Goodman integrated-control-board furnaces, the LED flash pattern printed inside the blower door tells you whether the board sees a limit trip, a flame-sense failure, or a pressure-switch problem, which narrows the cause considerably.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts return airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and the high-limit switch cuts the burners early. By far the most common cause.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — the flame rod gets coated and can't prove flame, so the board shuts the gas valve within seconds of ignition, then retries.
3. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or blocked returns starve airflow, mimicking a dirty filter.
4. **Thermostat problems** — dead batteries, poor location (near a supply vent or heat source), loose wiring, or an aggressive anticipator/cycle-rate setting.
5. **Overheating from a failing blower motor or dirty blower wheel** — reduced airflow trips the high limit repeatedly.
6. **Flue/pressure-switch or condensate issues (96% units)** — a blocked condensate drain, plugged flue, or weak inducer can trip the pressure switch and abort the cycle.
7. **Oversized furnace** — a unit too large for the load heats fast and satisfies the thermostat quickly. Common after non-load-calculated replacements.
8. **Faulty high-limit switch or control board** — a failed limit switch or a board with intermittent flame-sense/relay faults. Least common; diagnosed last.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted or older than 90 days, swap it and watch whether cycling improves. This is the #1 fix.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace batteries if it uses them, confirm it's set to "Heat" and the setpoint is above room temp, and make sure it isn't in direct sun or near a supply vent.
- **Walk the house and open supply/return registers.** Confirm at least most vents are open and returns aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Check the breaker.** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on; a partially tripped breaker can cause odd behavior. Reset it once only.
- **Look at the condensate drain (96% high-efficiency units).** A backed-up drain pan or clogged line can trip the pressure switch. You can clear a visible clog and empty a full pan.
- **Look, don't touch, at the burner flame.** A quick glimpse through the sight window is fine, but do **not** open the gas valve, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that has locked out.

If cycling continues after a fresh filter and thermostat check, stop here and call a licensed tech.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should:

- Read the board's LED flash code and note whether it's a **limit trip**, **flame-sense failure**, or **pressure-switch** fault.
- Check **temperature rise** across the furnace against the nameplate range — high rise confirms airflow restriction/overheating.
- Measure **flame-sense microamps** and clean or replace the flame sensor if the signal is weak.
- Inspect the **filter, blower wheel and evaporator coil** for restriction, and test the blower motor and capacitor.
- Test the **high-limit switch, pressure switch and inducer**, and check the flue and condensate path on 96% units.
- Verify the **thermostat wiring and cycle-rate setting**.
- If nothing else explains it, evaluate whether the furnace is **oversized** relative to a load calculation.

Sanity check: a quote to "replace the control board" without first proving airflow, temperature rise and flame-sense readings is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes then quits, restarts later | Dirty filter / airflow restriction | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, clean blower/coil, test limit |
| Fires 5–10 sec then shuts off, retries | Dirty or failing flame sensor | Note behavior only | Clean/replace flame sensor, check microamps |
| Cycles rapidly with no burner runtime | Thermostat/wiring or cycle-rate | Replace batteries, relocate items near stat | Test wiring, adjust cycle rate, replace stat |
| Inducer runs, burners never stay lit (96%) | Pressure switch / blocked flue or drain | Clear visible condensate clog | Test pressure switch, clear flue, inspect inducer |
| Heats house very fast then cycles often | Oversized furnace | None | Load calc, staging/airflow adjustment |
| Blower struggles, unit overheats | Failing blower motor / capacitor | None | Test/replace motor or capacitor |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US retail including labor unless noted; your area and unit age will vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries / minor thermostat fix:** $0–$30 DIY; new thermostat $150–$400 installed.
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $75–$250; the part itself is $10–$25 if you DIY.
- **Blower capacitor:** $150–$350.
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900 depending on ECM vs PSC.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$400.
- **Condensate drain/flue cleaning:** $100–$300.
- **Integrated control board:** $350–$600.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90–$180, often credited toward the repair.

Correcting an **oversized furnace** may mean equipment changes and is quoted separately.
