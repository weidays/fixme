## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single blinking fault code — it's a behavior. Your Goodman furnace fires its burners, runs briefly, then shuts off well before the thermostat is satisfied, and repeats the pattern. Depending on your board revision, the LED may flash a limit or flame-related code, or it may show no error at all between cycles.

Short cycling almost always means a safety device is doing its job — a high-limit switch, pressure switch, or flame-sensing circuit is interrupting the heat call because a condition isn't right. The furnace is protecting itself; your job (and your technician's) is to find *why* it keeps intervening.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts return airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and the high-limit switch cuts the burners. The single most common cause.
2. **Weak or dirty flame sensor** — the board sees an unstable flame signal and shuts down, then retries. Common on older units.
3. **Blocked or closed supply/return airflow** — too many closed registers, crushed ductwork, or a blocked return starves airflow and trips the limit.
4. **Faulty high-limit switch** — a failing limit opens prematurely even with adequate airflow.
5. **Pressure switch / flue issues** — a partially blocked flue, condensate backup, or failing inducer causes the pressure switch to open mid-cycle.
6. **Thermostat problems** — bad location (near a supply vent), loose wiring, or low batteries causing rapid on/off signals.
7. **Oversized furnace** — an oversized unit heats the space too fast and satisfies the thermostat quickly; this is a design issue, not a broken part.
8. **Control board (IFC) fault** — the least common, but a failing board can misread inputs and cycle the burners.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the gas valve manually, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a furnace that has locked out.

- **Filter:** Pull it out and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes many short-cycling cases.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it uses them. Make sure it's set to Heat and not sitting in direct sun or near a supply vent.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (flip fully off, then on). A partly tripped breaker can cause odd behavior.
- **Vents and registers:** Open all supply registers and clear anything blocking return grilles. Furniture, rugs, and closed rooms starve airflow.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency 90%+ models):** Check the condensate drain and trap for clogs or standing water. A backed-up drain can trip the pressure switch. Empty an overflow pan if you see one full.
- **Return path:** Make sure filter slots and blower doors are closed and sealed — a loose door can trip the door safety switch.

If it still short cycles after these, it's a pro job.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech will typically:

- Read the IFC board's LED flash code and cross-reference it with the label inside the blower door.
- Measure **temperature rise** across the heat exchanger and compare it to the rating plate — high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- Clean and test the **flame sensor**, checking microamp signal against spec.
- Test the **high-limit switch** and **pressure switch** continuity through a full cycle.
- Inspect the **inducer motor**, flue, and condensate path for restrictions.
- Check thermostat wiring and heat anticipator/cycle settings.
- Verify furnace sizing versus the home's load if cycling is very rapid with no other fault.

Sanity check: a quote should name the *specific* part or condition found, not just "replace the board."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fires, runs 1–3 min, shuts off, repeats | Dirty filter / overheating | Replace filter, open all vents | Measure temp rise, test limit switch |
| Burners light then die in seconds | Weak flame sensor | Replace filter first | Clean/replace flame sensor, check microamps |
| Inducer runs but burners won't stay lit | Pressure switch / flue block | Check condensate drain | Test pressure switch, clear flue |
| Rapid on/off, no burner issue | Thermostat fault/location | Replace batteries, relocate away from vents | Verify wiring, cycle rate settings |
| Very short even cycles, new install | Oversized furnace | None | Confirm load calc, adjust staging if applicable |
| Cycles with limit LED flashing | High-limit tripping | Replace filter, open vents | Test limit, check blower speed |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; varies by region and model):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** often part of a $90–$180 service call; **replacement** $120–$250.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$350 replaced.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$400 replaced.
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$600+ replaced.
- **Thermostat:** $150–$350 installed (basic to smart).
- **Control board (IFC):** $350–$700 replaced.
- **Oversizing correction:** varies widely; may involve staging adjustments or, in worst cases, equipment replacement.

Get the specific failed part named on your invoice before approving a board or motor swap.
