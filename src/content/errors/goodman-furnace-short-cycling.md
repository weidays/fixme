## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single Goodman fault code — it is a behavior. Your furnace starts a heating cycle, then shuts the burners off after only a short time, and repeats this over and over without properly warming the house. On Goodman boards you may see a diagnostic LED flash pattern (often a limit-open or airflow-related code) or no code at all, depending on the model.

The key distinction: normal furnaces cycle a few times per hour. Short cycling means cycles of roughly a minute or two, repeating rapidly. This wastes fuel, wears out ignition components, and — most importantly — often signals that the furnace is overheating and the safety limit is doing its job by shutting the burners down.

Because short cycling can stem from overheating, gas valve issues, or control board faults, we mark this **pro** severity. You can safely rule out the easy causes, but the deeper ones need a technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — Restricted airflow overheats the heat exchanger, tripping the high-limit switch and cutting the burners early. By far the most common cause.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — Too many closed registers or blocked returns choke airflow, causing the same overheating trip as a dirty filter.
3. **Dirty flame sensor** — A carbon-coated flame sensor fails to confirm flame, so the board shuts the gas valve seconds after ignition, then retries. This can look like short cycling.
4. **Oversized furnace** — An oversized unit heats the space quickly, satisfies the thermostat, and shuts off — then restarts soon after. Common on newer or replacement installs.
5. **Thermostat problems** — Bad location (near a supply vent), miswiring, dead batteries, or a failing thermostat can command rapid on/off cycles.
6. **Failing high-limit switch** — A weak or faulty limit switch can trip at too low a temperature, shutting burners off prematurely even with adequate airflow.
7. **Weak or dirty igniter / flame issues** — Marginal ignition can cause repeated ignition attempts and dropouts.
8. **Failing inducer motor or pressure switch** — A weak inducer or intermittent pressure switch can interrupt the cycle after startup.
9. **Control board fault** — A failing Goodman integrated control board can misread inputs and drop the cycle. This is diagnosed last, after other causes are excluded.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do — no panels bypassed, no gas or wiring work.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to Heat, a few degrees above room temperature. Replace batteries if it uses them. Make sure it isn't mounted right next to a supply register or in direct sun.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully ON. A tripped or half-tripped breaker can cause erratic behavior — reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Walk the house and open supply registers; keep at least 80% open. Make sure returns aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate (96%/high-efficiency models):** Check that the condensate drain and trap aren't clogged or overflowing, which can trip a pressure/float switch. Clear standing water only if you can reach it safely.

If short cycling continues after a fresh filter, open vents, and good batteries, it's time for a technician. **Do not** repeatedly reset a furnace that keeps locking out, and never disable or jump the limit switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Goodman tech should:

- **Read the LED diagnostic code** on the control board (flash pattern) and interpret it against the model's chart.
- **Measure temperature rise** across the heat exchanger and compare it to the rating-plate spec — a high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- **Check static pressure** in the ducts to find airflow restrictions beyond the filter (undersized ducts, collapsed flex, dirty blower wheel or coil).
- **Test the flame sensor** microamp signal and clean or replace it.
- **Inspect and test the high-limit switch, pressure switch, and inducer** for premature trips or weak operation.
- **Verify gas pressure** at the valve and check ignition sequence timing.
- **Review sizing** if the furnace is newer — confirm it matches a load calculation and check staging on two-stage/modulating models (GMVC/GCVC).

Sanity check: a quote that jumps straight to "replace the control board" or "replace the furnace" without first checking filter, airflow, temperature rise, and flame sensor is skipping the cheap causes.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cycles every 1-3 min, weak airflow | Dirty filter / airflow restriction | Replace filter, open vents | Measure static pressure, clean blower/coil |
| Burners light, drop out after ~5-7 sec | Dirty flame sensor | None (needs panel off) | Clean or replace flame sensor, check microamps |
| Heats fast then shuts off, repeats | Oversized furnace | Check if unit is new/replacement | Verify sizing, adjust staging, review load calc |
| Random rapid on/off, house comfy | Thermostat fault/location | Replace batteries, check placement | Rewire, relocate, or replace thermostat |
| Overheats with clean filter | Failing high-limit switch | None | Test and replace limit switch |
| Inducer stops mid-cycle | Weak inducer / pressure switch | Check condensate drain | Test inducer amps, replace pressure switch |
| Erratic, LED shows board fault | Control board failure | None | Confirm inputs, replace integrated control board |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your area and model vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** often folded into a $90–$180 service call; replacement part $15–$40 plus labor.
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 depending on model.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Hot surface igniter:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$650 installed.
- **Integrated control board:** $350–$600+ installed.
- **Duct/airflow correction or resizing:** highly variable, $300 to several thousand for major duct work.
