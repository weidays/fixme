## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single Goodman flash code — it's a behavior. Your furnace ignites, runs for a short time (often under 3-5 minutes), shuts off, then restarts a short while later, over and over. Depending on the underlying cause, your Goodman control board may show a high-limit fault, a flame-sense fault, or no code at all.

In almost every case, short cycling means the furnace is either **overheating and tripping a safety limit** or **losing its flame signal** before it can complete a normal heat call. Both are the furnace protecting itself. A normal cycle is 10-15 minutes of runtime; anything drastically shorter that repeats is a problem worth diagnosing.

Because the root cause ranges from a $15 filter to a failing control board or a cracked heat exchanger, and because some causes involve the flue and combustion, this is rated **pro** severity. Do the safe checks below first, then call a licensed HVAC tech if they don't resolve it.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter / restricted airflow.** The most common cause by far. Restricted return air lets the heat exchanger overheat and trip the high-limit switch, cutting the burner early.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents.** Too many closed registers or blocked returns create the same overheating condition as a dirty filter.
3. **Dirty flame sensor.** A carbon-coated flame rod fails to prove flame, so the board shuts the gas valve seconds after ignition, then retries. (See our Goodman Furnace Lockout page for the related lockout behavior.)
4. **Failing high-limit switch.** A weak or out-of-spec limit switch trips at a lower temperature than it should, ending cycles prematurely.
5. **Oversized furnace.** A furnace too large for the home heats quickly, satisfies the thermostat fast, and cycles frequently. Common in poorly sized installs.
6. **Thermostat problems.** Low batteries, poor location (near a vent or heat source), or a faulty thermostat can command rapid on/off cycles.
7. **Blocked flue / pressure switch fault.** A restricted exhaust or condensate-clogged pressure switch tubing (90%+ models) can interrupt cycles.
8. **Failing inducer motor or bad control board.** Less common; the board or inducer misreads conditions and aborts the cycle.
9. **Cracked heat exchanger.** Rare but serious — can cause erratic cycling along with other symptoms. Requires immediate professional inspection.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything feels beyond them.

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or older than 90 days, swap it. This alone fixes a large share of short cycling cases.
- **Open and unblock vents.** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or dust buildup.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace batteries if it uses them. Make sure it's set to Heat, and confirm it isn't mounted near a supply vent, lamp, or sunny window that could fool it.
- **Check the breaker.** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully ON (flip it fully off, then on if it looks tripped) — once only.
- **Inspect the condensate drain (90%+ furnaces).** If you see standing water or a full drain pan, a clogged condensate line can trip the pressure switch. Clearing a visible clog with a wet/dry vac is fine.

Do **not** bypass the limit switch, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a furnace that keeps locking out. Repeated resets can be dangerous.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech will confirm you're seeing genuine short cycling and work through the causes methodically:

1. **Read the flash code** on the control board through the blower-door sight glass and note the pattern against the label.
2. **Measure temperature rise** across the heat exchanger and compare it to the rating-plate spec. High rise points to airflow restriction; overheating trips the limit.
3. **Test static pressure** to confirm whether ductwork, filter, or coil is choking airflow.
4. **Check the flame sensor** microamp signal and clean or replace the rod if the signal is weak.
5. **Test the high-limit and rollout switches** for correct trip points and continuity.
6. **Inspect the inducer, pressure switch, and flue** for blockage or weak draft (90%+ models).
7. **Verify furnace sizing and gas pressure**, and inspect the heat exchanger for cracks if erratic cycling persists.

A good tech should show you the temperature-rise reading or flash code before quoting a part. Be cautious of a quote to replace the board or heat exchanger without any measurements shown.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cycles every few minutes, filter is dirty | Restricted airflow overheating heat exchanger | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, test airflow |
| Ignites then shuts off in seconds, repeats | Dirty flame sensor | None safe — call pro | Clean or replace flame rod, test microamps |
| Random short cycles, filter is clean | Failing high-limit switch | None | Test and replace limit switch |
| Heats fast, satisfies quickly, cycles often | Oversized furnace | Confirm thermostat placement | Verify sizing, adjust staging if two-stage |
| Cycles with erratic thermostat readings | Thermostat fault or bad location | Replace batteries, check location | Test/replace thermostat, check wiring |
| Standing water near furnace, cycling | Clogged condensate / pressure switch | Clear visible drain clog | Clear line, test pressure switch |
| Weak or noisy exhaust, cycling | Blocked flue or inducer fault | None | Inspect flue, test inducer/draft |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Your area and model affect pricing.

- **Air filter replacement:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate line cleaning:** $0 DIY – $150 professional
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80–$250
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150–$400
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 depending on model
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$350
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $300–$600
- **Control board replacement:** $350–$600
- **Heat exchanger replacement:** $1,500–$3,000+ (often makes replacing an older furnace more sensible)
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80–$180, frequently credited toward the repair
