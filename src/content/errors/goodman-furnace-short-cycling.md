## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single Goodman fault code — it's a behavior. Your furnace fires up, runs briefly, shuts down, and then restarts a few minutes later, without ever satisfying the thermostat. On Goodman furnaces with an integrated control board, short cycling is usually the visible result of a safety device doing its job: the board keeps interrupting the heat cycle because something upstream (airflow, ignition, or sensing) is wrong.

Depending on your board, you may or may not see a blinking LED diagnostic code while this happens. Some Goodman boards flash a limit-switch or flame-sense code during short cycling; others just cycle silently. **Check the LED behind the blower access panel and match it to the label inside the door** — the code varies by model and firmware.

The key point: short cycling is a symptom. The goal is to find *why* the furnace keeps shutting itself off.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — By far the most common cause. Restricted return airflow lets the heat exchanger overheat, tripping the high-limit switch. The burners shut off, the furnace cools, and it fires again.
2. **Restricted airflow (closed vents, blocked returns, dirty blower wheel)** — Same overheating mechanism as a dirty filter. Too many closed registers or a coated blower wheel starves the system of air.
3. **Dirty or failing flame sensor** — The board can't confirm flame, so it shuts the gas valve within seconds of ignition, waits, and retries. Classic "lights, then dies" cycling.
4. **Tripping high-limit or rollout limit switch** — A weak, aged, or genuinely tripping limit switch cuts the burners on overheating. Never bypass a limit switch.
5. **Thermostat problems** — Low batteries, poor location (near a vent or lamp), short-cycle "swing" settings set too tight, or loose wiring can command rapid on-off.
6. **Oversized furnace** — An oversized unit heats the space too fast, satisfies the thermostat quickly, and short cycles by design. Common in older or replaced systems.
7. **Faulty flame rollout / pressure switch or blocked flue** — A stuck or tripping pressure switch, or a partially blocked exhaust, interrupts the cycle.
8. **Failing control board or gas valve** — Least common, but a degraded board or intermittent gas valve can produce erratic cycling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all homeowner-safe. Do **not** open the gas valve, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a furnace that has locked out.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many short-cycling cases.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it uses them. Confirm it's set to Heat and not being fooled by a nearby vent, lamp, or sunny window.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house and open closed registers. Make sure furniture or rugs aren't blocking returns. Restricting airflow causes overheating.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker hasn't tripped or is stuck between on/off. Reset it once if needed.
- **Return air path:** Make sure the blower door is fully closed — an open panel trips a door switch and interrupts cycles on many Goodman units.
- **Vent terminations (outside):** For high-efficiency models, check that the outdoor intake/exhaust PVC pipes aren't blocked by snow, leaves, or a bird's nest.

If short cycling continues after a fresh filter, open vents, and good batteries, it's time for a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should:

1. **Read the LED diagnostic code** on the Goodman control board and note the flash pattern.
2. **Measure temperature rise** across the supply and return to confirm airflow is within the nameplate range — high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
3. **Inspect and clean the flame sensor,** then measure microamp flame signal (typically a few microamps; low or dropping signal explains cycling).
4. **Test the high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and trip behavior, and check the blower wheel and motor for airflow loss.
5. **Test the pressure switch and inspect the flue** for blockage or condensate backup.
6. **Check thermostat wiring, anticipator/swing settings, and staging** if the furnace is multi-stage.
7. **Verify gas valve operation and board voltages** last, since these are the least likely causes.

You can sanity-check a quote: if a tech jumps straight to "replace the control board" or "replace the gas valve" without measuring temperature rise or cleaning the flame sensor, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes, shuts off, restarts | Dirty filter / restricted airflow, high-limit trip | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, test limit switch, clean blower |
| Lights, burns ~5-10 sec, then dies, retries | Dirty/failing flame sensor | Replace filter first, then call pro | Clean/replace flame sensor, measure microamps |
| Rapid on-off with erratic thermostat display | Thermostat batteries/location/wiring | Fresh batteries, relocate away from vents | Check wiring, swing settings, staging |
| Cycles fast even when clean, heats fast | Oversized furnace | None | Confirm sizing, adjust staging/thermostat |
| Cycling with LED limit/rollout code | Tripping limit or rollout switch | Verify filter and vents only | Test switches, find overheating source |
| High-efficiency unit cycling in cold/wet weather | Blocked flue/intake or pressure switch | Clear outdoor PVC vents | Test pressure switch, clear condensate/flue |
| Random cycling, no clear pattern | Failing control board or gas valve | None | Test board voltages and gas valve last |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries / recalibration:** $0–$20 DIY; $80–$150 for a service call.
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80–$250.
- **High-limit or rollout switch:** $150–$350.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350.
- **Blower wheel cleaning / blower motor:** $150–$600 depending on whether it's cleaning vs. replacement.
- **Control board:** $350–$600.
- **Gas valve:** $300–$600.
- **Diagnostic / service call:** typically $80–$150, often credited toward the repair.

Most short-cycling cases resolve at the low end — a filter and a flame-sensor cleaning. Board and gas-valve replacements are uncommon and should only follow a documented diagnosis.
