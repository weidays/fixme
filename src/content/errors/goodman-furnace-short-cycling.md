## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single Goodman fault code — it's a symptom. Your furnace starts a heating cycle, runs for a short time (often 1–5 minutes), then shuts off before the thermostat is satisfied, and repeats. On Goodman integrated control boards you may see a diagnostic LED flash pattern through the small sight window; the exact meaning of each flash count varies by board revision, so always match the pattern to the label inside your furnace's blower door.

Short cycling almost always points to one of three things: the furnace is **overheating and tripping a safety limit**, the **flame isn't being sensed** so the board shuts the gas, or the **thermostat is calling and dropping the call** rapidly. Left alone, it stresses the heat exchanger, blower motor and igniter, and can end in a hard lockout.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, heat builds up, the high-limit switch trips and cuts the burners. The single most common cause.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — the board can't confirm flame, so it shuts the gas within seconds of ignition, then retries. Produces a repeating light-then-die pattern.
3. **Restricted airflow elsewhere** — closed or blocked supply/return vents, a collapsed flex duct, or an undersized return.
4. **Overheating high-limit trips** — from any airflow restriction or a failing blower motor/capacitor that spins too slowly.
5. **Thermostat problems** — bad location (near a supply register or heat source), loose wiring, or dead batteries causing intermittent calls.
6. **Blocked condensate (high-efficiency 90%+ models)** — a clogged drain or trip of the condensate/pressure switch on GMEC/GMVC condensing furnaces interrupts the cycle.
7. **Flue/intake blockage or pressure (inducer) issue** — a failing pressure switch or partially blocked flue stops the ignition sequence.
8. **Oversized furnace** — an oversized unit satisfies the thermostat too fast and cycles frequently by design; common after a mismatched replacement.
9. **Failing control board or gas valve** — less common, but a board that loses flame signal or a gas valve that drops out will cause repeated short cycles.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This resolves a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it uses them. Confirm it's set to Heat and the setpoint is above room temperature. Make sure it isn't mounted where a supply register blows on it.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house and open every register. Confirm returns aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Breaker / switch:** Check the furnace breaker and the wall switch on or near the unit. A tripping breaker is a separate electrical problem — call a pro.
- **Condensate (90%+ furnaces):** Look for standing water or a full drain pan near the furnace. If the drain line is obviously clogged, that can trip the safety and cause cycling.
- **Visible flue/intake termination:** Outside, check that the PVC intake/exhaust pipes aren't blocked by snow, nests or debris.

Do **not** bypass safety switches, open the gas valve manually, or repeatedly reset a furnace that keeps locking out. Repeated resets on a unit that keeps failing can be dangerous.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should:

- Read the **LED flash code** at the board and interpret it against the door label.
- Check **temperature rise** across the furnace against the rating plate spec — high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- Inspect and **clean or replace the flame sensor**, then verify microamp signal (typically the pro checks it's within spec).
- Test the **high-limit switch**, **rollout switch** and **pressure/condensate switches** for correct operation.
- Verify **blower motor amps and capacitor** to rule out a slow blower.
- Confirm **gas pressure** and inspect the **inducer, flue and condensate** path.
- On new installs, evaluate whether the furnace is **oversized** for the load.

If a quote jumps straight to a new control board or gas valve without checking the filter, flame sensor, and temperature rise first, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes then shuts off, restarts later | Overheat / high-limit trip from dirty filter | Replace filter, open all vents | Test limit, check temp rise & blower |
| Lights, then dies within seconds, repeats | Dirty flame sensor | Replace filter first; note flash code | Clean/replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycles rapidly, thermostat display flickers | Thermostat batteries/wiring/location | Replace batteries, relocate if near vent | Repair thermostat wiring/relocate |
| Cycling with water near furnace (90%+) | Clogged condensate / switch trip | Clear obvious drain blockage | Clear line, test condensate switch |
| Short cycles in mild weather, house comfortable | Oversized furnace | None safe | Verify sizing, adjust staging/airflow |
| Won't stay lit, LED shows pressure fault | Pressure/inducer/flue issue | Clear flue termination outside | Test pressure switch, inducer, flue |
| Frequent cycles + weak airflow | Failing blower motor/capacitor | Check filter | Test/replace capacitor or motor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor, varies by region and contractor):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 DIY
- **Thermostat batteries / minor thermostat fix:** $0–$40 DIY; $150–$350 for thermostat replacement
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80–$250
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350
- **High-limit or rollout switch:** $150–$400
- **Blower capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900+
- **Control board:** $300–$650
- **Gas valve:** $300–$700
- **Correcting an oversized/mismatched furnace:** significant — often a system redesign or replacement
