## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single Goodman fault code — it is a behavior. Your furnace fires up, runs for a short period (anywhere from a few seconds to a couple of minutes), then shuts off, only to restart again soon after. It rarely completes a full heating cycle and never lets the house reach the thermostat setpoint comfortably.

On Goodman furnaces with an integrated control board, short cycling is usually the visible symptom of a protective shutdown. The board's LED may flash a diagnostic code (often related to limit trips or flame sensing) while the unit is cycling. **The flash pattern varies by board and model**, so check the label inside the burner-compartment door for your specific legend before assuming a meaning.

Short cycling protects the furnace from damage, but living with it wears out expensive components fast. The goal is to find *what* keeps interrupting the cycle.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow overheats the heat exchanger and trips the high-limit switch, shutting off the burners.
2. **Dirty or failing flame sensor** — if the board can't confirm flame within a few seconds of ignition, it closes the gas valve. This produces a very short cycle right after ignition.
3. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or blocked returns starve the system of airflow, mimicking a dirty filter.
4. **Overheating from a dirty blower wheel or failing blower motor** — insufficient air movement across the heat exchanger trips the limit.
5. **Flue or venting restriction** — a blocked exhaust can trip the pressure switch mid-cycle on 80% and 96% models.
6. **Faulty high-limit switch** — a limit that trips prematurely, even with good airflow.
7. **Thermostat issues** — bad location (near a supply register), miswiring, or dead batteries causing erratic signals.
8. **Oversized furnace** — an incorrectly sized unit heats the space too fast and satisfies the thermostat quickly. This is a design problem, not a repair.
9. **Failing integrated control board** — less common, but a board that misreads sensors or drops the call for heat can cause cycling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe. Do not open the gas valve, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or more than 90 days old, swap it. This resolves a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace batteries if applicable. Make sure it's set to HEAT and not located in a draft or above a supply register. Try a slightly higher setpoint to confirm it's calling for heat steadily.
- **Check the breaker.** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on. A partially tripped breaker can cause odd behavior.
- **Open your vents.** Make sure supply registers throughout the house are open and unobstructed by furniture or rugs, and that return grilles aren't blocked.
- **Look at the condensate drain (96% high-efficiency models).** A clogged condensate line can trip a float switch and interrupt operation. Check for standing water or a full drain pan.
- **Note the LED flash code.** Watch the control board's LED through the sight glass and count the flash pattern. Write it down for the technician — it saves diagnostic time.

If a fresh filter and open vents don't fix it, stop and call a licensed HVAC pro. Repeated short cycling usually involves the flame sensor, limit circuit, or venting — areas that require tools and gas-safety knowledge.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech won't just guess. Expect them to:

1. **Read the LED flash code** and cross-reference it with the board legend for your model.
2. **Verify airflow** — inspect the filter, blower wheel, and ductwork, and measure temperature rise across the heat exchanger against the rating-plate spec. Excessive rise points to airflow restriction.
3. **Clean and test the flame sensor** — measure microamp flame current. A weak or dropping signal explains post-ignition shutdowns.
4. **Test the high-limit switch** for continuity and correct trip behavior.
5. **Check the pressure switch and venting** on 80%/96% models for restriction or a stuck switch.
6. **Inspect the condensate system** on high-efficiency units for clogs tripping the float.
7. **Confirm thermostat wiring and heat anticipation** and rule out a poorly located stat.
8. **Evaluate sizing** if everything else checks out and the unit is simply heating the space too quickly.

A quote that jumps straight to a control board without checking airflow and the flame sensor is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes, then shuts off, restarts | Overheating from dirty filter | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, clean blower, test limit |
| Dies within seconds of ignition | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None — call pro | Clean or replace flame sensor, check microamps |
| Cycles with limited airflow at registers | Blocked ducts or blower issue | Open vents, check filter | Clean blower wheel, inspect ductwork |
| Cycles on 96% model, water near unit | Clogged condensate/float switch | Check drain for standing water | Clear condensate line, test float switch |
| Trips mid-cycle, LED shows limit fault | Faulty high-limit switch | Note flash code | Test and replace limit switch |
| Erratic starts/stops, thermostat blank | Thermostat/batteries/location | Replace batteries, relocate stat | Verify wiring, replace thermostat |
| Heats fast, satisfies stat quickly | Oversized furnace | None | Load calculation, staging or replacement advice |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Your area and contractor rates will vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** $80–$150 as part of a service call.
- **Flame sensor replacement:** $150–$300.
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150–$300.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Blower wheel cleaning:** $150–$300; blower motor replacement $400–$700+.
- **Condensate line clearing / float switch:** $100–$250.
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$350 depending on model.
- **Integrated control board replacement:** $350–$600+.
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90–$180, often credited toward the repair.

If the furnace is genuinely oversized, no repair fixes short cycling — a two-stage or modulating unit, or a correctly sized replacement, is the real fix, which is a separate and larger project.
