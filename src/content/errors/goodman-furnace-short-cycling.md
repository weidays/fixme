## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single blink code on a Goodman furnace — it's a symptom. Your furnace fires up, runs for a minute or two, shuts the burners off, then re-ignites shortly after, repeating this pattern instead of running a full, steady heating cycle.

Goodman's integrated ignition control board may or may not log a fault when this happens. On many boards you'll see a limit-switch or flame-sense flash code at the LED; on others the furnace simply cuts out without a stored code. The exact LED behavior varies by board revision, so read the label inside the blower door for your specific flash-code chart.

Short cycling matters because it usually traces back to the furnace overheating and tripping its safety limit, a marginal flame signal, or a control/thermostat problem. Left alone, the repeated heating and cooling stresses the heat exchanger and shortens the life of the igniter and blower.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged or too-restrictive air filter** — the most common cause. Reduced airflow lets the heat exchanger overheat and trips the high-limit switch, cutting burners early.
2. **Dirty or failing flame sensor** — a weak flame signal makes the board drop out the burners repeatedly, then retry, producing a short-cycle pattern before a hard lockout.
3. **High-limit switch tripping** — from blocked airflow, a dirty blower wheel, closed/blocked supply registers, or a failing limit switch itself.
4. **Blower motor or capacitor problems** — a weak or failing blower can't move enough air, causing overheat trips.
5. **Thermostat issues** — dead batteries, poor wiring, a bad location near a supply register, or an anticipator/cycle-rate setting causing rapid cycling.
6. **Flue/venting or pressure-switch faults** — a partially blocked flue or intermittent inducer/pressure-switch signal can interrupt cycles.
7. **Oversized furnace** — a unit too large for the home satisfies the thermostat quickly, then re-fires often. This is a design issue, not a broken part.
8. **Control board failure** — less common; the board mis-reads inputs or drops the gas valve intermittently.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe and require no tools or opening sealed components:

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone resolves many short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat batteries:** Replace them if your thermostat uses batteries; low power causes erratic cycling.
- **Thermostat location/settings:** Make sure it isn't in direct sun or near a supply vent. Check for a "cycle rate" or "heat cycles per hour" setting that's too high.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house and open any closed registers and clear furniture, rugs, or boxes blocking returns. Restricted airflow overheats the furnace.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (flip fully off, then on) if the unit is behaving erratically.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency models):** Check that the condensate drain isn't clogged or the pan full, which can interrupt operation on 90%+ furnaces.

Do **not** bypass the limit switch, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out. If any of these don't fix it, call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will work in a logical order so you can sanity-check the quote:

- **Verify airflow first** — check filter, blower wheel cleanliness, blower amp draw, and static pressure across the system.
- **Monitor a full cycle** — watch when and why the burners drop out (limit trip vs. flame dropout vs. thermostat).
- **Read the flame signal** — measure microamps on the flame sensor; a low reading means clean or replace it.
- **Test the high-limit switch** — confirm it's opening at the correct temperature and isn't failing early.
- **Check the inducer and pressure switch** — verify the flue is clear and the switch closes and holds.
- **Inspect the heat exchanger** — for cracks or blockage that could cause overheating.
- **Test the control board** — confirm inputs and outputs, and scan stored fault/flash codes.

Ask which of these steps they performed. A quote to replace a board or gas valve without first ruling out airflow and the flame sensor is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 1–3 min, shuts off, restarts | Dirty filter / airflow restriction | Replace filter, open vents | Check static pressure, clean blower wheel |
| Burners light then drop out repeatedly | Dirty/weak flame sensor | None safe | Clean or replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Shuts off on overheat, blower keeps running | High-limit switch tripping | Clear airflow blockages | Test/replace limit switch, find overheat cause |
| Weak airflow at all vents | Blower motor/capacitor fault | None | Test motor amps, replace capacitor or motor |
| Rapid on-off, thermostat clicking | Thermostat batteries/settings | Replace batteries, lower cycle rate | Verify wiring and thermostat calibration |
| Inducer runs, burners never stay lit | Flue/pressure-switch issue | Check flue termination outside | Test pressure switch, clear flue blockage |
| Cycles fast even when clean/serviced | Oversized furnace | None | Load calculation, adjust or recommend sizing |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor; regional and model variation applies):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 DIY
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 DIY
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** $80–$180 service call; **replacement** $150–$300
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150–$350
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$350
- **Blower motor capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900+ (ECM motors cost more)
- **Control/ignition board:** $350–$650
- **Gas valve:** $300–$600
- **Heat exchanger:** $1,000–$2,500+ (often warranty on parts; labor is significant) — at this point, weigh against furnace replacement

Many short-cycling calls end with just a filter swap or flame-sensor cleaning, so start there before authorizing expensive parts.
