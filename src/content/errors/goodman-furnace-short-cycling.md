## What this code means

"Short cycling" describes a furnace that starts a heating cycle and shuts down too soon, then restarts a short time later — often several times an hour. On most Goodman gas furnaces this isn't a single stored fault code; it's a behavior you observe, sometimes accompanied by a diagnostic LED blink pattern on the integrated control board (flashes for limit-switch open, flame not sensed, or pressure-switch faults).

The furnace is doing its job of protecting itself: when a safety device (usually the high-limit switch or flame sensor) or the thermostat interrupts the burn, the board shuts down the gas valve and blower and restarts the sequence. Frequent tripping means something is wrong with airflow, flame sensing, thermostat wiring, or the board itself.

Because the exact LED blink code and behavior vary by board generation and furnace series, read the diagnostic label inside your Goodman's blower-door panel to match your unit's specific flash pattern.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the most common cause. Restricted airflow lets the heat exchanger overheat, opening the high-limit switch and cutting the burner mid-cycle.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — a carbon-coated sensor rod can't prove flame, so the board closes the gas valve within a few seconds of ignition, then retries. This produces very fast on/off cycling.
3. **Blocked return/supply airflow** — closed registers, crushed ductwork, or an undersized return starves the blower and overheats the unit.
4. **Thermostat problems** — poor placement (near a heat source), loose or corroded wiring, low batteries, or a mis-set anticipator/cycle rate causing premature shutoff.
5. **Failing high-limit or rollout switch** — a weak or faulty limit switch trips at too low a temperature.
6. **Pressure switch / flue issues** — a marginal inducer, blocked flue, or condensate blockage on 96% units causes the pressure switch to open and close during the cycle.
7. **Oversized furnace** — a unit too large for the home heats the thermostat too fast and shuts off before the space is evenly warm. This is a design issue, not a broken part.
8. **Failing integrated control board** — least common, diagnosed after ruling out the above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the gas valve, bypass a safety switch, or repeatedly reset a furnace that has locked out.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it and run the furnace. This fixes a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it uses them. Confirm it's set to **Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Make sure it isn't in direct sun or above a lamp/vent.
- **Registers and returns:** Walk the house and open every supply and return vent. Keep at least 80% of registers fully open.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (flip it off, then firmly on) and the furnace switch (looks like a light switch near the unit) is on.
- **Condensate (96% high-efficiency units):** Look for a full or blocked condensate drain/trap. A clogged drain can trip a float switch or pressure switch — but clearing it beyond a simple visible blockage is a technician job.
- **Blower door:** Make sure the blower access panel is fully seated; the door safety switch must be closed for the furnace to run at all.

If short cycling continues after a clean filter and open vents, call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheapest and most likely to most expensive:

- Reads the **control-board LED blink code** and cross-references the wiring-diagram label inside the unit.
- Checks **temperature rise** across the heat exchanger against the nameplate spec — high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- Inspects and cleans or replaces the **flame sensor**, then measures **flame-rectification microamps** to confirm proper flame proving.
- Tests the **high-limit and rollout switches** for correct open/close temperatures.
- Verifies **inducer and pressure-switch** operation, and checks the flue and condensate path.
- Inspects blower wheel, motor amp draw, and ductwork static pressure to rule out airflow restriction.
- Checks **thermostat wiring and cycle-rate/anticipator settings**.
- Only after these tests does a competent tech condemn the **control board**.

If a quote jumps straight to a board or blower motor without the tech mentioning temperature rise, flame microamps, or a limit-switch test, ask them to show their readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burner runs 1-2 min, shuts off, restarts | Overheating from dirty filter/blocked airflow | Replace filter, open all vents | Check temperature rise, test high-limit switch |
| Ignites then shuts off in ~5-7 seconds, retries | Dirty or failing flame sensor | None (sensor is inside burner box) | Clean/replace sensor, measure flame microamps |
| Rapid on/off, LED shows limit fault | Weak high-limit switch or airflow restriction | Replace filter, open returns | Test/replace limit switch, check blower & ducts |
| Cycles with clicking at thermostat | Thermostat wiring/battery/placement | New batteries, confirm setpoint | Repair wiring, reset cycle rate, relocate stat |
| Inducer runs, burner won't stay lit, LED pressure fault | Blocked flue or condensate, weak pressure switch | Check condensate drain for visible clog | Test pressure switch, clear flue/drain, check inducer |
| Heats fast then shuts off, even heat is uneven | Oversized furnace | None | Evaluate sizing, adjust staging/cycle rate |
| Random shutdowns with no clear pattern | Failing control board | None | Confirm with tests, replace board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included unless noted:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** often part of a $90–$180 service call; **replacement** $150–$300.
- **Thermostat repair/replacement:** $150–$400 depending on model.
- **High-limit or rollout switch:** $150–$350.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350.
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$600.
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900.
- **Integrated control board:** $400–$700+.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90–$180, often credited toward the repair.

Prices vary by region, furnace series, and whether the part is still under Goodman's parts warranty (many Goodman furnaces carry a 10-year parts limited warranty when registered — labor is usually separate).
