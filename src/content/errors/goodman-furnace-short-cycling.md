## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a specific Goodman fault code — it's a behavior. Your furnace lights, runs briefly, then shuts off well before the thermostat is satisfied, and repeats this pattern. On Goodman integrated control boards you may or may not see a diagnostic LED flash accompanying it, depending on *why* it's cycling.

Short cycling almost always means one of two things: the furnace is protecting itself (overheating and tripping a limit switch), or it's losing a signal it needs to keep running (flame sense, thermostat, or a pressure/flue problem). Because it can involve the heat exchanger and combustion safety devices, this is a **pro-level** issue if the simple checks below don't resolve it.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, heat builds up, and the high-limit switch shuts the burners off. By far the most common cause.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — the board can't confirm flame, so it drops the burners a few seconds after ignition, then retries. Often mistaken for short cycling.
3. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers starve airflow and cause the same overheating trip as a dirty filter.
4. **Failing high-limit switch** — a weak or faulty limit opens prematurely even when airflow is fine.
5. **Blocked flue or condensate issue (high-efficiency models)** — a plugged condensate line or restricted intake/exhaust trips the pressure switch mid-cycle.
6. **Thermostat problems** — bad location (near a supply vent), dead batteries, or short-cycle protection misconfigured.
7. **Oversized furnace** — heats the space so fast it satisfies the thermostat quickly; common with older or replacement installs.
8. **Failing control board or wiring** — intermittent signal loss on the ignition board. Least common, diagnosed last.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening the gas valve, bypassing a switch, or repeated resets of a locked-out unit.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it and see if cycling stops.
- **Thermostat batteries:** Replace them if your thermostat uses batteries. Confirm it's set to Heat with a setpoint above room temperature.
- **Thermostat location:** Make sure it isn't in direct sun or above a supply vent, which fools it into cycling.
- **Supply and return vents:** Walk the house and open all registers. Never keep more than a couple closed.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (not tripped or half-thrown).
- **Condensate (high-efficiency models):** Look for standing water near the furnace or a full condensate trap/pan. A clogged drain can trip the safety and cause cycling — clear visible blockage only.

If cycling continues after a fresh filter and open vents, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works the airflow and safety chain in order — you can sanity-check the quote against this:

1. Confirms the symptom and reads any board LED flash codes.
2. Measures **temperature rise** across the heat exchanger and compares it to the rating plate — high rise points to airflow restriction or a blower problem.
3. Inspects and cleans the **flame sensor**, then measures microamp signal.
4. Tests the **high-limit switch** and rollout switches for continuity and correct trip points.
5. Checks the **inducer, pressure switch, flue, and condensate** on high-efficiency units for blockage.
6. Inspects the **blower wheel and motor** for dirt or failure that reduces airflow.
7. Only after ruling out airflow and safeties do they suspect the **control board**.

Be cautious of a quote that jumps straight to a control board or heat exchanger without a temperature-rise reading or flame-sensor test.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2–4 min, shuts off, repeats | Dirty filter / overheating | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, test limit switch |
| Lights then drops flame in seconds | Dirty flame sensor | None safe | Clean/replace flame sensor, check microamps |
| Cycling with water near furnace | Clogged condensate (HE models) | Clear visible drain blockage | Clear trap, test pressure switch |
| Cycles even with clean filter | Weak high-limit switch | Confirm filter is clean | Test and replace limit switch |
| Satisfies too fast, warm house | Oversized unit / thermostat spot | Move/adjust thermostat if possible | Adjust staging, evaluate sizing |
| Random cutouts, no pattern | Control board / wiring | Check breaker | Diagnose board and wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Actual prices vary by region and contractor.

- **Air filter (DIY):** $8–$30
- **Flame sensor clean/replace:** $80–$250
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150–$350
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$400
- **Condensate line clearing:** $75–$250
- **Blower motor cleaning/replacement:** $200–$700
- **Integrated control board:** $350–$650
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$180 (often applied to the repair)

If a tech reports a **cracked heat exchanger** as the root cause, that's a separate and far more serious repair — expect $1,000+ or a case for furnace replacement.
