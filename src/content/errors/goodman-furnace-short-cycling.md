## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single Goodman error code — it's a behavior. Your furnace fires up, runs for a short period (anywhere from a few seconds to a couple of minutes), then shuts down, only to try again shortly after. On furnaces with a diagnostic LED, you may see a flashing code that points to the underlying trigger (a limit fault, flame-sense fault, or pressure-switch fault), but the *symptom* you notice is the constant on-off cycling.

Short cycling almost always means a safety device is doing its job and shutting the burner down because something is wrong — most often overheating from restricted airflow, or the flame sensor losing sight of the flame. It is not normal, and it wears out components fast. Because the causes range from a $10 filter to a failing heat exchanger, this is rated **pro** severity: some checks are DIY, but persistent short cycling should be diagnosed by a licensed HVAC tech.

Blink codes vary by board revision. Read the label on the inside of the blower door for your specific model's code chart before assuming a diagnosis.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — The #1 cause. A restricted filter starves the blower of return air, heat builds up in the cabinet, and the high-limit switch trips to shut off the burners.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — A carbon buildup on the flame-sense rod prevents the board from confirming flame. The gas valve closes within a few seconds, and the sequence restarts. Classic "runs 3-5 seconds then quits" pattern.
3. **Blocked airflow beyond the filter** — Closed supply registers, crushed flex duct, a dirty blower wheel, or a closed damper cause the same overheating as a dirty filter.
4. **Faulty or tripping high-limit switch** — If the limit switch is failing or the cabinet genuinely overheats, it opens and cuts the burners.
5. **Thermostat problems** — A thermostat mounted near a supply register, in direct sun, or with dying batteries reads temperature swings incorrectly and cycles the furnace rapidly. Loose thermostat wiring does the same.
6. **Flue / venting restriction (pressure switch)** — A blocked exhaust, obstructed intake, or failing inducer causes the pressure switch to open mid-cycle, shutting things down.
7. **Oversized furnace** — An improperly sized unit heats the space so fast it satisfies the thermostat in minutes, then cycles frequently. Common in replacements sized by "bigger is better."
8. **Failing control board or gas valve** — Less common, but a degrading board or valve can intermittently drop the call for heat.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** bypass any safety switch, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a locked-out unit.

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, swap it for the correct size. This resolves a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Open all supply and return vents.** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed dampers aren't blocking airflow. At least 80% of registers should be open.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace the batteries if it uses them. Confirm it's set to Heat and not near a heat source, sunlight, or a supply vent. If it's a smart thermostat, check for a low-battery or wiring warning.
- **Check the breaker.** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on (not tripped or half-way). Cycle it off, wait 30 seconds, and back on **once** — not repeatedly.
- **Look at the condensate drain (96%+ high-efficiency models).** A clogged condensate line or full drain pan can trip a float switch and interrupt operation. Clear standing water if you can see it safely.
- **Inspect the exhaust/intake pipes outside.** Make sure the PVC vent terminations aren't blocked by snow, leaves, nests, or ice.

If short cycling continues after a clean filter and open vents, stop and call a licensed technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech won't just swap parts. Expect them to:

- **Read the diagnostic LED blink code** on the control board and cross-reference your model's chart.
- **Check static pressure and temperature rise** across the furnace to confirm whether airflow is restricted and whether the unit is overheating — this identifies limit-trip causes.
- **Measure the flame-sense microamps** with a meter. A reading below spec confirms a dirty or failing flame sensor.
- **Test the high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and correct trip behavior.
- **Test the pressure switch and inducer** — checking inducer amp draw and vacuum pressure to rule out flue restriction.
- **Inspect the blower wheel and heat exchanger** for buildup or cracks.
- **Verify thermostat wiring, heat anticipator/cycle settings, and location.**

If a tech quotes a board or gas valve without first measuring flame current, checking the filter/airflow, and reading blink codes, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 5-10 min, then shuts off, blower keeps running | Overheating / dirty filter tripping high limit | Replace filter, open vents | Check static pressure, temp rise, limit switch |
| Fires 3-5 seconds then shuts off, retries | Dirty or failing flame sensor | None (pro job) | Clean/replace flame sensor, measure microamps |
| Cycles every 1-2 minutes, thermostat in sun | Thermostat location or dead batteries | Replace batteries, relocate/shade thermostat | Verify wiring, cycle rate settings |
| Inducer starts, unit quits before burners light | Pressure switch / flue restriction | Clear vent terminations outside | Test pressure switch, inducer, clear flue |
| Water near furnace, unit stops | Clogged condensate / float switch (96% units) | Clear visible standing water | Clear condensate line, test float switch |
| Heats fast, cycles many times per hour | Oversized furnace | None | Evaluate sizing, adjust staging if two-stage |
| Intermittent, no clear pattern | Failing control board or gas valve | None (pro job) | Test board outputs and gas valve |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US retail including parts and labor. Your area and contractor will vary.

- **Air filter (DIY):** $10–$40
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $150–$300
- **High-limit or rollout switch:** $150–$350
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$550
- **Condensate line/float switch service:** $100–$250
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400
- **Gas valve:** $300–$550
- **Integrated control board:** $400–$650
- **Blower wheel cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Cracked heat exchanger:** $500–$1,500+ (often makes full furnace replacement worth considering on older units)

A diagnostic service call typically runs $80–$150, frequently credited toward the repair.
