## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single Goodman fault code — it is a behavior. Your furnace starts a heating cycle, runs briefly, shuts off, and restarts sooner than it should. On Goodman integrated control boards this often shows up as a repeating flash-code pattern on the diagnostic LED (visible through the blower-door sight glass), commonly limit-switch or flame-loss related codes depending on the model.

The exact flash pattern varies by board and series, so read your unit's legend printed inside the blower door rather than assuming. Two things are always true: the furnace is not completing a full heat call, and something is telling the control board to shut down early. That "something" is usually a safety device doing its job — which is why chronic short cycling should be diagnosed, not ignored.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, overheats the heat exchanger, trips the high-limit switch. By far the most common cause.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — burners light, then flame is not "proven," so the board closes the gas valve within seconds and retries.
3. **High-limit switch tripping** — from restricted airflow (filter, closed vents, dirty blower wheel) or a failing limit switch itself.
4. **Blocked or partially closed supply/return vents** — reduces airflow like a dirty filter would.
5. **Thermostat issues** — bad location (near a supply register), dead batteries, loose wiring, or an oversized "cycle rate" setting.
6. **Clogged condensate drain / blocked pressure switch tubing** — on high-efficiency (90%+) Goodman models, a full drain or pinched hose trips the pressure switch and stops the cycle.
7. **Failing inducer motor or pressure switch** — the board can't confirm proper draft, so it shuts down early.
8. **Oversized furnace** — a unit too large for the home heats fast, satisfies the thermostat, and short cycles by design (a comfort/sizing problem, not a fault).
9. **Cracked heat exchanger** — can trip safeties and cause erratic shutdowns; a safety concern requiring professional inspection.
10. **Failing control board or gas valve** — least common, diagnosed only after ruling out the above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do — no tools inside the burner compartment, no bypassing safeties.

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or older than 90 days, swap it and run the furnace again. This alone fixes a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace batteries if it takes them. Confirm it's set to Heat and to a temperature above room temperature. If it's mounted in direct sun or near a register, that can cause false short cycles.
- **Check the breaker.** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on. A tripped breaker won't cause short cycling, but a marginal connection can cause odd behavior.
- **Walk the vents.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and unblocked by furniture, rugs, or curtains. Closing too many vents chokes airflow.
- **Check the condensate drain (90%+ models).** If you see standing water near the furnace or a full drain pan, a clogged condensate line may be tripping the pressure switch. Clearing a visible clog or emptying an overflow tray is fine; opening the burner box is not.

If none of these resolve it, stop here and call a licensed technician. Do **not** repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out, and if you smell gas or a burning odor, shut the furnace off and call your gas utility's emergency line.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical airflow-and-flame sequence, and you can sanity-check their quote against it:

1. **Reads the flash code** on the control board and notes the failure point (limit vs. flame vs. pressure switch).
2. **Checks airflow first** — filter, blower wheel condition, temperature rise across the heat exchanger compared to the nameplate spec. A rise above the rated range confirms an airflow restriction.
3. **Cleans or tests the flame sensor** — measures microamp current; a weak signal (typically below ~1–2 µA) confirms a failing or dirty sensor.
4. **Tests the high-limit switch** for continuity and correct trip behavior.
5. **Checks the pressure switch and inducer** — measures draft and verifies the switch closes as designed; inspects condensate drain and tubing on high-efficiency units.
6. **Inspects the heat exchanger** for cracks with a camera or combustion analysis — a safety-critical step.
7. **Tests the control board and gas valve** only after mechanical and airflow causes are ruled out.

Be cautious of any quote that jumps straight to "you need a new control board" or "new furnace" without documenting airflow and flame-sensor readings first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes, shuts off, restarts | Dirty filter / overheating limit | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, clean blower, test limit switch |
| Burners light then die in seconds | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None (inside burner box) | Clean or replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycles fast even after new filter | High-limit switch fault | Confirm vents open | Test/replace limit switch, verify airflow |
| Water near furnace, high-eff. model | Clogged condensate / pressure switch | Clear visible drain clog | Clear drain, inspect tubing & pressure switch |
| Inducer runs, then unit stops | Pressure switch / inducer fault | None | Test draft, replace pressure switch or inducer |
| Cycles fast, thermostat near vent | Thermostat placement/setting | Move thermostat away from sun/register, new batteries | Adjust cycle rate, rewire/relocate stat |
| Heats fast, cycles regardless | Oversized furnace | None | Verify sizing, recommend staging/load calc |
| Erratic shutdowns + odor | Possible cracked heat exchanger | Shut off, call pro | Inspect heat exchanger, combustion test |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts plus labor. Prices vary by region and by whether your Goodman is single-stage, two-stage, or modulating.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries / relocation:** $0–$250 depending on rewiring.
- **Flame sensor cleaning:** often bundled into a service call, **$100–$200**; replacement sensor **$120–$280**.
- **High-limit switch replacement:** **$150–$400**.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** **$150–$400**.
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** **$100–$250**.
- **Inducer motor replacement:** **$300–$650**.
- **Control board replacement:** **$400–$650**.
- **Gas valve replacement:** **$350–$650**.
- **Heat exchanger inspection/replacement:** inspection often part of a diagnostic; replacement is major (often $1,500+) and frequently makes furnace replacement worth pricing.

A basic diagnostic service call typically runs **$90–$180**, often credited toward the repair.
