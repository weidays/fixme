## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a specific Goodman flash code — it is a behavior. Your furnace lights, runs briefly (often under 5 minutes), shuts off, then restarts again minutes later, over and over. Sometimes the burners never even reach a full heat cycle before the blower or the control board cuts everything off.

Short cycling almost always points to one of two things: the furnace is **overheating and protecting itself** (a safety limit trips), or the furnace is being told to stop early by a **thermostat, sensor, or control fault**. Both are worth fixing quickly because the rapid on/off cycling accelerates wear on the ignitor, gas valve, inducer and blower motor.

On many Goodman models a related overheat or flame problem will eventually escalate into a hard lockout with a diagnostic LED flash pattern. If your furnace has stopped entirely and shows a repeating flash code, read the LED at the sight glass and see our **Goodman Furnace Lockout** page.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, heat builds up, and the high-limit switch trips to shut down the burners. By far the most common cause.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return airflow** — too many closed registers, crushed flex duct, or a blocked return produces the same overheat trip as a dirty filter.
3. **Dirty flame sensor** — a carbon-coated flame rod fails to prove flame, so the board shuts the gas valve seconds after ignition, then retries. This causes a very rapid, repeatable cycle.
4. **Thermostat problems** — dead batteries, poor location (sun, drafts, near a vent), loose wiring, or a failing thermostat that satisfies too early.
5. **Overheating from a failing blower motor or capacitor** — if the blower moves too little air, the limit trips even with a clean filter.
6. **Faulty high-limit switch** — a limit that trips at too low a temperature (or is intermittently open) shuts the furnace down prematurely.
7. **Flue/venting or pressure switch issues** — a marginal inducer or partially blocked flue can make the pressure switch open and close mid-cycle.
8. **Oversized furnace** — an oversized unit heats the space fast and satisfies the thermostat quickly; common in older installs and hard to fix without replacement.
9. **Control board fault** — less common, but a failing integrated control board can misread inputs and cut cycles short.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the gas valve manifold, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a furnace that has locked out.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it is gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it with the correct size (printed on the old filter's frame). This alone resolves many short-cycling cases.
- **Registers and returns:** Open closed supply vents and make sure furniture, rugs or boxes aren't blocking return grilles. The furnace needs unrestricted airflow.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it takes them. Confirm it's set to HEAT and the setpoint is above room temperature. Check that it isn't in direct sunlight or right next to a supply vent.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on. A breaker that trips and resets can mimic cycling.
- **Visible vents:** Outside, check that the intake/exhaust PVC termination (on 90%+ models) is clear of snow, leaves, nests or ice.
- **Condensate (96% models):** A full or clogged condensate line/pan can trip a float switch and cut cycles. If you see standing water, that's a clue for the technician.

If short cycling continues after a fresh filter and open airflow, it's a pro job.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work systematically. Use this to sanity-check the visit and quote:

1. **Confirm the symptom** and read any Goodman diagnostic LED flash code at the sight glass.
2. **Measure temperature rise** across the supply and return to see if the furnace is overheating (rise above the rating plate range confirms an airflow problem).
3. **Check the filter, blower wheel and blower motor** — a dirty wheel or weak motor/capacitor reduces airflow.
4. **Test the high-limit switch** for correct trip behavior and continuity.
5. **Inspect and clean the flame sensor**, then measure the microamp flame signal to confirm the flame is being proven.
6. **Test the pressure switch and inducer** and inspect the flue for blockage or excessive back-pressure.
7. **Check thermostat wiring and calibration**, including any anticipator or cycle-rate setting.
8. **Verify gas pressure and control board inputs** last, if simpler causes are ruled out.

A good tech starts with filter/airflow and flame sensor — the cheap, common causes — before condemning a board or limit switch.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 3–5 min then shuts off, restarts | Overheating from dirty filter/airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, check blower & limit |
| Lights, then flame drops out in seconds, retries | Dirty flame sensor | Replace filter first; sensor cleaning is a pro/careful DIY task | Clean/replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycles regardless of filter; weak airflow | Failing blower motor or capacitor | None | Test capacitor, motor amps, replace as needed |
| Cuts off early, room not warm | Thermostat location or fault | New batteries, relocate away from vents/sun | Recalibrate or replace thermostat |
| Inducer runs then stops mid-cycle | Pressure switch / flue blockage | Clear outside vent termination | Test pressure switch, inspect flue & inducer |
| Standing water near furnace (96%) | Condensate float switch tripping | Note it for the tech | Clear condensate line/pan, test float switch |
| Heats fast, satisfies quickly, no faults | Oversized furnace | None | Confirm sizing; recommend options |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Regional rates and model differences vary widely.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries:** under $10 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80–$250.
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Blower capacitor replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900 depending on ECM vs PSC.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $300–$650.
- **Integrated control board replacement:** $400–$700+.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$180, often credited toward the repair.

If the underlying cause is an **oversized furnace**, no repair "fixes" the short cycling — a properly sized replacement is the real solution, which is a much larger project best quoted separately.
