## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single Goodman error code — it's a behavior. Your furnace ignites, runs briefly, shuts down, then repeats far more often than it should. A healthy Goodman furnace runs roughly 7–15 minutes per heat call. Cycles of only 2–5 minutes, or a burner that lights and drops out repeatedly, indicate a problem.

Short cycling almost always comes down to one of two root issues: the furnace is **overheating** and a safety limit is protecting it, or the **flame is being lost** (or never proven) so the control board cuts the gas valve. Both are the furnace working exactly as designed — the fault is upstream.

On Goodman integrated control boards, a rapid string of flashes on the diagnostic LED often accompanies short cycling (e.g., open limit or failure to prove flame). The exact flash code varies by model and board revision, so check the wiring diagram inside your blower door panel rather than assuming.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — Restricts return airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and the high-limit switch shuts burners off. By far the most common cause.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return registers** — Too many closed vents starve airflow just like a dirty filter.
3. **Dirty flame sensor** — A carbon-coated sensor rod fails to prove flame, so the board drops the gas valve seconds after ignition. Furnace re-tries, then short cycles.
4. **Failing high-limit switch** — A weak or intermittent limit trips at normal temperatures.
5. **Oversized furnace or restrictive ductwork** — An oversized unit heats the thermostat fast and shuts down; undersized ducts cause chronic overheating.
6. **Thermostat problems** — Weak batteries, poor location (near a supply vent), or loose wiring cause erratic on/off signals.
7. **Flue or vent obstruction** — A blocked exhaust trips the pressure switch mid-cycle.
8. **Faulty control board** — Less common, but a failing board can misread sensors or drop the gas valve intermittently.
9. **Cracked or dirty heat exchanger** — Can cause flame disruption and limit trips; a safety issue requiring a pro.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the gas valve, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it and run the furnace. This resolves a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Registers and returns:** Walk the house and open any closed supply vents. Make sure furniture, rugs, or curtains aren't blocking return grilles.
- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries if it takes them. Confirm it's set to HEAT and the setpoint is above room temperature. Make sure it isn't mounted where a supply vent blows on it.
- **Breaker:** If the furnace tripped a breaker, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency models):** Check that the condensate drain and trap aren't clogged or overflowing — a backed-up drain can trip the pressure switch and cause cycling.
- **Vents outside:** On the exterior, confirm the intake/exhaust termination isn't blocked by snow, leaves, or a nest.

If short cycling continues after a clean filter and open vents, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will work through the same fault tree, with instruments you don't have:

- **Read the diagnostic LED** flash code on the control board against the model's legend.
- **Measure temperature rise** across the supply and return to see if the furnace is overheating (rise above the rating plate range points to airflow restriction).
- **Test static pressure** to identify restrictive ductwork or a clogged coil/filter.
- **Check flame sensor microamps** — a healthy signal is typically 1–5 µA; a weak reading means a dirty or failing sensor.
- **Test the high-limit and rollout switches** for continuity and correct trip behavior.
- **Verify the pressure switch and inducer** operation, and inspect the flue for blockage.
- **Inspect the heat exchanger** for cracks with a camera or combustion analyzer.

If a quote jumps straight to "new control board" or "new furnace" without a filter check, temperature-rise measurement, or flame-sensor reading, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 3–5 min then shuts off, restarts | Dirty filter / airflow restriction | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, check ducts/coil |
| Ignites, flame drops out in seconds, retries | Dirty flame sensor | Replace filter first; sensor cleaning is a pro task | Clean/replace sensor, verify µA signal |
| Cycles with LED flashing limit code | Failing high-limit switch | None safe | Test and replace limit switch |
| Cycles many times per hour, house comfortable | Oversized unit / thermostat placement | Move thermostat off vents, new batteries | Evaluate sizing, adjust staging |
| Cycles with gurgling or water near unit | Clogged condensate (HE models) | Clear visible drain blockage | Clear trap, verify pressure switch |
| Random dropouts, no clear pattern | Failing control board | None safe | Test board, replace if confirmed |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts plus labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY, $0 labor)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Flame sensor cleaning/replacement:** $80–$250
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$400
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$400
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Control board:** $350–$600
- **Ductwork modification (for chronic airflow issues):** $500–$2,000+
- **Heat exchanger repair/replacement:** often $1,000+; frequently makes furnace replacement more sensible

Diagnostic/service call fees typically run $75–$200 and are often applied toward the repair.
