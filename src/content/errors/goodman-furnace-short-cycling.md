## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a specific numbered fault code — it's a **behavior**: your Goodman furnace fires the burners, runs briefly (often 2 to 10 minutes or less), shuts down, then restarts far too soon. Sometimes the control board's diagnostic LED will flash a related code (open high-limit, flame sense lost, etc.), and sometimes it just cycles with no lockout.

On most Goodman gas furnaces (GMVC, GMEC, GMES, GC/GM9 families), short cycling is the furnace protecting itself. The heat exchanger area gets too hot, the **high-limit switch** opens, and the board cuts the gas until things cool — then the cycle repeats. Other times it's a flame-sensing problem, a thermostat issue, or a furnace that's simply too big for the home.

Because chronic short cycling accelerates wear on the igniter, inducer, blower and board — and because it can hide an airflow or heat-exchanger problem — this page is rated **pro**: safe checks first, then a technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the #1 cause. Restricted return airflow lets the heat exchanger overheat, tripping the high-limit switch.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or blocked returns starve airflow the same way a dirty filter does.
3. **Dirty flame sensor** — the sensor gets coated, the board thinks flame is lost, and it drops the burners a few seconds after ignition, then retries.
4. **Overheating / faulty high-limit switch** — a limit switch tripping on real heat (airflow) or a failing switch tripping falsely.
5. **Blower or airflow problems** — a failing blower motor, wrong blower speed tap, or restrictive/undersized ductwork.
6. **Thermostat issues** — bad placement (near a supply vent or heat source), dead batteries, loose wiring, or an incorrect "cycle rate"/anticipator setting.
7. **Blocked condensate drain (high-efficiency models)** — a clogged drain or tripped condensate/pressure switch can interrupt the cycle.
8. **Oversized furnace** — a unit too large for the home satisfies the thermostat quickly and cycles frequently. Common after a botched replacement.
9. **Failing control (ignition) board** — less common; the board misreads inputs and cuts the cycle short.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. **Do not** bypass safety switches, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a furnace that has locked out.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it and see if cycling stops. This alone fixes many cases.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. Make sure it isn't mounted right next to a supply vent, lamp, or sunny window.
- **Breaker:** Check the furnace breaker in the panel and the service switch (looks like a light switch) on or near the furnace. A tripped breaker won't cause short cycling itself, but confirm power is clean and steady.
- **Vents and registers:** Walk the house — open closed supply registers and clear anything blocking return grilles. Restricted airflow is a leading cause.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency models):** If you see standing water near the furnace or a full drain pan, the condensate line may be clogged. Clearing a visible, accessible clog is fine; anything involving the pressure switch is a tech job.

If cycling continues after these, stop and call a technician. Do **not** keep resetting a unit that repeatedly shuts down.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech won't just swap parts. Expect them to:

- **Measure temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the rating-plate spec. A rise that's too high confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- **Check static pressure** to find restrictive ductwork, undersized returns, or a dirty coil/filter.
- **Test the flame sensor** microamp signal and clean or replace it if it's low.
- **Read the board's diagnostic LED** flash code and pull any stored fault history.
- **Verify high-limit and rollout switches** and inspect the heat exchanger for cracks or blockage.
- **Confirm blower operation and speed tap** are correct for the installed configuration.
- **Assess sizing** — a Manual J or a review of runtime data if an oversized furnace is suspected.

Sanity check: if a tech proposes a control board without measuring temperature rise, static pressure, or flame current first, ask them to show the readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cycles every few minutes, filter is dirty | Restricted airflow, high-limit trips | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise & static pressure |
| Burners light then die after ~3-7 seconds | Dirty flame sensor | None safe — schedule service | Clean/replace flame sensor, test microamps |
| Cycling plus warm-then-cold air | High-limit switch tripping on heat | Check filter & vents first | Test/replace limit switch, find airflow cause |
| Runs short, standing water near furnace | Clogged condensate drain (hi-eff) | Clear visible drain clog | Clear line, test pressure/condensate switch |
| Fires up whenever thermostat is bumped | Thermostat placement/wiring/battery | Replace batteries, relocate check | Verify wiring, cycle-rate settings |
| New furnace that always cycled | Oversized unit | None | Confirm sizing, adjust staging/airflow |
| Random cycling, no pattern, no dirty filter | Failing control board | None | Diagnose board after ruling out inputs |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Simple diagnostic/service call fees ($90-$180) often apply on top of parts.

- **Air filter:** $10-$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries / minor thermostat fix:** $0-$25 DIY; $120-$250 for thermostat replacement.
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $150-$350.
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150-$350.
- **Condensate drain clearing (hi-eff):** $100-$250.
- **Blower motor / speed correction:** $300-$700 depending on motor type (PSC vs ECM).
- **Control (ignition) board:** $400-$700+.
- **Airflow/duct correction or oversized-system remedy:** $400-$900+, occasionally more if returns or duct sizing must be rebuilt.
- **Cracked heat exchanger (if found):** major repair — often points toward furnace replacement; get a written diagnosis.
