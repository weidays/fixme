## What this code means

Short cycling isn't a single Goodman fault code — it's a behavior: the furnace fires, runs for only a minute or two, shuts off, then restarts again shortly after. Many Goodman boards flash a diagnostic LED (for example, patterns indicating an open limit, flame loss, or pressure switch issues) that points at *why* it's cycling. The exact flash codes and board layout vary by model and year, so always read the label inside your blower door.

The common thread is that the furnace keeps starting and stopping without completing a normal heating cycle. That usually means either a **safety switch is tripping** (most often the high-limit from overheating), the **flame is being lost mid-cycle**, or the thermostat is telling it to start and stop too quickly. Each is a different problem with a very different fix.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter / restricted airflow.** The single most common cause. Reduced airflow lets the heat exchanger overheat and trip the high-limit switch, shutting the burners off early.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents.** Too many closed registers or blocked returns starve airflow the same way a dirty filter does.
3. **Dirty or failing flame sensor.** If the board can't confirm flame, it shuts the gas valve within seconds and retries — producing a fast on/off pattern.
4. **Overheating high-limit switch (tripping).** Caused by airflow problems, a weak blower motor, or a failing limit switch itself.
5. **Oversized furnace or thermostat placement issues.** A furnace too large for the space, or a thermostat in a hot/drafty spot, satisfies the call quickly and cycles frequently.
6. **Flame rollout or pressure switch faults.** Intermittent pressure switch opening or a rollout condition can interrupt cycles. (Rollout is a safety issue — see Related codes.)
7. **Failing control board.** Less common, but a board with intermittent output can cause erratic cycling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe. Do **not** open the gas valve, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a large share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to Heat, several degrees above room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's a battery model — weak batteries cause erratic signals.
- **Vents and returns:** Walk the house. Open closed supply registers and clear anything blocking return grilles.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker and the unit's power switch (often a light-switch-looking one near the furnace) are on.
- **Condensate (96% units):** On high-efficiency Goodman furnaces, a full or clogged condensate line/pan can trip a float switch. Check for standing water; you can clear a visible clog, but don't disassemble internal drains.

If it still short cycles after a fresh filter and open vents, stop and call a pro — especially if it keeps tripping a safety.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech won't just swap parts. Expect them to:

- Read the **diagnostic LED flash code** and note it against the model's chart.
- Measure **temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the rating-plate range — a high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
- Check **static pressure** to find airflow restrictions (ductwork, coil, filter).
- Inspect and test the **flame sensor** microamp signal, cleaning or replacing it.
- Test the **high-limit and rollout switches** for correct operation and continuity.
- Verify the **blower motor** amperage and speed tap.
- Inspect the **heat exchanger** for cracks if overheating has been chronic.
- Test **pressure switch** and inducer operation on cycling that starts before ignition.

Ask which of these they actually did. A quote to replace a control board without airflow/temperature-rise checks is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2–3 min, shuts off, restarts | Overheating on high-limit from dirty filter | Replace filter, open vents | Check temp rise, static pressure, blower amps |
| Burners light then die in seconds, retries | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None safe beyond filter check | Clean or replace flame sensor, verify microamps |
| Cycles with limit LED flashing | Airflow restriction or failing limit switch | Filter + vents | Test limit switch, inspect blower/heat exchanger |
| Cycles even with clean filter | Oversized unit or thermostat placement | Move nothing; note pattern | Evaluate sizing, relocate/adjust thermostat |
| Cycles before ignition, inducer runs | Pressure switch or vent restriction | Check outdoor vent termination | Test pressure switch, clear vent, check inducer |
| Random on/off, no clear pattern | Failing control board | Reset once only | Diagnose board outputs, replace if confirmed |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor unless noted:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Flame sensor:** $80–$250.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$350.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350.
- **Blower motor (ECM/PSC):** $400–$900 depending on type.
- **Control/integrated ignition board:** $350–$650.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90–$180, often credited toward the repair.
- **Heat exchanger replacement (if cracked from chronic overheating):** $1,500–$3,000+ — at this point, replacing the furnace is often the better value.

Costs vary by region, model, and whether the part is under Goodman's parts warranty (labor is usually still billed).
