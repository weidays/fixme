## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a specific Goodman fault code — it's a behavior. Your furnace fires up, runs briefly, shuts off, then repeats far too often instead of running a full, steady heat cycle.

On Goodman furnaces this usually means a safety device is doing its job: a limit switch, pressure switch, or flame-sensing circuit interrupts the burners because something is wrong (usually overheating or poor airflow). Sometimes it's simpler — a thermostat problem or an oversized unit.

Watch the LED diagnostic light on the control board through the sight glass. If short cycling ends in a hard shutdown with a flashing code, see our **Goodman Furnace Lockout** page — that's a distinct situation.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow.** Restricted return air overheats the heat exchanger; the high-limit switch trips and cuts the burners within a minute or two.
2. **Dirty or failing flame sensor.** The board can't confirm flame, drops the gas valve, retries, and repeats — producing rapid on/off cycling.
3. **Overheating from closed/blocked supply vents or an undersized duct system.** Same limit-switch trip as a dirty filter.
4. **Flaky pressure switch or partially blocked flue/condensate.** The switch opens mid-cycle, stopping the burners repeatedly.
5. **Thermostat issues.** Bad location (near a vent), loose wiring, dying batteries, or a too-tight anticipator/cycle-rate setting.
6. **Failing igniter or gas valve.** Weak ignition that lights then falters mid-cycle.
7. **Control board fault.** Miscounting flame or limit signals and cycling the unit unnecessarily.
8. **Oversized furnace.** On new installs, an oversized unit heats the space too fast and cycles frequently by design.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner:

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it — then watch a full cycle.
- **Thermostat:** Replace batteries. Confirm it's set to Heat and the fan is on Auto. Make sure it isn't mounted near a supply vent, lamp, or sunny window that fools it.
- **Supply and return vents:** Open all registers and clear rugs, furniture, or boxes blocking them. Closing too many vents starves airflow.
- **Return grille:** Vacuum dust off the return grille panel.
- **Breaker:** If the furnace lost power, check the breaker and the furnace switch (looks like a light switch nearby).
- **Condensate (96% AFUE models):** Look for a full drain pan or blocked condensate line/float switch, which can interrupt operation.

Do **not** bypass any limit or pressure switch, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out. Those are technician tasks.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will:

1. **Watch a full cycle** and read the LED flash code on the control board.
2. **Check temperature rise** across the heat exchanger against the nameplate spec — high rise points to airflow restriction or overheating.
3. **Measure the flame-sensor microamp signal**; a weak reading (typically well under ~2 µA) means it's dirty or failing.
4. **Test the high-limit and pressure switches** for opening mid-cycle, and inspect the flue and inducer.
5. **Verify igniter operation and gas valve** timing and pressure.
6. **Check the blower and duct static pressure** for undersized/restricted ductwork.
7. **Confirm sizing** on newer installs against a load calc if oversizing is suspected.

If a quote jumps straight to "new board" or "new furnace" without any of these measurements, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fires, runs ~1–2 min, shuts off, repeats | Dirty filter / overheating | Replace filter, open vents | Check temp rise, test limit switch |
| Burners light then die within seconds | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None (leave to pro) | Clean or replace sensor, verify µA |
| Inducer runs, burners cut in and out | Pressure switch / flue block | Check condensate drain | Test switch, inspect flue & inducer |
| Cycles regardless of furnace condition | Thermostat wiring/location/batteries | Replace batteries, relocate check | Verify wiring, cycle-rate setting |
| Weak or slow ignition each cycle | Igniter or gas valve fault | None | Test igniter, gas pressure & valve |
| New install, heats fast then off often | Oversized furnace | Confirm it's a new unit | Load calc, staging/airflow adjust |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor, varies by region):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 DIY.
- **Flame sensor clean/replace:** $80–$250.
- **Pressure switch:** $100–$300.
- **High-limit switch:** $120–$350.
- **Hot-surface igniter:** $150–$350.
- **Gas valve:** $300–$600.
- **Control board:** $300–$650.
- **Duct modification (undersized returns):** $500–$2,000+.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$180, often credited toward the repair.

A dirty filter costs you nothing but a new filter. Board and gas-valve failures are the pricey end — hence the "pro" severity rating.
