## What this code means

Short cycling is a behavior, not a specific Goodman fault code: your furnace starts a heat call, runs briefly, shuts the burners off, then restarts a short time later — over and over. On Goodman furnaces with an integrated ignition control board, short cycling is usually the board doing its job by shutting down when a safety switch (like the high-limit) trips, or by reacting to a flame that keeps dropping out.

Because it is a symptom rather than a stored code, the cause could be anything from a dirty filter to a failing control board. The good news is that the underlying triggers are well understood, and a couple of them are safe for a homeowner to rule out before paying for a service call.

If you also see a hard lockout (a specific LED flash pattern that stops the furnace from retrying), see the related Goodman Furnace Lockout page. If you smell burning or see flames outside the burner area, stop and read the Flame Rollout page immediately.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter / restricted airflow.** The most common cause by far. Low airflow lets the heat exchanger overheat, tripping the high-limit switch, which cuts the burners. Once it cools, it fires again — short cycling.
2. **Overheating from blocked vents or a failing blower.** Closed supply registers, blocked returns, a dirty blower wheel, or a weak blower motor produce the same overheating trip as a dirty filter.
3. **Dirty or failing flame sensor.** If the board can't confirm flame, it shuts the gas off within seconds and retries — producing very short cycles, often ending in lockout.
4. **Thermostat problems.** A thermostat located near a supply vent, with a very tight temperature differential, or with weak batteries can cycle the furnace rapidly.
5. **Clogged or blocked condensate drain (high-efficiency models).** A tripped condensate float or pressure switch on GMVC/GMEC-style furnaces interrupts the heat call.
6. **Pressure switch / inducer / venting issues.** A restricted flue, weak inducer, or marginal pressure switch can open mid-run and cut off combustion.
7. **Control board or gas valve faults.** Less common, but a failing board or intermittent gas valve can cause erratic cycling. This is a pro diagnosis.
8. **Oversized furnace.** If the unit is too large for the home, it satisfies the thermostat quickly and cycles often. This is a design issue, not a repair.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do not bypass safety switches, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Air filter.** Pull it and hold it to a light. If it's gray/clogged, replace it with the correct size. This alone fixes many short-cycling cases.
- **Thermostat.** Replace the batteries if it uses them. Make sure it's set to Heat and that the temperature differential (cycle rate) isn't set unusually tight. Confirm it isn't in direct sun or above a heat source.
- **Supply and return vents.** Walk the house and open any closed registers. Clear furniture, rugs, or boxes off return grilles. Restricted airflow overheats the furnace.
- **Breaker.** If the furnace lost power, check that the breaker and the furnace service switch are on. Cycle the breaker once only — never repeatedly.
- **Condensate drain (high-efficiency furnaces).** Look for standing water near the furnace or a full drain line. A clogged drain can trip a float switch. You can clear a visible blockage, but don't disassemble internal switches.

If short cycling continues after a clean filter, open vents, and fresh thermostat batteries, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech won't just swap parts. Expect them to:

- **Read the LED flash code** on the control board to see what safety it's tripping (high-limit, flame loss, pressure switch, etc.).
- **Measure temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the rating-plate spec. High rise points to an airflow/overheating problem; that guides them to the filter, blower, or ductwork.
- **Inspect and clean the flame sensor** and measure microamp signal. A weak signal means the burners drop out.
- **Check the blower motor and wheel** for buildup, correct speed tap, and amp draw.
- **Test pressure switches, the inducer, and the flue** for restrictions on high-efficiency models.
- **Verify gas pressure and the gas valve operation.**
- **Assess sizing/duct static pressure** if nothing else explains rapid cycling.

If a quote jumps straight to a control board or gas valve without a temperature-rise reading and a flame-sensor check, ask why — those are the cheapest, most common culprits and should be ruled out first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burners run a few minutes then shut off, blower keeps running | Overheating / high-limit trip from dirty filter | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, clean blower, check limit |
| Ignites, flame drops out in seconds, retries | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None safe beyond filter | Clean/replace sensor, test microamps |
| Rapid on/off every couple minutes in mild weather | Thermostat differential or placement | Fresh batteries, check location/setting | Reprogram or relocate thermostat |
| Standing water, cycling on high-efficiency unit | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear visible drain blockage | Clear drain, test float and pressure switch |
| Inducer runs but burners cut mid-cycle | Pressure switch / flue restriction | Check for obvious vent blockage outside | Test pressure switch, inspect flue/inducer |
| Erratic cycling with odd LED codes | Control board or gas valve fault | None | Diagnose and replace board/valve |
| Cycles fast, house heats quickly, no fault | Oversized furnace | None | Load calculation, staging/airflow options |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Your area and contractor rates vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat replacement:** $120–$350 depending on model.
- **Flame sensor clean:** often included in a service call ($90–$180); **replacement** $150–$300.
- **Blower cleaning / motor repair:** $150–$450; full blower motor replacement $400–$700.
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$250.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $300–$600.
- **Control board replacement:** $350–$600.
- **Gas valve replacement:** $300–$600.
- **Oversizing correction / duct modifications:** varies widely; get a Manual J load calc before major work.
