## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single Goodman fault code — it's a behavior. Your furnace starts a heat call, runs briefly, shuts down, then restarts (or stays off) far sooner than it should. A healthy Goodman furnace typically runs multi-minute cycles; short cycling means run times of seconds to a couple of minutes, repeating over and over.

On most Goodman boards the underlying cause **is** flagged by an LED blink code or fault memory — for example a "limit switch open," "flame sensed but lost," or "pressure switch" fault. The exact codes and blower behavior vary by control board and model series, so read the diagnostic label inside your blower door for your specific board.

Short cycling almost always traces back to one of three things: **restricted airflow causing overheating, a weak flame signal, or a safety switch tripping.** It protects the equipment, but the repeated stress on the igniter, gas valve and heat exchanger makes it worth diagnosing quickly.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts return airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and the high-limit switch cuts the burners. The #1 cause by a wide margin.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — the burners light but the board can't confirm flame, so it drops the gas valve within seconds and retries. Classic "runs 5-10 seconds then dies" pattern.
3. **Blocked or closed supply/return airflow** — too many closed registers, a collapsed flex duct, or an undersized return starves the blower and overheats the unit.
4. **Failing high-limit switch** — a worn or out-of-spec limit trips early even with adequate airflow.
5. **Flue/pressure switch or venting issues** — a partially blocked flue, weak inducer, or cracked pressure-switch tubing causes intermittent trips.
6. **Thermostat problems** — bad location (near a supply vent), miswired anticipator, low batteries, or a failing thermostat that satisfies too quickly.
7. **Oversized furnace** — heats the space fast and satisfies the thermostat before completing a full cycle. Common in retrofits.
8. **Control board fault** — a failing board misreads sensors or cuts cycles erratically. Least common, diagnosed by elimination.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. **Do not** bypass limit or pressure switches, open the gas valve, or repeatedly reset a locked-out furnace.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a huge share of short-cycling calls.
- **Thermostat:** Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. Make sure it's set to **Heat** and the temperature is a few degrees above the room. Check that it isn't mounted near a supply vent, lamp, or sunny window.
- **Breaker:** Confirm the furnace breaker is fully on. Flip it fully off, then on once if it's tripped — but do not repeatedly cycle it.
- **Supply and return vents:** Open all registers and make sure furniture, rugs or boxes aren't blocking supply or return grilles. Closing too many vents causes overheating.
- **Blower door:** Confirm the panel covering the blower is fully seated. A loose panel trips the door safety switch and mimics short cycling.
- **Condensate (high-efficiency models):** On 90%+ furnaces, check the condensate drain and trap for clogs — a backed-up drain can trip the pressure switch.

If short cycling continues after a clean filter and open vents, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech won't just swap parts. Expect them to:

1. **Pull the board's fault history / blink code** to see whether it's a limit, flame, or pressure-switch trip.
2. **Measure temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the rating-plate range. High rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
3. **Check static pressure** to find restricted ducts, filters, or a coil.
4. **Read the flame-sense microamp signal** with a meter — a low reading (typically under ~1-2 µA against spec) confirms a dirty or failing sensor.
5. **Test the high-limit and pressure switches** for continuity and correct trip points.
6. **Inspect the inducer, flue, and condensate trap** for blockage on high-efficiency units.
7. **Confirm blower speed** matches the furnace's requirements.

If they quote a control board without checking airflow, flame signal, and the limit switch first, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs several minutes, then trips, restarts when cool | Dirty filter / restricted airflow overheating limit | Replace filter, open all vents | Measure temp rise & static pressure, clear ducts |
| Lights for 5-10 sec then shuts off, retries | Dirty or failing flame sensor | Replace filter first, then call | Clean/replace flame sensor, read µA signal |
| Trips even with clean filter and open vents | Failing high-limit switch | None | Test and replace limit switch |
| Inducer runs, burners never fully light | Pressure switch / flue / condensate blockage | Check condensate drain | Test pressure switch, clear flue/trap |
| Cycles fast, house warm quickly | Oversized unit or thermostat too responsive | Check thermostat location & settings | Adjust staging/anticipator, evaluate sizing |
| Erratic cycling, no clear pattern | Control board fault | None | Diagnose by elimination, replace board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Diagnostic fees are often applied to the repair.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90–$150.
- **Flame sensor clean:** often bundled into a service call; **replacement $150–$300**.
- **High-limit switch:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Pressure switch:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Inducer motor:** $300–$600 installed.
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 installed.
- **Control board:** $350–$650 installed.
- **Duct/airflow correction:** varies widely, $200–$1,000+ depending on scope.

If short cycling is caused by an oversized furnace, there's no cheap fix — but airflow and staging adjustments can reduce it significantly.
