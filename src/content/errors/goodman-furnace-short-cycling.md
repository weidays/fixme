## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a specific flash-code on a Goodman board — it's a behavior. Your furnace lights, runs briefly, then shuts off, and repeats this too often (typically starting a new cycle within a few minutes rather than staying on until the thermostat is satisfied). On Goodman integrated control boards you may see a blinking status LED alongside this, but short cycling itself is a pattern you observe, not a stored trouble code.

It almost always means the furnace is being told to stop before it should — either by a safety switch protecting the unit from overheating, by a dirty flame sensor losing sight of the flame, or by a thermostat/wiring issue sending inconsistent calls for heat.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts return airflow, the heat exchanger overheats, and the high-limit switch shuts the burners off. The most common and cheapest cause.
2. **Dirty flame sensor** — a carbon-coated sensor rod fails to detect the flame within a few seconds, so the board closes the gas valve. The furnace relights, fails again, and cycles.
3. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents or ductwork** — same overheating effect as a dirty filter. Closed registers, crushed flex duct, or a blocked return grille.
4. **Overheating tripping the high-limit switch** — from any airflow restriction above, or a failing blower motor moving too little air.
5. **Thermostat problems** — dying batteries, a thermostat mounted near a supply register or heat source, loose wiring, or a poorly-configured "cycle rate" setting.
6. **Flue/venting or pressure switch issues** — an intermittent pressure switch or partially blocked exhaust can drop out mid-cycle.
7. **Failing high-limit switch or control board** — a limit switch that opens prematurely, or a board with faulty relays/staging logic.
8. **Oversized furnace** — a unit too large for the home heats the thermostat's area quickly and shuts off before the whole house is warm. This is a design issue, not a broken part.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or older than 90 days, swap it. This resolves a large share of short-cycling calls on its own.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace the batteries if it uses them. Make sure it's set to Heat and not sitting in direct sun or above a supply vent. Confirm the fan setting is on Auto.
- **Walk the house and open vents.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are unblocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers. Aim to keep the vast majority of registers open.
- **Check the breaker.** A furnace tripping and resetting can look like short cycling — confirm the breaker is fully on and not partially tripped.
- **Look at the condensate drain (high-efficiency models).** A clogged drain or full trap can trip a float switch on 90%+ furnaces. Clear visible standing water/debris only if you can reach it safely.

Do **not** bypass the limit switch, pressure switch, or any safety, and don't repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out — repeated resets can create a dangerous condition. If you smell gas, stop, leave, and call your gas utility's emergency line or 911.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will work from cheapest to most involved:

1. **Verify airflow** — inspect the filter, measure temperature rise across the furnace, and check for duct restrictions. A temperature rise above the rating plate range points to overheating.
2. **Time the cycles** — how long from ignition to shutoff tells them whether it's a flame-sense dropout (seconds) versus an overheat limit trip (a couple of minutes).
3. **Check flame sensor microamps** — clean the sensor and measure the flame-rectification current; low readings confirm a dirty or failing sensor.
4. **Test the high-limit switch and pressure switch** — with a meter, to see if either is opening prematurely.
5. **Inspect the blower motor and wheel** — a weak motor or dirt-loaded wheel starves airflow and mimics a filter problem.
6. **Read the board status LED and check thermostat wiring** — for intermittent connections and correct staging.
7. **Evaluate sizing** — if everything checks out but a large unit heats one zone too fast, they'll discuss load calculation and zoning.

If a quote jumps straight to "replace the control board" without checking the filter, airflow, flame sensor, and limit switch first, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2–3 min then shuts off, heat exchanger hot | Overheat / high-limit trip from dirty filter | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, test limit, service blower |
| Lights then drops out after a few seconds, repeatedly | Dirty or failing flame sensor | None (leave to pro) | Clean/replace sensor, verify microamps |
| Cycles with clean filter, all vents open | Failing limit switch or blower motor | Confirm filter is clean | Test limit, inspect/replace blower motor |
| Cycles randomly, thermostat display flickers | Weak batteries or loose t-stat wiring | Replace batteries | Check wiring, thermostat settings |
| Cycles in cold weather, exhaust looks poor | Pressure switch / flue restriction | Check flue termination outside for blockage | Test pressure switch, inspect venting |
| House warm near t-stat but cold elsewhere | Oversized furnace or poor thermostat location | Relocate t-stat away from vents/sun | Load calc, discuss zoning/staging |

## Repair costs

Ranges below are typical US pricing including labor. Your area and contractor rates will vary.

- **Air filter:** $8–$40 DIY.
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 DIY.
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80–$250 (part is cheap; you're paying for the service call and expertise).
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900+ depending on ECM vs. PSC and part cost.
- **Integrated control board:** $350–$600.
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $75–$200, often credited toward the repair.
- **Oversizing correction:** varies widely — zoning or thermostat relocation can be a few hundred dollars; replacing an oversized furnace is a full system quote.
