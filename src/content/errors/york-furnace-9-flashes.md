## What this code means

Nine flashes on a York integrated furnace control (IFC) board means **115 VAC reversed polarity**. The control board expects line voltage (hot) on one terminal and neutral on the other, with a solid earth ground. When it detects that hot and neutral are swapped — or that the ground reference is missing — it halts operation and flashes the 9-flash code instead of firing the burners.

This is a **safety and sensing lockout**, not a mechanical failure. York boards rely on correct polarity and a good ground to properly sense flame through the flame rod. With polarity reversed, flame sensing can read incorrectly, so the board simply refuses to run until the wiring is corrected.

Because the fault is in the incoming 115 VAC supply, it is classified as **pro-level**. The furnace itself is usually fine — the wiring feeding it is not. Once correct polarity and ground are restored, the code clears automatically; there is normally no manual reset to press.

> Note: flash-code legends vary slightly between York board revisions. Always confirm the meaning of "9 flashes" against the diagnostic decal on your furnace's blower door before acting.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Miswired outlet or receptacle** the furnace plugs into — hot and neutral reversed at the outlet (very common after DIY outlet replacement).
2. **Reversed line and neutral in the furnace junction box** — hot and white/neutral leads landed on the wrong terminals during install or service.
3. **Missing or poor equipment ground** — the board cannot establish a neutral/ground reference, which it reports as a polarity fault.
4. **Upstream panel or subpanel wiring error** — neutral and hot swapped at the breaker or in a junction, often after recent electrical work.
5. **Generator or transfer-switch supply** with reversed or floating neutral feeding the furnace during a power interruption.
6. **Failed or noisy neutral connection** causing the board's polarity-sensing circuit to misread.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform. Everything else is technician work.

- **Recall any recent electrical work.** If a new outlet, breaker, generator, or panel change came just before this, that is almost certainly the cause — mention it when you call.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it is set to Heat and the setpoint is above room temperature; replace batteries if it is battery-powered.
- **Check the breaker.** Find the furnace breaker and the dedicated furnace switch (looks like a light switch near the unit). If tripped, reset the breaker once.
- **Check the air filter.** A clogged filter doesn't cause 9 flashes, but ruling it out helps the tech and keeps your system healthy.
- **Confirm the furnace is plugged in fully** if it uses a cord-and-outlet connection, and that the outlet isn't part of a recently changed circuit.

Do **not** open the furnace cabinet, test outlets with a meter, rewire receptacles, or touch line-voltage wiring. Reversed polarity means live conductors are in the wrong place — that is a job for a licensed pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech or electrician will:

1. Confirm the 9-flash code and cross-check the board's legend for the exact revision.
2. Use a plug-in receptacle tester and a multimeter to verify **hot, neutral, and ground** at the outlet or furnace junction box.
3. Check that line voltage (~120 VAC) reads between hot and ground, and near 0 V between neutral and ground.
4. Trace the miswire — usually the receptacle or the furnace junction terminals — and correct it, or refer to an electrician for panel/subpanel issues.
5. Verify a solid equipment ground back to the panel.
6. Restore power and confirm the code clears and the furnace completes a normal heat cycle.

If the tech finds the furnace wiring correct, the problem is upstream in the home's wiring — a licensed electrician handles receptacle, junction, or panel corrections.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 flashes right after new outlet/wiring | Reversed polarity at receptacle | Note the recent work; call a pro | Verify and rewire the outlet correctly |
| 9 flashes, furnace on a dedicated circuit | Swapped leads in junction box | Reset breaker once only | Correct hot/neutral at furnace terminals |
| 9 flashes with flickering lights nearby | Missing/loose neutral or ground | Leave it; call an electrician | Repair neutral/ground back to panel |
| Code appears only on generator power | Floating or reversed generator neutral | Return to utility power if possible | Correct transfer-switch/generator bonding |
| Code clears then returns | Intermittent neutral/ground | Don't repeatedly reset | Trace and repair loose connection |

## Repair costs

- **$0** — if the fix is returning to correct utility power or you simply flip a plug into a correctly wired outlet (rare; polarity is fixed at the outlet, not the plug).
- **$100 – $200** — HVAC service call to diagnose and correct reversed leads at the furnace junction box.
- **$150 – $250** — licensed electrician to rewire a miswired receptacle or correct hot/neutral in a junction box.
- **$250 – $600+** — panel, subpanel, or transfer-switch corrections, depending on scope and local rates.

Because 9 flashes is a wiring/ground fault, there are **no furnace parts to buy** — that is why the parts list is empty. Paying to "replace the board" for this code is a red flag; the board is doing its job by detecting the miswire.
