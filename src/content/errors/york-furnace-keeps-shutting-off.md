## What this code means

"Keeps shutting off" is a symptom rather than a single York error code. Your furnace starts a heat cycle — the inducer runs, the igniter glows, the burners may even light — then the control board shuts the gas valve and blower back down before the thermostat is satisfied. It then tries again, or locks out entirely.

York residential gas furnaces use an integrated ignition control board with a diagnostic LED, usually visible through a sight glass in the blower door. When the furnace shuts itself down, that LED flashes a pattern that tells you *why*. **Flash-code meanings are not the same across every board** — they differ between board part numbers and model years, so read the legend printed on the board itself or on the label inside the blower door rather than assuming a pattern you found online applies to your unit.

Common culprits are flame-proving failures, a tripped high-limit, or a pressure-switch fault. **Flame-related and ignition faults typically lock out after three or four failed tries and hold until you cycle power; limit and pressure faults often auto-reset once the condition clears.** Behavior varies by board, so again — confirm against your own board's legend.

If you ever smell gas, stop reading, leave the house, and call your gas utility's emergency line or 911 from outside.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty flame sensor** — the most common cause. The furnace lights but can't prove flame, so it shuts the gas off within seconds and retries. After a few tries it locks out.
2. **Clogged air filter or blocked return** — restricted airflow overheats the heat exchanger and trips the high-limit switch, shutting the furnace down mid-cycle.
3. **High-limit switch tripping** — from the airflow restriction above, a failing blower motor, closed supply registers, or a dirty blower wheel.
4. **Pressure switch opening mid-cycle** — a partially blocked flue, condensate-logged tubing, or a weak inducer causes the switch to drop out after ignition.
5. **Flame sensor wiring or ground problems** — a loose sensor wire or poor burner ground mimics a dirty sensor.
6. **Failing inducer motor** — weakens enough to light the burners but not hold the pressure switch closed under load.
7. **Control board fault** — intermittent relays or flame-sense circuitry on the board. Diagnosed last, after other causes are ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do. Everything else lives inside the cabinet and is technician work.

- **Replace the air filter.** A clogged filter is the #1 DIY-fixable cause of overheating shutdowns. Check the size printed on the edge of the filter you pull out and swap in a clean one of the same size.
- **Check the thermostat.** Set it to Heat, raise it 5°F above room temp, and replace the batteries if it's battery-powered (open the cover to see whether it takes AA or AAA).
- **Open supply and return vents.** Closed or blocked registers choke airflow and trip the limit. Open them all and clear furniture or rugs.
- **Check the breaker and furnace switch.** Make sure the furnace breaker is on and the switch on the unit (looks like a light switch) is on.
- **Look at the condensate drain.** On high-efficiency York units, a clogged drain or full trap can open the pressure switch. If you can see standing water or a blocked line near the furnace, that points your technician to the right place.
- **One reset only.** Flip the furnace switch off for 30 seconds, then on. If it shuts off again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech reads the flash code first — against the legend on your board — then works the condition the code indicates:

- Reads the LED flash code and checks the board's fault history.
- Watches a full heat cycle to see exactly *when* it shuts down (before ignition, right after, or minutes in) — timing points straight to the cause.
- Measures flame-sense microamps and cleans or replaces the flame sensor if it reads low.
- Tests the high-limit switch and measures temperature rise across the heat exchanger to confirm airflow.
- Checks pressure-switch operation against inducer draft readings in inches of water column; inspects the flue and condensate tubing for blockage.
- Inspects the blower wheel and motor amp draw.
- Tests control-board relays and the flame-sense circuit last, replacing the board only if everything upstream checks out.

Sanity check: if a tech quotes a control board before even cleaning the flame sensor or checking airflow, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lights, then shuts off in 3–7 sec, retries | Dirty flame sensor | None safe inside cabinet | Clean/replace sensor, verify microamps |
| Runs several minutes, then shuts off | High-limit trip from low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Check blower, heat exchanger, limit switch |
| Inducer runs, shuts off before/just after ignition | Pressure switch opening | Clear visible condensate blockage | Test pressure switch, inspect flue & inducer |
| Short, rapid on/off cycles | Thermostat placement, settings or wiring; an oversized furnace; or a limit opening and reclosing. Flame-sense wiring or a board relay is a later suspect | Check thermostat settings and batteries, move heat sources away from the thermostat, replace the filter | Verify thermostat wiring and cycle rate, check airflow and temperature rise, test limit, then wiring, ground and board flame circuit |
| Shuts off, no restart, flashing lockout code | Ignition or safety lockout | One reset only | Diagnose per the flash code on your board |
| Blower runs but no heat before shutdown | Weak inducer / draft fault | None | Measure draft, replace inducer if weak |

Most "keeps shutting off" complaints are resolved at the flame-sensor or airflow end of this range, not the board — but if a board or inducer is genuinely bad, expect a quote in the four-figure neighborhood.
