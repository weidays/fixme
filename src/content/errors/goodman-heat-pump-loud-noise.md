## What this code means

"Loud noise" is not a flashing code. It describes a symptom: your Goodman heat pump is making a sound that is louder than normal or is new to you. Most Goodman heat pumps do not display a dedicated diagnostic code for noise. Some newer communicating or inverter models may store related faults on the control board, but this varies by model.

The type of sound is the best clue to the cause:

- **Normal sounds** include a short whoosh during defrost changeover, a low steady hum, and the fan stopping and starting at the end of a defrost cycle.
- **Abnormal sounds** include grinding, screeching, banging, loud buzzing that does not stop, rattling, and hard clunks when the unit starts or stops.

Because nearly every real noise source sits inside the cabinet, this is professional work. Your safe role is to rule out the simple causes and then shut the unit off so it does not suffer further damage. There is no code to reset. If the unit has shut down for some separate reason, see the related pages listed at the bottom.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost and reversing valve sounds.** A whoosh, hiss or brief steaming in cold weather is expected and needs no repair.
2. **Loose exterior panels, screws or debris.** Twigs, leaves or a loose panel can cause rattling or ticking.
3. **Outdoor fan motor bearings failing, or the fan blade striking something.** This causes screeching, grinding or a rhythmic ticking. The blade can strike ice, debris, a bent guard, or a loose blade.
4. **A failing capacitor or contactor.** A weak capacitor can cause loud buzzing or humming at startup. A pitted contactor can chatter.
5. **Compressor problems.** These include worn mounts or broken internal springs (heavy clunking or knocking), hard starting (a loud hum followed by a click or a trip), or internal wear (grinding or rattling).
6. **Refrigerant line or tubing vibration.** Lines touching the cabinet or a wall can cause buzzing or rattling.
7. **Indoor blower issues.** Inside the air handler, a worn blower motor, a loose blower wheel or debris can cause squealing or rattling.
8. **A unit sitting unlevel or on a sunken pad.** This increases vibration and noise.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Identify the sound and when it happens.** Listen for whether it occurs only in heat, only during defrost, at startup, or constantly. A brief whoosh with steam in winter is normal defrost.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm the mode is set correctly and replace weak batteries. Odd cycling can make normal sounds more frequent.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** A clogged filter makes the indoor blower strain and whistle.
- **Open vents and registers.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked. Closed vents can cause whistling.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from the outside.** Clear leaves, sticks and snow from around the unit, keeping about 2 feet of clearance. Do not reach through the fan guard.
- **Check the exterior panels.** Make sure the access panels are seated properly. Do not open them.
- **Shut the unit off if you hear grinding, screeching or banging.** Turn it off at the thermostat, and turn off the breaker if needed. One breaker reset is fine for an unrelated trip, but do not keep restarting a unit that is making mechanical noises.
- **Check the condensate line.** If gurgling comes from the indoor unit, confirm the condensate line is draining.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Runs the system and pinpoints where the sound comes from: the compressor, the outdoor fan, the contactor area, the lines, or the indoor blower.
- Disconnects power, then checks fan blade clearance, set screws and motor bearing play.
- Tests the run and start capacitor values against their ratings and inspects the contactor for pitting or chattering.
- Measures compressor amp draw and winding resistance, and checks for hard starting and for the condition of the mounts.
- Checks refrigerant pressures if the compressor is noisy, because low charge or flooding can cause abnormal sounds. Only EPA-certified technicians may do this.
- Watches a forced defrost cycle to confirm the reversing valve and defrost control are working normally.
- Inspects line set routing, the pad level, and the indoor blower wheel and motor.

A good quote names the specific part and the test result that condemned it, such as "capacitor measured 30 µF against a 45 µF rating."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whoosh or hiss with steam in winter | Normal defrost or reversing valve shift | None; this is normal | Check only if it happens constantly |
| Rattling from the outdoor unit | Loose panel, screws or debris | Seat the panels and clear debris outside | Tighten hardware, isolate the lines |
| Screeching or grinding outdoors | Fan motor bearings | Turn the unit off | Replace the fan motor |
| Ticking or slapping fan | Blade hitting ice, debris or the guard | Clear debris outside; turn off | Realign or replace the blade or guard |
| Loud buzz or hum, slow start | Weak capacitor or failing contactor | Turn off | Test and replace the capacitor or contactor |
| Heavy clunk or knock at start or stop | Compressor mounts or internal wear | Turn off | Compressor diagnosis, mounts or replacement |
| Constant vibration buzz | Line set touching the cabinet or wall, or unlevel pad | Note where the noise is | Re-route or insulate the lines, level the pad |
| Squeal or whistle indoors | Dirty filter, closed vents, blower motor | Replace the filter, open the vents | Service the blower motor or wheel |
| Gurgling indoors | Condensate drain issue | Check the condensate line | Clear the drain and trap |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service call and diagnosis | $75 – $200 |
| Tighten panels or hardware, isolate the line set | $100 – $250 |
| Run or dual capacitor | $120 – $350 |
| Contactor | $150 – $350 |
| Outdoor fan blade | $150 – $350 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $300 – $700 |
| Indoor blower motor (PSC) | $400 – $900 |

| Leveling or replacing the pad | $100 – $400 |

Goodman parts warranties are often 10 years if the unit was registered, which can lower part costs. Labor is usually not covered.
