## What this code means

"Loud noise" is not a stored fault code on York air conditioners. It is a symptom. Most York condensers have no display that would report a noise problem, so the type of sound and where it comes from are your best clues:

- **Grinding or screeching:** usually motor bearings.
- **Buzzing or humming:** usually electrical (capacitor or contactor).
- **Rattling or banging:** loose panels, debris, or failing compressor mounts.
- **Hissing or squealing:** pressure-related.

There is no reset tied to the noise itself. If the noise comes with the unit shutting down, the cause may overlap with problems covered on other pages. Examples are a tripped breaker or a compressor that stops running.

This page is rated "pro" because nearly every mechanical noise source is inside the cabinet. Running a noisy unit risks further damage.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failing outdoor fan motor bearings.** These cause grinding, squealing or screeching from the top of the condenser. The noise often gets worse as the motor heats up.
2. **Weak or failed run capacitor.** This causes a loud buzz or hum while the fan or compressor struggles to start.
3. **Pitted or chattering contactor.** This causes buzzing or rapid clicking from the electrical panel of the outdoor unit.
4. **Loose panels, screws, fan guard, or debris.** Twigs or leaves in the fan, or a loose top grille, cause rattling or ticking.
5. **Damaged or loose condenser fan blade.** This causes knocking, thumping or a wobbling sound.
6. **Worn compressor mounts or internal compressor wear.** This causes clanking, banging at start or stop, or a loud metallic rattle. On units with a sound blanket, a missing or displaced blanket makes normal compressor noise louder.
7. **Indoor blower problems.** These include a worn blower motor, a loose blower wheel, or a clogged filter. They cause rumbling, squealing or whistling from the air handler or furnace.
8. **Refrigerant-side issues.** A leak or a restriction can cause hissing, gurgling or a high-pitched shriek. A screech from the compressor itself can indicate dangerously high internal pressure. Shut the unit off right away.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Switch to OFF if the noise is grinding, screeching, banging, or buzzing without movement. Replace weak batteries if the display is dim, since erratic signals can cause repeated starts.
- **Air filter:** Replace a dirty filter if the noise is whistling or roaring from the indoor unit.
- **Visible vents and registers:** Open closed or blocked supply registers and clear return grilles. Restricted airflow causes whistling.
- **Around the outdoor unit:** Look only, from outside the cabinet, without reaching in. Clear leaves, sticks or toys from the top grille and sides, and keep 2 feet of clearance. Do not put hands or tools through the fan guard.
- **Exterior panels:** If an outer access panel is visibly loose, make sure it is seated properly. Do not remove panels.
- **Breaker:** If the breaker tripped, you may reset it once. If it trips again or the noise returns, leave it off and call a pro.
- **Condensate line:** Gurgling at the indoor unit can come from a partly blocked drain. Check that the drain line and pan are flowing. Clear the outlet end if it is accessible.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Run the system with the power and cover in place to locate the noise by sound: fan motor, compressor, electrical panel, or indoor blower.
- Disconnect power and spin the condenser fan by hand to check for bearing drag, play, or blade damage.
- Test the run capacitor with a meter (microfarads against the rated value).
- Inspect the contactor for pitting, chatter, and voltage at the coil.
- Measure compressor amps against the rated load amps (RLA) and the locked-rotor amps (LRA) at startup. Check the mounts and the sound blanket.
- Measure refrigerant pressures, superheat and subcooling if hissing, shrieking or poor cooling suggests a charge or restriction problem.
- Inspect the indoor blower motor, wheel and mounting.

A legitimate quote should name a specific component and a test result, for example "capacitor reads 28 µF, rated 45." A vague "it's noisy, needs a new unit" is not enough.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grinding or screeching from top of outdoor unit | Fan motor bearings failing | Turn system off | Replace condenser fan motor (often with capacitor) |
| Loud buzz, fan not spinning | Failed capacitor or seized motor | Turn off; reset breaker once only if tripped | Test and replace capacitor or motor |
| Buzzing or rapid clicking from outdoor panel | Pitted or chattering contactor | Check thermostat batteries | Replace contactor, check control voltage |
| Rattling or ticking | Debris, loose grille or panel | Clear debris from outside; seat panels | Tighten hardware, inspect fan blade |
| Knocking or wobble | Bent or loose fan blade | Turn system off | Replace blade, check motor shaft |
| Clanking or banging at start/stop | Worn compressor mounts or internal wear | Turn system off | Inspect mounts, test compressor, quote repair or replacement |
| Shrieking from outdoor unit | High pressure or compressor distress | Shut off immediately | Pressure diagnosis, repair restriction or charge |
| Hissing or gurgling | Refrigerant leak or blocked drain | Check condensate line | Leak search and repair, recharge |
| Whistling at vents or indoor unit | Dirty filter, closed registers | Replace filter, open registers | Check duct static pressure and blower |
| Rumbling or squeal from indoor unit | Blower motor or wheel problem | Replace filter | Replace or balance blower motor or wheel |

## Repair costs

Typical US prices, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service call / diagnosis | $75 – $200 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $120 – $350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150 – $400 |
| Tightening panels, clearing debris (with service call) | $75 – $200 |
| Condenser fan blade | $150 – $400 |
| Condenser fan motor | $300 – $750 |
| Indoor blower motor | $400 – $1,200 (ECM motors cost more) |
| Compressor sound blanket | $100 – $300 |
| Refrigerant leak repair and recharge | $300 – $1,500+ (depends on the refrigerant type and the leak location) |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500 – $2,800+ |

If the unit is still under York warranty, parts may be covered, but you still pay labor. Units 12–15+ years old needing a compressor are often better candidates for replacement.
