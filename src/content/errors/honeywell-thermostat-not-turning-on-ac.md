## What this code means

"Not turning on AC" is a symptom, not a displayed error code. You set your Honeywell thermostat to cool, but the outdoor unit and indoor blower never start. Sometimes the display shows nothing related to cooling at all.

On Honeywell thermostats, the AC starts only when all of these conditions are met:

- The system mode is **Cool** (or **Auto**).
- The setpoint is **below** the current room temperature.
- No delay or hold is blocking the call.
- The thermostat sends a signal on its Y (cooling) terminal to the HVAC equipment.

If any link in that chain is missing, nothing happens.

How "Cool On" looks tells you where to start:

- **"Cool On" solid:** the thermostat is already calling for cooling. If nothing runs, the problem is downstream. Possible causes are power, a breaker, a float switch, the contactor, wiring or the equipment. Waiting will not fix it.
- **"Cool On" flashing, or "Wait" or a similar indicator on some models:** the compressor protection delay is counting down. It clears automatically after about five minutes.

This page mentions the delay only briefly, as cause #4. Our Cool On flashing page covers it in full. A completely blank display is also outside this page's scope. See our blank screen pages for that.

The thermostat itself doesn't "lock out" here. Once the cause is corrected, it resumes cooling calls automatically, with no reset required.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

We suggest checking in roughly this order, from the quickest and most common causes to the least: system mode and setpoint, delay, power and batteries, breaker, then wiring and setup.

1. **System mode not set to Cool.** The thermostat is left in Heat or Off, or in Auto with a setpoint above room temperature.
2. **Setpoint not low enough.** The thermostat calls for cooling only when the room is warmer than the setpoint. On many models, the room must exceed it by about 1°F (the cycle rate/differential setting).
3. **Schedule or hold overriding your change.** A programmed schedule, a Temporary Hold expiring, or a geofence/away setting on Wi-Fi models raises the setpoint.
4. **Compressor protection delay.** After a power blip or a recent cycle, Honeywell models wait about five minutes before starting the compressor. Depending on the model, this appears as "Cool On" flashing, "Wait," or a clock icon. It clears on its own. See our Cool On flashing page for details. A *solid* "Cool On" is not the delay.
5. **Low or dead batteries.** This applies to battery-powered models, and to models where batteries back up the memory or power. Some show a low-battery warning first; others simply stop working.
6. **Tripped AC breaker or outdoor disconnect.** The thermostat calls correctly, often with "Cool On" solid, but the condenser or air handler has no power.
7. **Incorrect system setup after installation or reset.** The equipment type is set wrong, such as heat pump versus conventional, or the reversing valve O/B setting. On heat pumps, a wrong O/B setting makes the system heat when you ask for cooling.
8. **Wiring or equipment fault.** This includes a loose Y or C wire, a blown control fuse, a tripped float/condensate safety switch, or a failed contactor. These need a technician, except checking a visibly overflowing condensate pan.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Confirm mode and setpoint.** Set the mode to **Cool** and the setpoint at least 3°F below the room temperature. If "Cool On" flashes or "Wait" appears, give the protection delay five minutes to end. If "Cool On" is solid and nothing runs, skip the wait and go to the breaker, disconnect and condensate checks below.
- **Check schedules and holds.** Choose a Permanent Hold (or tap Hold) so the schedule doesn't override you. On Wi-Fi models, check the Resideo/Honeywell Home app for away, geofence or vacation settings.
- **Replace the batteries.** Use fresh alkaline AA or AAA batteries, depending on your model. Battery access differs between models, so see your model's manual for battery access.
- **Restart the thermostat once.** Turn off the HVAC breaker or furnace switch for 30 seconds, then turn it back on. Wait out the five-minute delay.
- **Check the breakers.** Look at the AC/condenser breaker and the furnace or air-handler breaker in the panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Check the outdoor disconnect.** Make sure the disconnect box near the outdoor unit is in the On position. Don't open the unit itself.
- **Inspect the air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty. A badly clogged filter can freeze the coil and trip safeties on some systems.
- **Look at the condensate drain.** If the drain pan under the indoor unit is full, or the drain line is visibly clogged, a float switch may be cutting off cooling. Clear the line's accessible end, or vacuum it out with a wet/dry vac.
- **Confirm the furnace or air-handler door is fully seated.** A loose door can keep the blower from running.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically:

1. **Confirm the thermostat setup.** This includes the equipment type, O/B setting, cycle rate, and any compressor lockout settings on Pro models.
2. **Check the thermostat's control voltage.** They measure about 24 VAC between R and C at the wall plate.
3. **Jumper or meter the cooling circuit.** They test R to Y at the wall plate and at the control board. This shows whether the problem is the thermostat or the equipment.
4. **Inspect the control board.** They check the 3–5 A fuse and the board's status LED codes.
5. **Check the safety switches.** This includes the float/condensate safety switch and any refrigerant-pressure safety switches.
6. **Test the outdoor unit.** They check the contactor, the capacitor, and the 24 V signal arriving at the condenser.

If the thermostat is sending Y correctly, a new thermostat will not fix the problem. Question any quote that pushes thermostat replacement without this test.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing happens, no Cool On shown | Mode not Cool or setpoint too high | Set Cool, lower setpoint 3°F | None usually |
| Temperature rises back up later | Schedule or app away setting | Use Permanent Hold, check app | None |
| Cool On flashing, or "Wait" shown, for a few minutes | Compressor protection delay | Wait 5+ minutes | None |
| Cool On solid, but nothing runs | Thermostat is calling; fault is downstream (power, float switch, contactor, wiring or equipment) | Check breakers (reset once), outdoor disconnect and drain pan; don't just wait | Diagnose contactor, wiring and equipment |
| Display dim or low-battery icon | Weak batteries | Replace batteries | None |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped condenser breaker or disconnect | Reset breaker once | Contactor, capacitor, wiring |
| Blows warm air on a heat pump | Wrong O/B setting | Check setup in the manual | Correct configuration and wiring |
| Nothing runs, water in the drain pan | Float switch tripped | Clear the condensate line | Clear the drain, test the switch |
| Thermostat calls but no 24 V at the equipment | Blown fuse or loose wire | None | Replace fuse, find the short, repair wiring |
