## What this code means

"Blowing cold air in heat mode" is not a stored fault code on most American Standard heat pumps. It is a symptom. Air comes from the vents when the thermostat calls for heat, but it feels cool or room temperature.

Before assuming a fault, rule out two normal behaviors:

- **Defrost cycles:** Supply air can turn cool for a few minutes while the outdoor coil defrosts.
- **Normal heat pump air:** Heat pump supply air is naturally lukewarm compared with furnace air.

A real fault usually means one of three things:

- The system is not reversing into heating.
- The outdoor unit is not running.
- There is not enough refrigerant to move heat.

Some AccuLink communicating thermostats display an alert alongside the symptom, and some outdoor control boards flash a diagnostic LED. These vary by model. The symptom itself does not "reset." It persists until the cause is fixed.

This page covers the case where the **indoor blower runs but the air stays cold**. For a system that produces no heat at all, see "Not heating." For defrost or ice problems, see the defrost and ice pages listed under Related codes.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost cycle or normally cool heat pump air.** Cool air for a few minutes, often with steam rising from the outdoor unit, is expected. Supply air of roughly 85–100°F also feels "cool" on your hand.
2. **Thermostat setup or settings.** Possible problems include:
   - Fan set to ON instead of AUTO, which circulates unheated air between cycles.
   - Wrong mode selected.
   - A newly installed or replaced thermostat configured as an AC/furnace (O/B setting wrong). A wrong O/B setting makes the system cool when you ask for heat.
3. **Outdoor unit not running while the indoor blower runs.** Possible reasons include:
   - A tripped outdoor breaker or disconnect.
   - A failed capacitor or contactor.
   - A safety lockout.

   With the outdoor unit off, the blower moves unheated air.
4. **Reversing valve stuck or its solenoid coil failed.** The valve stays in cooling position, so the home gets cool air on a heat call.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** The system cannot move enough heat. Air is lukewarm and capacity drops sharply in cold weather.
6. **Backup heat not engaging when needed.** Possible reasons include:
   - Failed heat strips, sequencer or limit.
   - An auxiliary or outdoor-temperature lockout set incorrectly.

   Below the balance point, the heat pump alone feels cool.
7. **Outdoor unit failures.** A compressor or control board failure, or a defrost control stuck in defrost, falls into this group. These are less common but costly.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:**
  - Confirm the mode is HEAT and the setpoint is several degrees above room temperature.
  - Set the fan to AUTO.
  - Replace the batteries if the thermostat uses them.
  - Note any alert message shown on AccuLink or other American Standard thermostats.
- **Wait out a possible defrost.** If the outdoor unit is steaming or quiet with the fan stopped, give it 10–15 minutes.
- **Breakers:**
  - Check the panel for both the outdoor unit and the indoor air handler.
  - Reset a tripped breaker once.
  - If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.
  - Make sure the outdoor disconnect switch is on.
- **One power reset.** Turn the thermostat off and switch off both breakers for 5 minutes. Then restore power and call for heat. Do this once only.
- **Air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty. A clogged filter cuts airflow and can trip high-pressure or limit safeties.
- **Vents and registers.** Make sure they are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit.** Look from outside without opening any panels:
  - Clear snow, leaves or ice buildup around the base.
  - Confirm the access panels are seated.
  - Confirm the fan is spinning when the system runs.
- **Condensate line.** If the indoor unit has a float switch and the pan or line is full, clear the visible drain line opening.
- **Stopgap.** If it is very cold, switch to Emergency Heat until help arrives.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically work through these steps:

1. **Measure temperatures.** They measure supply and return air temperatures, then confirm the outdoor unit is actually running on a heat call.
2. **Check the thermostat.** They confirm the thermostat type and the O/B configuration.
3. **Check outdoor power and controls:**
   - Voltage at the contactor.
   - The 24V Y and O/B signals from the thermostat.
   - The capacitor, using a meter.
4. **Read diagnostics.** On communicating systems, they read fault history at the thermostat or outdoor board.
5. **Test the reversing valve.** They verify the solenoid coil gets voltage and has continuity. They then compare line temperatures, or pressures, to see whether the valve shifts.
6. **Check the refrigerant charge.** They connect gauges and check pressures, superheat and subcooling against American Standard charging data. If the charge is low, they leak-search before topping off.
7. **Check backup heat.** They test the strips, sequencers and limits, plus any outdoor-temperature lockouts.
8. **Check the defrost control.** They confirm defrost initiation and termination, including the coil sensor.

Be wary of a quote that adds refrigerant without a leak search. Also question a reversing valve replacement that was not preceded by a solenoid coil test.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool air for 5–10 min, steam from the outdoor unit | Normal defrost | Wait it out | None |
| Air feels lukewarm but the house slowly warms | Normal heat pump air temperature | Set fan to AUTO | Temperature split check if unsure |
| Cold air right after a thermostat swap | O/B configured wrong | Recheck the thermostat installer settings per its manual | Correct the O/B configuration |
| Indoor blower runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, capacitor, contactor | Reset the breaker once | Test and replace the capacitor or contactor |
| Outdoor unit runs, air still cold | Stuck reversing valve or failed coil | Use Emergency Heat | Test the coil; replace the coil or valve |
| Weak, lukewarm heat worsening over weeks | Refrigerant leak | Use Emergency Heat if needed | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Cold air only in very cold weather | Backup heat not working | Check the air handler breaker | Repair strips, sequencer or limit |
| Breaker trips again after reset | Electrical or compressor fault | Leave it off | Electrical and compressor diagnosis |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Thermostat reconfiguration or service call | $100–$250 |
| New thermostat (DIY) | $30–$250 |
| New thermostat (installed) | $150–$500 |
| Run capacitor | $150–$400 |
| Contactor | $150–$350 |
| Reversing valve solenoid coil | $150–$400 |
| Reversing valve replacement (brazing, evacuation, recharge) | $900–$2,000 |
| Leak search and repair plus R-410A or R-454B recharge | $400–$1,500+ |
| Heat strip element or sequencer | $200–$600 |
| Defrost control board or sensor | $250–$700 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800–$3,500+ |

Compressor replacement often prompts a discussion about replacing the whole system instead. Many repairs are covered for parts under American Standard's limited warranty if the unit was registered, but labor usually is not.
