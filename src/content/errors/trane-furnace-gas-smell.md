## What this code means

**If you smell gas right now, stop reading and do this first:**

1. **Don't** touch light switches, the thermostat, the furnace switch, the breakers or appliances. **Don't** light anything.
2. **Get everyone out** of the house now, pets included. Leave the door open on your way out to let fresh air in.
3. **From outside and well away from the house**, call your gas utility's emergency line or **911**.
4. **Don't go back inside** until the utility or the fire department tells you it's safe.

"Gas smell" isn't a Trane blink code. It's something you notice yourself. Utilities add a sulfur or rotten-egg odorant (mercaptan) to natural gas and propane so you can smell a leak. Trane furnace boards monitor ignition, flame sensing, pressure switches and limits. **They can't detect gas escaping into your home.** That means the furnace may run with a normal status light while gas is leaking. Nothing resets on its own, and power cycling isn't a fix. The cause has to be found and repaired by a professional.

The risks are fire or explosion if gas builds up, and possibly carbon monoxide if burning isn't venting properly. If you have a CO alarm going off, or anyone has a headache, nausea or dizziness, call 911.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

These are typical causes found by technicians after the utility has made the home safe. Trane doesn't publish a troubleshooting order for gas odor.

1. **Leaking gas fitting, flex connector or union** at the furnace, in the line between the shutoff valve and the gas valve. Loose or corroded fittings and old flex connectors are the most common source.
2. **Leak at the gas valve itself.** This could be a leaking inlet or outlet connection, a pressure tap screw that wasn't tightened after service, or, less often, a failing valve body.
3. **Delayed ignition**, which lets unburned gas collect briefly before lighting. This gives a short smell at startup and is often paired with a bang. It's caused by dirty burners or ignition problems (see Loud bang on startup).
4. **A leak elsewhere** near the furnace, such as the water heater, the dryer, the main gas line or the meter. The smell is often blamed on the furnace because it sits in the same room.
5. **Burning smell confused with gas**, such as dust burning off the heat exchanger at the start of the season. It's harmless, but only a professional should rule out a leak. A cracked heat exchanger usually causes CO more than a gas smell, but techs check for it.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

With a gas smell, **you don't do any furnace checks before calling.** Get out of the house and call the utility or 911 first. That's the only safe first step.

Once the utility or fire department has cleared the home and you're waiting for an HVAC technician:

- **Leave the furnace off** if the utility shut off or tagged the gas or the appliance. Don't turn the gas back on yourself.
- **Set the thermostat to OFF** so the furnace won't try to fire if service is restored before repairs.
- **Check that the outdoor vent and intake pipes** (PVC on high-efficiency models) are visible and not blocked by snow or debris. Only look at them; don't take anything apart.
- **Test or replace the batteries in your CO and smoke alarms.** If you don't have a CO alarm near bedrooms and the furnace, get one.
- **Note when the smell happened**: all the time, only at startup, or only during the heating cycle. This helps the technician.

Don't open the furnace cabinet, spray soapy water on fittings, or reset the furnace to "see if it happens again."

## How a technician will diagnose it

Use this to check whether a quote and the work make sense:

- **Gas detector sweep** with an electronic combustible-gas detector along the supply line, shutoff valve, flex connector, gas valve and manifold.
- **Leak test solution** (bubble test) on any suspect fittings once the area is ventilated.
- **Pressure test** of the gas piping to check for a drop. This is often required by code after repairs.
- **Gas valve check**: whether the inlet and outlet pressure taps are sealed, and whether manifold pressure matches the rating plate.
- **Ignition observation**: watching the startup for delayed ignition, rollout, or burners that don't all light. This includes checking the burners, the igniter and the flame sensor.
- **Combustion and venting check**: CO in the flue and the room, draft and vent condition, and a heat exchanger inspection if the burn or CO readings look off.
- **Other appliances** near the furnace checked too, if the furnace isn't the source.

A legitimate repair ends with a leak-free retest and a clean ignition cycle. Get a written note of what leaked and how it was fixed.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strong, constant rotten-egg smell | Leaking fitting, flex connector or line | Get out, call the utility or 911 from outside | Find the leak, replace the fitting or connector, pressure test |
| Smell strongest at the furnace's gas valve | Valve connection, loose pressure tap, failing valve | Get out, call the utility or 911 | Reseal or tighten the tap, replace the gas valve if needed |
| Short smell at startup, sometimes a bang | Delayed ignition | Thermostat OFF, call an HVAC tech | Clean the burners, check the igniter, flame sensor and gas pressure |
| Smell plus CO alarm or headaches | Leak and/or poor burning or venting | Get out, call 911 | Combustion test, vent and heat exchanger inspection |
| Smell in the utility room, furnace checks out fine | Water heater, dryer or main line leak | Get out, call the utility | Leak sweep of all gas appliances and piping |
| Burning dust smell at first fall startup | Dust burning off the heat exchanger | If unsure, treat it as gas; replace the filter once cleared | Confirm there's no leak, routine tune-up |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor. These vary by region and urgency.

- **Utility emergency leak check:** usually $0
- **HVAC emergency or after-hours service call:** $100 – $350
- **Tighten or reseal a fitting or pressure tap:** $100 – $250
- **Replace the flex gas connector or sediment trap:** $150 – $400
- **Replace a Trane gas valve:** $300 – $800 (two-stage and modulating valves cost more)
- **Clean burners and fix delayed ignition:** $150 – $400
- **Gas line repair or pressure test (inside the house):** $200 – $700+
- **Heat exchanger replacement (if found cracked):** $1,200 – $3,000+. Often a reason to consider a new furnace instead, especially if the warranty has expired.

Trane parts warranties (often 10 years for registered units) may cover the gas valve, but labor usually costs extra.

## Related codes

Loud bang on startup – delayed ignition can cause both a bang and a short gas smell.
Trane Furnace 5 Flashes: Flame Sensed Without Gas – a flame sensing fault, not an external leak.
Trane Furnace 7 Flashes: Gas Valve Circuit Error Fixes – an electrical valve fault, which doesn't mean gas is leaking.
Won't ignite – if the furnace won't light after gas service is restored.
Ignition lockout – if the furnace locks out after a leak repair.
