## What this code means

"Ice on outdoor unit" is not a fault code that Lennox displays. It is a symptom you can see. Your first job is to sort normal frost from a real problem.

**Normal frost**
- In heating mode, the outdoor coil runs colder than the outside air. Moisture freezes onto it.
- Lennox heat pumps clear this with a defrost cycle. The unit reverses to cooling briefly, the outdoor fan stops, and the coil heats up.
- Defrost usually lasts a few minutes. Steam rising from the unit is normal.

**Signs of a real problem**
- The whole coil is encased in solid ice.
- Ice covers the top or the fan blades.
- Ice keeps building over several hours.
- Ice appears in cooling mode.

**How each defrost type responds**
- **Demand defrost boards** start defrost based on the coil and outdoor temperature sensors.
- **Timed boards** check for defrost every 30, 60 or 90 minutes, depending on a jumper setting.
- **Communicating systems** (iComfort, S30, S40) may also post an alert on the thermostat.

Icing caused by your surroundings will not set a code. Neither will slow refrigerant loss. Ice that does not clear needs attention. Running a heat pump heavily iced for long periods strains the compressor.

If the unit runs but never defrosts at all, the deeper defrost-control troubleshooting is covered on our separate page, Lennox Heat Pump Frozen and Not Defrosting.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal frost during heating.** This happens in weather from about 25 to 40°F with high humidity. It clears on its own within roughly 30 to 90 minutes.
2. **Water dripping or pooling on the unit.** Common sources are a leaking gutter, a roof drip line, or drainage that refreezes on the top or base. Defrost can't keep up with water constantly landing on the coil.
3. **Snow drifts or a low pad.** Snow buries the coil, or meltwater can't drain away and refreezes into the bottom of the coil. Lennox calls for the unit to be raised above the typical snow line in snowy regions.
4. **Restricted airflow.** A dirty filter, closed supply registers, or a dirty indoor coil reduces airflow. In cooling, this is a top cause of ice on the indoor coil and suction line. In heating, it raises head pressure and strains the system, but it does not by itself cause frost to build up on the outdoor coil.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Icing becomes heavier and uneven, and heating or cooling capacity drops.
6. **Defrost control or sensor fault.** This includes a failed coil or ambient sensor, a loose sensor clip, or a failed defrost board.
7. **Outdoor fan or reversing valve problem.** Examples are a fan that doesn't run, a weak capacitor, or a reversing valve that won't shift into defrost.
8. **Dirty or matted outdoor coil.** Debris blocks airflow and holds moisture.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the thermostat.**
  - Confirm it is set to HEAT or COOL as intended, at a reasonable setpoint.
  - Replace the batteries if the display is weak or blank.
  - If the unit is heavily iced in heating, switch to EM HEAT (emergency heat). This keeps the house warm and stops the outdoor unit while you wait for service.
- **Replace the air filter.** Use the correct size. A clogged filter is a common homeowner-fixable cause of icing in cooling mode, and it adds strain on the system in any mode.
- **Open your registers.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Watch for one defrost cycle.**
  - Observe the unit for up to 90 minutes.
  - During defrost, the fan stops, you hear a whoosh, steam rises, and the coil clears.
  - If this happens, the system is working.
- **Look for outside water sources.**
  - Check for gutters or roof edges dripping onto the unit.
  - Redirecting a downspout or fixing a gutter is outdoor work you or a gutter contractor can do.
- **Clear loose snow gently.**
  - Use a soft broom to brush loose snow off the top and away from the sides.
  - Do not chip or scrape ice, and do not pour water on the coil.
- **Reset the system once at the thermostat.**
  - Turn the system off at the thermostat. Wait 5 minutes. Turn it back on.
  - Do this only once. Do not keep resetting.
  - Check your electrical panel. If the breaker for the heat pump or air handler has tripped, leave it off and tell your technician.

Anything inside the outdoor cabinet is technician work. That includes sensors, the defrost board, the fan motor, the capacitor, wiring and refrigerant.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will usually work through these steps:

1. **Confirm the mode and pattern.** They will check whether the icing happened in heat or cool, and whether ice forms evenly or only on the lower or partial coil. Partial icing often suggests a low charge.
2. **Check airflow.** This means the filter, the blower, the indoor coil, and the static pressure of the ductwork.
3. **Force a test defrost.** Lennox boards have test pins or a test mode for this. The tech confirms the reversing valve shifts, the fan stops, and the defrost terminates properly.
4. **Test the sensors.** They check the coil and ambient sensors against the resistance-temperature chart in the Lennox service literature. They also confirm the coil sensor clip is tight on the correct tube.
5. **Read diagnostic codes.** These come from the board's status LEDs or the communicating thermostat. Codes and LED patterns vary by board.
6. **Measure refrigerant.** They read pressures, superheat and subcooling to judge the charge. If the charge is low, they should find the leak rather than just topping it off.
7. **Check the outdoor fan.** This covers the fan motor, the capacitor, and the contactor.
8. **Inspect the installation.** They look at pad height, drainage, and nearby water sources.

When reviewing a quote, watch for this red flag: a refrigerant "top-off" with no leak search, or a board replacement without a forced-defrost test and sensor readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light white frost that clears with steam every so often | Normal defrost operation | None, keep observing | None needed |
| Thick ice on top or one side, under a roofline | Gutter or roof water dripping | Fix the gutter or redirect the water | Install a hood or raise the unit if needed |
| Ice packed at the base of the coil | Snow drift, poor drainage, low pad | Brush away loose snow; don't chip | Raise the unit on a riser or stand |
| Weak airflow at the vents and a dirty filter | Restricted airflow (system strain; icing mainly in cooling) | Replace the filter, open registers | Clean the indoor coil, check the blower |
| Partial or uneven icing, poor heating or cooling | Low refrigerant from a leak | Switch to EM HEAT, call for service | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Ice never clears and the fan never stops for defrost | Sensor, defrost board, or reversing valve fault | One reset at the thermostat, then call | Forced defrost test, sensor or board replacement |
| Fan not spinning, coil icing | Fan motor or capacitor failure | Turn the unit off | Replace the capacitor or fan motor |
| Ice on the suction line or indoor coil in summer | Low airflow or low charge | Cooling off, fan ON to thaw, check the filter | Airflow and charge diagnosis |
