## What this code means

A blinking or flashing indicator lamp on a Goodman mini-split indoor unit is a fault code from the unit's built-in diagnostics. On most models, the indoor board monitors the indoor sensors and fan. It also receives fault reports from the outdoor inverter board over the communication wire.

When something goes out of range, the system usually stops or limits operation. It then reports the fault in one of these ways:

- **Lamp combination:** which lamp is flashing (Operation/Run, Timer, or another indicator).
- **Blink count:** a pattern of flashes, often followed by a pause, then repeated.
- **Display code:** on units with a digital display, an alphanumeric code instead of blinks.

**Important:** blink patterns are not standardized across Goodman ductless series. The same number of flashes can mean different faults on different models. To read your code accurately:

1. Count the flashes between pauses.
2. Note which lamp is flashing.
3. Find the model number on the indoor unit's side label.
4. Match the pattern against that model's fault chart. The chart is in the installation or service manual, and sometimes on a label inside the front cover.

This page covers ductless mini-splits only. Ducted Goodman heat pumps are covered under Goodman Heat Pump Flashing Light.

**Reset behavior varies.** Some protection trips (high pressure, overcurrent, high discharge temperature) can clear on their own after a restart delay once conditions return to normal. Sensor, communication, EEPROM and board faults typically persist until repaired. A single power cycle at the breaker may clear a one-time glitch.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Indoor/outdoor communication fault.** This is one of the most common mini-split codes. Possible causes include:
   - a loose or miswired interconnect cable or a corroded terminal
   - a tripped outdoor breaker or disconnect while the indoor unit still has power
   - a failed outdoor board
2. **Temperature sensor (thermistor) fault.** An open or shorted sensor can trigger a code. This includes the indoor room sensor, indoor coil sensor, outdoor coil sensor, ambient sensor or discharge sensor. Damaged sensor wiring causes the same result.
3. **Outdoor unit protection trip.** Possible triggers include:
   - high pressure from a dirty or blocked outdoor coil
   - overcurrent
   - inverter (IPM) module protection
   - high discharge temperature, often linked to low refrigerant
   - voltage out of range
4. **Indoor fan motor fault.** The board detects no fan speed feedback. Common causes are a seized or failed motor, a blower wheel obstruction, or bad motor wiring.
5. **Outdoor fan motor fault.** This applies on models that monitor outdoor fan speed. Causes include a failed motor or debris blocking the fan blade.
6. **Condensate or float-switch fault.** This applies on models with a condensate pump or float switch. A clogged drain trips the switch and stops the unit.
7. **Control board or EEPROM fault.** The board itself fails, often after a power surge or lightning strike.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Record the code first.** Count the blinks, note which lamp is flashing, and take a short phone video. This saves diagnostic time.
- **Check the breakers and the outdoor disconnect.** Make sure both the indoor and outdoor circuits are on. A tripped outdoor breaker commonly produces a communication-style code.
- **Do one power reset.** Turn the system off at the breaker for 3–5 minutes, then restore power. If the light returns, stop and call a technician. Do not keep resetting.
- **Replace the remote batteries.** Then confirm the remote is set to the correct mode and a reasonable temperature. This rules out command problems; it will not clear a true fault.
- **Clean the indoor filter screens.** Lift the front panel and slide out the washable mesh filters. Rinse them, let them dry fully, and reinstall them. Heavy clogging can contribute to coil-temperature and protection faults.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove leaves, snow or debris from the outside of the outdoor unit and keep about 2 feet of clearance. Do not open the panels.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Make sure the drain line outlet is not kinked, crushed or blocked outdoors.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Use this to sanity-check what a technician tells you:

- **Looks up the code:** finds your exact model's fault chart, and may retrieve fault history from the board where supported.
- **Communication faults:** checks line voltage at both units. Inspects the interconnect wiring and terminals for tightness, correct terminal order and corrosion. Measures the communication signal.
- **Sensor faults:** measures thermistor resistance against the manufacturer's temperature/resistance chart and inspects the sensor connectors.
- **Fan faults:** checks the motor windings and speed feedback, and inspects for obstructions.
- **Protection trips:**
  - measures the compressor and inverter current
  - checks system pressures and superheat/subcooling to look for low charge or restrictions
  - inspects and cleans the outdoor coil
  - checks the IPM and board outputs
- **Board faults:** condemns a board only after ruling out the wiring, sensors and supply voltage.

A good technician confirms the root cause. They should not simply swap the most expensive board first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light blinks, outdoor unit completely silent | Outdoor breaker tripped or communication fault | Check the outdoor breaker and disconnect; do one reset | Test the interconnect wiring and terminals; check the outdoor board |
| Blinks shortly after startup, fan runs briefly | Sensor fault | Do one power reset | Test the thermistors and replace any failed sensor |
| Blinks during hot weather, unit was running | High-pressure or overcurrent protection | Clear debris around the outdoor unit; clean the indoor filters | Clean the coil; check charge, fan and compressor amps |
| Indoor fan does not spin, light blinks | Indoor fan motor fault | None beyond a power reset | Test or replace the fan motor; check the blower wheel |
| Water near the indoor unit plus a code | Condensate or float switch tripped | Check the drain outlet for kinks or blockage | Clear the drain and test the pump or float switch |
| Code returns immediately after a power surge | Control board or EEPROM fault | One reset only | Diagnose and replace the board |
| Weak cooling for weeks, then a code | Low refrigerant or high discharge temperature | None | Find and repair the leak, evacuate and recharge |
