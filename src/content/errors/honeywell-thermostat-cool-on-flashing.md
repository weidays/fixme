## What this code means

On Honeywell thermostats that have a **Cool On** indicator, a flashing Cool On usually means the **compressor protection delay** is running. After the air conditioner shuts off, or after power is restored, the thermostat waits before turning the compressor back on. Honeywell manuals describe this wait as up to about 5 minutes. The delay lets refrigerant pressures equalize so the compressor doesn't try to start against high pressure, which can damage it.

While Cool On flashes, the thermostat has accepted your cooling request but is holding it. When the timer ends, Cool On goes solid and the outdoor unit should start.

- **Auto-reset:** Yes. It clears on its own when the delay ends. You do not need to cycle power.
- **Model differences:** Newer Honeywell Home T-series and smart thermostats typically show **Wait** or a "cooling will start soon" message instead of a flashing Cool On. The delay length and whether an installer can adjust related settings vary by model. Check your model's manual.

A dark or blank display is a different issue. See the related page listed under Related codes.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal delay after a recent cooling cycle.** The AC shut off within the last few minutes and you, or the schedule, asked for cooling again. This is the most common cause and needs no action.
2. **Power was just restored.** A power outage, a tripped and reset breaker, or a battery change starts the protection timer, so Cool On flashes when power comes back.
3. **Setpoint or mode change.** Switching from Heat or Off to Cool, or lowering the setpoint right after the system stopped, triggers the delay.
4. **Frequent short cycling.** If Cool On flashes at the start of nearly every cycle, the system may be turning on and off too often. Common reasons include:
   - A thermostat mounted near a heat source, in direct sun or in a drafty spot.
   - Schedule or setpoint changes made close together.
   - Installer cycle-rate settings that don't suit the equipment.
5. **Intermittent thermostat power.** Weak batteries, or a loose or missing C (common) wire on hardwired models, can cause the thermostat to briefly reboot. Each reboot restarts the delay, so Cool On seems to flash far longer than 5 minutes.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Wait it out.** Give the thermostat a full 5 to 10 minutes without touching it. Repeatedly changing settings or pulling the thermostat off the wall can restart the timer.
- **Confirm your settings.** Make sure the mode is **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the schedule isn't about to override you.
- **Replace the batteries** if your model uses them, even if no low-battery warning shows. Use fresh alkaline AA or AAA cells as your manual specifies. Expect one delay cycle afterward.
- **Look at where the thermostat is mounted.** Close blinds if sun hits it, and move lamps, TVs or other heat sources away from it.
- **Check breakers.** Make sure the indoor air handler or furnace and the outdoor AC breakers or disconnects are on. Reset a tripped breaker **once only**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it's dirty. A clogged filter can cause poor cooling and cycling.
- **Check visible vents and registers.** Make sure they are open and not blocked by furniture.
- **Check the condensate drain.** If the drain line or pan is visibly backed up, a float switch may be cutting power to the system. If you can't safely clear it from the outside, call a technician.
- **Check the furnace or air handler door.** Make sure the front panel is fully seated. Many units won't run if it's loose.

If Cool On still flashes after 15 minutes with fresh batteries and correct settings, call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Watch the thermostat through a full delay to confirm the system behaves normally afterward.
- Check thermostat wiring at the wall plate and the equipment control board, including the R and C wires. Measure voltage to confirm steady 24 VAC power.
- Review installer setup settings, such as equipment type, cycle rate and compressor protection options, against your equipment.
- If the system short cycles, investigate the cause. This can include thermostat location, airflow, the condensate float switch, refrigerant-side problems or outdoor unit controls.
- If power and settings check out but the thermostat still misbehaves, recommend a replacement thermostat.

If you're quoted for a major component such as a compressor or control board based only on a flashing Cool On, ask for the specific test results that support it. The flashing indicator by itself points to a delay, not a failed part.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool On flashes for a few minutes, then goes solid and AC starts | Normal compressor protection delay | None; wait | None |
| Flashes right after an outage, breaker reset or battery swap | Delay timer restarted at power-up | Wait up to 5 minutes | None |
| Flashes after switching to Cool or lowering setpoint | Delay triggered by the new call | Wait; avoid repeated changes | None |
| Flashes at the start of almost every cycle | Short cycling | Fix sun or heat exposure near the thermostat; replace the filter; open vents | Check location, cycle-rate setup, airflow and system controls |
| Flashes well past 5 minutes, or display flickers or resets | Weak batteries or intermittent 24 V power | Replace batteries; reset the breaker once | Repair loose R or C wiring, check the transformer, verify voltage |
| Stops flashing but no cooling | Separate equipment problem | Check filter, breakers and condensate | Diagnose the outdoor unit and controls |
