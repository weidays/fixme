## What this code means

Five flashes on the diagnostic LED of an Amana furnace control board means **flame was sensed when there was no call for heat** — the flame sensor is detecting a flame signal while the gas valve should be fully closed.

There are two documented reasons this happens:

1. **The gas valve is leaking through** — allowing a small amount of gas to burn at the burners even when the valve is commanded off.
2. **A flame sensor or wiring fault** — the board is receiving a false flame signal (shorted or misrouted sensor wiring, moisture, or a grounding problem) when no flame is actually present.

Because option 1 involves a **gas valve that is not fully sealing**, this code is rated **pro-level**. Do not ignore it. On most Goodman/Amana integrated controls, the board responds to this condition by running the inducer and the circulating blower to purge the furnace until the flame signal disappears — so the usual symptom is air moving with no heat. Details, including whether the control holds until power is cycled, vary by board revision; that is for the technician to confirm on site against the board's own documentation.

> If you smell gas at any point, stop. Leave the house, and from outside call your gas utility's emergency line or 911 before doing anything else.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **False flame signal from the flame sensor or its wiring** — the most common non-emergency cause. A shorted, pinched, or moisture-affected sensor wire, or a poor board connection, can make the board "see" flame that isn't there.
2. **Flame sensor grounding / mounting fault** — a sensor touching a grounded surface or a bad burner-ground path can produce a spurious signal.
3. **Control board fault** — the flame-sensing circuit on the ignition control board itself is misreading input.
4. **Gas valve leaking through (mechanical failure)** — the valve is not fully closing, letting gas reach the burners after the heat call ends. This is the serious case and is why the code is treated as pro-only.

A technician works these in order — cheapest and most likely first (sensor/wiring), then the board, and finally confirms or rules out the gas valve, which is the highest-consequence failure.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Keep to these homeowner-safe steps only:

- **Note what the furnace is actually doing.** If the blower and inducer keep running with no heat coming from the registers, that matches the purge behavior this code triggers — tell the technician, it helps confirm the fault.
- **Cycle the power once.** Turn the furnace switch or breaker off, wait 30 seconds, and back on — **one** reset attempt. This is a test, not a repair: even if 5 flashes do not come back, still book service, because a valve that leaks through can seal on one cycle and leak on the next.
- **Look at visible vents and registers.** Make sure supply and return grilles aren't blocked.
- **Check the condensate line** (high-efficiency models) for obvious clogs or standing water at the trap.
- **Write down the model number** from the exterior rating label so the technician can bring the right parts.

Do **not** open the cabinet or the burner compartment, touch the flame sensor, gas valve, wiring, or board, or reset the unit repeatedly. Anything involving flame sensing or the gas valve is a technician job — and a possible gas-valve leak is why.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. **Read the flash code and stored history** on the control board to confirm 5 flashes and check for related faults.
2. **Inspect the flame sensor and its wiring** for damage, pinching, moisture, poor connections, or a compromised ground.
3. **Watch the flame-sense signal with a meter** through a full cycle — confirming whether a flame signal is present with the valve commanded closed.
4. **Verify the gas valve fully closes** at the end of the heat call, checking for gas passing through when it should be off. This is the critical safety test.
5. **Test the control board's** flame-sensing circuit if the sensor and valve check out.

Sanity check on a quote: a good tech should be able to tell you **whether the problem is a false signal (sensor/wiring/board) or an actual leaking gas valve** — those are very different repairs and price points. Be wary of a valve replacement quoted without any confirmation that the valve is actually leaking through.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 flashes, no obvious gas smell | False flame signal from sensor/wiring | Cycle power once; book service either way | Inspect/repair flame sensor wiring, ground, connections |
| 5 flashes returns immediately after reset | Sensor grounding fault or board misread | Stop resetting; call a pro | Meter-test flame-sense circuit; repair ground or replace board |
| Faint gas smell near furnace, 5 flashes | Gas valve leaking through | Shut off, ventilate, call gas utility/911 | Verify and replace leaking gas valve |
| Blower and inducer run but no heat | Board purging until the flame signal clears | One reset; do not keep resetting | Diagnose flame-sense circuit and valve seal |
| Code appears after recent service | Disturbed or pinched sensor wiring | None | Re-inspect and correct wiring from prior work |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180
- **Flame sensor replacement or wiring repair:** $150 – $350
- **Control (ignition) board replacement:** $400 – $1,200
- **Gas valve replacement (leaking-through valve):** $400 – $600+

There is no legitimate $0 outcome for this code. A power cycle may make the flashing stop, but a gas valve that is not fully sealing can leak intermittently — the code can clear while the hazard is still there. Any 5-flash event warrants professional verification even if it does not immediately recur. The safe outcome is a technician confirming which of the two failure modes you actually have.

## Related codes

- **3 flashes** — Amana Furnace 3 Flashes: Pressure Switch Stuck Open Fix
- **4 flashes** — Amana Furnace 4 Flashes: Open High-Limit Switch Fix

Codes 1 and 5 both involve flame sensing, but they are opposites: **1 flash** is the furnace failing to prove flame during a heat call (ignition lockout), while **5 flashes** is flame sensed with **no** call for heat. Note that 1 flash means ignition lockout on the common Goodman/Amana integrated board set, but it has been used for other faults on some older boards — the same "varies by board revision" caveat applies there as it does to 5 flashes, so let the technician confirm against the correct documentation for your board. Gas-supply and ignition problems belong to the ignition/flame-proving codes above, not to this one.
