## What this code means

"Won't turn on" is not a stored fault code. It describes a system that does nothing when you call for cooling: the indoor blower stays off, the outdoor unit stays silent, and the thermostat is often blank or unresponsive.

This page covers a dead system. If the indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit doesn't, see the related pages listed at the end.

On a conventional American Standard split system, cooling depends on a 24-volt control circuit. That circuit starts at a transformer in the furnace or air handler and runs to the thermostat. From there it goes out to the contactor in the outdoor unit. Anything that cuts this circuit stops the whole system at once:

- No power to the indoor unit
- A blower door safety switch that is open
- A condensate overflow switch that has tripped
- A blown low-voltage fuse
- A failed transformer

Whether the system recovers on its own depends on the cause:

- **Condensate float switches** reset automatically once the water drains.
- **A blown fuse or failed transformer** stays dead until a technician repairs it.
- **Built-in delays** on the thermostat and many outdoor controls hold off a restart for about five minutes after power returns. A short wait is normal, not a fault.

**AccuLink communicating systems** (on some Gold and Platinum variable-speed models) work differently. They usually show an alert on the thermostat instead of going fully dark. Follow the on-screen message and your model's service literature, because the checks differ from conventional 24V wiring.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat problem.** Dead batteries, the mode not set to Cool, a setpoint above room temperature, or a scheduling hold.
2. **Indoor unit power off.** A tripped breaker or a service switch turned off at the furnace or air handler. The switch often looks like a light switch.
3. **Condensate overflow or float switch tripped.** A clogged drain line or full drain pan opens the switch and cuts 24V power. This is very common in attic and closet air handlers.
4. **Blower compartment door not seated.** The door safety switch stays open, so the indoor unit won't run.
5. **Blown low-voltage fuse on the furnace or air handler control board.** This is usually caused by a shorted or chafed thermostat wire. Fuse rating and location vary by board.
6. **Failed 24V control transformer.**
7. **Damaged thermostat wiring or failed thermostat.** Look for wire damage at the thermostat, the indoor unit, or the line to the outdoor unit.
8. **AccuLink communication or control fault.** This applies to communicating models only.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Check the thermostat.**
   - Replace the batteries if the screen is blank or dim.
   - Confirm the mode is set to Cool and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature.
   - Wait 5 minutes for any built-in delay to pass.
2. **Check breakers and switches.**
   - At the main panel, find the breakers for the furnace or air handler and for the outdoor AC.
   - If one is tripped, turn it fully off, then back on. Do this once only.
   - Make sure the service switch near the indoor unit is on.
3. **Check the condensate drain.**
   - Look for standing water in a secondary drain pan, a wet float switch, or water dripping from a secondary drain outlet.
   - If the line is clogged, clear it from the accessible outside end or cleanout. A wet/dry vac works well.
   - The float switch should reset on its own once the water level drops.
4. **Seat the exterior panels.**
   - Press the furnace or air handler access panels firmly into place so the door switch closes.
   - Do not open or remove internal panels.
5. **Check the filter.**
   - Replace a heavily clogged filter.
   - A clogged filter won't usually cause a totally dead system. It can contribute to the drain overflowing and the coil icing.
6. **Write down any thermostat alerts** (AccuLink systems) before calling.

If the system is still dead after these checks, stop and call a technician. Do not reset a tripped breaker again.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician should work through the system in this order:

- **Line voltage:** verify line voltage at the indoor unit and at the outdoor disconnect.
- **Transformer:** check for 24V across R and C at the control board, then measure the transformer's primary and secondary sides.
- **Fuse:** inspect the board's low-voltage fuse. If it is blown, they should find the short before replacing it. That means checking thermostat wire runs, the outdoor control wiring, and any wire chafed against sheet metal.
- **Safety switches:** check the door switch and the float or overflow switch for continuity.
- **Thermostat:** jumper or test the thermostat's call for cooling to confirm the thermostat and its wiring are good.
- **AccuLink systems:** read the alert history and check the communication wiring.

A sound quote names the specific failed part and the cause, such as a blown fuse caused by a shorted wire. Be wary of a quote that recommends replacing a board or the whole system without showing a measured fault.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat blank | Dead batteries or no 24V power | Replace batteries; check breaker and switch | Test transformer, fuse, and R/C wiring |
| Thermostat works, nothing runs | Wrong mode or setpoint, or the 5-minute delay | Set to Cool, lower the setpoint, wait 5 minutes | Verify the call for cooling at the board |
| Water in the secondary pan | Float or overflow switch tripped | Clear the drain line from its accessible end | Clear the trap, repair the pan, test the switch |
| Dead after panel work or a filter change | Blower door switch open | Reseat the exterior panels firmly | Test or replace the door switch |
| Breaker tripped | Electrical fault | Reset once only | Find the short; see the breaker page |
| Dead after a storm or wiring damage | Blown low-voltage fuse or transformer | None beyond the checks above | Locate the short, replace fuse or transformer |
| Alert shown on an AccuLink thermostat | Communication or control fault | Record the message | Diagnose the communicating control |
