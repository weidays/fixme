## What this code means

"Breaker keeps tripping" is not a diagnostic code on the Amana control board. It means a circuit breaker in your electrical panel, or one built into the air handler, is detecting too much current or a short to ground and cutting power.

The general overload causes are covered on our Amana AC breaker page. This page covers what is specific to heat pumps. A heat pump usually sits on at least two circuits:

- **Outdoor unit breaker.** Powers the compressor, the outdoor fan, the defrost control and, on many models, a crankcase heater.
- **Air handler or heat kit breaker(s).** Power the indoor blower and the electric heat strips used for auxiliary heat, emergency heat and defrost tempering. Larger heat kits are often split across two breakers. Some Amana and Goodman air handlers have these breakers on the front of the unit rather than in the main panel.

**Which breaker trips tells you most of what you need to know.** Note when it trips: heat mode or cooling, at startup, during defrost, or only on very cold days.

A tripped breaker does not reset itself. It stays off until it is reset by hand. On inverter or communicating Amana models, the outdoor drive may also shut down on its own current protection and log a fault. That protection varies by model.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

This ranking assumes heating season, when the heat strips run. In cooling season the strips rarely run, so the outdoor-unit causes (items 2 to 4) lead.

1. **Heat strip or heat kit fault (air handler breaker).** This is the most common winter cause. Possible problems include:
   - a heating element shorting to the cabinet,
   - a burned or loose connection at the element terminals or the breaker lugs,
   - a sequencer or relay failure that brings all the strips on at once.

   This breaker typically trips when aux or emergency heat engages, or during defrost.
2. **Compressor drawing excessive current (outdoor breaker).** Possible problems include:
   - a compressor that is hard-starting or locked rotor,
   - windings that are shorted or grounded.

   A failing run capacitor on its own more often causes hard starts or overload cycling than a breaker trip. It can still contribute to the locked-rotor or high-amp starts that do trip the breaker. Cold-weather starts and the pressure swing at the end of defrost add load. The breaker typically trips a few seconds after the compressor tries to start.
3. **Outdoor fan motor shorted or seized (outdoor breaker).** Ice buildup and water get into heat pump fan motors more often than straight AC units. A grounded motor can trip the breaker the moment the unit is called to run.
4. **Moisture or damaged wiring in the outdoor unit.** Possible problems include:
   - chafed wires touching the cabinet,
   - water or ice in the control box or disconnect,
   - pitted contactor contacts,
   - a failed crankcase heater, which can trip the outdoor breaker even when the unit is idle.
5. **Circuit sizing or breaker problems.** Possible problems include:
   - a breaker or wire smaller than the unit's nameplate requirement,
   - a heat kit added later without upgrading the circuit,
   - an aging breaker that has weakened from repeated trips.
6. **System running abnormally hard.** A badly clogged filter or restricted indoor airflow raises current in both heating and cooling. Usually this contributes to a trip rather than causing it on its own.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check for burning or smoke first.** Do this before you reset anything.
  - If you smell burning or see smoke or scorch marks at the panel, the air handler or the outdoor unit, leave the breaker off and do not reset it. Call for service.
  - If there is active smoke or fire, leave the house and call 911.
- **Identify the breaker.** Check the main panel and the front of the air handler. Is it the outdoor unit, the air handler/heat kit, or both? Write it down.
- **Reset once.** Only reset if there is no burning smell, smoke or scorching. Set the thermostat to OFF. Push the tripped breaker fully to OFF, then back to ON. Wait 5 minutes, then restore the thermostat setting.
  - If it trips again, leave it off and call for service.
  - Do not reset it repeatedly.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm the mode (Heat vs. Emergency Heat) and replace the thermostat batteries if it uses them. Note whether "Aux" or "Em Heat" was showing when the breaker tripped.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** Restricted airflow makes both the strips and the compressor work harder.
- **Open supply and return vents.** Make sure registers are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Look at the outdoor unit.** Do this without touching anything inside the cabinet.
  - Clear snow and leaves away from the outside of the unit.
  - Note heavy ice and tell the technician.
  - If you notice a burning smell or scorch marks, turn off or leave off the breaker and do not reset it.
  - Do not chip ice off the coil.
- **Make sure exterior access panels are seated and closed.** Do not remove them.
- **Use emergency heat if needed.** If only the outdoor breaker trips and the strip circuit is fine, set the thermostat to Emergency Heat to stay warm until service.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Confirms which circuit trips and under what operating condition (heat, cool, defrost, startup or idle).
- Inspects the breakers, wire gauge and lug connections against the nameplate minimum circuit ampacity and maximum overcurrent rating for both the outdoor unit and the heat kit.
- Measures the current draw of each heat strip stage and checks the elements for continuity and shorts to ground. Inspects the sequencers or relays and looks for burned terminals.
- Tests the run capacitor in µF. Measures compressor winding resistance and checks for a short to ground with a megohmmeter. Watches startup amperage.
- Checks the outdoor fan motor for a grounded winding or seized bearings.
- Checks the contactor, the crankcase heater and the wiring for chafing or moisture.
- On inverter or communicating models, reads stored faults from the outdoor board or thermostat.

A credible quote names the specific failed component and the readings that prove it. "Replace the breaker" alone, with no load check, is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

If you smell burning or see smoke or scorching with any of these symptoms, leave the breaker off and do not reset it. If there is active smoke or fire, leave the house and call 911.

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air handler breaker trips when aux or emergency heat comes on | Shorted heat element, burned connection, failed sequencer | Reset once if there is no burning smell; replace filter; turn off Emergency Heat | Test elements and sequencers, repair connections, replace element or heat kit |
| Outdoor breaker trips seconds after compressor starts | Hard-starting or grounded compressor; weak run capacitor contributing to high-amp starts | Reset once if there is no burning smell; use Emergency Heat if the strip circuit is OK | Test capacitor and windings, replace capacitor or compressor |
| Outdoor breaker trips immediately on any call | Shorted fan motor, grounded wiring, water in control box | Leave the breaker off; note any ice; do not reset if you smell burning | Megohm test the motor and wiring, replace motor or repair wiring |
| Outdoor breaker trips while the unit is idle | Shorted crankcase heater, moisture in the disconnect | Leave the breaker off | Replace crankcase heater, dry out or repair the disconnect |
| Trips only on the coldest days or during defrost | Undersized circuit for the heat kit, weak breaker | Reset once if there is no burning smell; replace filter | Verify breaker and wire size against the nameplate, upgrade the circuit |
| Trips after long run times | Restricted airflow, dirty filter | Replace filter; open vents | Check blower and static pressure |
