## What this code means

Ignition lockout means your Rheem furnace's control board (IFC) ran through its full sequence of ignition attempts — typically three trials — and failed to detect (prove) a flame each time. To keep unburned gas from building up, the board shuts everything down and refuses to try again until the fault clears.

On most Rheem and Ruud boards this is shown as a specific flash code on the diagnostic LED (the exact pattern varies by board — check the label on the inside door or blower panel). The common behavior is a **one-hour soft lockout**: the board waits roughly an hour, then automatically retries the ignition sequence on its own. Some boards instead use a **hard lockout that holds until power is cycled** at the furnace switch or breaker. Either way, lockout is the furnace protecting itself after repeated failed lights — it is a symptom, not the root cause.

This page covers the lockout state itself. If your unit never attempts ignition at all, see the related "won't ignite" and "won't turn on" pages.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Weak or failed hot surface igniter** — the most common cause. A cracked or aged igniter won't glow hot enough to light gas, so the board times out and locks out.
2. **Dirty or misaligned flame sensor** — the furnace lights but the sensor can't "see" the flame, so the board thinks ignition failed and locks out after retries.
3. **Insufficient gas delivery to the burners** — low pressure, a partially closed manual valve, or a failing gas valve prevents a stable flame.
4. **Blocked or dirty burners** — debris or corrosion disrupts flame carryover and flame proving.
5. **Flue or combustion-air restriction / pressure switch issues** — a marginal draft can prevent clean ignition and flame proving on each trial.
6. **Grounding or wiring faults** — poor chassis ground interferes with flame sensing, causing repeated false "no flame" readings.
7. **Failing control board (IFC)** — less common; the board misreads ignition or mistimes the sequence.

Standard diagnostic practice is to verify the igniter, the flame-sensing circuit, and the gas supply before replacing the control board.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

**If you smell gas, do not touch any switch, breaker, or thermostat. Leave the house immediately and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.** This matters most on a furnace that has repeatedly tried and failed to light.

Otherwise, these are the only steps a homeowner should do:

- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to Heat and the setpoint is above room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** This is general maintenance rather than a fix for failed flame proving — a clogged filter causes high-limit trips and overheating shutdowns, not ignition lockout. Swapping in a clean filter of the correct size rules that out and costs nothing.
- **Check the breaker and furnace switch.** Make sure the furnace breaker is on and the service switch (looks like a light switch near the unit) is on.
- **Look at your vents and registers.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked.
- **Check the condensate drain (high-efficiency models).** If the drain line or trap is clogged, a safety switch can interrupt operation. Clear visible blockages at the drain only.
- **Try ONE power reset.** Turn the furnace off at the switch or breaker, wait 30 seconds, and turn it back on. If it locks out again, stop and call a technician.

Do **not** open the burner compartment, touch the igniter or flame sensor, bypass any switch, or reset the furnace repeatedly. Repeated resets on a unit that keeps failing to light can be dangerous.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Read the stored flash/fault code and ignition-attempt history from the IFC board.
- Inspect and ohm-test the **hot surface igniter** for resistance and visible cracks.
- Clean and test the **flame sensor**, measuring microamp signal during a trial for ignition.
- Verify **gas supply and manifold pressure** with a manometer, and confirm the manual valve is open.
- Inspect **burners** for debris, rust, or misalignment affecting flame carryover.
- Check **venting, combustion air, and pressure switches** for restriction.
- Confirm proper **chassis grounding** and inspect wiring/connectors.
- Replace the IFC board only after the igniter, sensor, gas, and grounding all check out.

If a quote jumps straight to a new control board without checking the igniter and flame sensor first, ask why — those are the usual culprits.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

Some of the symptoms below — whether the igniter glows, whether a flame lights and drops out, flame color and steadiness — are observations a technician makes with the burner compartment open. As a homeowner, don't remove panels to look. You can often infer the same thing from sound and timing: a burst of air with no heat, a brief whoosh that dies within seconds, or short run cycles followed by a long silent wait.

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Igniter glows but no flame, then lockout (tech observation) | Weak igniter or low gas | One reset; check filter | Test/replace hot surface igniter; check gas pressure |
| Burner lights briefly then shuts off, lockout (brief whoosh, then silence) | Dirty/misaligned flame sensor | One reset | Clean or replace flame sensor; verify microamp signal |
| Igniter never glows, lockout after retries (tech observation) | Failed igniter or board | One reset | Test igniter and IFC board |
| Weak yellow/uneven flame then lockout (tech observation) | Dirty burners or draft issue | Replace filter | Clean burners; check venting and pressure switch |
| Random lockouts, no clear pattern | Grounding or board fault | One reset | Check grounding/wiring; test control board |
| Lockout clears after ~1 hour then repeats | Recurring ignition failure | Stop resetting; call pro | Full ignition-sequence diagnosis |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80–$180
- **Hot surface igniter:** $150–$350 installed
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $100–$250
- **Burner cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Gas valve replacement:** $300–$800
- **Pressure switch or venting correction:** $150–$400
- **Control board (IFC):** $400–$1,200 installed, depending on the board and your local market
- **DIY filter or reset:** $0–$40

Many lockouts are resolved with a flame-sensor cleaning or igniter swap on the first visit, keeping total cost in the $150–$350 range. The worst realistic case — a new igniter *and* a new control board — lands around $550–$1,550 all in.
