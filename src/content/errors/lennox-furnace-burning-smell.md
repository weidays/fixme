## What this code means

"Burning smell" is not a Lennox diagnostic code. It is a symptom, and it ranges from harmless to dangerous.

**If the smell is electrical, smoky, or getting stronger, take these steps first:**
1. Set the thermostat to **Off**.
2. Turn off the furnace service switch, which usually looks like a light switch near the unit, or the furnace breaker.
3. If you see smoke or flame, or the smell intensifies after shutdown, **leave the house and call 911**.
4. If the smell is rotten eggs (gas), do not touch any switches. Leave immediately and call your gas utility's emergency line or 911 from outside.

Do not restart the furnace until it has been inspected.

The one common harmless case is dust burn-off. The first heating cycles of the season, or the first cycles after a long shutdown, often produce a dry, dusty smell that fades within a few hours.

Anything else needs a technician:
- smells like hot plastic, rubber, metal or electrical insulation
- odors that return every cycle
- odors that come with a code, a tripped breaker, or a blower that stops

Lennox control boards do not detect odor. Any related code (for example a limit trip) appears only as a side effect. Codes clear according to the underlying fault: a limit trip usually auto-resets once the furnace cools, while repeated trips can escalate to a lockout on some boards.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dust burn-off at season start.** Normal. It fades within hours, with no smoke and no electrical odor.
2. **Clogged air filter causing overheating.** Restricted airflow overheats the heat exchanger and strains the blower motor. It may trip the high-limit switch (E250 or a limit flash code).
3. **Failing or overheating blower motor.** Worn bearings or failing windings give a hot-electrical or burning-varnish smell. On PSC motors, a failing run capacitor can contribute.
4. **Overheated wiring or connections.** Loose terminals, melted wire insulation, or a burnt control-board relay or terminal give a sharp burnt-plastic smell.
5. **Inducer (draft) motor overheating.** Common on SL/SLP/EL/ML series. Bearing failure causes a hot-motor odor and often noise.
6. **Foreign object or debris near the burners or in the ducts.** Plastic, toys or paper fell into a register or near the cabinet.
7. **Rubber or plastic smell from a slipping belt.** Rare on modern Lennox units, which use direct-drive blowers. It applies to some older belt-drive G-series units.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Shut down first** if the smell is anything other than mild dust. These checks are for a mild odor with no smoke, done with the furnace off.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and look. If it is gray, clogged or bowed, replace it with the correct size, with the airflow arrow pointing toward the furnace.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it is set to Heat with a reasonable setpoint. Replace the batteries if the display is weak.
- **Breaker:** If the furnace breaker has tripped together with a burning smell, **do not reset it**. A trip plus an electrical odor means a fault. Call a technician.
- **Registers and vents:** Open all supply registers. Check returns for blockage and for objects dropped into floor registers that you can see and safely retrieve.
- **Panels:** Make sure the blower door and front panels are fully seated, because a loose door can stop operation.
- **Dust smell only:** If it is clearly dust at season start, run the furnace with windows cracked for a few hours. If the smell isn't gone by then, call for service.

You may restart the furnace **once** after a filter change, and only if the smell was mild and non-electrical. If the odor returns, shut it off and leave it off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Uses smell and visual inspection with the panels removed to locate the source: motor, wiring, board, or burner area.
- Reads the control-board LED or thermostat fault history for limit trips or motor faults.
- Checks the blower and inducer motors for bearing drag, overheating, and amp draw against the nameplate rating.
- Tests the run capacitor on PSC blower motors.
- Inspects wiring harnesses, terminals and board relays for discoloration or melting.
- Measures temperature rise against the range on the rating plate to confirm airflow and catch overheating.
- Inspects the heat exchanger and burners for debris or damage. Overheating can crack heat exchangers, so a CO check is standard practice.

A good quote names the specific part that failed and explains how it was confirmed.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dusty smell, first use of season, fades | Dust burn-off | Ventilate and replace the filter if dirty | None needed |
| Hot smell plus weak airflow, limit code | Clogged filter or blocked returns | Replace the filter and open the registers | Check the limit switch and temperature rise |
| Hot electrical or varnish smell, blower noisy or slow | Failing blower motor or capacitor | Shut off power and call | Test and replace the motor or capacitor |
| Sharp burnt-plastic smell, breaker tripped | Shorted or overheated wiring, or board relay | Leave the breaker off and call | Repair wiring and replace the board if damaged |
| Hot-motor smell plus whine or rattle at startup | Inducer motor bearings | Shut off and call | Replace the inducer motor |
| Smoke visible | Electrical fire risk | Power off, evacuate, call 911 | Full inspection before any restart |
| Rotten-egg smell | Gas leak | Leave the house and call the utility or 911 | Leak repair by utility or licensed tech |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges with parts and labor. Lennox OEM parts and variable-speed components cost more.

- **Dust burn-off:** $0
- **Air filter:** $10–$60 DIY, depending on size and MERV rating (5-inch media filters cost more)
- **Service call and diagnosis:** $90–$250, often more for nights or weekends
- **Run capacitor (PSC blower):** $120–$300
- **Wiring or terminal repair:** $150–$400
- **Inducer motor:** $400–$900
- **Blower motor, PSC:** $400–$800
- **Blower motor, variable-speed ECM:** $700–$1,500+
- **Control board:** $350–$900
- **Heat exchanger (if overheating caused cracking):** $1,500–$3,500, often weighed against replacing the furnace
