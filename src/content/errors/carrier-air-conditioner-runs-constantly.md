## What this code means

"Runs constantly" is a symptom, not a numbered Carrier fault code. It means one of two things:

- **The system runs but can't satisfy the thermostat.** It cools, but not fast enough to reach the setpoint.
- **The outdoor unit keeps running when it shouldn't.** The thermostat is satisfied or switched off, but the compressor and fan keep going.

Some long running is by design. Single-stage units can run continuously during peak heat. Two-stage and variable-speed Infinity units deliberately run long, low-capacity cycles for better dehumidification and efficiency.

Watch for these warning signs instead:

- Indoor temperature climbing or stuck several degrees above setpoint.
- The unit still running in mild evening weather.
- Rising electric bills.
- The outdoor unit running with the thermostat off.

There is no auto-reset or lockout tied to this symptom. However, an Infinity/Côr communicating thermostat or the outdoor control board may log related faults, such as low-pressure or high-pressure events. A technician can read these.

This page covers long run times specifically. If the air from the vents is warm, see the pages listed under Related codes.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter.** Restricted airflow cuts cooling capacity, so the system runs longer. This is also the most common cause of a frozen coil.
2. **Thermostat setting or placement issues.** Common examples:
   - The fan is set to ON rather than AUTO, which can look like constant running.
   - The setpoint is unrealistically low. Most systems can only hold about 15–20°F below outdoor temperature.
   - Weak batteries.
   - The thermostat is in direct sun or near a heat source.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil.** Cottonwood, grass clippings and dirt block heat rejection, which lowers capacity and raises pressures.
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Reduced capacity leads to long or continuous runs, and sometimes ice on the suction line.
5. **Stuck or welded contactor in the outdoor unit.** The compressor keeps running even after the thermostat stops calling for cooling.
6. **Shorted thermostat wire or control fault.** A damaged Y wire, or a thermostat or control board holding the cooling call on, keeps the system running.
7. **Dirty evaporator coil or blower problems indoors.** Low airflow across the coil reduces capacity.
8. **Duct leaks or a disconnected duct in the attic.** Cooled air is lost before it reaches your rooms.
9. **Undersized system, or a home with high heat gain.** Poor insulation, new additions or large sun-facing windows can outpace the unit's capacity.
10. **Weak compressor or failing run capacitor.** These reduce pumping capacity in aging units.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the thermostat.**
  - Confirm it is set to COOL and the fan is on AUTO.
  - Confirm the setpoint is realistic.
  - Replace the batteries if it uses them.
  - Make sure no schedule or hold is forcing a low temperature.
- **Replace the air filter** if it is gray, clogged or more than about 90 days old. Use the size printed on the old filter's frame.
- **Open supply registers and clear return grilles.** Make sure furniture, rugs or curtains aren't blocking them.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from outside.**
  - Clear leaves, weeds and debris from around it. Keep about 2 feet of clearance.
  - Do not remove the panels or reach inside.
- **Check the condensate drain.** If the drain pan or line is backed up and the float switch shuts the unit down, that is a different symptom. Clear only an accessible line and cleanout.
- **Confirm the exterior panels and indoor access panels are seated.**
- **If the outdoor unit runs with the thermostat off,** switch off the outdoor disconnect or breaker and call a technician. Do not keep resetting it.
- **If you see ice on the indoor coil or refrigerant lines,** turn cooling off and run the fan only to thaw it. Then call for service. See Frozen evaporator coil.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will typically:

1. **Measure the temperature split** between return and supply air. On most systems, a 16–22°F drop is normal.
2. **Check airflow.** This includes the filter, the blower speed setting, static pressure and the condition of the evaporator coil.
3. **Attach gauges or digital probes.** They will read suction and liquid pressures, then calculate superheat and subcooling against Carrier's charging chart for your model. This shows whether the charge is low or overcharged.
4. **Run a leak search** if the charge is low. Methods include an electronic detector, bubble solution or a nitrogen pressure test.
5. **Inspect the contactor** for pitted or welded contacts. They will also check for 24V at the contactor coil while the thermostat is satisfied, which indicates a wiring short or a control fault.
6. **Test the run capacitor** and measure compressor amp draw.
7. **Inspect and clean the condenser coil.** They will also check that the outdoor fan is running correctly.
8. **Read stored faults** on Infinity/Côr communicating systems, from the thermostat's service menu or the outdoor board.
9. **Evaluate ductwork and sizing** if the equipment checks out. This may include a load calculation (Manual J).

**Red flags on a quote:**

- A refrigerant top-off with no leak search on a repeat low charge.
- A compressor replacement recommended without amp or pressure readings.
- No temperature split measured at all.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs long, house slowly cools, filter is dark | Dirty air filter | Replace the filter | Check the coil for dirt or ice if the problem continues |
| Air moves constantly even when not cooling | Fan set to ON | Set the fan to AUTO | None usually needed |
| Never reaches setpoint on very hot days only | Normal capacity limit or undersized unit | Raise the setpoint a few degrees and close blinds | Load calculation and sizing review |
| Long runs, outdoor coil matted with debris | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris around the unit from outside | Chemical coil cleaning |
| Long runs, ice on the copper line, weak cooling | Low refrigerant or a leak | Turn cooling off and call for service | Leak search, repair, evacuation and recharge |
| Outdoor unit runs with the thermostat off | Welded contactor or shorted Y wire | Shut off the disconnect or breaker | Replace the contactor or repair the wiring |
| Some rooms hot, attic very hot | Duct leak or disconnected duct | Check that registers are open | Duct inspection and sealing |
| Older unit, weak cooling, humming | Failing capacitor or worn compressor | None | Capacitor test and replacement, compressor evaluation |
| Low airflow at registers, filter clean | Dirty evaporator coil or blower issue | Confirm registers are open and the filter is correct | Coil cleaning, blower service |
