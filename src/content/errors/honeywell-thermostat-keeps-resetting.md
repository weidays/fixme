## What this code means

"Keeps resetting" is a symptom, not a numbered error code. It covers three behaviors:

- **Rebooting:** the screen goes dark briefly and then shows the startup or logo screen again.
- **Lost settings:** the clock or schedule reverts to defaults.
- **Lost Wi-Fi pairing:** the thermostat drops its connection after a restart.

In almost every case, the thermostat is briefly losing power or can't hold its memory. Honeywell does not show a specific on-screen code for this. The thermostat resets itself on its own, and the cycle repeats until the power issue is fixed.

This page is different from the related blank-screen pages. Here, the display comes back on its own and loses settings, rather than staying dark.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Weak or wrong batteries.** On battery and battery-backup models, low alkaline batteries can't hold memory during a power dip. Rechargeable batteries are also a problem because their voltage is lower. Many hardwired units keep their program in non-volatile memory regardless, so on those models weak batteries mostly affect the clock. Honeywell recommends fresh alkaline batteries once a year.
2. **No C-wire, or unstable 24V power (Wi-Fi models).** "Power stealing" models can brown out when the furnace or AC starts. This often shows up as a reboot each time the system kicks on.
3. **Thermostat not seated firmly on its wallplate.** A loose faceplate breaks contact with the terminals intermittently.
4. **Power interruptions at the HVAC unit.** Causes include a tripped breaker, a furnace service switch bumped off, or a furnace door safety switch opening because the panel isn't seated.
5. **A system safety cutting 24V power.** A clogged condensate line can trip a float switch, and on some systems that kills thermostat power. Another cause is a blown control fuse in the air handler.
6. **Firmware glitch or corrupted settings (Wi-Fi models).** This usually clears after an update or one factory reset.
7. **Failing thermostat electronics.** This is likely when resets continue despite good batteries and verified power.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the batteries with fresh name-brand alkalines.** Do this even if the low-battery icon isn't showing. Check the battery compartment for corrosion.
- **Reseat the thermostat.** Pull it straight off the wallplate and push it back until it clicks evenly.
- **Check the breaker and furnace switch.** Look for a tripped HVAC breaker, and make sure the furnace service switch (it looks like a light switch) is on.
- **Seat the furnace front panel.** Make sure it is fully closed so the door switch stays engaged. Do not open the cabinet or press the switch by hand.
- **Check the condensate drain and pan.** If you see standing water or a full overflow pan, clear the line at the accessible cleanout. Use a wet/dry vac at the outdoor end of the drain line.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** Severe restriction can trip limit switches. That makes the system short-cycle, which can be mistaken for a thermostat problem even though it does not normally reboot the thermostat.
- **Wi-Fi models:** open your thermostat's Honeywell/Resideo or Total Connect Comfort app and confirm the thermostat is online. Firmware updates usually install automatically. You can also do one factory reset from the menu. Follow your model's manual, since the menu path varies.
- **Note the timing.** Write down whether resets happen when the heat or AC starts. A technician will want to know.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- They will measure the 24VAC across R and C at the thermostat, with the system idle and with it running, to look for voltage sag.
- They will check for an unused C conductor in the wall cable. If one exists, they connect it at both ends. If not, they install a Honeywell C-wire adapter or run new cable.
- They will inspect the control-board fuse, the transformer output and the low-voltage wiring for shorts or chafing.
- They will test the float or condensate switch and the door switch.
- They will confirm the thermostat and wallplate are compatible with the system type (heat pump, multi-stage).
- If the voltage tests are good, the thermostat becomes the likely culprit. Intermittent wiring faults and wallplate terminal issues can still pass a spot voltage test.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clock or schedule lost after an outage | Weak batteries | Install fresh alkaline batteries | Usually none; frequent outage-related losses on a hardwired model may warrant a power check |
| Reboots each time heat or AC starts | No C-wire or voltage sag | Note the timing and confirm the thermostat is online in its app | Add a C-wire or adapter, or check the transformer |
| Random restarts, screen flickers | Loose faceplate | Reseat it on the wallplate | Inspect terminals and wiring |
| Resets plus no heat or cool for a while | Breaker, switch or door panel | Reset the breaker once, check the switch, seat the panel | Find the cause of the trips |
| Resets with water near the indoor unit | Float switch tripped | Clear the condensate line | Clear the trap, test the float switch |
| Keeps resetting despite fixes above | Failed thermostat | Replace the thermostat | Install and configure, verify power |

## Repair costs

- **Batteries:** $5–$10.
- **Honeywell C-wire adapter:** about $25–$35 for the part, or $100–$200 installed.
- **New C-wire run:** $150–$350.
- **Service call and diagnosis:** $80–$150.
- **Control-board fuse replacement:** $90–$180 including the visit.
- **Condensate line clearing by a pro:** $75–$200.
- **Transformer replacement:** $150–$300.
- **Replacement thermostat:** $30–$250 for the unit, plus $100–$250 for installation.
