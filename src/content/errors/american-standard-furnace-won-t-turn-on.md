## What this code means

"Won't turn on" is not a flash code. It describes an American Standard furnace that does nothing when the thermostat calls for heat: no inducer motor, no click, no blower, and often no LED on the control board.

Here is how to read the board LED (visible through the sight glass on the blower door on most models):

- **LED off:** The board has no 24-volt power. This points to line power, the door switch, the board fuse, or the transformer.
- **LED steady on (normal status):** The board is powered but isn't receiving a call for heat. This points to the thermostat or its wiring.
- **LED flashing a code:** This is a different problem. See the flash-code pages listed under Related codes.

Exact LED behavior varies by board generation, so check the diagnostic label on your furnace door.

There is no lockout specific to this condition. If power was simply interrupted, the furnace resumes when power is restored.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat problem.** Dead batteries, the thermostat set to Cool or Off, the setpoint below room temperature, or a blank display.
2. **Power switched off or breaker tripped.** The furnace service switch (it looks like a light switch, usually on or near the unit) is often mistaken for a light switch and flipped off. A tripped breaker does the same.
3. **Blower door not seated.** The door safety switch cuts all power when the lower panel is loose or off, often after a filter change.
4. **Blown low-voltage fuse on the control board.** This is usually caused by a short in the thermostat wire or in an accessory such as a humidifier or the outdoor unit's wiring.
5. **Failed 24-volt transformer.**
6. **Broken or disconnected thermostat wiring** between the thermostat and the board.
7. **Failed control board.**

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set it to Heat, raise the setpoint 3–5°F above room temperature, and replace the batteries. If the display stays blank on a thermostat without batteries, it isn't getting power. That points back to the furnace.
- **Furnace switch and breaker:** Make sure the service switch is On. Find the furnace breaker, switch it fully Off, then back On, once. If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.
- **Blower door:** Press the lower panel firmly into place so its tabs seat and the door switch is pushed in. Do not tape or hold the switch closed.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it's dirty. While you're there, make sure the filter isn't keeping the door from closing fully.
- **Condensate (95% models):** Make sure the drain line isn't visibly kinked or overflowing. Some models stop working on a condensate overflow switch.
- **Vents:** Make sure supply registers are open and outdoor intake and exhaust pipes are clear of snow or debris.
- **One reset:** If the LED shows a code, write it down before power cycling. Then do one off/on cycle only.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Check line power.** The tech measures 120 V at the board and confirms the door switch is closing.
- **Check the transformer.** The tech measures 24 V AC at its secondary.
- **Inspect the board fuse.** If it's blown, the tech isolates the short by disconnecting thermostat and accessory wires one at a time before replacing the fuse.
- **Test the thermostat circuit.** The tech jumpers R to W at the board to see whether the furnace responds without the thermostat. This separates a thermostat or wiring fault from a furnace fault.
- **Replace the board only after the steps above.** A good tech confirms power in, 24 V present, and a call for heat at W with no response before condemning the board.

A quote for a new board that skips these steps deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat display blank | Dead batteries or no 24 V | Replace the batteries | Check the transformer, fuse and wiring |
| Thermostat fine, furnace silent, LED steady | No call for heat reaching the board | Check the Heat mode and setpoint | Jumper test R-W and repair the wiring |
| No LED, no sound | Switch off, breaker tripped, or door open | Check the switch and breaker, reseat the door | Verify 120 V and the door switch |
| No LED, power confirmed on | Blown fuse or failed transformer | None | Find the short, replace the fuse or transformer |
| Breaker trips again after one reset | Electrical short | Leave the breaker off | Diagnose the motor or wiring short |
| Power and 24 V present, still no response | Failed control board | None | Replace the board |
