## What this code means

**First, if you smell gas, hear a CO alarm, or anyone feels dizzy, nauseated or headachy:**

1. Leave the house now. Do not operate light switches.
2. Call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from outside.
3. If none of those apply, set the thermostat to OFF and switch off the furnace power switch.
4. Open windows.
5. Do not run the furnace again until a technician has inspected it.

Flame rollout means burner flame or very hot combustion gases came out of the front of the burner box instead of being drawn through the heat exchanger and up the vent. York furnaces have one or more rollout switches mounted on the burner assembly to catch this. When a switch overheats, it opens, and the control board shuts off the gas valve.

How this appears depends on the board:

- **Many York models:** the rollout switches share a circuit with the main limit, so the LED shows 4 flashes. See the 4 flashes page for the limit side of that code.
- **Some boards:** these display a separate rollout indication.

Most York rollout switches are **manual-reset**. They stay open until physically reset, and power cycling does not clear them. A tripped rollout switch is a symptom of a combustion or venting problem, not a part failure in itself.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted vent/flue.** Common culprits are a bird or animal nest, ice or snow at the termination, a disconnected or sagging vent pipe, or a blocked chimney on older 80% units. Exhaust backs up and pushes flame forward.
2. **Restricted heat exchanger passages.** Soot or debris in the primary or secondary heat exchanger blocks flue gas flow. This often follows burner problems or years without service.
3. **Cracked or failed heat exchanger.** Air from the blower disrupts the flame and drives it out of the burner box. This is the most serious cause and is common on older units.
4. **Weak or failing inducer motor.** The inducer moves just enough air to close the pressure switch but not enough to draw combustion gases properly.
5. **Over-firing.** Manifold gas pressure is set too high, or the orifices are wrong. This often happens after a natural-gas/LP conversion that was done incorrectly.
6. **Misaligned or dirty burners.** Rust scale or debris causes poor flame carryover and lazy, rolling flames.
7. **A weak rollout switch that trips without a real rollout.** This is possible, but a technician should rule out every cause above before blaming the switch.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do **not** remove the burner door or press the rollout reset. These checks are the limit of safe homeowner work:

- **CO alarm:** Confirm your carbon monoxide alarms work. Replace their batteries if they are due.
- **Thermostat:** Leave it set to OFF until the furnace is inspected. Do not keep calling for heat.
- **Outdoor vent terminations:** On 90%+ York furnaces, look at the two PVC pipes outside. Gently clear any snow, ice, leaves or visible nests from the openings without reaching inside the pipes.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it is dirty. A clogged filter doesn't cause rollout, but the technician will want a clean one when testing.
- **Area around the furnace:** Keep stored items and combustibles away from the cabinet.
- **Condensate line (90%+ units):** If you can see an obvious clog or kink in the external drain tube, note it for the technician.

Even if you clear a blocked vent, the rollout switch still needs a technician to inspect the furnace and reset it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A proper visit should include:

1. **Checking which switch is open.** The technician confirms whether the rollout switch or the main limit is open, since they share a code on many boards.
2. **Inspecting the burner area for scorching.** Look for discolored paint, melted wire insulation, or soot at the burner box. These confirm a real rollout.
3. **Inspecting the vent.** The full venting run is checked, along with the inducer's draft or pressure reading against York's specifications.
4. **Measuring gas pressure.** Manifold pressure is checked against the rating plate, and the orifices are verified for the fuel type.
5. **Inspecting burners.** Burners are checked for alignment, rust and proper flame carryover.
6. **Evaluating the heat exchanger.** This is done with a camera, by watching the flame as the blower starts, and with a combustion/CO analysis.
7. **Resetting and test-firing.** Only after the cause is fixed does the technician reset the rollout switch and test-fire while watching the flame. They should also test CO levels at the flue and in the living space.

Be wary of anyone who just resets the switch and leaves, or who replaces it without finding a cause.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 flashes, furnace dead after a snowstorm | Vent termination blocked by snow or ice | Clear the outside vent openings, leave furnace off, call for service | Inspect the vent, check for damage, reset the rollout switch, test-fire |
| Soot or scorch marks near the burners | Restricted heat exchanger or flue | Keep furnace off and ventilate | Clean the heat exchanger, inspect the vent, replace damaged wiring |
| Flame dances or changes when the blower starts | Cracked heat exchanger | Keep furnace off, check CO alarms | Camera inspection and combustion test, then heat exchanger replacement or furnace replacement |
| Rollout after a gas conversion or service | Over-firing or wrong orifices | None | Correct manifold pressure and orifices |
| Inducer noisy or weak, intermittent rollout | Failing inducer | None | Measure draft, replace the inducer |
| Repeated trips, no evidence of rollout | Weak rollout switch | None | Replace the switch only after the other causes are ruled out |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, parts and labor included:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic or service call | $100–$250 (higher for emergency or after-hours visits) |
| Clearing or repairing a vent, reconnecting pipe | $150–$500 |
| Rollout switch replacement | $150–$350 |
| Burner cleaning and realignment | $150–$350 |
| Gas pressure adjustment or orifice correction | $100–$300 |
| Inducer motor replacement | $400–$1,000 |
| Heat exchanger cleaning | $250–$600 |
| Heat exchanger replacement | $1,500–$3,500 or more |
| New furnace, if the heat exchanger has failed on an older unit | $4,000–$8,000 installed |

York heat exchangers often carry long or limited-lifetime warranties if the furnace was registered. The part may be covered even when labor is not, so check before you agree to a replacement.
