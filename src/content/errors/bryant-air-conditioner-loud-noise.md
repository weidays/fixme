## What this code means

"Loud noise" is a symptom, not a numbered Bryant fault code. Most Bryant split-system air conditioners will not display or flash anything when they become noisy. Communicating Evolution systems may record a related fault in the thermostat's service history, but noise itself is not logged. Since there is no code, there is nothing to auto-reset or clear.

Some sounds are normal on a Bryant AC:

- a steady hum from the outdoor unit
- airflow whoosh at the registers
- a soft click from the contactor at start and stop
- a brief pitch change on two-stage and variable-speed models as they ramp

The sound itself tells you most about the cause:

- **Grinding or screeching** points to motor bearings.
- **Rattling or banging** points to loose panels, debris, or internal compressor problems.
- **Loud buzzing** points to electrical components.
- **Hissing or gurgling** can point to refrigerant issues.

If the noise is coming from the indoor ductless head, see the separate mini-split page.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Debris or loose exterior panels on the outdoor unit.** Sticks, leaves or toys in the fan area, or a loose service panel or grille screw, cause rattling, ticking or vibration.
2. **A weak run capacitor or a pitted, chattering contactor.** These cause a loud buzz or hum. The fan or compressor may struggle to start, or the contactor may chatter. The outdoor-fan and compressor-not-running pages cover the no-start side of this problem.
3. **Failing condenser fan motor bearings or a damaged fan blade.** These cause a screech, squeal or grinding from the top of the outdoor unit, or a blade that wobbles or strikes the grille.
4. **An indoor blower issue on the furnace or air handler.** A loose or unbalanced blower wheel, a worn motor or a loose access panel causes rattling or whining at the indoor unit.
5. **Compressor mechanical or mounting problems.** Worn internal parts, broken internal mounts or degraded rubber isolation feet cause clanking, banging, or a rattle at startup or shutdown.
6. **Refrigerant system issues.** A leak can make hissing or bubbling sounds, and high head pressure causes a labored, loud compressor. A brief hiss or whoosh right after the AC shuts off can be normal. It is the sound of system pressures equalizing. Cooling-only air conditioners do not have a reversing valve. That part is found only on heat pumps, so if you have a heat pump, see the separate heat pump page.
7. **Refrigerant lines vibrating against the house or framing.** This makes a rattle or hum that carries through the walls.
8. **Duct or register noise.** A restricted filter or closed registers increase static pressure and cause whistling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Stop if you smell burning or see smoke.** Turn the system off at the breaker or outdoor disconnect and call a technician. Do not keep diagnosing. If smoke or fire is present, call 911.
- **Pinpoint the noise.** Note whether it comes from the outdoor unit, the indoor unit, or the ducts. Note whether it happens at startup, during the whole run, or at shutdown. A short phone video helps the technician.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty. A clogged filter causes whistling and makes the blower strain.
- **Open the supply registers and return grilles.** Make sure they are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.**
  1. Turn the system off at the thermostat.
  2. Turn off the outdoor disconnect.
  3. Remove leaves, branches and debris from the sides and top grille surface by hand.
  4. Keep about 2 ft of clearance around the unit.
  
  Do not reach through the grille or remove it.
- **Seat the exterior panels.** Confirm the outdoor service panel and the indoor furnace/air-handler door are fully seated. Press them into place, but do not open them.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Make sure the line is not gurgling loudly or backing up. A gurgle can come from a partly clogged drain or trap.
- **Shut down if the noise is severe.** Grinding, screeching, banging or a loud buzz with no fan spinning means you should turn the system off at the thermostat and leave it off. Running it can damage the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically take these steps:

1. **Listen and localize.** They run the system and identify whether the noise is from the fan motor, compressor, contactor or blower.
2. **Test the electrical components.** With power isolated, they inspect the condenser fan motor and spin the blade by hand to check for bearing drag or wobble. They also:
   - test the run capacitor with a meter
   - inspect the contactor contacts for pitting or chatter
   - measure motor and compressor amperage against nameplate values
3. **Check the compressor.** They check the compressor mounts and isolation grommets. They listen for internal knocking and may compare amp draw and pressures to judge internal wear.
4. **Check the refrigerant.** They connect gauges to check pressures and superheat/subcooling. If a leak is suspected, they leak-test. Only certified technicians may handle refrigerant.
5. **Inspect the indoor blower.** They check the wheel, motor mounts and the blower door switch area at the furnace or air handler.
6. **Check fault history on Evolution systems.** They review stored faults for related events.
7. **Inspect the refrigerant lines.** They check line-set routing and add clamps or isolation where lines touch the structure.

If the quote calls for a compressor replacement, it should be supported by the measurements above, not by sound alone.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rattling from outdoor unit | Debris or loose panel | Power off, clear debris around unit, seat panel | Tighten fasteners, check fan blade |
| Loud buzz, fan not spinning | Failed capacitor or stuck contactor | Turn system off at thermostat | Test and replace capacitor or contactor |
| Screech or squeal from top of outdoor unit | Condenser fan motor bearings | Turn system off | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Burning smell or smoke | Overheating electrical component | Turn off at breaker or disconnect; call 911 if smoke or fire is present | Find and replace the failed component |
| Clanking or banging at start/stop | Compressor mounts or internal wear | Turn system off | Inspect mounts, diagnose compressor |
| Hissing or bubbling | Refrigerant leak | Turn system off | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Brief hiss or whoosh right after shutdown | Pressures equalizing (normal) | None needed | None needed |
| Whistling at registers | Dirty filter or closed registers | Replace filter, open registers | Check static pressure and ducts |
| Rattle or whine at furnace/air handler | Blower wheel or motor, loose door | Seat the access door | Balance or replace wheel or motor |
| Hum through walls | Line set touching structure | Note location | Re-clamp and isolate lines |

## Repair costs

Typical US price ranges, parts and labor:

| Repair | Cost |
|---|---|
| Debris removal, filter change (DIY) | $0–$40 |
| Service call/diagnostic | $80–$200 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $120–$350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Condenser fan motor replacement | $300–$750 |
| Fan blade replacement | $150–$350 |
| Indoor blower motor replacement | $400–$1,500 (variable-speed ECM motors cost more) |
| Line-set re-securing or isolation | $100–$300 |
| Refrigerant leak repair and recharge | $300–$1,500+ (depends on leak location and refrigerant type) |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800–$3,500+ |

Bryant's limited warranty can cover parts such as motors and compressors. Registering the unit, typically within 90 days of installation, generally extends that parts coverage. Unregistered units usually still carry a shorter base parts warranty, so check your coverage before paying for parts. Labor is usually extra unless you have a labor plan.
