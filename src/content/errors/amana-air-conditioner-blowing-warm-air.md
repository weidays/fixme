## What this code means

"Blowing warm air" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. The indoor blower is moving air, but that air comes out of the vents at or above room temperature. On most Amana split systems there is no display to tell you why. Communicating ComfortBridge models may log a fault on the outdoor board's display, which a technician can read.

This page covers air that is clearly warm. Two related situations have their own pages:

- **Cool but inadequate air:** see "Not cooling."
- **Room-temperature air with a quiet outdoor unit:** see "Fan runs but no cold air."

The causes that point specifically to *warm* air are:

- The outdoor unit is stopped while the blower keeps running.
- Heat is being added indoors, by the wrong thermostat mode or electric heat strips that stay energized.
- Hot attic air is leaking into the ductwork.

Reset behavior depends on the cause:

- Compressor thermal overloads and many high- and low-pressure switches **auto-reset** after cooling down. This often shows up as cooling that comes and goes.
- Some ComfortBridge controls **hold a lockout until power is cycled.**

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat set to Heat or Auto, or blower set to ON.** With the fan set to ON, the blower runs between cooling cycles and pushes air that has warmed in the ducts.
2. **Outdoor unit not running: failed run capacitor or contactor.** These are the most common hardware failures in Amana condensers. The indoor blower runs normally, but the compressor and outdoor fan do not.
3. **Outdoor unit tripped on a safety: dirty condenser coil or stopped condenser fan.** High head pressure trips the compressor overload or the high-pressure switch. Air turns warm until the unit cools down and restarts.
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Cooling capacity drops until the air is barely cool or warm. On models with a low-pressure switch, the compressor may shut off entirely.
5. **Electric heat strips staying on.** Air handlers with heat kits can have a stuck sequencer or relay that keeps the strips heating during cooling. Air can be noticeably hot.
6. **Duct leaks or a disconnected duct in a hot attic or crawlspace.** A loose return can pull 120°F+ attic air into the system. Often only certain rooms get warm air.
7. **Low-voltage control problem.** A blown control-board fuse, a broken thermostat wire, or a tripped float switch can stop the outdoor unit's call while the blower still runs. Behavior varies by air handler or furnace board.
8. **Failing compressor.** This is less common. Suspect it only after the capacitor, contactor and refrigerant charge have been ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set it to **Cool** with the fan on **Auto**, and the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it is dirty. A clogged filter can reduce airflow enough to cause icing, which then shows up as poor or warm air.
- **Breakers and disconnect:** Check the AC breaker and the furnace or air-handler breaker. Also check that the outdoor disconnect is on. You can reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and see "Breaker keeps tripping."
- **Outdoor unit:** With the system calling for cooling, look and listen from outside. Is the top fan spinning and the unit humming? **Don't touch anything inside it.** Clear leaves, grass clippings or debris from around the outside of the coil fins, keeping about 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate:** If your system has a float switch on the drain pan or line and the pan is full, the system may have shut off cooling. Clear the visible drain line end, or use a wet/dry vac at the outside drain outlet.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return registers are open and not blocked by furniture.
- **One reset:** Turn the thermostat off and switch off the breaker for 5 minutes. Then restore power and call for cooling **once**. If warm air returns, call a technician. Don't keep resetting it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Measures the temperature drop** between return and supply air. A normal system shows roughly 16–22°F cooler air at the supply. A warmer supply confirms heat is being added.
- **Checks the outdoor unit for a cooling call (24 V)** at the contactor. Then checks contactor function, and tests the dual run capacitor with a meter against its rated µF.
- **Reads any ComfortBridge fault codes** on the outdoor board, if equipped.
- **Measures compressor and condenser-fan amps.** Checks for an open internal overload or high-pressure switch, and inspects condenser coil cleanliness.
- **Connects gauges or probes** to check superheat or subcooling against Amana's charging chart. If the charge is low, they should leak-search rather than just "top off."
- **Checks heat-kit sequencers and relays** for voltage to the strips during cooling.
- **Inspects the low-voltage side:** the control board fuse, thermostat wiring and float switches.
- **Inspects accessible ductwork** in the attic or crawlspace for disconnected or leaking runs, especially the return.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, outdoor unit silent | Capacitor, contactor or no 24 V signal | Check thermostat mode and breakers, then one reset | Test and replace capacitor or contactor, check control fuse and wiring |
| Outdoor unit hums but fan doesn't spin | Failed capacitor or condenser fan motor | Turn system off | Replace capacitor or fan motor |
| Cools a while, then warm, then cools again | Overload or high-pressure trip from a dirty coil or weak fan | Clear debris around the outdoor unit | Clean coil, check fan, check pressures |
| Lukewarm air, long runtimes, possible ice | Low refrigerant charge or leak | Replace filter, turn off if iced | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Air noticeably hot, high electric bill | Heat strips stuck on | Turn system off | Replace sequencer or relay, check board |
| Only some rooms warm, attic is hot | Duct leak or disconnection | Check registers are open | Reconnect and seal ducts |
| Warm air with the fan set to ON only | Thermostat fan setting | Set fan to Auto | None needed |

| Repair | Typical US cost |
|---|---|

| Contactor replacement | $150–$350 |

| Condenser fan motor | $350–$750 |

| Duct reconnect or sealing | $200–$1,000+ |

| Evaporator coil replacement | $1,200–$2,800 |

Amana's compressor warranty is long, but labor and refrigerant are usually not covered. Ask whether the unit was registered.
