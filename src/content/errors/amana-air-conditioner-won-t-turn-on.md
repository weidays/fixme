## What this code means

"Won't turn on" isn't a stored fault code. It describes a symptom: you set the thermostat to cool, and nothing happens. The indoor blower doesn't start, the outdoor condenser stays silent, and the thermostat may be blank.

On a standard Amana split system, the indoor furnace or air handler supplies the 24-volt control power for the whole system. Its transformer feeds the thermostat, and the thermostat then calls the outdoor unit (Y) and the blower (G). If line power, the transformer, the low-voltage fuse or the control circuit is interrupted, the entire system stays dead.

This is different from pages where part of the system runs:
- If the indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit doesn't, see "Compressor not running" or "Fan runs but no cold air."
- If the breaker trips repeatedly, see "Breaker keeps tripping."

How the system recovers depends on the cause:
- **Float or condensate switches** reset automatically once the water drains.
- **Blown fuses** stay open until a technician replaces them.
- **Communicating Amana systems** (ComfortBridge or CoolCloud-equipped AVXC models) may display a fault at the indoor board LED or the thermostat and hold it until power is cycled. Behavior varies by model, so check your unit's wiring-diagram label.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat issue.** The thermostat may be blank because of dead batteries, set to Heat or Off, set too high, or on a schedule or hold that blocks cooling.
2. **Power switched off or breaker tripped.** This could be the furnace or air handler service switch (often mistaken for a light switch), the indoor breaker, the outdoor breaker, or the outdoor disconnect.
3. **Condensate safety or float switch tripped.** A clogged drain line or full pan can open the switch and cut the 24V cooling call.
4. **Blown low-voltage fuse on the indoor control board.** This is usually a 3A or 5A automotive-style fuse. It's often blown by a shorted thermostat wire or a chafed outdoor control wire.
5. **Blower door safety switch open.** If the indoor unit's panel isn't fully seated, the door switch removes power from the board.
6. **Failed 24V transformer.** With no 24V output, the thermostat and outdoor contactor get no power.
7. **Damaged thermostat wiring.** Broken, corroded or loose wires at the thermostat, the board or the outdoor unit can stop the signal.
8. **Failed indoor control board or a communication fault.** This applies on communicating models.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set it to Cool and lower the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature. Set the fan to Auto, and cancel any hold or schedule.
- **Batteries:** If the display is blank or shows a low-battery icon, replace the batteries with fresh AA or AAA cells.
- **Breakers:** Check the breaker panel for the furnace or air handler and the AC condenser. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Switches:** Make sure the furnace or air handler service switch near the unit is ON. Also check that the outdoor disconnect is in place and switched on.
- **Condensate:** Look for water in the drain pan or a tripped float switch on the drain line. Clear the drain line opening with a wet/dry vacuum from outside, then wait about 15 minutes for the switch to reset.
- **Panels:** Make sure the indoor unit's front panels are fully seated and latched. Don't open or reach into the cabinet.
- **Filter and vents:** Replace a dirty filter and confirm the supply registers are open. These rarely cause a dead system on their own, but they prevent follow-on problems.
- **One full reset:** Turn the thermostat off, switch off both breakers for five minutes, restore power, and then call for cooling. If nothing happens, call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically:
- **Verify line voltage** (120V or 240V) at the indoor unit and 240V at the outdoor disconnect.
- **Measure transformer output** and check for 24V between R and C at the board.
- **Inspect the board fuse.** If it's blown, they'll find the short before replacing it, usually in the thermostat cable, the outdoor wire run or the contactor coil.
- **Check the door switch and float switch** for continuity.
- **Jumper R to Y and G at the board** to test whether the problem is the thermostat or the equipment. This is a diagnostic step only, not a repair.
- **Read the board's diagnostic LED** or the communicating thermostat's fault history, and compare it to the code chart on the unit door.

Be wary of a quote to replace the condenser or compressor when the indoor unit also won't power up. A completely dead system almost always points to the power or control side.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat display blank | Dead batteries or no 24V power | Replace batteries; check furnace switch and breaker | Test transformer and fuse; replace if needed |
| Display on, nothing runs on Cool | Wrong mode or setting, or a schedule hold | Set to Cool, lower setpoint, cancel hold | Check thermostat wiring and the R/Y/G signal |
| Water in pan or near unit, system dead | Float switch tripped | Clear drain line; let pan drain | Clean drain and trap; inspect or replace switch |
| Worked until recently, now fully dead | Blown board fuse | None; don't open the cabinet | Find the short; replace fuse or repair wiring |
| Dead after a filter change or panel removal | Door switch open | Reseat and latch the panels | Test or replace the door switch |
| Breaker trips again after one reset | Electrical short | Leave it off | Diagnose the short (see "Breaker keeps tripping") |
| Board LED shows a fault code (communicating models) | Control or communication fault | Cycle power once | Read the code and repair wiring or the board |
