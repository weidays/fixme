## What this code means

"Runs constantly" is a symptom, not a fault code. Your Trane air conditioner keeps running for very long stretches, or never shuts off, and the indoor temperature either never reaches the thermostat setpoint or the unit keeps going after it does.

There are two different problems that look alike:

1. **The AC runs but can't catch up.** The system is cooling, just not enough to satisfy the thermostat. Common reasons are poor airflow, low refrigerant, dirty coils, heavy heat load or an undersized unit.
2. **The AC runs with no call for cooling.** The thermostat is satisfied or set to OFF, but the outdoor unit keeps running. That is an electrical control fault. The usual culprits are a welded contactor, a shorted thermostat wire or a thermostat fault.

**Variable-speed and two-stage models behave differently.** Trane's variable-speed models (XV18, XV20i) and two-stage models (XL18i and similar) are designed to run long, low-capacity cycles. On these systems, long runtime with a comfortable home is normal operation.

There is no lockout or reset tied to this symptom. Some Trane communicating thermostats display alerts about a related fault. If yours shows one, write it down for the technician.

If the AC is blowing warm air or the indoor coil is icing, see the related pages listed at the bottom. This page covers runtime itself.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter.** This is the most common cause. Restricted airflow cuts cooling capacity, so the system runs longer to move the same heat.
2. **Extreme outdoor heat or heavy heat load.** Most residential ACs are sized to hold about a 20°F indoor/outdoor difference. On very hot days, long cycles are expected. Sun-facing windows, attic heat and air leaks make it worse.
3. **Thermostat setting, placement or fault.** Possible problems include:
   - A setpoint that is unrealistically low.
   - The fan set to ON rather than AUTO, which makes it look like the system "always runs."
   - Weak batteries.
   - A thermostat mounted in a hot spot, such as direct sun or near a lamp.
   - A failing thermostat that keeps calling for cooling.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil.** Grass clippings, cottonwood fluff and dirt block heat rejection. This lowers capacity and raises head pressure.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** The system can't remove enough heat, so it runs long. Over time this often leads to a frozen evaporator coil.
6. **Dirty indoor evaporator coil or blower problems.** A dirty coil, a dirty blower wheel or a weak blower motor reduces airflow across the coil.
7. **Contactor stuck closed (welded contacts) or a shorted thermostat wire.** The outdoor unit runs even when the thermostat is satisfied or OFF. This is a control fault that needs prompt attention.
8. **Duct leakage or disconnected ducts.** Cooled air dumps into the attic or crawlspace instead of the living space.
9. **Undersized system or failing compressor/valves.** This shows up as long-term poor capacity even after the system has been fully serviced.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the air filter.** If it is gray or clogged, replace it with the correct size. Check monthly in peak summer.
- **Check the thermostat:**
  - Confirm it is set to COOL, the fan is set to AUTO, and the setpoint is realistic. A setting of 72–78°F is typical in extreme heat.
  - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or shows a low-battery icon.
- **Test whether it shuts off.** Set the thermostat 5°F above the current room temperature, or switch it to OFF, and wait a few minutes.
  - If the outdoor unit stops, the controls are working.
  - If the outdoor unit **keeps running**, turn it off at the outdoor disconnect or breaker and call a technician. Do not keep resetting it.
- **Open the supply vents and clear the returns.** Make sure furniture, rugs or curtains aren't blocking registers or return grilles.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove leaves, weeds and debris to about 2 feet around the cabinet, using your hands only. Do not open the unit.
- **Check the condensate line and drain pan area** for standing water. A clogged drain can trip a float switch, which may shut the unit down rather than make it run constantly. Clear an accessible drain line opening if your system has one.
- **Look for ice.** If the copper line at the outdoor unit or the indoor coil area is frosted, turn cooling OFF and set the fan to ON to thaw it. Then call a technician.
- **Reduce heat load.** On hot days, close blinds on sunny windows, and avoid using the oven or dryer during the afternoon.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirm the complaint.** The tech will measure the return and supply air temperatures. A temperature drop (split) of roughly 15–22°F is typical, depending on humidity. They will also compare indoor and outdoor conditions.
- **Check airflow.** This includes the filter, blower wheel, blower speed settings, static pressure and the evaporator coil.
- **Inspect the condenser coil.** The tech will check the outdoor coil for dirt and confirm the condenser fan is running correctly.
- **Check refrigerant with gauges.** They will compare superheat and subcooling against the target on the Trane unit's data plate or chart, typically subcooling for TXV systems. If the charge is low, they should **leak-search** rather than just add refrigerant.
- **Test the electrical controls.** This involves:
  - Checking whether the contactor drops out when the Y (cooling call) signal goes away.
  - Inspecting the contactor for pitted or welded contacts.
  - Checking the thermostat wiring for a short between R and Y.
- **Check thermostat calibration and placement.** On communicating systems, the tech will also review the stored diagnostics.
- **Evaluate the ducts and sizing if the equipment checks out.** That means a duct leakage inspection and, if needed, a Manual J load calculation.

**When reading a quote:** a "recharge" with no leak search or explanation is a red flag. So is a replacement recommendation made before anyone has checked airflow and charge.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs long, weak airflow at vents | Dirty filter | Replace the filter | Clean the blower wheel and evaporator coil if dirty |
| Runs all afternoon on very hot days, house is OK by evening | Normal heat load | Raise the setpoint a little, close blinds | None needed, or a load and duct check if it is chronic |
| Variable-speed unit runs nearly nonstop, home comfortable | Normal low-speed operation | None | None |
| Fan runs nonstop but compressor cycles | Thermostat fan set to ON | Set the fan to AUTO | None |
| Outdoor unit runs with thermostat OFF | Welded contactor or shorted thermostat wire | Shut off the disconnect or breaker | Replace the contactor, repair the wiring |
| Runs constantly, house slowly warms, ice on lines | Low refrigerant or airflow problem | Turn cooling off, run fan to thaw, check filter | Leak search and repair, recharge, airflow repair |
| Outdoor coil visibly matted with debris | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris around the outside of the unit | Clean the coil chemically |
| Thermostat reads wrong or display blank | Weak batteries or failing thermostat | Replace batteries, check settings | Diagnose and replace or relocate the thermostat |
| Some rooms hot, attic very cold or dusty | Leaky or disconnected ducts | Check that registers are open | Seal and repair the ducts |
