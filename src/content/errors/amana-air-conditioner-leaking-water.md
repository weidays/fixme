## What this code means

"Leaking water" is a symptom, not a diagnostic code. Neither Amana outdoor condensers nor their matched furnace or air handler controls display a code for condensate problems.

When your AC runs, the indoor evaporator coil pulls moisture out of the air. That water collects in a drain pan under the coil and leaves the house through a PVC drain line, sometimes with help from a condensate pump. A leak means that water isn't making it out.

Many installations include a float safety switch in the pan or drain line. When the water rises, the switch cuts power to the cooling call. The AC simply stops cooling, with no flash code. Once the water drains, the switch resets automatically.

A little water around the outdoor unit is usually not a problem. Condensation on the copper suction line is normal in humid weather. The concern is water at the indoor unit: around the furnace or air handler, from the ceiling below an attic unit, or on the floor.

This page covers only condensate leaks. If you also see ice on the coil or lines, read "Amana AC Frozen Evaporator Coil" first.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged condensate drain line.** Algae, sludge, dust, or insect nests block the line or its P-trap. This is the most common cause, especially in summer.
2. **Dirty air filter causing coil icing and meltoff.** Low airflow freezes the coil. When it thaws, more water comes off than the pan can handle, and some drips outside the pan.
3. **Failed condensate pump.** This applies to basement, closet, and attic installs that need a pump. The motor, the float, or the check valve fails, or the pump's tank fills with sludge.
4. **Disconnected, cracked, or poorly sloped drain piping.** A PVC fitting separates, the trap is missing or dried out, or the line doesn't slope downhill toward the drain.
5. **Rusted or cracked drain pan.** This is common on older metal secondary pans and some aging coil pans.
6. **Overflowing secondary (emergency) pan in an attic.** This means the primary drain has already failed. Water may come out of the secondary drain over a window, which is a warning sign.
7. **Low refrigerant charge or a blower problem causing freezing.** These are technician diagnoses. See the frozen coil page.
8. **Sweating ducts or uninsulated suction line.** Condensation drips from these in humid attics or crawlspaces. This is an installation or insulation issue, not a drain issue.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Stop the water first.** Set the thermostat to OFF, not just to a higher temperature. Mop up water and protect anything below the unit.
- **Check the air filter.** If it's gray or caked, replace it with the correct size, with the airflow arrow pointing toward the blower.
- **Look for ice.** If you see frost on the visible copper line, leave cooling OFF and set the fan to ON for 2–4 hours so it thaws. Don't chip at the ice.
- **Check the condensate drain where it ends.** Follow the PVC line to its outlet, usually outside near the foundation or into a floor drain. If it isn't dripping while the system runs, it's likely clogged. Clear debris from the exit.
- **Vacuum the drain line.** Place a wet/dry shop vac tightly over the outdoor end of the line and run it for 1–2 minutes. Clogs often come out this way.
- **Flush through the access port.** Many lines have a capped T-fitting near the indoor unit. Slowly pour in a cup of water, or a cup of vinegar diluted with water. Don't use bleach if your drain has PVC cement joints or the pan is metal and rusted.
- **Check the condensate pump, if you have one.** This is the small box with a tube, usually plugged into an outlet. Confirm it's plugged in and the outlet works. If the reservoir is full and the pump isn't running, the pump needs service or replacement. Call a technician; don't open the pump's electrical parts.
- **Check the visible secondary pan, if accessible.** In attic installs, look for standing water in the external pan under the unit, but only if you can reach it safely. Standing water confirms the primary drain is blocked.
- **Reseat panels and check the breaker.** Make sure the blower door is fully seated. If the system stopped because of a float switch, it should restart by itself once the water drains. You don't need to reset anything.

If the drain flows freely, the filter is clean, there's no ice, and water still appears, call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Find where the water is actually coming from: the coil pan, the drain connection, the pump, a duct, or the line set.
- Check the primary drain pan for cracks, rust, or debris, and confirm the coil is level so water flows toward the drain outlet.
- Clear the drain with a pump or nitrogen/CO₂ blowout, rebuild a missing or incorrect P-trap, and correct the slope (about 1/4 in per foot).
- Test the condensate pump's motor, float, check valve, and overflow safety switch wiring, and replace the pump if it's failing.
- Install or test a float safety switch so the next clog shuts cooling off instead of flooding.
- If icing was the cause, measure airflow, static pressure, blower operation, and refrigerant superheat/subcooling to find the root cause. Low charge means a leak search, not just adding refrigerant.
- Inspect duct and suction line insulation for condensation in humid spaces.

A good quote names the specific failed part. Be cautious about "add refrigerant" quotes when the problem is a clogged drain, and about coil replacement quotes when the pan wasn't inspected.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water around furnace or air handler; no drip at outside drain exit | Clogged drain line or trap | Shut off cooling, shop-vac the drain exit, flush the access port | Power-clear the line, rebuild the trap, add a float switch |
| AC stopped cooling, pan full, no error code | Float safety switch tripped by a clog | Clear the drain; the unit restarts once water drops | Verify switch wiring and placement |
| Pump box full of water, not running | Failed condensate pump | Confirm it's plugged in and the outlet works | Replace the pump and check valve, wire the overflow shutoff |
| Ice on copper line, then a flood after shutdown | Dirty filter / low airflow / low charge | Replace the filter, thaw with fan ON | Airflow and charge diagnosis, leak repair |
| Water dripping onto ceiling below an attic unit | Overflowing or rusted secondary pan | Shut off cooling, protect ceiling, check the pan if safely reachable | Replace the pan, clear the primary drain, add a float switch |
| Drips where a pipe joint separated | Disconnected or cracked PVC | Note the location, keep cooling off | Re-glue or replace the fitting, correct slope |
| Water leaks even with a clear drain | Cracked or rusted primary pan, unlevel coil | None beyond shutting off | Level the coil, replace the pan or coil |
| Water dripping from ducts or lines in attic or crawlspace | Condensation on uninsulated surfaces | Replace the filter, check registers are open | Insulate or seal ducts and suction line |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including labor:

- **DIY drain flush / filter:** $0–$40
- **Professional drain line cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Float safety switch installed:** $100–$250
- **Condensate pump replacement:** $150–$450
- **Drain line / trap repair or rerouting:** $100–$400
- **Secondary (emergency) drain pan replacement:** $150–$500
- **Primary drain pan replacement (when separate from the coil):** $300–$900. Many coil pans are built in, so it may mean replacing the coil.
- **Refrigerant leak search and repair (if icing):** $200–$1,500+. Cost depends on the leak's location and refrigerant type (R-410A or R-454B).
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,800+. Amana's parts warranty may cover the coil if registered, but labor usually isn't covered.
- **Duct or line insulation:** $150–$800

Water damage repairs to drywall, flooring, and mold remediation are separate and often cost more than the HVAC repair. Stop leaks quickly.
