## What this code means

Eight flashes on your Goodman furnace's diagnostic LED means an **igniter relay problem — check igniter**: the control board is not seeing the expected current draw through the hot-surface igniter circuit. In plain terms, when the board tries to energize the igniter, the current it expects isn't there.

This is a **control-circuit fault**, not a gas or flame problem. The failure lives in one of three places:

- The **hot-surface igniter** itself (cracked, open, or worn out)
- The **wiring, plug, or connector** feeding the igniter
- The **igniter relay on the control board** that switches power to it

Because every one of these repairs is inside the cabinet and involves line voltage, this is a **pro-level** code. The one thing a homeowner can safely do is a single power reset — everything else is technician work.

> Flash-code legends can differ between board revisions. Confirm the meaning against the label printed inside your furnace's blower-door before acting.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or cracked hot-surface igniter.** These are consumable parts. A hairline crack or an open element stops drawing current, and the board reports no igniter current — the single most common trigger.
2. **Loose, corroded, or damaged igniter wiring/connector.** A backed-out pin or chafed wire breaks continuity between board and igniter.
3. **Control board fault (igniter relay or related electronics).** The relay contacts that switch power to the igniter can stick or burn open so voltage never reaches a perfectly good igniter, or the board's current-sensing electronics can misreport the circuit. Both look identical from the homeowner's side — no glow, eight flashes — and both are fixed the same way: board replacement.

Standard practice is to verify the igniter and its wiring **before condemning the board**, so causes 1 and 2 should be tested and ruled out first.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should attempt:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Heat** and the setpoint is above room temperature. Replace the thermostat batteries if it uses them.
- **Breaker / furnace switch:** Make sure the furnace breaker is on and the wall switch by the unit hasn't been flipped off.
- **One reset:** Turn the furnace off at the breaker or switch, wait 30 seconds, and turn it back on to give the board a single clean ignition attempt.
- **Blower door:** Make sure the exterior blower-access panel is fully seated — the door switch must be closed for the furnace to run.

*Unrelated no-heat basics:* while you're there, confirm the air filter is clean and seated and that supply and return vents are open — neither causes an 8-flash code, but both cause no-heat complaints.

If the 8-flash code returns after that single reset, stop and call a licensed HVAC technician. **Do not open the cabinet, and do not repeatedly reset a unit that keeps locking out.**

## How a technician will diagnose it

A pro will work the igniter circuit in this order, so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Read the flash count and history** to confirm 8 flashes and rule out an intermittent fault.
2. **Power down and inspect the igniter** — visual check for cracks, then measure resistance with a multimeter. An open reading confirms a bad igniter.
3. **Check the igniter wiring and connectors** for continuity, corrosion, and secure seating at both the board and igniter ends.
4. **Measure board output voltage** at the igniter connector during the igniter-on period of a heat call. Voltage present but no current (no glow) confirms an igniter/wiring fault; no voltage at the connector points to the relay or board.
5. **Confirm the fix** by cycling a full heat call and verifying the igniter glows, gas lights, and flame proves without the code returning.

Expect a clear answer on whether the igniter, the wiring, or the board is at fault before any part is replaced.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 flashes, igniter never glows, no heat | Cracked/open igniter | One reset, then call a pro | Test resistance, replace hot-surface igniter |
| 8 flashes intermittent, sometimes heats | Loose/corroded igniter connector | One reset, verify panel seated | Inspect, re-pin or repair wiring/connector |
| Voltage present at the igniter connector during the igniter-on period, but no glow | Open or cracked igniter (not the board) | None — call a pro | Confirm open igniter by resistance, replace igniter |
| **No** voltage at the igniter connector during the igniter-on period | Igniter relay / control board fault | None — call a pro | Verify board output, replace integrated control board |
| Code returns after every reset | Unresolved igniter-circuit or board fault | Stop resetting, call a pro | Full circuit diagnosis; replace igniter, wiring, or board as found |
| No flash / no power at all | Breaker or door switch | Check breaker, seat blower door | Test door switch, fuse, transformer |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts plus labor:

- **Homeowner reset / seated panel:** **$0**
- **Hot-surface igniter replacement:** **$150 – $350** (part is often $20–$70; the rest is labor and diagnosis)
- **Igniter wiring / connector repair:** **$120 – $250**
- **Integrated control board replacement:** **$400 – $1,200** — the high end applies to two-stage and variable-speed/ComfortNet-style boards, tight or attic/crawlspace access, and after-hours or emergency calls
- **Diagnostic / service-call fee alone:** **$80 – $180**, usually credited toward the repair

Costs vary by region, furnace access, and whether your unit is under warranty. Goodman parts warranties can cover the igniter or board, leaving you the labor charge — but the extended coverage generally requires that the unit was **registered within the enrollment window after installation** and applies to the **original owner** at the original installation address. If it wasn't registered, expect the shorter base parts warranty instead.
