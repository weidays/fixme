## What this code means

This is not a flash-code fault — it is a performance complaint. Your York furnace is energized and burning, the blower is moving air, but the home never reaches the thermostat setpoint, so the furnace never shuts off. There is no lockout and no stored error on the control board in most cases; the furnace is doing exactly what it is told, it simply can't deliver enough usable heat.

On two-stage and modulating York furnaces (Affinity and many LX models — board design varies by model year), this very often means the furnace is stuck in **low stage** and never ramps to high fire. On single-stage units, it usually points to an airflow restriction, a duct or heat-loss problem, or reduced combustion heat output.

Because nothing has locked out, there is no auto-reset or power-cycle behavior to report — the unit will keep running until the thermostat is satisfied or power is removed.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow means the furnace heats air it can't push into the house, so the thermostat is never satisfied.
2. **Closed, blocked, or too few supply/return registers** — reduces delivered heat and can trip the high-limit, leaving the blower running without burner heat.
3. **Thermostat stuck in continuous fan or miswired for staging** — fan set to ON (not AUTO) feels like constant running; a staging thermostat that never calls for second stage leaves a two-stage York in low fire.
4. **Furnace locked in low stage (two-stage/modulating models)** — a failed stage-two pressure switch, a second-stage fault at the gas valve, or a staging control fault keeps the burner at reduced output.
5. **Leaky or disconnected ductwork** — heated air escapes into attics, crawlspaces, or garages before it reaches the rooms. The fix here is finding and sealing or reconnecting the ducts.
6. **Undersized equipment or high house heat loss** — a furnace that is too small for the home, or a home losing heat through poor insulation and air leaks, will run continuously in cold weather even when everything works. The fix is insulation and air-sealing work, or correctly sizing a replacement furnace — not a furnace repair.
7. **Dirty burners or partially blocked heat exchanger passages** — reduces combustion efficiency and heat transfer, so less heat reaches the air stream.
8. **Weak or dirty flame sensing** — worth noting mainly because homeowners often suspect it. A marginal flame-sense signal does **not** make the furnace run at reduced output; the control board has no gas-modulation authority tied to flame current. Instead, the burner lights and then drops out, the furnace retries, and after a set number of tries it goes to lockout. That shows up as cycling or a furnace that quits, not as steady low heat.
9. **Cracked or failing heat exchanger** — can cause limit trips and poor heat delivery, but the real concern is safety: a cracked exchanger can spill combustion gases, including carbon monoxide, into the air you breathe. If a cracked exchanger is suspected, the furnace should not be run until a licensed technician has inspected it, and you should have working CO alarms on every level of the home.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Replace the air filter.** If it looks gray or you can't see light through it, swap it. This resolves a large share of "runs but won't heat" complaints.
- **Check the thermostat.** Set the mode to HEAT and the fan to AUTO (not ON). Confirm the setpoint is actually higher than room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Walk the house and open registers.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by rugs, furniture, or drapes. Leave interior doors open to help return airflow.
- **Check the furnace switch and breaker.** Confirm the furnace switch (looks like a light switch near the unit) is on. At the panel, switch the furnace breaker fully OFF, then back ON once. Don't repeat it — if the furnace keeps locking out, leave it off and call a technician.
- **Look at the condensate drain** (high-efficiency models). A clogged drain can cause nuisance shutdowns; if you see standing water or overflow, clear the visible line or call a pro.
- **Seat the exterior blower-door panel.** With the furnace switch off, push the outer blower-door panel flat into place so the door switch makes contact. Do not open the burner compartment or remove any interior covers — that is technician work.

If these don't restore proper heat, the remaining causes are inside the cabinet and belong to a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

- Confirm the thermostat is actually commanding the correct stage and verify control wiring (W1/W2 or communicating data).
- Measure **temperature rise** across the furnace (return vs. supply) and compare it to the York rating-plate spec. High rise points to airflow restriction; low rise points to under-firing.
- Check the gas **manifold pressure** and verify the furnace reaches high stage on two-stage/modulating models, testing the stage-two pressure switch and the gas valve.
- Measure the **flame-sense microamp signal** and inspect/clean the flame sensor and burners.
- Inspect the blower wheel, motor amp draw, and capacitor for weak airflow.
- Perform static-pressure readings to identify duct restrictions and inspect the heat exchanger for cracks or blockage.

A good quote names what they measured — temperature rise, manifold pressure, flame signal — not just "it needs a part."

## Repair costs

- **Air filter:** $8–$30 DIY.
- **Thermostat (replacement/upgrade):** $25–$300 depending on model; staging thermostat needed for two-stage furnaces.
- **Flame sensor clean/replace:** $90–$250.
- **Stage-two pressure switch:** $150–$400.
- **Gas valve (staging fault):** $300–$700.
- **Blower capacitor:** $150–$350.
- **Blower motor:** $450–$1,800 installed; ECM/variable-speed motors used on Affinity two-stage and modulating units sit at the high end.
- **Duct sealing/repair:** $400–$1,500 depending on scope.
- **Burner cleaning / tune-up:** $100–$250.
- **Heat exchanger replacement:** $1,500–$3,500+ once parts and the labor to pull the furnace apart are counted — which is why replacing an older furnace is often the better value.

Simple airflow and thermostat fixes are frequently $0–$30. Costs climb quickly once staging components or the heat exchanger are involved.
