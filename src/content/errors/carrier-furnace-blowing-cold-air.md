## What this code means

"Blowing cold air" is not a single Carrier fault code — it is a symptom you can see and feel, and it usually points to one of several documented conditions on the control board. In every case the blower is running but the burners are either not lit, have shut off early, or the fan is running independently of a heat call.

On Carrier furnaces with an LED diagnostic light, the underlying reason often shows as a numbered status code at the control board. Many models have a sight glass on the blower door so you can see the LED without opening anything; some newer units report the status through the thermostat or user interface instead. Reading that code — and matching it to Carrier's own troubleshooting chart — is the fastest way to know whether you are dealing with an ignition lockout, a limit trip, or simply a thermostat setting.

Because the exact codes and reset behavior vary by board generation (Infinity, Performance and Comfort series differ), always confirm the flashing pattern against the label inside your blower compartment door.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat fan set to ON, not AUTO.** With the fan in ON, the blower runs continuously — including between heat cycles — so it pushes room-temperature or cool air. This is the single most common reason and is not a furnace fault at all.
2. **Ignition failure / lockout.** If the furnace tries to light but fails, the control eventually locks out. The blower may still run on its off-cycle, moving unheated air. This typically shows as a Carrier ignition or flame-proving code. Reset behavior depends on the board: many Carrier boards auto-reset after about three hours, while some require you to cycle power at the breaker — check the label inside your blower door.
3. **Flame sensor problem.** A dirty or failing flame sensor causes the burners to light briefly, then shut off when flame is not proven. The blower keeps running and delivers cooling air.
4. **High-limit / overheating trip.** A dirty filter or blocked airflow overheats the heat exchanger, tripping the limit switch. The burners shut off for safety while the blower runs to cool things down.
5. **No gas or interrupted gas supply.** If gas is off, the furnace goes through its ignition sequence with no flame; the blower may run its cycle and blow cold air.
6. **Failed gas valve or control board.** Less common, but a faulty gas valve or board can prevent the burners from firing while the blower still operates.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the thermostat.** Make sure it is set to HEAT and the temperature is above the current room reading. Set the fan to **AUTO**, not ON — this alone fixes a large share of "cold air" complaints.
- **Replace the thermostat batteries** if it is battery-powered and the display is dim or blank.
- **Check the air filter.** A clogged filter restricts airflow and can trip the high limit. Replace it if it is dirty.
- **Check the breaker.** If the furnace breaker is tripped or the service switch is off, reset it once.
- **Look at your supply and return vents.** Make sure they are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Check the condensate line and drain** on high-efficiency models — a clogged drain can trigger a safety shutoff. Clear a visible blockage at the drain opening only.
- **One reset only.** If the furnace is locked out, cycle power at the breaker once. If it locks out again, stop and call a technician — repeated resets are unsafe.

Everything else — fuses, door switch, wiring, flame sensor, igniter, gas valve, burners — is inside the cabinet and is technician work.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Read the LED status code at the control board and match it to Carrier's troubleshooting sequence.
- Watch a full heat cycle to see exactly where it fails — no ignition, flame drop-out, or limit trip.
- Test the flame sensor microamp signal and clean or replace it as needed.
- Check the igniter, inducer operation and pressure switch.
- Verify gas supply and gas valve output with a manometer.
- Measure temperature rise across the heat exchanger to confirm airflow and limit behavior.
- Test the control board relays and thermostat wiring for the heat call.

This lets you sanity-check a quote: a diagnostic that jumps straight to replacing the board without reading codes or testing the flame sensor is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows constantly, even between cycles | Fan set to ON | Set thermostat fan to AUTO | None needed |
| Burners never light, blower runs | Ignition lockout / no gas | Reset breaker once; verify gas is on | Test igniter, gas valve, board; clear lockout |
| Burners light briefly then quit | Dirty/failing flame sensor | None | Clean or replace flame sensor |
| Cold air after furnace runs a while | High-limit trip from low airflow | Replace filter; open vents | Diagnose airflow, inspect heat exchanger, test limit |
| Blank thermostat, no response | Dead batteries or tripped breaker | Replace batteries; reset breaker | Check thermostat wiring and power |
| Water pooling near furnace, cold air | Clogged condensate causing shutoff | Clear visible drain blockage | Clear line, test condensate switch |

## Repair costs

- **Thermostat setting or battery fix:** $0 DIY.
- **Air filter replacement:** $10–$40 DIY.
- **Flame sensor cleaning/replacement:** $80–$250.
- **Igniter replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Gas valve replacement:** $400–$800.
- **Control board replacement:** $400–$1,200, with Infinity-series communicating boards at the high end.
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90–$180, often applied toward the repair.

Costs vary by region, furnace model and whether parts are under warranty. Carrier furnaces often carry a parts warranty (frequently 10 years if registered), so ask whether your part is covered — you may only pay labor.

## Related codes

- **Carrier Furnace Code 22: Abnormal Flame-Proving Signal** — flame is sensed while the gas valve is de-energized, and the inducer keeps running until the flame clears. This is not a "clean the flame sensor" code; it can point to a leaking or slow-closing gas valve, so it is technician work.
- **Carrier Furnace Code 33: Causes, Fixes & Costs** — limit circuit faults, which cover not only a tripped high limit from restricted airflow but also an open flame rollout switch or a blocked vent.
- **Carrier Furnace Code 34: Ignition Proving Failure & Fixes** — burners lighting then dropping out, the usual code when a dirty or failing flame sensor is at fault.
- **Carrier Furnace Keeps Shutting Off: Causes, Fixes & Costs** — closely related no-heat behavior.
