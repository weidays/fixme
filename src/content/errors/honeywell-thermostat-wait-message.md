## What this code means

"Wait" is not a fault code. It is Honeywell's **compressor protection** notice. After your air conditioner or heat pump compressor shuts off, the thermostat makes it stay off for a minimum time, usually up to about 5 minutes. This gives the refrigerant pressures time to even out before the compressor starts again. Restarting a compressor too soon can stall it or damage it.

While Wait is displayed:

- The thermostat has already registered your call for cooling (or heat pump heating).
- It is holding that call on purpose. It will send the call to the equipment once the timer ends.
- The timer **resets on its own**. You do not need to power-cycle anything.

How this looks depends on the model:

- **Newer models** (T-series, Lyric) show the word "Wait."
- **Many older FocusPRO and VisionPRO models** flash "Cool On" or "Heat On" for the same purpose. Those are covered on separate pages.
- **Gas or electric heat with no heat pump:** compressor protection usually does not apply to the heat call itself. Still, the delay can show briefly after a mode change on some models.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal compressor protection after a cycle ended.** The system just shut off and you, or the schedule, asked for more cooling. This is expected and clears within about 5 minutes.
2. **Recent power interruption or thermostat restart.** Any of these restart the timer, so Wait appears even if the compressor was not running:
   - A power outage or a tripped breaker
   - Changing the batteries
   - A firmware update on Wi-Fi models
   - Removing the thermostat from its wallplate
3. **Rapid setpoint or mode changes.** Switching between Off, Cool, and Heat, or nudging the temperature again and again, can keep restarting the delay.
4. **The thermostat keeps rebooting from weak power.** Low batteries, or a marginal or missing C-wire on Wi-Fi models, can cause repeated restarts. Each restart re-triggers Wait. A sign of this is the screen dimming, flickering, or rebooting.
5. **Equipment short-cycling.** If the outdoor unit or furnace keeps shutting off early, the thermostat keeps entering protection. Wait then shows up often. The thermostat is reporting the problem, not causing it.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Wait 5 full minutes.** Do not touch any controls during that time. In most cases, Wait clears and the system starts.
- **Set the temperature once.** Make sure the mode is set correctly: Cool for AC, Heat for heat. The setpoint needs to be at least a couple of degrees beyond room temperature.
- **Replace the thermostat batteries** if your model uses them. Use fresh alkaline batteries. Expect one more Wait period after you swap them.
- **Check the breakers** for the air handler or furnace and for the outdoor unit. Also check the furnace's on/off switch. Reset a tripped breaker **once only**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** A clogged filter can cause short-cycling, which makes Wait appear more often.
- **Open the supply vents and return grilles** and clear anything blocking them.
- **Check the condensate drain line or pan** if you can see it. On many systems, a full pan or a tripped float switch shuts off cooling. Clear a visible clog at the drain line opening, or ask a technician to do it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Confirm that Wait clears normally after the delay and that the equipment then runs.
- Check the thermostat's power. They will measure voltage at R and C and look for a loose or missing C-wire. This matters most on Wi-Fi models that reboot.
- Review the installer settings. These include the equipment type and compressor protection or cycle settings on Pro models. They correct a setting only if it is wrong.
- If Wait keeps appearing because the system shuts off early, look for the cause of short-cycling. Common causes include:
  - Pressure or limit switch trips
  - The condensate float switch
  - Problems with the capacitor or contactor
  - Refrigerant charge
- Replace the thermostat only after power and wiring check out good.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wait shows, then clears within 5 minutes | Normal compressor protection | None, just wait | None |
| Wait appears after an outage or battery change | Timer restarted on power-up | Wait 5 minutes | None |
| Wait returns each time you adjust settings | Repeated setpoint or mode changes | Set once and leave it alone | None |
| Wait keeps cycling, and the screen dims or reboots | Low batteries or weak C-wire power | Replace the batteries | Test 24V, repair or add a C-wire |
| Wait appears often, and the system runs only briefly | Equipment short-cycling | Replace the filter, open vents, check the condensate line | Diagnose switches, capacitor, contactor, refrigerant |
| Wait clears but nothing runs | Equipment or wiring fault | Check breakers (one reset) | Wiring and control board diagnosis |
