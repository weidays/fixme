## What this code means

"Leaking water" is not a stored error code on most Bryant ductless systems. It is a symptom.

Every mini-split pulls moisture out of the air in cooling and dry modes. That water drips into a drain pan under the indoor coil, then runs out through a drain hose. The hose usually leaves through the same wall hole as the refrigerant lines and empties outdoors or into a pump.

When water shows up inside, one of three things is happening:

- **The drain path is blocked or badly sloped**, so the pan overflows.
- **Too much water is coming off the coil at once**, usually from ice melting.
- **Condensation is forming where it shouldn't**, such as on poorly insulated pipes.

Wall-mounted Bryant heads generally have no float switch. They keep running while they leak.

Ceiling cassettes and slim ducted units are different. They use a built-in lift pump with a float switch. When that switch trips, the unit stops cooling and may display a fault. The code varies by model, so check your service manual.

A tripped float switch clears once the water level drops. It does not need to be reset. If the root cause remains, it will trip again.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged condensate drain line.** Algae, slime, dust and insects block the hose. This is the most common cause on units more than a year or two old, especially in humid climates.
2. **Kinked, crushed, or uphill drain hose.** The hose runs through the wall with the line set. If it was taped above the pipes, sags inside the wall, or was pinched, water backs up. This is common on recent or DIY installs.
3. **Dirty filters or a dirty evaporator coil.** Low airflow can let the coil get too cold and frost. Water can also be blown off a grimy coil before it reaches the pan.
4. **Indoor unit not level, or tilted away from the drain side.** The mounting plate must be level or pitched slightly toward the drain outlet.
5. **Frozen coil from low refrigerant or a very low setpoint.** When the ice melts, the pan floods. See the Frozen evaporator coil page.
6. **Failed condensate pump or float switch** (cassette, ducted, or add-on pump installs only).
7. **Cracked drain pan or a loose drain connection** at the back of the unit.
8. **Sweating refrigerant lines or a missing/torn insulation sleeve** inside the wall or at the wall penetration. This shows up as a wet wall rather than drips from the unit.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the unit off** with the remote, then switch it off at the breaker or disconnect if water is near the outlet or wiring. Move belongings and put down towels.
- **Clean the filters.** Lift the front panel and slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry fully, and reinstall them. Do not go further inside than the filters.
- **Find the outdoor end of the drain hose.** Make sure it isn't buried in mulch, frozen, crushed, submerged in a puddle, or plugged with mud or insects. Clear the tip by hand, and trim it back if it sits under dirt.
- **Gently clear the open end.** A wet/dry vacuum held over the outdoor end for a minute or two often pulls the clog out. Don't push wires or stiff rods up the hose from inside.
- **Check the visible hose path.** Make sure the hose runs downhill the whole way you can see it, with no loops or upward bends at the wall hole.
- **Check your settings.** Very low setpoints (in the low 60s °F) in humid weather, or running on low fan, encourage freezing. Use Cool or Dry at a moderate setting.
- **Look for ice.** If you see frost on the coil behind the filters, switch to Fan only and let it thaw completely before running Cool again.
- **Reset once.** If a cassette or ducted unit stopped with a fault, turn the breaker off for a few minutes and back on, one time only. If it faults again, call a technician.

If the leak returns within a day or two, or water is coming from the wall rather than the unit, call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should do most of the following:

- **Pour water into the drain pan** and watch where it goes, to confirm flow, leaks and slope.
- **Flush or blow out the drain line** with nitrogen or a pump. Many will treat the pan with an algaecide or condensate pan tablet.
- **Check that the indoor head is level** on its mounting plate, and confirm the drain hose is attached on the correct side. Factory drain ports can be swapped left or right; the unused port must be plugged.
- **Inspect the coil and blower wheel** for dirt and mold. Many will recommend a coil and blower cleaning with a bag kit if the unit is fouled.
- **Measure superheat/subcooling or check pressures** against Bryant specs if they find icing. A low charge means a leak search, not just a top-up.
- **Test the condensate pump and float switch** on cassette and ducted units, and check the pump's wiring and discharge line.
- **Check line-set insulation** at the wall penetration.

Be wary of a quote that jumps to refrigerant recharge or a new indoor unit without first testing drain flow and level.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady drip from bottom of indoor head during cooling | Clogged drain line | Clear outdoor hose end; vacuum it out | Flush line, clean drain pan, treat for algae |
| Leak started right after install | Drain hose uphill/kinked, unit not level, wrong drain port | Check visible hose slope | Re-level unit, reroute hose, plug unused port |
| Water spits out of the air outlet | Dirty filters/coil or coil frosting | Clean filters, run Fan to thaw | Coil and blower deep clean |
| Ice on coil, then a big leak | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Clean filters, raise setpoint, thaw | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Cassette/ducted unit stops and shows fault, pan full | Failed condensate pump or float switch | One breaker reset | Replace pump/float, clear discharge line |
| Wet wall below or beside the unit | Sweating lines or drain leak inside the wall | Turn off, dry area | Re-insulate lines, repair drain connection |
| Leak even when drain flows freely | Cracked drain pan | None | Replace pan or indoor unit parts |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| DIY filter cleaning and clearing the outdoor drain end | $0 |
| Service call with drain line flush and pan treatment | $100 – $250 |
| Re-level indoor head and reroute drain hose | $150 – $400 |
| Deep coil and blower cleaning (wall unit) | $200 – $500 |
| Condensate pump replacement (cassette/ducted or add-on) | $200 – $500 |
| Drain pan replacement | $250 – $600 (parts availability varies by model) |
| Line insulation repair at the wall | $100 – $300 |
| Refrigerant leak search, repair and recharge | $400 – $1,500+ |
| Drywall/water damage repair | Separate; often $200 – $900 |

Check your warranty before paying out of pocket. Bryant parts warranties are commonly 10 years when the unit is registered. Labor and installation-related drain problems are usually the installer's responsibility.
