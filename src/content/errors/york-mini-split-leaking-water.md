## What this code means

"Leaking water" is a symptom, not a stored fault code. It means condensate from your York mini-split's indoor unit is not draining the way it should.

In cooling and dry modes, the indoor coil pulls moisture out of the air. That water collects in a drain pan. It then flows by gravity, or through a condensate pump, out the drain hose to the outside or to a drain.

When anything interrupts that path, water overflows. You will usually see it:

- Dripping from the bottom of the indoor head
- Running down the wall
- Spitting from the air outlet

Most wall-mounted York heads do not show an error for this. Models with a condensate pump and float switch, which is common on ceiling cassettes, may shut off cooling and flash a fault code. That behavior depends on the model.

There is nothing to reset. The leak stops once the cause is fixed.

If the unit is also flashing a light, see the York Mini-Split Blinking Light Error page. For ducted York central AC leaks, see York AC Leaking Water.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged condensate drain line.** Algae, dust, slime or insect nests block the drain hose or the drain pan outlet. This is the most common cause on ductless units.
2. **Kinked, crushed or poorly sloped drain hose.** The line must slope continuously downhill. Low spots, upward runs, or a hose pinched behind the unit or in the line-set cover can trap water.
3. **Dirty air filters.** Restricted airflow makes the coil colder. It can frost, then melt and overflow the pan. Clogged filters also send dust into the drain.
4. **Indoor head not level, or tilted away from the drain side.** Water pools at the wrong end of the pan. This is common right after installation or if the mounting plate shifts.
5. **Condensate pump failure, where one is fitted.** A failed pump, blocked pump reservoir, tripped float switch or disconnected discharge tube leads to overflow. This applies mainly to cassettes, ceiling units and installs where gravity drainage isn't possible.
6. **Low refrigerant charge (a leak).** Low charge freezes the evaporator coil. The ice melts in large amounts when the unit cycles off, which overwhelms the drain. Frost on the lines at the indoor or outdoor unit is a strong clue.
7. **Dirty or plugged indoor coil or blower wheel.** Debris restricts airflow and can splash water off the coil or blower. This can cause water spitting out of the air outlet.
8. **Cracked drain pan or loose drain fitting.** Less common. It is more likely on older units or after rough handling.
9. **Sweating on uninsulated lines or the wall penetration.** High humidity can cause condensation on poorly insulated refrigerant lines or fittings. This can look like a unit leak.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the unit off** with the remote. If water is near outlets or wiring, switch off the breaker or outdoor disconnect. Put down towels or a bucket.
- **Clean the filters.**
  - Open the front panel as described in your owner's manual.
  - Slide out the mesh filters and rinse them with lukewarm water.
  - Let them dry fully, then reinstall them.
  - Clean them every 2–4 weeks in cooling season.
- **Check the remote settings.** Very low setpoints with low fan speed in high humidity raise the risk of icing. Try a moderate setpoint with Auto or High fan.
- **Inspect the drain hose outside.**
  - Find where the drain line exits, usually near the line set or down the outside wall.
  - Make sure it isn't kinked, crushed, buried in mulch, under water or plugged with debris or insects.
  - Clear only the open end you can reach.
- **Check the visible slope.** Where you can see the drain hose, confirm it runs downhill with no sagging loops.
- **Look for frost.** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper lines, turn the unit off. Let it thaw with the fan only, if your model allows. Then call a technician. Do not chip away the ice.
- **Check whether the head is level.** Use a small level on top of the indoor head. If it is visibly tilted, the mounting plate needs adjustment. This is technician work.
- **Reseat the front panel** properly after cleaning the filters.

Do not remove the indoor unit cover beyond the filter panel. Do not pull out the drain pan or open a condensate pump. Do not pour chemicals into the unit or blow compressed air into the drain from inside. These can damage components or void the warranty.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent technician will usually:

1. **Confirm the source.** They check whether the water is coming from the drain pan, coil, fittings, pump or line-set sweating, not from roof or plumbing leaks.
2. **Inspect and flush the drain.** They remove the cover, check the drain pan and outlet, and clear the line with a wet/dry vacuum or pump. They then test flow by pouring water into the pan.
3. **Check slope and routing.** This includes the hose inside the wall penetration and the line-set cover. They correct sags, kinks or upward runs.
4. **Level the indoor head** by adjusting the mounting plate if needed.
5. **Test the condensate pump and float switch,** if fitted. They check power, operation and discharge, and clean the reservoir.
6. **Check airflow.** They inspect the coil and blower wheel and may recommend a coil and blower cleaning.
7. **Check the refrigerant if icing is present.** They measure pressures and temperatures, superheat and subcooling, against York specifications. If the charge is low, they leak-search before recharging. Topping off without finding the leak is not a proper repair.
8. **Inspect insulation** on the refrigerant lines and fittings for sweating.

A reasonable quote names the specific cause. Be cautious of quotes that jump to replacing the whole indoor unit without first clearing and testing the drain.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady drip from bottom of indoor head | Clogged drain line or pan outlet | Check and clear the exterior hose end; clean filters | Flush the drain line and pan; sanitize |
| Water runs down wall behind unit | Kinked or poorly sloped hose; head tilted | Check visible hose; check level | Re-route hose; level the mounting plate |
| Water spits from air outlet | Dirty coil or blower wheel; overflowing pan | Clean filters | Coil and blower wheel cleaning |
| Leak with frost on coil or lines | Low refrigerant or restricted airflow | Turn off; thaw; clean filters | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Cassette stops cooling, shows error, pan full | Condensate pump or float switch failure | Turn off; note the error code | Test, clean or replace the pump or float switch |
| Leak only in high humidity, wet line insulation | Sweating lines or fittings | Note where the water appears | Repair or replace insulation |
| Small leak that persists after clearing the drain | Cracked pan or loose drain fitting | Turn off; contain the water | Replace pan or fitting |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges and vary by region and access:

- **Filter cleaning:** $0 DIY
- **Service call or diagnostic:** $75–$200
- **Drain line flush or clear:** $100–$250
- **Re-routing or re-sloping the drain hose:** $150–$400, more if wall access is required
- **Leveling the indoor head or adjusting the mounting plate:** $100–$300
- **Coil and blower wheel deep cleaning:** $150–$450 per head
- **Condensate pump replacement:** $200–$500 installed
- **Drain pan replacement:** $200–$600, depending on parts availability
- **Line-set insulation repair:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak search, repair and recharge:** $400–$1,500+, depending on leak location and refrigerant type. York mini-splits typically use R-410A; newer models may use different refrigerants.

Check your warranty before paying out of pocket. York warranties typically require registration and professional installation. Parts may be covered even when labor is not.
