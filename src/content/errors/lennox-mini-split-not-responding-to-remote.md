## What this code means

"Not responding to remote" is a symptom, not a Lennox diagnostic code. It usually looks like one of these:

- The indoor head does not beep when you press a button.
- The display does not change.
- The unit ignores power, mode or temperature commands.

The fault sits somewhere in the signal path:

1. The handheld remote sends an infrared (IR) signal.
2. The IR receiver on the indoor head picks it up.
3. The indoor control board acts on the command.

This is different from a unit that accepts commands but won't cool or heat. If the head beeps and the display changes but the air is wrong, see Lennox Mini-Split Not Cooling or Not Heating instead.

How the unit behaves depends on the cause:

- **Remote problems** clear as soon as the remote is fixed.
- **Glitches from a power event** often clear after one power cycle at the breaker.
- **A receiver or board fault** will not clear on its own and needs a technician.

If the head is also flashing lights, the board may be reporting a fault. In that case, use Lennox Mini-Split Blinking Light to interpret it.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dead or weak remote batteries.** This is by far the most common cause. A dim or blank remote screen is a strong clue.
2. **Remote settings or lock.** Several Lennox remotes have a child lock or key lock. Some have a °F/°C setting or a mode that makes the remote seem dead, or the remote is paired to a different unit or address. Remote features vary by model.
3. **Blocked or overpowered IR path.** The signal can be blocked by:
   - furniture, curtains or distance from the head
   - bright direct sunlight on the receiver window
   - some LED or fluorescent lighting that interferes with the signal
4. **Control glitch after a power event.** A surge or brownout can leave the indoor board unresponsive until power is cycled.
5. **Faulty remote.** The remote may have been dropped, have liquid damage, or have corroded battery contacts or worn button contacts.
6. **Failed IR receiver or display board on the indoor head.** These are technician-replaced parts.
7. **Indoor control board fault, or loss of power to the indoor head.** This includes interconnect wiring or communication issues between the indoor and outdoor units. These are technician-only and are sometimes accompanied by blinking-light codes.
8. **Wi-Fi module or wired controller conflict** (on units with these accessories). On some setups, a wired wall controller or app control can override or disable IR remote input. This varies by model and installation.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

**1. Replace the remote batteries.** Use fresh alkaline batteries (usually AAA) and check that the polarity is correct. If the battery contacts have light corrosion, wipe them gently with a dry cloth.

**2. Check the remote screen.** Look for a lock icon. If you see one, unlock the remote following your owner's manual; it is often a two-button press. Then confirm the remote is set to a valid mode and temperature.

**3. Test the IR signal with a phone camera.** Point the remote at the camera and press a button. A visible flicker on screen means the remote is transmitting.

**4. Improve the line of sight.** Stand within about 20 feet, aim directly at the indoor head, and remove any obstructions. If the head is in direct sun, close the blinds or test at night. Try turning off nearby LED or fluorescent lights.

**5. Check the breaker or disconnect.** Look for a tripped breaker or a switched-off disconnect. If the indoor display is completely dark, the unit may not have power at all.

**6. Do one power cycle.** Turn the mini-split breaker off, wait 3–5 minutes, then turn it back on and try the remote. Do this only once. If the breaker trips again, stop and see Lennox AC Breaker Keeps Tripping.

**7. Try the manual button.** Use the manual or emergency button only as your owner's manual describes. If the unit runs from the button but ignores the remote, the problem is in the remote or the receiver.

**8. Check for other controllers.** If you use the Lennox app or a wired controller, check whether it is controlling the unit or has locked out the remote.

Do not open the indoor head's covers or electrical compartment. Do not attempt to test or replace the receiver or the control board yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically:

- Confirm the remote transmits, using an IR test or a known-good remote.
- Check the indoor display and any fault or flash codes on the indoor and outdoor units.
- Verify line voltage at the outdoor unit and power to the indoor head across the interconnect wiring.
- Inspect the display and receiver board connector and the wiring harness for loose or corroded pins.
- Test or swap the IR receiver/display board.
- Check the indoor control board for power, burn marks and communication. Communication is checked with the manufacturer's diagnostic procedure for your series.
- Check any Wi-Fi module or wired controller settings and dip switches that affect remote input.

**Questions to ask before you approve a quote:**

- Did they confirm the remote itself works before quoting a board? A board quote without that step is a red flag.
- Is the part an OEM part matched to your model number?

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remote screen blank or dim | Dead batteries | Replace the batteries | None needed |
| Remote screen shows a lock icon | Child or key lock on | Unlock per your manual | None needed |
| Phone camera shows no IR flicker | Faulty remote | Replace with an OEM remote | None needed |
| IR flicker visible, no beep, display normal | Receiver or display board fault | One power cycle | Diagnose and replace the receiver/display board |
| Works at night, not in direct sun | IR interference | Block sun or lights, then retest | Relocate the head only if the problem is chronic |
| Indoor display completely dark | No power or board failure | Check the breaker and disconnect | Test supply and interconnect, check the board |
| Unresponsive after a storm or outage | Control glitch or surge damage | One power cycle | Test and replace the control board if needed |
| No remote response, lights flashing | Board-reported fault | Note the flash pattern | Diagnose per the error code |
| Remote ignored, but app or wall controller works | Controller priority setting | Check app and controller settings | Reconfigure the controller or module |

Typical US ranges:

|---|---|
