## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. The large fan on top of your Rheem heat pump moves air across the outdoor coil.

- **In cooling mode**, the fan gets rid of the heat pulled from your house.
- **In heating mode**, the fan pulls heat from the outdoor air.

When the fan stops while the compressor keeps running, the system loses most of its capacity. Head pressure and compressor temperature also climb quickly.

**First, rule out normal defrost.** In heat mode, Rheem heat pumps stop the outdoor fan during defrost while the compressor keeps running. You may see steam and hear a whoosh when the reversing valve shifts. Defrost ends on its own within minutes, and the fan restarts.

If the fan stays off outside of defrost, or never runs in cooling mode, there is a fault.

**How reset works depends on the model:**

- **Single- and two-stage units** usually have no fan-specific lockout. The compressor may trip on its internal overload, which resets by itself once it cools.
- **Variable-speed (communicating) units** may log a fan or high-pressure fault on the outdoor board. Some of these faults hold until power is cycled.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor (or the fan side of a dual capacitor).** This is the most common cause on single- and two-stage Rheem units with PSC fan motors. Typical signs:
   - The fan hums or won't start.
   - The fan may spin if nudged.
   - The capacitor may look bulged on top.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor.** Possible failures include worn bearings that seize, an open winding, or a motor that cuts out on its thermal overload when hot. On variable-speed units, an ECM fan motor or its control module can fail.
3. **Contactor or wiring fault.** Possible faults include:
   - Pitted contacts that feed the compressor but not the fan circuit.
   - A burned or loose fan lead.
   - Wiring chewed by rodents.
4. **Defrost/outdoor control board fault.** On many Rheem heat pumps, the defrost board switches the fan off during defrost. A failed relay on that board can leave the fan off all the time. On communicating units, the outdoor board drives the ECM fan directly.
5. **Physical obstruction.** Possible obstructions include:
   - Ice buildup around the blade in winter (see the defrost-related pages).
   - Sticks or debris.
   - A blade loose on its shaft, which spins the motor but not the blade.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Wait out a possible defrost.** In heat mode, watch for 15 minutes. If the fan restarts on its own, the system is fine.
- **Check thermostat settings.** Confirm the mode (heat or cool) and the setpoint. Replace the thermostat batteries if the display is dim or blank.
- **Check the breaker and outdoor disconnect.** Look for a tripped breaker at the panel and check the disconnect box near the unit. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** A clogged filter won't stop the outdoor fan directly. It does make the system work harder, and a technician will ask about it.
- **Look, don't touch.** From a distance, look through the top grille for sticks, leaves or heavy ice around the blade. Clear debris from around the outside of the unit by hand, keeping about 2 feet clear. Never reach through the grille.
- **Shut the system off** at the thermostat if the fan stays still while the unit hums or runs. This protects the compressor. Use emergency heat if needed.

Anything behind the service panel is technician work. That includes the capacitor, contactor, motor, wiring and control board, and it carries live high voltage even when the system is off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

1. **Confirm the call.** The technician checks for 24V at the contactor coil and for line voltage at the unit, and confirms the unit isn't in defrost.
2. **Test the capacitor.** They discharge the capacitor and measure it with a meter. A reading more than about 6–10% below its rating, or a bulged case, means it should be replaced.
3. **Test the motor.** They check:
   - Winding resistance.
   - Whether the windings are shorted to ground.
   - Whether the shaft turns freely by hand with the power off.
   - Amp draw once the motor is running.

   A hot motor that won't start may be tripped on its internal overload.
4. **Inspect the contactor and wiring.** They check for pitted contacts, voltage drop across the contactor, and burned or loose fan leads.
5. **Check the defrost/outdoor board.** They confirm whether the board's fan relay is sending voltage to the motor. On variable-speed units, they read stored fault codes from the board or a diagnostic tool.
6. **Check the compressor.** If the fan has been off for a while, they confirm the compressor isn't damaged and check pressures.

**Sanity check for quotes:** A capacitor alone is a common, inexpensive fix. Be cautious of a quote for a new motor or board without meter readings to back it up.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan stops in heat mode, steam rises, fan restarts within minutes | Normal defrost cycle | None, this is normal | None |
| Fan hums but doesn't turn; compressor runs | Weak or failed capacitor | Turn off the system and call | Test and replace the capacitor |
| Fan silent, compressor runs | Failed motor, bad fan wiring, or failed board relay | Turn off the system and call | Meter the motor and board output; repair wiring or replace the part |
| Fan runs briefly, then stops when hot | Motor overheating on thermal overload, failing bearings | Turn off the system and call | Replace the motor (often with a new capacitor) |
| Nothing outside runs at all | Tripped breaker or disconnect, contactor, or no 24V signal | Check the breaker once, check thermostat batteries | Diagnose the contactor and control circuit |
| Motor sounds like it's running but blade doesn't turn | Loose blade on the shaft | Turn off the system and call | Re-secure or replace the blade |
| Blade blocked by ice or debris | Obstruction, defrost problem | Clear the area around the outside; don't reach in | Clear the obstruction and diagnose defrost |
| Variable-speed unit shows a fault on the outdoor board | ECM motor or board fault | Note the code, cycle power once | Read the codes; replace the ECM motor or board |
