## What this code means

Three flashes on your York furnace control board means the **pressure switch is open while the inducer motor is running**. The pressure switch is a safety device that confirms the inducer draft motor is actually pulling combustion gases through the heat exchanger and out the vent. When the inducer starts, the switch should close. If it stays open — or opens during the cycle — the board reads 3 flashes and refuses to light the burners.

This is a **venting and draft-proving fault**, not an ignition or flame problem. York's documented triggers point to the vent path, the inducer, the tubing that connects the switch to the inducer, condensate backup, or the switch itself. Because this code guards against carbon monoxide spillage, it is a pro-level diagnosis.

On most York boards the fault clears automatically once the switch closes and a normal cycle completes; on some board revisions repeated faults lead to a soft lockout that requires cycling power. Check the legend printed inside your unit's access panel to confirm which behavior applies.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Blocked or restricted vent/intake terminal** — snow, ice, leaves, insect nests, or a bird screen clogging the exterior PVC vent or intake pipe. This is the most common real-world trigger and a leading safety concern.
2. **Clogged condensate line or trap** — a backed-up condensate drain lets water accumulate and hold the pressure switch open (common on high-efficiency condensing models).
3. **Kinked, cracked, or disconnected pressure-switch tubing** — the small rubber hose between the inducer housing and the switch can slip off, split, or fill with condensate.
4. **Weak or failing inducer motor** — a worn inducer can't generate enough draft to close the switch even when the vent is clear.
5. **Faulty pressure switch** — a switch that is out of calibration or has failed open will not close at the correct pressure.
6. **Blocked inducer housing or drain port** — debris or condensate inside the inducer assembly restricts airflow.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to Heat and above room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it's dirty — but understand this is **routine maintenance, not a fix for this code**. A dirty filter restricts supply airflow, while the pressure switch monitors the combustion draft path. Changing the filter is very unlikely to clear a 3-flash fault, so don't burn a heating cycle waiting to see if it does.
- **Breaker / furnace switch:** Verify the furnace breaker and the service switch by the unit are on. You may do **one** reset by cycling power if the unit is locked out — do not repeat resets.
- **Outdoor vent and intake pipes:** Go outside and check the PVC vent/intake terminals. Clear away snow, ice, leaves, nests, or anything blocking the openings.
- **Condensate line:** If you see standing water or a clogged condensate drain, clear the visible drain line and empty any condensate pump per its instructions.

If the code returns after these checks, stop and call a licensed technician. Do **not** open the cabinet, bypass the switch, or attempt repeated resets.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Inspect the full vent and intake path for restrictions, sagging pipe, or improper slope.
- Measure the pressure switch's actuation with a manometer, comparing readings against the switch's rated inches of water column.
- Check the pressure-switch tubing for cracks, kinks, water, or disconnection.
- Test the inducer motor's amp draw and confirm it produces adequate draft.
- Inspect the inducer housing and drain port for blockage or condensate.
- Verify the condensate trap and drain are clear and correctly plumbed.
- Confirm the switch actually closes and holds during a live call for heat.

This sequence lets you sanity-check a quote: a good tech proves whether the fault is the vent, the tubing, the inducer, or the switch before recommending a part.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inducer runs, no ignition, 3 flashes | Blocked vent/intake terminal | Clear snow, ice, nests from outdoor pipes | Inspect and correct full vent path |
| 3 flashes on humid or freezing days | Clogged condensate line/trap | Clear visible condensate drain, empty pump | Clean trap, inducer drain port |
| Intermittent 3 flashes | Cracked/loose pressure-switch tubing | None — internal | Replace or reseat tubing |
| Inducer sounds weak or noisy | Failing inducer motor | None — internal | Test amp draw, replace inducer |
| Fault persists with clear vent | Faulty pressure switch | None — internal | Manometer test, replace switch |
| Standing water at furnace base | Blocked condensate drain | Clear visible drain line | Clear trap and inducer drain |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts plus labor; regional pricing varies):

- **Clearing a blocked vent or condensate line (DIY):** $0
- **Service call / diagnosis:** $90 – $200, with after-hours, weekend, and emergency visits higher. Many contractors credit the diagnostic fee toward the repair if you approve the work — ask when you book.
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Pressure-switch tubing replacement:** $80 – $180
- **Condensate trap/drain cleaning by a tech:** $100 – $200
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $400 – $1,200 installed, depending on model. OEM assemblies for two-stage and variable-speed condensing furnaces sit at the top of that band.

If a simple vent or drain blockage is the culprit, your only cost may be the service call. Replacing the inducer is the most expensive likely outcome, so a four-figure quote on a high-efficiency two-stage unit is not automatically out of line — ask the tech to show you the draft and amp-draw readings that led to the diagnosis.

## Related codes

- **York Furnace 2 Flashes: Pressure Switch Stuck Closed** — the opposite fault, where the switch is closed when it should be open. If you're seeing both codes intermittently, mention it to your technician, as it points to a switch or tubing problem rather than a vent restriction.
