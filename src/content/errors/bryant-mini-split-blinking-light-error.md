## What this code means

A blinking light on a Bryant mini-split is not one single fault. The indoor unit's controller is reporting a diagnostic code through its LED. Depending on the series, you'll see one of these:

- A repeating number of flashes on the operation or run LED
- Alternating flashes between the run and timer LEDs
- A two-character code on the front display, such as E1 or P4

Bryant ductless equipment is built on platforms shared with Carrier/Midea. Code tables differ between series and model years, so the same blink count can mean different things on different units. To read it correctly:

1. Count the flashes between pauses.
2. Note which LED is blinking.
3. Look up that pattern in the installation or service manual for your exact model number. The model number is on the label on the side of the indoor unit.

**Reset behavior:**
- Protection codes (high or low pressure, overcurrent, high discharge temperature) often clear automatically after a timed restart, then lock out if they keep repeating.
- Sensor, EEPROM and communication faults usually stay active until the cause is fixed and power is cycled.

**Normal blinking that isn't a fault:** a slow blink in heat mode during defrost or preheat.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Indoor/outdoor communication error.** This is one of the most common blinking-light codes on Bryant ductless systems. Likely causes:
   - Loose or miswired interconnect wiring
   - A damaged wire from vibration or pests
   - Outdoor power loss
   - A failing outdoor or indoor board
2. **Temperature sensor (thermistor) fault.** The room, indoor coil, outdoor coil, outdoor ambient or discharge sensor reads open or shorted.
3. **Outdoor unit protection trip.** The usual triggers are:
   - High pressure, often from a dirty outdoor coil or blocked airflow
   - Low pressure or a low refrigerant charge
   - High discharge temperature
   - Compressor overcurrent
4. **Indoor fan motor fault.** The controller can't detect the fan speed it expects.
5. **IPM/inverter module or outdoor board fault.** Typical causes are power surges, voltage problems or component failure.
6. **EEPROM or main control board fault.** This is less common and is usually confirmed only after the items above are ruled out.
7. **Condensate float or pump fault.** This applies only to cassette and ducted heads with a condensate pump.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Write down the pattern first.** Note the flash count, which LED is blinking, and whether it happened in heat or cool mode. This saves diagnostic time.
- **Rule out defrost.** In heat mode, wait about 10 minutes. If the unit resumes heating and the blinking stops, it was normal defrost.
- **Check the remote.** Replace the batteries, then confirm the mode and setpoint are correct.
- **Clean the indoor filters.** Lift the front panel, slide out the mesh filters, rinse them, and let them dry fully before reinstalling.
- **Check the breaker and disconnect.** Confirm the breaker hasn't tripped and the outdoor disconnect is on.
- **Do one power reset.** Turn the unit off with the remote, switch off power for about 5 minutes, then restore it. If the code returns, do not keep resetting.
- **Check airflow around the outdoor unit.** Clear leaves, snow and debris, and keep about 2 feet of clearance. Don't open the unit.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Make sure the drain hose outlet is not kinked or blocked, and look for water dripping from the indoor head.
- **Check the panels.** The indoor front panel and outdoor access panels should be seated and closed.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically:

1. Read the code against the service manual for your exact model. Many Bryant heads can recall stored fault history through the remote or a check function.
2. Measure supply voltage at the outdoor unit.
3. Check the communication signal across the interconnect terminals (often labeled S or 1/2/3), and inspect the wiring for damage.
4. Measure thermistor resistance and compare it to the manufacturer's temperature-resistance chart.
5. Inspect and clean the outdoor coil.
6. Check refrigerant pressures, superheat and subcooling with gauges if a protection code points that way.
7. Test the fan motor feedback, the compressor windings and the IPM module.

**What to expect on the quote:** a board should be quoted only after wiring, voltage and sensors are verified. A recharge should come with a leak search, not just a top-off.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slow blink in heat mode, then heating resumes | Defrost or preheat (normal) | Wait 10 minutes | None needed |
| Blinking with no outdoor operation | Communication error or outdoor power loss | Check breaker and disconnect, one reset | Test interconnect wiring and boards |
| Blinking, unit runs then stops | Protection trip (pressure, temperature, overcurrent) | Clean filters, clear outdoor airflow | Clean coil, check charge, test compressor and IPM |
| Blinking, poor or no cooling or heating | Sensor fault or low charge | Note the code, clean filters | Test thermistors, leak search and repair |
| Blinking, indoor fan won't run | Indoor fan motor or board | One reset | Test fan motor feedback, replace motor or board |
| Blinking on cassette or ducted head, water present | Condensate pump or float fault | Check the drain outlet | Clean or replace the pump or float switch |
| Blinking after a storm or surge | Board or IPM damage | One reset | Diagnose and replace the damaged board |
