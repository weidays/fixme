## What this code means

"Leaking water" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. On most American Standard wall-mounted heads, nothing will appear on the display. You may see water dripping from the bottom of the indoor unit, running down the wall, or spitting from the air outlet.

The indoor coil pulls moisture from the air during cooling and dry modes. That water collects in a drain pan and leaves through a drain hose. The hose drains either by gravity or through a condensate pump.

A leak means water is not getting out of the pan, or more water is forming than the drain can carry away. If your system uses a condensate pump with a safety float switch, the unit may shut off cooling when the float trips. Some setups also flash an error. Whether this happens, and whether the unit restarts by itself once the water clears, depends on the pump and how it was wired. Check your installer's documentation.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged or slow condensate drain.** Algae, slime, dust or insect nests block the drain pan outlet or hose. This is the most common cause by a wide margin.
2. **Dirty air filters.** Low airflow makes the coil too cold. It frosts, then the ice melts in a flood that overwhelms the pan. Dirty filters also let dust into the pan, which feeds clogs.
3. **Kinked, crushed or poorly sloped drain hose.** The hose must fall continuously from the unit. Problems include a kink behind the head, a sag that traps water, or an outlet sitting in a puddle or frozen outdoors.
4. **Indoor head not level, or tilted toward the front.** Water pools at the wrong end of the pan and spills over. This often follows a loose or shifted mounting plate.
5. **Failed or tripped condensate pump.** This applies only to installs where the drain cannot run by gravity.
6. **Dirty indoor coil or blower wheel.** Debris redirects condensate, and the blower can throw droplets out of the air outlet.
7. **Low refrigerant charge (leak).** The coil freezes, then the ice melts. You will usually also notice weak cooling.
8. **Very high indoor humidity or condensation on the outside of the unit.** Common with doors or windows left open. It can also come from poorly insulated refrigerant lines sweating at the wall penetration.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Switch the unit off at the remote** if water is reaching outlets, electronics or floors. Put a towel or pan underneath.
- **Clean the filters.** Lift the front panel and slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry fully, and reinstall. Do not touch anything behind the filters.
- **Check the visible drain hose.** Look where it exits outside or into a drain.
  - Is it kinked, crushed, sagging, buried in mulch, or blocked at the tip?
  - Clear debris from the outlet end. Make sure the hose slopes downhill all the way.
  - In winter, check that the outlet isn't frozen.
- **Flush the drain from the outside end.** If the outlet is accessible, you can gently clear a clog with a wet/dry vacuum held to the hose end. Do not push wires up the line or remove the indoor cover to get at the drain pan.
- **Look at the unit's level.** If the head is visibly tipped forward or to one side, note it for the technician. Do not remount it yourself; the refrigerant lines are attached.
- **Close windows and doors** while cooling to reduce humidity.
- **If the unit is iced or stopped with a pump fault,** turn it off at the breaker or disconnect for a few minutes. Then restart it **once**. If the leak or shutdown returns, stop and call a pro.
- **Make sure the front panel and filters are fully seated** after you check them.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Inspect the drain pan and outlet. Clear and flush the drain line, often with a pump-up sprayer or nitrogen. They may also treat the pan with an algaecide tablet.
- Check the drain slope. Look for traps or sags behind the wall, and check line routing.
- Check that the mounting plate is level. Re-level or re-secure the head if needed.
- Test the condensate pump and float switch, if one is installed. Replace the pump if it has failed.
- Clean the evaporator coil and blower wheel with a mini-split cleaning bag kit.
- Measure temperatures and pressures to check refrigerant charge. If the charge is low, they will leak-search before recharging.
- Check line-set insulation and the wall sleeve for sweating.

A quote that jumps straight to "recharge" without a leak search, or to a new unit for a plain drain clog, deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady drip from bottom of head in cooling mode | Clogged drain line or pan | Clean filters; clear the outside hose outlet | Flush drain and pan, treat for algae |
| Water drips only from one end | Head not level | Note the tilt; don't remount | Re-level mounting plate |
| Leak follows ice on the coil | Dirty filters or low refrigerant | Clean filters; restart once | Check charge, find leak, repair |
| Water spits from the air outlet | Dirty coil or blower wheel, overfull pan | Clean filters | Deep-clean coil and blower |
| Unit stops cooling and water backs up | Failed or tripped condensate pump | Check the pump is plugged in; restart once | Test or replace pump |
| Water on the wall near the line set | Sweating lines or poor insulation | Reduce humidity | Re-insulate or seal lines |
| No water coming out of the outside hose | Kinked, crushed or frozen hose | Straighten or clear the visible hose | Re-route drain line |
