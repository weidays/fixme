## Stop — emergency conditions first

This code can be caused by **flame rollout**, a combustion fault that can produce carbon monoxide. Before you do anything else, check for these signs:

- **Flames visible outside the burner box** or licking out around the furnace cabinet
- A **burning, hot-metal or soot smell**, or soot marks around the furnace
- A **gas smell** anywhere near the furnace or in the house
- A **carbon monoxide alarm sounding**

**If any of these are present:** do not open panels, do not press any reset button, and do not attempt the safe checks below. Shut the furnace off at the furnace switch or its breaker **only if you can do so without approaching the flames or lingering in the area**, then get everyone out of the house and call your **gas utility's emergency line or 911 from outside**. Do not go back in until the utility or fire department says it is safe. If anyone feels dizzy, nauseated or has a headache, leave immediately and treat it as a medical emergency.

If none of those signs are present, continue below.

## What this code means

Four flashes on the diagnostic LED of an American Standard furnace control means an **open temperature limit circuit** — either the high-limit switch or a rollout switch has opened. Both are safety devices that watch for overheating.

- The **high-limit switch** opens when the air moving through the heat exchanger gets too hot, almost always because airflow is restricted. It typically **auto-resets** once the furnace cools and airflow returns.
- A **rollout switch** opens when flames "roll out" of the burner box instead of drawing cleanly into the heat exchanger. This is a **manual-reset** device and will **hold** until a technician presses the button — but only after finding why it tripped.

Because the code covers both an airflow problem and a combustion problem, the fix ranges from a simple filter change to serious repair. On some board revisions the legend differs, so confirm the 4-flash meaning on the label inside your blower door.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Restricted airflow from a dirty air filter** — the most common trigger. A clogged filter chokes return air and lets heat build up until the high-limit opens.
2. **Closed, blocked, or too few supply/return registers** — furniture, rugs, or shut vents reduce airflow the same way a dirty filter does.
3. **Dirty blower wheel or a failing blower motor/capacitor** — the blower can't move enough air, so the heat exchanger overheats. (Technician work.)
4. **Restricted or collapsed ductwork / undersized returns** — chronic airflow starvation that trips the limit on longer heat cycles.
5. **A weak or out-of-spec high-limit switch** — a limit that opens below its rated temperature. (Technician diagnosis.)
6. **Flame rollout from a plugged heat exchanger or blocked flue** — trips the manual-reset rollout switch; a serious combustion fault requiring a pro. See the emergency section above.
7. **Loose wiring or a faulty control board** in the limit circuit — less common, diagnosed after airflow and switches are ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do — and only if none of the emergency signs above are present:

- **Replace the air filter** if it looks gray or clogged. This alone clears many 4-flash events. Match the size printed on the old filter or the filter rack.
- **Check your registers and vents** — open every supply and return, and move any rugs or furniture blocking them.
- **Set the thermostat** to Heat with a call for heat, and if it's battery-powered, install fresh batteries.
- **Cycle power once** at the furnace switch or breaker to attempt a single reset — **but do not do this if there is any burning smell, soot odor, gas odor, or visible scorching**, since power-cycling an active rollout condition just repeats the trip. If the code clears and stays clear, great. If four flashes return, stop and call a pro.
- **Look at the condensate line** (on high-efficiency models) for standing water or blockage that could affect operation.
- Make sure the **blower door panel is fully seated** — the door switch must be closed for the furnace to run.

Do **not** open the burner compartment, press the rollout reset button, or bypass any limit switch. Repeated resets on an overheating furnace can crack the heat exchanger.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. Confirm the flash code and read the board's fault history.
2. Measure **temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the data-plate spec — high rise confirms an airflow problem.
3. Inspect the filter, blower wheel, and duct static pressure; check blower motor amp draw and the run capacitor.
4. Test the **high-limit and rollout switches** with a multimeter for continuity and correct trip points.
5. Inspect the **heat exchanger and flue** for cracks, soot, or blockage if the rollout switch tripped.
6. Check limit-circuit wiring and connections, and test the control board only after mechanical causes are ruled out.
7. Correct the root cause, then reset any manual-reset rollout switch and verify a full, safe heat cycle.

If a quote jumps straight to a control board or heat exchanger without checking airflow and temperature rise first, ask why.

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges for 2026, parts and labor:

- **Air filter replacement (DIY):** $10–$40
- **Opening vents / clearing registers (DIY):** $0
- **Blower wheel cleaning:** $150–$300
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$350
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150–$350
- **Rollout switch replacement:** $150–$400
- **Duct modification for airflow:** $300–$1,000+
- **Control board (IFC) replacement:** $400–$1,200 installed
- **Blower motor replacement:** $450–$1,800 (variable-speed ECM motors at the top of that range)
- **Cracked heat exchanger:** $1,500–$3,500 (often a case for full furnace replacement)

Many 4-flash cases end at the cost of a filter. The expensive outcomes appear when overheating has already damaged the blower, board, or heat exchanger.
