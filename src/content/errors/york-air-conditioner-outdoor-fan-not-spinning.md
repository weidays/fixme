## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. It means the condenser fan on top of your York outdoor unit isn't turning while the system is calling for cooling. Without it, the outdoor coil can't reject heat. Cooling drops off, and the compressor overheats.

Most York condensers with PSC fan motors have no diagnostic display for this problem. Some higher-tier and variable-speed units (for example, Affinity models with an ECM fan and communicating or inverter control) may show a fan or high-pressure fault on the outdoor board. What you see depends on the model.

If the fan is stopped and the compressor is running or humming, switch the thermostat to OFF to protect the compressor.

This page covers York **air conditioners**. Heat pump owners should see the separate heat pump page, since defrost control also affects fan operation there.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor.** This is the most common cause on PSC-motor York units. The fan side of the dual capacitor fails, so the motor hums or stays still. The fan may spin if pushed. Heat accelerates this failure.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor.** Possible failures include seized bearings, an open winding, or a motor that trips its internal thermal overload and stops after running a while.
3. **Contactor problems.** Pitted or stuck contacts, or a coil that doesn't pull in. If the contactor doesn't close, neither the fan nor the compressor runs. If one leg is burnt, the fan can lose power while other loads behave oddly.
4. **No power or no call for cooling.** Causes include a tripped breaker, the outdoor disconnect switched off, a blown disconnect fuse, a thermostat set wrong or with dead batteries, or a broken low-voltage wire. In this case the whole outdoor unit is usually silent, not just the fan.
5. **Physical obstruction.** Debris, a bent blade, or a loose blade hub on the motor shaft.
6. **Wiring or control faults.** Examples include a burnt or loose fan wire, or rodent damage. On units with outdoor control boards or ECM motors, the cause may be a failed fan relay, board output, or ECM module.
7. **Safety lockout.** On models with pressure switches or diagnostic boards, a high-pressure trip (often *caused* by the fan failing) can lock out the outdoor unit. The lockout holds until power is cycled.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL with the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is blank or weak.
- **Breakers and disconnect:** Check the AC breaker in the main panel. Reset it once if it's tripped. Confirm the outdoor disconnect handle or pull-out is in the ON position. If the breaker trips again, leave it off and call a technician.
- **One power reset:** Turn the system off at the thermostat and the breaker for 5 minutes, then restore power. Do this only once. If the fan still doesn't spin, stop.
- **Air filter:** Replace a dirty filter. A clogged filter won't stop the outdoor fan, but it adds strain and can confuse the diagnosis.
- **Look, don't touch:** From outside the unit, check for sticks, leaves, or debris on the top grille, and clear the area around the unit to at least 2 feet. Do not reach through the grille or poke the blade.
- **Panels:** Make sure the service panel on the outdoor unit is fully seated.
- **Shut it off:** If the fan stays stopped while the compressor hums or runs, set the thermostat to OFF and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Verify line voltage (240V) at the disconnect and 24V at the contactor coil during a cooling call.
- Inspect the contactor for pitting and confirm it pulls in. Measure voltage across its load side.
- Discharge and test the run capacitor with a meter rated for microfarads. Compare the reading to the label rating, which typically has a ±6% tolerance. A bulging or leaking capacitor gets replaced outright.
- Spin the motor shaft by hand with power off to check for seized bearings. Measure the winding resistance and test for a short to ground.
- Check the fan wiring and connectors for burning or looseness. On ECM or variable-speed units, check board outputs, fan communication, and any stored fault codes.
- After the repair, confirm normal amp draw on the fan and compressor, and check that operating pressures are reasonable. This helps rule out compressor damage from overheating.

When reading a quote, look for a measured capacitor value, motor amp or winding readings, and a clear statement of which part failed. "Replaced everything" without test results is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan still, compressor humming or running | Failed fan side of the run capacitor, or a seized motor | Turn the thermostat OFF | Test and replace the capacitor or fan motor |
| Fan spins after being pushed (seen by the tech) | Weak run capacitor | Don't push the blade yourself | Replace the capacitor |
| Fan runs for a while, then stops | Motor overheating on its thermal overload; failing bearings | Clear debris around the unit; turn the system OFF | Replace the fan motor (and usually the capacitor) |
| Whole outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, disconnect off, thermostat issue, or contactor not pulling in | Check the thermostat, batteries, breaker, and disconnect; reset once | Test the 24V signal, contactor, and fuses |
| Buzzing at the unit, nothing turns | Bad contactor or capacitor | Turn power OFF | Replace the contactor or capacitor |
| Grinding, scraping, or wobbling blade | Bent blade, loose hub, or obstruction | Clear visible debris from the outside only | Replace or tighten the blade; check the motor shaft |
| Fan stopped, fault light on outdoor board (some models) | High-pressure lockout, ECM or board fault | One power reset | Read the fault code; repair the fan, board, or ECM |

## Repair costs

Typical US prices, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic or service call | $80 – $200 |
| Run capacitor (dual) replacement | $120 – $350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150 – $400 |
| PSC condenser fan motor (often with capacitor) | $350 – $750 |
| Fan blade replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Wiring repair | $100 – $300 |
| ECM / variable-speed fan motor (Affinity and similar) | $700 – $1,500 |
| Outdoor control board | $400 – $1,200 |

Expect after-hours or peak-summer premiums. If the compressor was damaged from running without the fan, that is a far larger repair, often $1,800–$4,000+.
