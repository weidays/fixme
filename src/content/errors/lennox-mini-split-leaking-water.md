## What this code means

"Leaking water" is a symptom, not an error code. Lennox mini-splits generally do not show a fault code for a drain problem. The exception is some cassette and ducted heads with a float or pump switch, which may stop cooling or show a fault. What fault appears depends on the model, so check your indoor unit's manual. On a standard wall-mounted head, the unit simply keeps running while the water overflows.

When the unit cools, the indoor coil pulls moisture out of the air. That water collects in a drain pan inside the head. It then leaves through a drain hose that runs either:

- through the wall to the outside by gravity, or
- to a small condensate pump.

Water coming out of the indoor unit means one of three things:

- The water cannot leave the pan.
- The water is missing the pan.
- There is more water than the drain can handle, usually from a coil that iced up and then melted.

There is nothing to reset. Once the cause is fixed, the leak stops.

This page covers ductless indoor heads only. For central systems, see Lennox AC Leaking Water.

Severity is rated "pro" for two reasons. Most of the fixes happen inside the head or on the line set. And a leak that goes unnoticed causes expensive damage to the wall and to the electronics.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged drain line or drain pan.** Algae, dust and slime build up and block the outlet. This is the most common cause, especially on units that are a few years old.
2. **Dirty air filters.** Restricted airflow makes the coil run too cold. It frosts, then melts faster than the pan can drain. It also sends dust into the pan, which feeds clog #1.
3. **Drain hose kinked, crushed, sagging, or sloped wrong.** This is often an installation problem. The hose must slope downward the whole way. A dip or a section that rises traps water. Exterior hoses can also get crushed behind the line-set cover.
4. **Indoor head not level.** If the head tilts away from the drain-outlet side, water pools at the wrong end and spills. Lennox installation instructions call for the head to be mounted level or with a slight pitch toward the drain.
5. **Failed or clogged condensate pump.** This applies to installs where gravity drainage isn't possible, and to cassette or ducted heads. Signs are a pump that hums but doesn't move water, a stuck float, or a clogged pump reservoir.
6. **Dirty coil or blower wheel.** Heavy buildup diverts water past the pan, or the blower throws droplets out through the vanes.
7. **Low refrigerant charge.** This causes repeated coil icing and melting. It is less common, but it is the most expensive cause. See Lennox Mini-Split Not Cooling if cooling is also weak.
8. **Leaking drain connection or poorly insulated lines.** Sweating from uninsulated or damaged refrigerant-line insulation can drip near the wall penetration. It looks like a leak from the unit.
9. **Frozen exterior drain outlet in winter.** If water drains out of the hose while outdoor temperatures are below freezing, it can freeze at the hose end and block it. Water then backs up and overflows the indoor pan.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Turn the unit off with the remote.** Then switch off the breaker or disconnect if water is near any outlet or wiring. Put a towel and a pan under the head.
2. **Clean the filters.** Lift the front panel as the owner's manual shows, then slide out the mesh filters.
   - Rinse them with lukewarm water and let them air-dry completely before you reinstall them.
   - Do this every 2–4 weeks during the cooling season.
3. **Look for ice.** With the panel open, look at the coil without touching it. If you see frost, leave the unit off, or in fan-only mode if your remote has it, for a few hours so it thaws. Keep the pan in place.
   - Do not chip, scrape or pick ice off the coil. The fins and tubing are easy to damage.
4. **Follow the drain hose outside.** Make sure the end isn't buried in mulch, sitting in a puddle, blocked by debris or a wasp nest, or frozen. Clear what you can by hand.
   - Gently straighten any visible kinks or sags you can reach without taking anything apart.
   - Do not pull on the hose where it enters the wall.
5. **Check the condensate pump, if you have one and it is outside the unit.** Make sure it is plugged in and its reservoir isn't overflowing. If it is full and silent, or hums without pumping, call a pro.
6. **Check your settings.** A very low setpoint, such as 62°F, combined with low fan speed in humid weather encourages icing. Use Auto fan or a higher setpoint.
7. **Run a short test.** Restart in cool mode and watch for 30–60 minutes.
   - If it drips again, turn it off and book service.
   - Do not pour bleach, drain cleaner or vinegar into the head's drain pan. Chemicals and pressure can damage the pan or push water into the electronics.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Checks the head is level with a torpedo level, and checks the hose slope from the head to the outlet.
- Removes the front cover and inspects the drain pan, drain outlet and coil. They flush the drain with low-pressure water or use a vacuum pump at the exterior end. They should not blow high-pressure air back toward the head.
- Inspects the blower wheel and coil for buildup. If either is dirty, they quote a deep cleaning, which usually means a bib-style cleaning bag and a coil-safe cleaner.
- Measures temperatures and pressures. If the coil was icing even with clean filters, they check the refrigerant charge and look for a leak. Federal leak-repair requirements target larger-charge equipment, not typical home mini-splits, but finding and fixing the leak before adding refrigerant is still best practice, and a reputable tech will do it.
- On pump installs, tests the pump motor, float switch and discharge line.
- Checks the line-set insulation and the wall penetration for sweating.

**Red flag:** you get a quote for "add refrigerant" with no explanation of why the charge is low, or for a whole new head when the drain hasn't even been flushed.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady drip from the bottom of the head in cooling mode | Clogged drain pan or line | Clean filters, check the exterior hose end | Flush the drain and clean the pan |
| Water drips from the indoor head after the unit shuts off (defrost water from the outdoor unit is normal and is not this) | Iced indoor coil melting | Clean filters, thaw in fan mode, raise the setpoint | Check airflow, check charge, repair the leak |
| Droplets blowing out of the louvers | Dirty blower wheel or coil | Clean filters | Deep clean the coil and blower |
| Water drips from one end only | Head not level, or drain on the wrong side | Watch where it drips and note it for the tech | Re-level the mounting plate or re-route the drain |
| No water from the exterior drain hose | Kink, sag, blockage or frozen outlet | Clear the outlet and straighten visible kinks | Repair or replace the hose, fix the slope |
| Pump reservoir full, or pump humming | Failed condensate pump or stuck float | Confirm the pump is plugged in | Clean or replace the pump |
| Dripping where the lines enter the wall | Sweating lines or a bad drain joint | Note where it happens | Re-insulate the lines, reseal the joint |
| Weak cooling plus leaking | Low refrigerant | Turn the unit off and call | Find and repair the leak, then recharge |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges. They vary by region and by how easy the head is to reach.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter cleaning (DIY) | $0 |
| Replacement filters (DIY) | $15–$40 |
| Service call and diagnosis | $90–$200 |
| Drain line flush and pan cleaning | $120–$300 |
| Deep coil and blower cleaning (per head) | $250–$600 |
| Re-level the head or re-slope or replace the drain hose | $150–$400 |
| Condensate pump replacement | $200–$450 installed |
| Line-set insulation repair | $100–$300 |
| Refrigerant leak search, repair and recharge | $400–$1,500+ (depends on the leak's location and the refrigerant type) |
| Drywall and mold remediation from a long-term leak | $300–$2,000+ |

Annual maintenance, which includes a drain flush, usually runs $100–$250 per system. It prevents most of these calls.
