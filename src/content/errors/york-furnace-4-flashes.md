## What this code means

Four flashes on a York furnace control board means the **high-limit switch (or an auxiliary limit) has opened**. This safety switch is mounted near the heat exchanger and blower. It trips when the furnace runs hotter than its rated temperature — almost always because not enough air is moving across the heat exchanger to carry the heat away.

When the limit opens, the board shuts off the gas valve and runs the blower to cool things down. On most York boards, the fault **clears automatically once the switch cools and closes again**, and the furnace will try to run. If the airflow problem isn't fixed, it will overheat and trip repeatedly — you'll get short, incomplete heating cycles.

This is a *symptom* code: the limit switch itself is usually fine and is protecting the heat exchanger. The real fix is restoring proper airflow. Because chronic overheating can damage the heat exchanger, this is rated a **pro** issue if the simple airflow checks below don't resolve it.

Legend note: flash-code meanings vary slightly by board revision. Confirm 4 flashes = open limit against the legend printed inside your blower door.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. A restricted filter starves the blower of return air, so heat builds up until the limit trips.
2. **Closed, blocked, or too few supply/return registers** — furniture over vents, closed dampers, or an undersized duct return reduces airflow.
3. **Dirty blower wheel or evaporator (A-coil)** — buildup on the blower or the coil above the furnace chokes airflow. (Technician cleaning.)
4. **Blower motor running slow or failing** — a weak capacitor, worn motor, or wrong speed tap moves too little air. (Technician diagnosis.)
5. **Collapsed, disconnected, or undersized ductwork** — restricts return or supply air. (Technician.)
6. **Weak or failing limit switch itself** — an aging switch can open below its rated temperature. Replaced only after airflow is confirmed good. (Technician.)

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do:

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or older than 1–3 months, swap it for the correct size. This resolves a large share of 4-flash calls.
- **Open and unblock all vents.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and clear of rugs, furniture, or boxes. Don't close off more than one or two rooms.
- **Check the thermostat.** Set it to Heat and a few degrees above room temperature. Replace thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Confirm the blower door is fully seated.** Never run the furnace with the front panel off or ajar — push it firmly back into place before restoring power.
- **Cycle power once.** Turn the furnace switch (or breaker) off for 30 seconds, then on. Let it run one full cycle. **Do this only once** — repeated resets on an overheating unit are unsafe.

If the code returns after a fresh filter and open vents, stop and call a technician. Do **not** open the cabinet or bypass the limit switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Read the flash code and any stored history on the board.
- Measure **temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the rating-plate spec — high rise confirms restricted airflow.
- Inspect and measure airflow: filter, blower wheel condition, coil cleanliness, duct static pressure.
- Test the **blower motor and run capacitor**, and verify the correct speed tap for heating.
- Check the **limit switch continuity and trip point** with a multimeter; replace it only if it opens below spec with airflow confirmed good.
- Inspect the **heat exchanger** for cracks caused by repeated overheating.

You can sanity-check a quote: if a tech proposes replacing the limit switch without first checking airflow and temperature rise, ask why — the switch is usually the messenger, not the culprit.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furnace heats then shuts off, 4 flashes | Dirty filter restricting airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Verify temp rise, confirm airflow |
| Weak airflow from registers | Closed vents / dirty blower wheel | Open all registers | Clean blower wheel & coil |
| Short heating cycles, blower runs long | Blower motor slow / bad capacitor | None | Test motor, capacitor, speed tap |
| Trips even with clean filter | Duct restriction or failing limit | None | Measure static pressure, test/replace limit |
| Code returns after one reset | Chronic overheating | Stop, call pro | Inspect heat exchanger for cracks |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor unless noted):

- **Air filter (DIY):** $10–$40
- **Service call / diagnostic:** $90–$180
- **Blower wheel or coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Blower run capacitor:** $150–$300
- **Limit switch replacement:** $150–$350
- **Blower motor, PSC:** $450–$900
- **Blower motor, ECM (variable-speed):** $700–$1,800
- **Duct modification for restriction:** $200–$1,000+
- **Heat exchanger inspection:** often included in diagnostic; replacement is major ($1,500+) and may favor furnace replacement

Most 4-flash calls end at a filter change or a cleaning — the expensive repairs apply only when a motor, duct, or heat exchanger problem is confirmed.
