## What this code means

"Blowing warm air" is a symptom, not a Goodman fault code. It differs from the related pages in one way: the air is **warmer than the room**, not just weakly cool.

- **Fan runs but no cold air** covers air at roughly room temperature.
- **Running but not cooling** covers a system that cools, but not enough.

Truly warm supply air points to a few specific situations:

- **The indoor blower is running while the outdoor unit is not.** In summer, ducts in attics and garages heat up. When the blower pushes that air out, the first few minutes (or the whole cycle) feel warm.
- **A heat source is running during a cooling call.** This could be furnace burners or electric heat strips in the air handler.
- **Hot unconditioned air is entering the system.** This usually comes through a disconnected or leaking return duct in the attic.

Standard Goodman condensers (GSX, GSXN, GSXH) have no diagnostic display, so this symptom produces no code. ComfortBridge communicating models (GSXC/GSZC) may store a fault on the outdoor board. What is logged and how it clears varies by board, so have a technician read it rather than guessing. Since this is a symptom rather than a lockout, there is nothing to auto-reset.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat setting or wiring issue.** The thermostat may be set to HEAT or the fan set to ON. A failed or miswired thermostat may not be sending the cooling (Y) call, or may be calling heat (W) at the same time.
2. **Outdoor unit not running.** Common reasons include:
   - a tripped breaker or pulled outdoor disconnect
   - a failed run capacitor
   - a pitted or failed contactor
   - a condensate safety float switch that has cut the cooling call

   See "Compressor not running" and "Outdoor fan not spinning" for the full breakdown.
3. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** The outdoor unit runs, but the coil can't absorb heat. Supply air ends up near or above room temperature, especially in an attic install.
4. **Return duct leak or disconnect in the attic.** The system draws in 120°F+ attic air, which overwhelms the coil.
5. **Heat running during cooling.** Possible sources:
   - a stuck electric heat sequencer or relay in the air handler
   - a furnace gas valve that didn't close
   - a control board fault energizing heat

   This is less common but important because it wastes energy and can overheat equipment.
6. **Compressor failure.** The compressor may have failed internally, be stuck on its overload, or have a failed start component.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:**
  1. Set the mode to COOL and the fan to AUTO, not ON.
  2. Lower the setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature.
  3. Replace the batteries if the display is dim, blank, or shows a low-battery icon.
- **Listen at the outdoor unit:** Check whether the fan is spinning and the compressor is humming. Look only, and don't remove panels or touch anything inside.
- **Breakers:**
  1. Check the panel breakers for both the outdoor unit and the furnace or air handler.
  2. Reset a tripped breaker **once**.
  3. If it trips again, stop and see "Breaker keeps tripping."
  4. Make sure the outdoor disconnect is fully seated or switched on.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it's dirty. A clogged filter can freeze the coil, and after that the system may blow poorly cooled air.
- **Condensate:** Look at the drain pan and line near the indoor unit. If the pan is full, the safety float switch may have shut off cooling. You can clear the line at the access port with a wet/dry vacuum from outside the drain line. See "Leaking water."
- **Registers and panels:** Open all supply registers and make sure nothing blocks the returns. Confirm the furnace or air-handler doors are firmly seated.
- **Feel for heat:** If the air is clearly hot, like heating mode, set the thermostat to OFF and call a technician. Do not open the furnace or air handler.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician will typically work through these steps:

1. **Verify the call:** Measure 24V at Y (cool) and confirm there is **no** voltage at W (heat) during a cooling call. They'll check thermostat wiring at the furnace or air-handler board and the outdoor unit.
2. **Outdoor unit electrical:** Test supply voltage, contactor coil and contacts, run capacitor (microfarads against the rating), and compressor windings and overload.
3. **Read fault codes** on ComfortBridge outdoor boards, if equipped.
4. **Refrigerant performance:** Measure superheat and subcooling against Goodman's charging chart for that model. If the charge is low, they should leak-search with an electronic detector or bubble test, not just top off.
5. **Air side:** Measure the temperature split between supply and return (roughly 16–22°F is normal) and check the return-side temperature for attic air leaks. They'll inspect ducts, the coil, and the blower.
6. **Heat source:** Confirm the burners are off or the heat strips are de-energized during cooling. They may test the sequencers/relays or the gas valve.

Red flag: a quote to "add refrigerant" with no leak search, or a compressor replacement without capacitor and contactor tests first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, disconnect off, float switch tripped | Reset breaker once, check disconnect, clear condensate line | Test contactor, capacitor, low-voltage circuit |
| Warm air, outdoor unit hums but fan/compressor won't start | Failed capacitor or stuck compressor | Turn system OFF | Test and replace capacitor or start components; evaluate compressor |
| Air hot like heating mode | Thermostat calling heat, stuck heat sequencer or gas valve | Check thermostat mode; if it persists, turn system OFF | Check W circuit, sequencers/relays, gas valve, board |
| Warm air only with fan set to ON | Blower circulating attic-heated duct air between cycles | Set fan to AUTO | None needed unless it persists |
| Outdoor unit runs, air only slightly warm, ice on line | Low refrigerant or restricted airflow | Replace filter, open registers | Leak search, repair, recharge to chart |
| Outdoor unit runs, warm air, hot return air | Return duct leak in attic | Note symptoms only | Seal or reconnect return duct, check duct system |
| Blank thermostat | Dead batteries or no 24V power | Replace batteries | Check transformer, fuse, wiring |
