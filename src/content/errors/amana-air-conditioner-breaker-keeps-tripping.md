## What this code means

"Breaker keeps tripping" is not a code that shows on an Amana display. It means the circuit breaker feeding your outdoor condenser is cutting power. That happens because it detects one of two things:

- **Overcurrent:** the unit is drawing more amps than the circuit is rated for.
- **A short or ground fault:** current is leaking somewhere it shouldn't.

Breakers trip to prevent wire overheating and fire. Treat repeated trips as a real electrical fault.

How it behaves:

- **No auto-reset.** The breaker stays off until it is reset by hand.
- **When it trips tells you something:**
  - Instantly when you reset it: usually a dead short.
  - Seconds after the compressor starts: a starting or locked-compressor problem.
  - After 10–30 minutes of running: overheating or high current draw.

Note the timing for the technician. It speeds up diagnosis.

This page covers the electrical trip only. If the unit runs but doesn't cool, or cycles on and off without tripping anything, see the related pages listed at the bottom.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor.** A bad capacitor makes the compressor or fan motor struggle and pull high current. This is a very common failure on Amana condensers.
2. **Burned or pitted contactor, or loose or corroded wiring.** Arcing contacts and loose lugs overheat and can short to the cabinet.
3. **Wiring rubbed through or chewed.** Vibration and rodents can damage insulation inside the condenser, creating a ground fault.
4. **Condenser fan motor failing.** A seized or shorted fan motor draws excess current. A fan that doesn't move air also raises system pressure, which overloads the compressor.
5. **High head pressure from a dirty condenser coil or restricted airflow.** The compressor works harder and draws more amps until the breaker trips.
6. **Weak or undersized breaker.** Old breakers trip below their rating. Some installs also use the wrong breaker size, which should match the nameplate MOCP.
7. **Hard-starting compressor (locked rotor).** The compressor pulls locked-rotor amps on every start attempt.
8. **Shorted or grounded compressor windings.** This is the most serious cause and usually means the compressor or the whole condenser must be replaced.
9. **Inverter models only (AVXC20):** a fault in the inverter drive or control board can also trip the breaker. These units have their own diagnostic codes for this.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Reset the breaker once.** Turn the thermostat to OFF first. Then switch the breaker fully to OFF and back to ON.
  - Wait about 5 minutes. Many thermostats and Amana controls build in a compressor delay anyway.
  - Then set the thermostat to COOL.
  - If it trips again, **leave it off and call a technician.** Do not keep resetting it.
- **Check the outdoor disconnect.** This is the switch box near the condenser. Make sure it is fully seated and the cover is closed. Don't open the wiring section.
- **Replace a dirty air filter.** A clogged filter alone rarely trips a breaker, but it adds strain on the system.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove leaves, grass clippings and covers, and keep about 2 feet of clearance on all sides. Look for obvious damage, but don't reach inside.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace its batteries, and make sure it isn't set extremely low on a very hot day.
- **Watch for warning signs, and call right away if you notice any:**
  - A burning smell or scorch marks
  - Buzzing at the condenser with no fan movement
  - A breaker that feels hot

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent technician should do the following. Use this list to sanity-check what you're told:

1. **Inspect the breaker and circuit.** They should check wire size and the breaker rating against the nameplate MOCP and MCA.
2. **Megohm (insulation) test the compressor**, plus a resistance test across its common, start and run terminals, to check for grounded or shorted windings. Ask for the readings if you're told the compressor is bad.
3. **Test the capacitor** with a microfarad meter against the rating printed on it.
4. **Inspect the contactor and wiring.** They look for pitting, burned lugs, chafed insulation and rodent damage.
5. **Measure amp draw** on the compressor and fan motor while the unit runs, and compare it with the nameplate RLA and FLA.
6. **Check the condenser fan motor** and the cleanliness of the condenser coil. If the unit can run, they may check refrigerant pressures.
7. **Read the board fault history on inverter models.**

A compressor diagnosis without the megohm and winding tests is a red flag. Get a second opinion before paying for a compressor or a new system.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trips instantly when reset | Dead short in wiring or a grounded compressor | Reset once, then leave it off | Megohm test, wiring inspection, repair or compressor replacement |
| Trips a few seconds after the compressor tries to start | Bad capacitor, hard-start or locked compressor | Leave it off after one reset | Test or replace the capacitor, add a hard-start kit, test the compressor |
| Hums or buzzes, then trips | Failed capacitor or seized motor | Turn it off at the breaker | Replace the capacitor or fan motor |
| Runs 10–30 minutes, then trips | High amp draw from a dirty coil, failing fan or weak breaker | Clear debris around the unit, replace the filter | Measure amps, clean the coil, replace the fan motor or breaker |
| Burning smell, scorch marks | Arcing contactor or overheated connection | Leave it off and call promptly | Replace the contactor and repair the terminals or wiring |
| Old breaker feels warm, trips intermittently | Worn or undersized breaker | Don't swap it yourself | Verify sizing and replace the breaker |

## Repair costs

These are typical US prices, including the service call:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic or service call | $90–$200 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Hard-start kit | $200–$400 |
| Contactor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Wiring repair (chafed, rodent or burned lugs) | $150–$500 |
| Breaker replacement (HVAC tech or electrician) | $150–$350 |
| Condenser fan motor | $350–$800 |
| Condenser coil cleaning | $100–$250 |
| Inverter control board, AVXC models | $600–$1,500+ |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800–$3,500+ |
| Full condenser replacement | $3,500–$7,500+ |

Compressor replacement is often covered parts-only under Amana's warranty when the unit is registered (lifetime compressor coverage on many models). Labor and refrigerant are usually extra, so check your registration before you decide.
