## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a stored error code. It is a symptom. The indoor head runs, or seems to, but the room does not get cold.

Ductless systems have their own causes for this problem. They have no ducts and no central filter. They are controlled by an infrared remote and use inverter-driven compressors with electronic expansion valves. The indoor and outdoor units talk to each other over a communication wire.

If your York mini-split also shows a code or flashing lights on the indoor display, write it down. The meaning of those codes varies by series, so look it up in your model's manual. Whether a fault clears on its own or holds until power is cycled also depends on the series and the fault.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Wrong mode or setpoint on the remote.**
   - Fan, Dry or Heat mode will not cool.
   - Auto mode with a high setpoint may barely cool.
   - Dead remote batteries mean the setting you think you sent never reached the unit.
2. **Clogged washable filters on the indoor head.** These filters load up fast and choke airflow across the coil.
3. **Dirty indoor coil or blower wheel.** Built-up dust and biofilm on the squirrel-cage wheel sharply cut airflow. This is common in heads that are several years old.
4. **Blocked outdoor unit.** Debris, vegetation, snow guards or a fence too close can block the outdoor coil and starve it of airflow, so it cannot reject heat.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** Flare connections at the indoor head or at the outdoor service valves are the most common leak points on ductless systems.
6. **Faulty temperature sensor (thermistor).** A bad room, coil or outdoor sensor makes the board think the setpoint is satisfied, or slows the compressor down.
7. **Electronic expansion valve (EEV) or communication fault.** The outdoor unit may not respond correctly to the indoor head. Multi-zone systems are more prone to this.
8. **Inverter board or inverter compressor failure.** The fan runs but the compressor never ramps up.
9. **Undersized or poorly placed head.** The unit was never able to keep up with the room's heat load. Sun exposure, open floor plans and kitchens are common reasons.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Remote and mode.**
  - Replace the remote batteries.
  - Confirm the remote is set to Cool, not Dry, Fan or Auto.
  - Set it at least 5°F below room temperature with the fan on High.
  - Watch for the indoor unit's beep or light confirming it received the signal.
- **Filters.**
  - Turn the unit off.
  - Lift the front panel as your owner's manual describes.
  - Slide out the mesh filters, rinse them with lukewarm water and let them dry fully before reinstalling.
  - Do not spray or reach into the coil or blower wheel.
- **Breaker or disconnect.**
  - Make sure the breaker is on.
  - If the unit shows an error, power-cycle it ONCE: breaker off for 5 minutes, then back on.
  - Do not keep resetting it.
- **Indoor airflow.**
  - Make sure curtains or furniture are not blocking the head.
  - Make sure the louvers are not stuck closed.
- **Outdoor unit (from outside, no tools).**
  - Clear leaves and vegetation to roughly 1–2 feet around the unit.
  - Confirm nothing is stacked against it.
  - Check whether its fan is spinning while in Cool mode.
- **Condensate.**
  - Check that the drain hose outlet outside is not crushed or clogged.
  - A blocked drain can trigger a shutdown on some models.
- **Close windows and doors in the room.** Then give the unit 30 minutes.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Read the fault history.** They will pull any error codes from the indoor display or board LEDs and check service mode on series that support it.
- **Measure temperatures.**
  - Supply and return air temperatures at the head. A healthy unit usually shows a split of around 15–25°F, depending on conditions.
  - Outdoor coil temperatures.
- **Check refrigerant.** They will attach gauges at the outdoor service port. Then they will compare pressures, superheat and subcooling to York's specifications for that model and the current outdoor temperature.
- **Leak-search** flare joints and the coils with an electronic detector or bubble solution if the charge looks low.
- **Test thermistors.** They will measure sensor resistance against the temperature chart in the service manual.
- **Check communication voltage** between the indoor and outdoor units, and confirm the EEV is opening.
- **Test the inverter board and compressor windings.**
- **Inspect and clean** the indoor coil and blower wheel, often with a bag-style mini-split cleaning kit.

**Red flags in a quote:**
- "Just needs a recharge" with no leak search.
- A new board quoted without any sensor or communication testing.
- Recommending full system replacement before measuring anything.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but feels room temperature | Wrong mode, remote not communicating | Replace batteries, set Cool, lower setpoint | None if this fixes it |
| Weak airflow, slight cooling | Clogged filters or dirty blower wheel | Wash filters | Deep-clean coil and blower wheel |
| Outdoor fan off, indoor running | Communication, board or compressor fault | Power-cycle once | Test communication wire, inverter board, compressor |
| Ice on indoor coil or line at outdoor valves | Low charge or airflow restriction | Wash filters, switch to Fan to thaw | Leak search, repair flare, vacuum, weigh in charge |
| Oily residue at flare nuts | Refrigerant leak | None | Re-flare or replace fitting, recharge |
| Cools briefly, then stops early | Faulty room or coil thermistor | None | Test and replace sensor |
| Error code on display | Model-specific fault | Note the code, power-cycle once | Diagnose per the service manual |
| Runs nonstop, never reaches setpoint on hot days | Undersized head or high heat load | Close blinds and doors | Perform a load calculation, add a zone if needed |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges, including labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Filter cleaning, mode fix, batteries | $0 |
| Service call and diagnosis | $100–$250 |
| Indoor coil and blower wheel deep cleaning | $150–$400 per head |
| Thermistor replacement | $150–$350 |
| Flare leak repair plus vacuum and recharge | $300–$900 |
| Leak in an indoor or outdoor coil | $800–$2,000, or replace the unit |
| EEV coil or valve | $300–$800 |
| Indoor control board | $300–$700 |
| Outdoor inverter board | $500–$1,200 |
| Inverter compressor | $1,500–$2,500+ |

Check your warranty before approving parts. Many York ductless systems carry longer parts coverage if they were registered after installation. Labor coverage usually depends on the installer.
