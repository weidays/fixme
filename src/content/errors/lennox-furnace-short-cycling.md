## What this code means

"Short cycling" isn't a single Lennox fault code—it's a behavior. Your furnace fires up, runs for a short time (often under a few minutes), shuts off, then restarts shortly after, repeating this pattern without properly warming your home.

On Lennox furnaces with SureLight and similar integrated control boards, short cycling is usually the **result** of a protective device doing its job. The most common trigger is the **high-limit switch** opening because the heat exchanger is overheating from restricted airflow. Depending on the model and what's actually going wrong, the board may log a fault—typically a limit-open fault, a flame-sense fault, or a pressure-switch fault—or it may simply cycle without flagging anything.

How that fault is displayed varies by model and control board generation: some boards show a numeric code, some an alphanumeric E-code, and some only flash an LED pattern. Don't try to match a code you see on the board to a number you found on a general-purpose website—read it against the code table in the installation or service manual for your specific unit, which is usually printed on a label inside the blower door.

Because the underlying causes range from a $20 filter to a $1,200 board or an oversizing design flaw, persistent short cycling on a Lennox furnace is rated **pro**: safe first checks are limited, and most real fixes are inside the cabinet.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — Restricted return airflow lets the heat exchanger overheat, tripping the high-limit switch and stopping the burners until it cools. By far the most common cause.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return registers** — Too many closed vents or blocked returns mimics a dirty filter and overheats the unit.
3. **High-limit switch tripping (overheating)** — Caused by low airflow, a failing blower motor, or a dirty blower wheel. The limit repeatedly cuts the burners.
4. **Dirty or weak flame sensor** — A marginal flame signal causes the board to shut down burners shortly after ignition, then retry, producing a short-cycle pattern.
5. **Thermostat problems** — Dead batteries, poor placement (near a supply register or heat source), loose wiring, or an aggressive cycle-rate setting.
6. **Blocked or restricted flue / pressure-switch cycling** — A partly blocked intake/exhaust or condensate backup causes the pressure switch to open mid-cycle.
7. **Failing inducer or blower motor** — Intermittent motor operation drops airflow or pressure and interrupts the heat call.
8. **Control board fault** — A degraded SureLight board can misread inputs and cycle the furnace erratically.
9. **Oversized furnace** — An improperly sized furnace heats the space too quickly, satisfies the thermostat in short bursts, and cycles frequently by design.

Whether the furnace **auto-resets** or **locks out** depends on the trigger. A tripped high-limit typically clears itself once the unit cools, so the furnace keeps cycling. Flame and ignition faults work differently: the control retries a set number of times, and **only after those retries are used up does it go into lockout**. On SureLight-family controls, that lockout generally clears itself automatically after about an hour, at which point the board tries the ignition sequence again. That means waiting it out—not cutting power—is the normal behavior. Killing power to force an early reset just hides the fault, and doing it repeatedly lets a furnace with a real problem keep trying to light. Stick to the one-reset rule below, then call a technician.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do:

- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, swap it. This alone resolves a large share of short-cycling calls. Read the size off the edge of the old filter before buying a replacement—Lennox installs use everything from thin 1" filters to thick media cabinets.
- **Open closed vents and clear returns.** Make sure supply registers aren't shut and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or drapes. As a rule of thumb, don't close off more than a couple of registers.
- **Check the thermostat.** Replace batteries if applicable, confirm it's set to **Heat** with a target above room temperature, and make sure it isn't in direct sun or above a heat source.
- **Reset the breaker once.** If the furnace tripped, flip the breaker (or the furnace switch) fully off, wait 30 seconds, and back on—**one** reset only.
- **Check the condensate line and drain.** On high-efficiency Lennox models, a clogged condensate trap or line can trip the pressure switch. Clear any obvious external blockage or standing water at the drain.
- **Confirm the outdoor intake/exhaust vents are clear** of snow, ice, leaves, or nests.

If short cycling continues after these steps, stop and call a licensed HVAC technician. Do not open the cabinet, bypass any switch, or attempt repeated resets.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified Lennox tech will typically:

1. **Read stored fault codes** on the SureLight board and interpret them against the code table in that unit's service literature, to see if a limit, flame, or pressure fault is logged.
2. **Measure temperature rise** across the furnace and compare it to the rating-plate spec—high rise confirms an airflow/overheating problem.
3. **Inspect airflow components**: filter, blower wheel, blower motor amp draw, and ductwork static pressure.
4. **Test the high-limit switch and its wiring** for proper operation and correct trip point.
5. **Check the flame sensor microamps** and clean or replace the sensor if the signal is weak.
6. **Verify the pressure switch and inducer** operation, checking for flue/condensate blockages and correct negative pressure.
7. **Inspect the heat exchanger** for cracks or restrictions.
8. **Evaluate sizing** with a load calculation if no mechanical fault is found—an oversized unit is diagnosed by exclusion.

A good quote names the specific cause (e.g., "weak flame sensor" or "high static pressure from undersized ducts"), not just "it's short cycling."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs a few minutes, quits, restarts | Dirty filter / low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Measure temp rise, check blower & static pressure |
| Burners light then go out in seconds | Weak/dirty flame sensor | None | Test microamps, clean or replace flame sensor |
| Cycles more in cold snaps | High-limit overheating | Replace filter, clear returns | Test limit switch, inspect blower wheel & motor |
| Rapid on/off, thermostat display odd | Thermostat batteries/placement/wiring | Replace batteries, relocate check | Verify wiring, cycle-rate setting, replace stat |
| Cuts off with a click mid-cycle | Pressure switch / flue or condensate blockage | Clear condensate drain & outdoor vents | Test pressure switch, clear flue, inspect inducer |
| Cycles even when clean | Oversized furnace or bad control board | None | Load calculation, board diagnostics |
| Faint humming, weak airflow | Failing blower/inducer motor | None | Amp-test motor, replace if failing |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor unless noted):

- **Air filter (DIY):** $10 – $40
- **Thermostat batteries (DIY):** under $10
- **Programmable/smart thermostat replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Flame sensor clean or replace:** $80 – $250
- **High-limit switch replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Condensate/flue clearing:** $100 – $300
- **Inducer motor replacement:** $400 – $1,200 (OEM Lennox inducer assemblies alone often run past $350)
- **Blower motor replacement:** $450 – $1,800 (variable-speed ECM motors and their modules sit at the high end)
- **SureLight control board replacement:** $400 – $1,200
- **Duct modification for oversizing/static pressure:** $500 – $2,500+
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $90 – $180 (often credited toward the repair)

Costs vary by region, model series, and whether parts are under Lennox warranty. Get an itemized quote naming the specific cause.

## Related faults

Codes differ between Lennox control board generations, so these are described by fault type rather than by number. Check the code table in your unit's installation or service manual to see how your board reports each one.

- **Limit-open fault (high-limit trip)** — the overheating limit trip that most often drives short cycling.
- **Pressure-switch fault** — flue, intake, or condensate issues that interrupt a heat cycle.
- **Weak flame-sense / low flame signal fault** — a marginal flame signal causing burners to drop out shortly after ignition.
- **Ignition lockout after failed retries** — where repeated failed ignition attempts end the sequence in lockout.
- **Lennox Furnace Won't Ignite: Causes, Fixes & Costs** — when the furnace fails to start rather than cycling.
