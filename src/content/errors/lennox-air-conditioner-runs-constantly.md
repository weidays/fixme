## What this code means

"Runs constantly" is a symptom, not a diagnostic code. Lennox does not have a dedicated fault code for it. It comes in two forms, and the difference matters.

1. **The system runs nonstop but can't reach the setpoint.** The AC is working, just not well enough to satisfy the thermostat. Typical reasons are restricted airflow, a dirty outdoor coil, low refrigerant, an undersized system or very hot weather.
2. **The system won't shut off even after the setpoint is reached, or with the thermostat turned off.** This is a control problem. The usual causes are a thermostat fault, a shorted thermostat wire, or a stuck outdoor contactor.

Some long run times are expected:

- **Variable-capacity models:** Lennox variable-capacity units, such as the XC25 and SL28XCV paired with iComfort, S30 or S40 thermostats, are designed to run long, low-speed cycles. Continuous low-speed running with steady comfort is normal for these.
- **Heat waves:** On extreme days, any system may run continuously.

There is nothing to reset. The condition continues until the underlying cause is fixed. Communicating systems may log related alerts on the thermostat, and the exact alerts vary by model and control.

If your problem is warm air, ice, or the system turning on and off rapidly, see the related pages listed at the end instead.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter.** This is the most common cause. A restricted filter cuts airflow across the indoor coil, which reduces capacity and lengthens run time.
2. **Thermostat setting, placement or fault.**
   - The setpoint may be unrealistically low. Most systems can only cool about 15–20°F below the outdoor temperature.
   - The fan may be set to ON rather than AUTO. The fan then runs continuously, which some homeowners mistake for the AC running.
   - The thermostat may be in direct sun or near a heat source.
   - Weak batteries or a failing thermostat can cause erratic readings.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil or blocked airflow.** Debris such as cottonwood fluff, grass clippings, dense shrubs or a nearby fence keeps the condenser from shedding heat, so capacity drops.
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Capacity drops and the system runs longer and longer. This often comes with a partially iced suction line or coil.
5. **Stuck or welded outdoor contactor.** The contactor's contacts fuse closed, so the compressor and condenser fan run even with no call for cooling. This is common on older units and in areas with ants or insects that nest in the control box.
6. **Shorted or damaged thermostat wiring.** A short between the R and Y wires, for example from wire nicked by a mower or chewed by rodents, keeps the condenser energized.
7. **Duct leaks or poor insulation, especially in attics.** Cooled air escapes before it reaches the rooms.
8. **Dirty evaporator coil or blower problems.** These reduce airflow and heat transfer indoors. Examples include a weak blower motor, failing capacitor, or incorrect blower speed setting.
9. **Undersized or mismatched equipment.** The system is too small for the home's load, or the outdoor unit and indoor coil are mismatched.
10. **Failing compressor (reduced efficiency).** Worn valves reduce pumping capacity, so the compressor runs but moves less heat. This is less common and is diagnosed only after other causes are ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check and replace the air filter.** If it's gray or caked, replace it with the correct size. Avoid ultra-restrictive filters unless your system was designed for them.
- **Check the thermostat.**
  - Make sure it's set to COOL and the fan is set to AUTO.
  - Try a realistic setpoint, around 75–78°F on very hot days.
  - Replace the batteries if your model uses them.
  - Make sure the thermostat isn't in direct sun or near a lamp, TV or kitchen heat.
- **Check whether it really won't shut off.** Raise the setpoint 5°F above room temperature, or turn the system OFF at the thermostat. Wait 5 minutes.
  - If the outdoor unit **keeps running**, switch it off at the outdoor disconnect or the breaker and call a technician. Running continuously this way can freeze the coil and harm the compressor.
- **Clear the area around the outdoor unit.** Remove leaves and debris and trim plants back to at least 2 feet. Gently hosing loose debris from the **outside** of the fins, with power off at the disconnect, is fine. Do not remove panels.
- **Open supply vents and check return grilles.** Make sure registers are open and returns aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Check the condensate drain.** A clogged drain can trip a float switch on some setups. Clear visible clogs at the outside drain end, or pour a cup of distilled vinegar into the access tee if one is present.
- **Make sure exterior panels are seated.** Loose blower or coil access panels leak air and reduce performance.

If none of these steps helps, the remaining causes involve refrigerant, electrical components or the inside of the cabinet. Those are technician work.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good diagnosis should include most of the following:

1. **Confirm the complaint type.** The tech checks whether the system fails to satisfy the thermostat or fails to shut off. They cycle the thermostat and watch whether the contactor drops out.
2. **Check thermostat and low-voltage circuit.** They measure 24V at the Y terminal with the thermostat off, and check for shorted or damaged thermostat cable between the house and condenser.
3. **Inspect the contactor.** They look for pitted or welded contacts or insect debris, and verify that the coil de-energizes.
4. **Measure temperature split.** They measure return versus supply air temperature. Roughly 16–22°F is typical, depending on conditions.
5. **Check airflow.**
   - Filter and evaporator coil condition
   - Blower speed and motor operation, including capacitor testing on PSC motors
   - Static pressure across the system
6. **Check refrigerant charge.** Gauges are connected to measure superheat (fixed-orifice systems) or subcooling (TXV systems, which are most current Lennox units) against the Lennox charging chart on the unit.
7. **Leak search if the charge is low.** Methods include an electronic detector, bubble solution or nitrogen pressure test. Common leak points include the evaporator coil, service valves and line set.
8. **Check condenser coil cleanliness and condenser fan operation.**
9. **Measure compressor amps.** Amps are compared to the nameplate RLA. If the charge and airflow are correct but performance is poor, the tech evaluates compressor efficiency.
10. **Ductwork and load assessment.** This is done if the equipment checks out but the home still won't cool. It may involve a duct leakage test or a Manual J load calculation.

**Red flags in a quote:**
- Recommending a refrigerant "top-off" without mentioning a leak
- Condemning the compressor without gauge readings and amp measurements
- Recommending full system replacement before a filter, coil and contactor check

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs all day, house never reaches setpoint, weak airflow | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Check blower and evaporator coil if airflow stays weak |
| Outdoor unit runs with thermostat OFF | Stuck/welded contactor or shorted thermostat wire | Shut off at disconnect or breaker and call a technician | Replace contactor; repair or replace thermostat wire |
| Indoor fan runs nonstop, no cooling sound outside | Fan set to ON | Set fan to AUTO | Check the fan relay or control board if the fan won't stop on AUTO |
| Runs nonstop on hot afternoons only, cools fine at night | Heat load at design limit, dirty condenser | Clear debris around unit, raise setpoint slightly | Clean condenser coil, verify charge |
| Runs long, ice on copper line or indoor coil | Low refrigerant or airflow restriction | Replace filter, turn system off to thaw | Leak search, repair, recharge to spec |
| Runs constantly, air only slightly cool | Low charge, dirty evaporator coil, weak compressor | Check filter and vents | Gauge readings, coil cleaning, compressor evaluation |
| Runs constantly, some rooms hot, attic very hot | Duct leaks or poor insulation | Open registers, clear returns | Duct sealing or insulation, airflow balancing |
| Variable-speed unit runs continuously at low speed, home comfortable | Normal design operation | None | None needed |
| Thermostat reads wrong or displays erratically | Dead batteries, bad thermostat location, failed thermostat | Replace batteries, try a new thermostat | Relocate or rewire thermostat, configure communicating controls |
