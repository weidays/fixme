## What this code means

Six flashes on your York furnace control board is a **pressure switch cycle lockout**. During a single call for heat, the pressure switch opened and reclosed **five times**, and on the fifth cycle the board gave up and locked the furnace out for **one hour**.

The pressure switch is a safety device that confirms the inducer (draft) motor is pulling proper venting airflow before the furnace lights the burners. When it opens mid-cycle, the board shuts off the gas and inducer, then retries. If that happens repeatedly, the furnace assumes something is wrong with venting or the inducer and stops trying.

This is a **soft lockout**: the furnace **auto-resets after 60 minutes**, or you can clear it sooner by cycling power once. But the lockout is telling you the switch is toggling for a reason — that reason needs to be found and fixed, or the furnace will just lock out again.

Unlike a stuck-open code (3 flashes) that trips instantly, 6 flashes means intermittent venting or airflow problems that come and go during operation.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Partially blocked or restricted vent/flue** — snow, ice, nests, debris, or a sagging condensate-trapped flue that intermittently restricts draft, so the switch opens as pressure fluctuates.
2. **Weak or failing inducer motor** — a motor that spins but loses speed under load produces borderline draft pressure that dips below the switch setpoint, then recovers.
3. **Blocked or kinked condensate drain / trap (high-efficiency models)** — water backing up in the vent or trap causes intermittent pressure changes that toggle the switch.
4. **Cracked, loose, or water-logged pressure switch tubing** — the small rubber hose to the switch develops condensation or a bad connection, giving erratic readings.
5. **Pressure switch out of calibration** — a switch drifting near its setpoint opens and closes with normal draft variation.
6. **Blocked inducer drain port or condensate collector** — restricts the inducer housing and lowers pressure intermittently.
7. **High wind / improper vent termination** — gusts momentarily push back against the flue on certain terminations, briefly opening the switch.

Pressure-switch cycle faults are normally traced in that order: the venting path and inducer performance first, because those actually generate the draft, then the switch and its tubing, which only report it.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do:

- **Check the thermostat** — confirm it is set to Heat and the target temperature is above room temperature.
- **Replace the air filter** — a filter that is only moderately dirty won't cause this code, but a fully clogged one can disturb airflow; swap it if it's dirty.
- **Look at the outdoor vent/intake terminations** — walk outside and make sure the vent and intake are clear of snow, ice, leaves, or nests. On a 90%+ condensing furnace these are the PVC pipes coming through the wall or roof; on an 80% AFUE furnace the venting is metal pipe running to a chimney or a sidewall cap, and you should only look at the visible exterior termination — never touch, open, or disassemble metal venting. Clear anything you can safely reach from outside.
- **Check the condensate drain line** — if you see standing water near the furnace or a clogged drain, clear the visible external line and empty a full condensate pan.
- **Check the breaker and furnace switch** — confirm the furnace breaker isn't tripped and the service switch is on.
- **Reset once** — cut power to the furnace for 30 seconds and restore it, or wait out the one-hour lockout. If 6 flashes returns, stop and call a technician.

Do **not** open the furnace cabinet, remove the pressure switch, jump the switch terminals, or reset repeatedly. Those are technician tasks and safety hazards.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

- Read the flash code and the board's fault history to confirm the cycle lockout.
- Put a **manometer** on the pressure switch port to measure actual inducer draft pressure against the switch's rated setpoint.
- Inspect the **entire vent and intake run** for restriction, slope, and condensate pooling.
- Check the **inducer motor** for correct amp draw, RPM, and any bearing noise or wear.
- Inspect the **pressure switch tubing** for cracks, water, or blockage and blow it out or replace it.
- Clear the **inducer drain port / condensate trap** and check for water backup.
- Test the **pressure switch** for correct open/close values, replacing it if out of spec.

A good quote should name which of these was the actual cause — not just "replaced the pressure switch" without measuring draft.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 flashes, furnace dead for ~1 hour | Cycle lockout tripped | Reset once; wait out the hour | Confirm fault history, measure draft |
| Code returns in cold/snowy weather | Vent or intake blocked by snow/ice | Clear visible outdoor terminations | Inspect full vent run for restriction |
| Water near furnace, gurgling | Clogged condensate trap/drain | Clear visible external drain line | Clear inducer drain port and trap |
| Inducer sounds weak or loud | Failing inducer motor | None — call a pro | Test amps/RPM, replace inducer |
| Runs fine then locks out randomly | Cracked/wet switch tubing or drifting switch | None — call a pro | Replace tubing or pressure switch |
| Locks out on windy days | Vent termination affected by wind | Note the pattern for the tech | Correct/reposition vent termination |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included unless noted:

- **DIY reset / clearing a blocked outdoor vent:** $0
- **Diagnostic service call:** $100 – $250, higher for after-hours, weekend, or holiday visits
- **Clearing condensate trap / inducer drain port:** $120 – $250
- **Pressure switch tubing replacement:** $100 – $200
- **Pressure switch replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Inducer motor / draft assembly replacement:** $400 – $1,200 — the OEM inducer assembly by itself often runs $300 – $600 before any labor, so quotes in the upper half of this band are not unusual
- **Vent termination correction / re-piping:** $200 – $500 depending on run

If the cause is simply a blocked vent you cleared yourself, this fix is free. If the inducer motor is failing, it's the most expensive common outcome — and the one where a quote that looks high is often simply the real price of the part plus labor.
