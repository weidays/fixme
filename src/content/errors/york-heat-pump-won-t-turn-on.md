## What this code means

"Won't turn on" is a symptom, not a stored fault code. It means the York heat pump system shows no response to a thermostat call: no indoor blower and no outdoor fan or compressor. Often the thermostat screen is blank too.

This page covers a system that is completely dead or almost dead. Other problems have their own pages:

- If the indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit doesn't, see York Heat Pump Compressor Not Running or York Heat Pump Outdoor Fan Not Spinning.
- If the unit runs but makes no heat, see York Heat Pump Not Heating.

A no-power condition is not a lockout, so there is nothing to reset in the usual sense. Once power or the 24-volt control circuit is restored, the system resumes on its own. Expect a short anti-short-cycle delay first, typically around five minutes on many York outdoor controls.

Some York outdoor defrost/control boards do hold a lockout after repeated pressure-switch trips. This varies by model. Those lockouts usually clear by cycling power, but they point to a refrigerant-side problem that needs a technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Tripped breaker or disconnect.** The indoor unit and outdoor unit are on separate circuits. The furnace or air handler service switch is often mistaken for a light switch and turned off.
2. **Thermostat problems.** Dead batteries, the wrong mode, or a schedule or hold setting can all prevent a call. A blank display from a battery-powered or C-wire thermostat can mean either the thermostat itself or a loss of 24 V.
3. **Tripped condensate float or overflow switch.** A clogged drain line raises water in the pan, and the switch opens the 24 V circuit. This is very common in summer cooling, but it can happen in any season.
4. **Blown 3- or 5-amp low-voltage fuse on the indoor control board.** This is usually caused by a chafed thermostat wire or a shorted outdoor control wire, such as wire nicked by a trimmer or chewed by rodents.
5. **Open door/panel interlock switch.** Some York air handlers and furnaces cut power when the blower door isn't fully seated.
6. **Failed 24 V transformer.**
7. **Failed indoor or outdoor control board, or a damaged thermostat wire run.**
8. **Outdoor lockout on some models.** Repeated high- or low-pressure switch trips can lock out the outdoor board. The outdoor unit then stays off while indoor parts may still respond. This varies by board.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set the mode to HEAT (or COOL) and set the temperature at least 3°F past room temperature. Replace the batteries if your model uses them. Remove any hold or vacation settings.
- **Breakers:** At the main panel, find the breakers for the air handler or furnace and for the heat pump. Turn a tripped breaker fully OFF, then ON, **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Switches:** Confirm the service switch near the indoor unit is ON. Confirm the outdoor disconnect is in the ON position. Only operate the handle or pull-out block as designed, and don't open the box beyond that.
- **Panels:** Make sure the indoor unit's front panels are fully seated and latched. A loose panel can hold the door switch open.
- **Condensate:** Look for water in the drain pan or around the indoor unit, or a float switch on the drain line. Clear the drain at its exterior outlet or cleanout with a wet/dry vac. Water draining from the pan may let the float switch reset.
- **Filter:** Replace a clogged filter. It won't cause a total no-power condition, but it prevents follow-up trips.
- **Wait:** After restoring power, wait 5–10 minutes for the delay timer before deciding the unit has failed.

Stop here. Anything behind panels involves live 240 V and is technician work.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Measure line voltage at the indoor unit and at the outdoor disconnect.
- Check the transformer primary voltage and the 24 VAC secondary voltage.
- Inspect the board fuse. If it's blown, they should find the short (thermostat cable, outdoor control wire, a contactor coil) before replacing the fuse. Replacing the fuse alone just leads to it blowing again.
- Test the door switch and the float switch for continuity.
- Jumper R to G, Y and O/B at the board to separate a thermostat or wiring problem from an equipment problem.
- Read the indoor board LED and any outdoor control fault LED against the model's chart.
- If the outdoor board shows a pressure lockout, check refrigerant pressures and the condenser coil and fan.

A sound quote names the specific failed part and the cause, especially for a blown fuse.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everything dead, thermostat blank | Breaker, service switch, or transformer | Check the breakers and switch once | Test voltage, replace the transformer |
| Thermostat on, nothing runs | Thermostat setting or wiring | Check the mode and setpoint, replace batteries | Jumper test, repair wiring |
| Water in pan, system dead | Float switch tripped | Clear the condensate drain | Clean the pan, verify the switch |
| Repeatedly dead after storms or yard work | Blown low-voltage fuse from a shorted wire | None beyond the checks above | Find the short, replace the fuse and wire |
| Dead after filter change | Door interlock open | Reseat the panel | Test or replace the door switch |
| Indoor runs, outdoor dead with fault LED | Outdoor pressure lockout (model-specific) | One power cycle at the breaker | Diagnose the pressure fault |
| Breaker trips immediately | Shorted motor, compressor or wiring | Do not reset again | Electrical fault diagnosis |
