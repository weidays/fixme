## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a numeric fault code on most Trane air conditioners — it's a physical symptom. The fan inside your outdoor condenser unit (the large box outside) is supposed to spin whenever the compressor runs, pulling air across the coil to dump heat from your home outside.

When that fan stops turning while the compressor is calling to cool, heat has nowhere to go. Refrigerant pressure and temperature climb quickly, and the unit will either trip its internal high-pressure or thermal protection or, worse, damage the compressor. Some Trane communicating systems (XV-series with a ComfortLink board) may log a related fault, but standard XR and XL units simply stop cooling with no code displayed.

Because a running compressor with a dead fan is a real risk to your most expensive component, treat this as a **pro-level** problem once basic checks are done.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — The single most common cause. The dual-run capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt of torque it needs to start. When it weakens or fails, the fan hums but won't spin, or won't start at all.
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor** — Bearings wear out, especially on older units. A motor that's hot, smells burnt, or won't spin freely by hand is likely done.
3. **Faulty contactor** — The relay that sends 240V to the motor and compressor can pit, stick, or fail to close, cutting power to the fan.
4. **Debris jamming the fan blade** — Leaves, sticks, ice, or a bent blade physically blocking rotation.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — Power loss to the outdoor unit or the 24V control circuit.
6. **Failed control board or relay** (communicating XV units) — Less common; the board that commands the fan has a fault.
7. **Wiring damage** — Rodent-chewed or corroded wires between the disconnect, contactor, and motor.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop the AC at the thermostat first so it isn't calling to cool with a dead fan.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the temperature is set below room temperature. Replace thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A clogged indoor filter can cause freeze-ups and nuisance shutdowns. Replace it if dirty.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor and outdoor breakers in your electrical panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Visible debris:** With the system off, look through the top grille for leaves, sticks, or ice on the blade. Clear away obvious debris around the unit.
- **Condensate:** If the indoor coil iced over from restricted airflow, let it fully thaw with the fan running (blower on) before restarting.

Do **not** open the outdoor unit's electrical panel, discharge or replace a capacitor, or spin the fan with a stick while it's powered. Capacitors hold a dangerous charge even with power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech works through the outdoor unit in a predictable order — this list helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm power** at the disconnect and 24V at the contactor coil.
2. **Test the run capacitor** with a meter against its rated microfarad (µF) value. A weak or shorted cap is condemned.
3. **Check the contactor** for pitted contacts and proper pull-in when energized.
4. **Test the fan motor** — resistance across windings, free spin by hand, and whether it starts with a known-good capacitor.
5. **Inspect wiring** for burns, corrosion, or rodent damage.
6. **On communicating XV units**, read the board's fault history and verify the fan command signal.

A good tech won't just swap the capacitor and leave — they'll verify the motor spins freely and doesn't draw high amps, since a bad motor often kills a new capacitor.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Shut off system; do not spin blade | Test & replace capacitor |
| Fan dead silent, no hum | Contactor, wiring, or motor | Check breaker (reset once) | Test contactor & motor |
| Fan spins slow or stops when hot | Weak capacitor or worn motor | None | Replace capacitor/motor |
| Blade physically blocked | Debris or bent blade | Power off, clear obvious debris | Straighten/replace blade |
| Whole outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker or blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Find cause of trip, replace fuse |
| Burnt smell from unit | Burned motor or wiring | Shut off immediately | Replace motor/wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts plus labor. Regional rates and unit access vary.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor replacement:** $350 – $600 (higher for premium or variable-speed motors)
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Control board (communicating XV units):** $400 – $800+
- **Wiring repair:** $120 – $350 depending on extent

If your unit is old (12+ years) and needs a motor plus capacitor, ask the tech whether repair or replacement makes more sense before committing.
