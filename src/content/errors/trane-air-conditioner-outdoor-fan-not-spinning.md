## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a numeric fault code — it's a physical symptom on your Trane condenser (the outdoor unit). The fan on top of the outdoor unit is supposed to run whenever the compressor runs, pulling air across the condenser coil to shed the heat your system pulled out of the house.

When that fan stalls, the compressor loses its ability to reject heat. Pressure and temperature inside the compressor spike quickly, and the unit will either trip on its internal overload or, worse, damage the compressor. This is why we rate it **pro-level**: the safe checks are limited, and the common repairs involve stored electrical energy and 240V wiring.

On most Trane split systems the fan is a standard PSC (permanent split capacitor) motor. On some variable-speed and communicating models the outdoor fan is an ECM controlled by the board — diagnosis differs, and those are firmly technician territory.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — By far the most common cause. The capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. When it weakens or fails, the fan hums but won't turn, or won't start at all.
2. **Seized or failed fan motor** — Worn bearings, an overheated winding, or a burned-out motor. May turn hard by hand or not at all.
3. **Failed or stuck contactor** — The relay that sends power to the fan and compressor. Pitted or stuck contacts can interrupt power.
4. **Debris jamming the fan blade** — Sticks, ice, or a bent blade physically blocking rotation.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuse** — No power reaching the outdoor unit at all (compressor usually won't run either).
6. **Control board / relay fault** — More common on variable-speed and communicating Trane units where the board commands the ECM fan.
7. **Loose or corroded wiring** — Vibration over years can loosen a fan lead or corrode a connection.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do — everything else involves live 240V and stored charge.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to Cool and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause the indoor system to freeze up and mimic outdoor problems. Replace if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Visible debris:** With the unit **off at the breaker**, look through the top grille for sticks, leaves, or ice on the blade. Only remove obvious loose debris from outside the grille — do not reach inside a powered unit.
- **Supply vents:** Make sure indoor vents are open and unobstructed.
- **Condensate:** Check that the condensate drain isn't backed up, which can trigger a safety float switch and shut the system down.

If the fan still won't spin after these checks, **turn the system off and call a technician.** Do not let the compressor keep running.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech will typically:

1. Verify 240V power at the disconnect and 24V control signal at the contactor.
2. **Discharge and test the run capacitor** with a meter, comparing measured microfarads (µF) to the rating printed on the label. A weak reading confirms replacement.
3. Test the contactor for proper pull-in and clean contacts.
4. Check the fan motor's windings for continuity and shorts, and spin it by hand for bearing drag.
5. On ECM/variable-speed Trane units, check board outputs and motor communication.
6. Inspect wiring and terminals for burns or corrosion.

A capacitor test takes minutes — if a quote jumps straight to a full motor replacement without testing the cheap capacitor first, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't turn | Failed run capacitor | Shut unit off | Discharge & replace capacitor |
| No hum, fan dead, compressor also silent | Tripped breaker / blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Test disconnect fuses & power |
| Fan turns hard or grinds by hand (power off) | Seized motor bearings | Note it, shut off | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Fan starts if spun by hand with a stick | Weak capacitor | Shut off, don't repeat | Replace capacitor |
| Clicking at outdoor unit, no fan | Stuck/failed contactor | Shut off | Replace contactor |
| Blade physically blocked | Debris or bent blade | Remove loose outside debris (power off) | Straighten/replace blade, clear jam |
| Fan won't run on variable-speed model | Board or ECM fault | Shut off | Diagnose board/ECM |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your region and model affect the total.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **PSC condenser fan motor (with capacitor):** $300 – $650
- **ECM / variable-speed fan motor:** $500 – $1,000+
- **Control board (variable-speed models):** $400 – $900
- **Blade replacement:** $100 – $250

The capacitor is the cheapest and most common fix — always worth confirming first. If a seized motor ran the compressor hot for a while, have the tech verify the compressor is undamaged before you approve a motor-only repair.
