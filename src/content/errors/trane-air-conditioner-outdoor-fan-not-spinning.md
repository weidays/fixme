## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a blink code — it's a physical symptom on your Trane outdoor unit (the condenser). The fan on top of the condenser pulls air across the coil to reject heat the system pulled out of your home. When it stops turning while the compressor runs, the unit can't shed heat, pressure climbs fast, and the system will either stop cooling or trip an internal safety.

On most Trane single-stage units (XR13, XR14, XR16 and similar), the fan is a permanent split-capacitor (PSC) motor started by a run capacitor. On variable-speed models (XV18, XV20i), the fan is an ECM motor driven by a control board, so the failure modes and parts are different — we note where that matters below.

This is rated **pro** because the most common fixes involve high-voltage components inside an energized cabinet.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — By far the most common cause. The capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. When it weakens, the fan hums but won't turn, or won't start at all. Capacitors are cheap but sit at line voltage and hold a charge.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor** — Bearings seize or windings burn out. The motor may be hot, smell burnt, or not respond even with a good capacitor.
3. **Contactor not pulling in** — If the contactor doesn't close, neither the compressor nor fan gets power. You may hear no hum at all outside.
4. **Obstructed or damaged fan blade** — Debris, ice, or a bent blade jamming against the shroud can physically stop rotation.
5. **Tripped internal high-pressure or overload switch** — A safety has opened because the unit ran hot; often a downstream symptom of one of the above.
6. **Control board or ECM fault (variable-speed models)** — On XV18/XV20i units, a board fault or bad ECM motor can stop the fan. These require Trane-specific diagnostics.
7. **Loss of 24V control signal or wiring fault** — A broken low-voltage wire, bad thermostat call, or corroded connection.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the condenser cabinet or touch any electrical component.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Indoor air filter:** A clogged filter can cause icing and system shutdowns. Replace it if dirty.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor and outdoor disconnect breakers in your panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated resets can be dangerous.
- **Visible obstructions:** From outside, look for leaves, cottonwood, or debris packed against the condenser or blocking the fan. Turn the system off at the thermostat before looking closely, and never reach into the unit.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure they're open and unblocked inside.
- **Condensate:** A full condensate pan or clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop the system.

If the outdoor fan still won't spin after these checks, turn the system off and call a technician. Do not keep running or repeatedly restarting it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** and confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor unit.
2. **Discharge and test the run capacitor** with a meter — checking microfarad rating against the label. A weak capacitor is the number-one finding.
3. **Check the contactor** for proper pull-in and clean, non-pitted contacts.
4. **Test the fan motor** for continuity, shorted windings, and free rotation of the shaft by hand (power off).
5. **Inspect the blade and shroud** for damage or obstruction.
6. **Read pressures** to confirm the system didn't trip a high-pressure safety, and check for a locked-out compressor.
7. **On variable-speed units,** pull board diagnostics or fault history for the ECM fan circuit.

A fair quote should identify the specific failed part — not just "replace the motor" if a $25 capacitor is the actual culprit.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Turn system off; don't force blade | Test and replace capacitor |
| No sound at all outside | Contactor not closing or no 24V | Check/reset breaker once | Test contactor, control wiring |
| Fan silent, motor hot/burnt smell | Failed fan motor | Shut unit off immediately | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Blade visibly jammed by debris | Obstruction | Power off, remove loose debris outside only | Inspect blade/shroud, straighten or replace |
| Compressor runs, fan dead, then shuts off | High-pressure trip / overheating | Stop running the unit | Diagnose root cause, check pressures |
| Fan dead on XV18/XV20i, error on board | ECM motor or board fault | Note any fault codes | Trane diagnostics, replace ECM/board |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your area and unit age will vary.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC):** $300 – $650
- **Fan blade replacement:** $100 – $250
- **ECM fan motor (variable-speed XV models):** $500 – $1,100+
- **Control board (variable-speed):** $600 – $1,400+

If your unit is old (12–15+ years), still uses R-22, and needs a motor plus other parts, ask whether repair or replacement makes more financial sense before authorizing major work.
