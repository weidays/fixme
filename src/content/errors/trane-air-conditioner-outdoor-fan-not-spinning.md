## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a stored fault code — it's a symptom. On most Trane air conditioners the outdoor (condenser) fan should run any time the compressor runs, pulling air across the coil to reject heat. If the compressor is humming or the disconnect is on but the top fan sits still, something in the fan's power path or the motor itself has failed.

This matters because the condenser fan is what keeps the system from overheating. **Running the compressor without the fan spikes head pressure and can ruin the compressor in minutes.** Treat this as a stop-and-repair situation, not a "keep limping along" one.

On variable-speed Trane units (XV18, XV20i), the fan is driven by an ECM motor and control board, so the diagnosis differs from older fixed-speed PSC units. We'll flag where that matters.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — The single most common cause on fixed-speed units. The capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque. When it weakens, the fan hums, spins slowly, or won't start at all.
2. **Seized or burned-out fan motor** — Worn bearings lock the shaft, or the windings fail. Often the motor is hot and smells burnt, or the fan won't turn freely by hand.
3. **Failed contactor** — The relay that sends power to the outdoor unit. Pitted or stuck contacts can leave the compressor humming while the fan gets no voltage (or vice versa).
4. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuses** — Loss of power to the whole outdoor unit. Sometimes only one leg is lost.
5. **Debris jamming the blade** — Sticks, leaves, or a warped blade physically blocking the fan.
6. **Control board / ECM fault (variable-speed units)** — On XV18/XV20i and similar, a failed board or ECM module stops the fan. These require a Trane tech with model-specific tools.
7. **Loose or corroded wiring** — Especially at the fan motor leads or capacitor terminals.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. **Do not open the electrical compartment, touch the capacitor, or bypass any switch.**

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A badly clogged filter can cause the system to short-cycle or ice up, masking or mimicking outdoor problems. Replace if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it once. If it trips again immediately, stop — that's an electrical fault for a pro.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** The pull-out box near the condenser should be fully seated. Don't remove it or probe inside.
- **Visible debris:** With the system OFF at the thermostat and breaker, look through the top grille for leaves, sticks, or a bird's nest jamming the blade. Remove obvious debris.
- **Indoor vents:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked.
- **Condensate:** A full drain pan with a float switch can shut the system down — check for standing water at the indoor unit.

If the fan still won't spin after these, shut the system off and call a technician. Do not repeatedly reset a unit that keeps failing.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will work in this order so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Confirm 240V power** at the disconnect and both legs at the contactor.
2. **Check the contactor** — measure 24V at the coil and confirm the contacts pull in and pass line voltage.
3. **Test the run capacitor** with a meter, comparing measured microfarads (MFD) to the rating printed on the can. A reading well below spec confirms a bad capacitor.
4. **Check the fan motor** — verify voltage reaching the motor, test windings for continuity/resistance, and check that the shaft turns freely and bearings aren't seized.
5. **Inspect wiring** at the motor, capacitor, and contactor for burns or loose lugs.
6. **On variable-speed units**, connect Trane diagnostics to read board/ECM status and confirm whether the board or motor module is at fault.

A capacitor swap is a 20-minute job. A motor replacement takes longer and requires matching HP, voltage, RPM, and rotation.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Turn unit off so motor doesn't cook; note the hum | Test & replace capacitor |
| Fan spins slow or wobbly | Weak capacitor or worn bearings | Shut down, don't run it | Replace capacitor or motor |
| No hum, fan dead, compressor humming | Bad contactor or motor | Check breaker once | Test contactor, motor, wiring |
| Whole outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker / blown fuses | Reset breaker once | Find electrical fault, replace fuses |
| Fan blade physically stuck | Debris or bent blade | Power off, remove visible debris | Straighten/replace blade, check motor |
| Fan won't start (variable-speed unit) | Control board / ECM fault | None — call Trane pro | Board/ECM diagnosis & replacement |
| Burnt smell from outdoor unit | Overheated/failed motor | Shut off immediately | Replace motor, inspect wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Run capacitor:** $150 – $350 installed
- **Contactor:** $150 – $350 installed
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC):** $350 – $700+ installed, depending on HP and access
- **Fan blade:** $75 – $250 installed
- **Disconnect fuses:** $75 – $200
- **ECM motor / control board (variable-speed XV models):** $500 – $1,500+ — parts are model-specific and pricey
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $75 – $200, often credited toward the repair
- **DIY breaker reset or debris removal:** $0

Get an itemized quote. If a tech pushes a full motor replacement without first testing the far-cheaper capacitor, ask why.
