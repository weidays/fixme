## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a blink code — it's a physical symptom on your Trane condenser (the outdoor unit). The large fan on top of that unit is supposed to run whenever the compressor runs, pulling air across the coil to dump heat outside. When it stops, the compressor has no way to reject heat.

This is a **pro-level problem** for one important reason: your compressor may still be trying to run while the fan is dead. That builds heat and pressure fast and can destroy the most expensive part in the system. If the fan isn't turning, **turn the system off at the thermostat** and leave it off until it's diagnosed.

On variable-speed Trane units (XV18, XV20i), a stopped fan may also throw a fault on the outdoor control board or communicate an error to the indoor system. On single-stage units (XR13–XR16), there's usually no code at all — just a fan that won't move.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — By far the most common cause. The dual run capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. When it weakens, the fan hums but won't spin, or won't start at all.
2. **Seized or failed fan motor** — Worn bearings lock up the motor, or the windings burn out. Often you'll smell a hot electrical odor or see the motor discolored.
3. **Debris jamming the blade** — A stick, leaves, or ice can physically block the fan.
4. **Bad contactor** — The relay that sends power to the outdoor unit can fail or get pitted, cutting voltage to the fan (and compressor).
5. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — No power to the condenser at all.
6. **Control board fault (variable-speed models)** — On ECM/communicating units, a failed board or motor module can stop the fan.
7. **Bad wiring or loose connection** — Corroded terminals or a chewed wire (rodents love these units).

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do without tools or opening the electrical panel:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A badly clogged filter can cause the indoor system to freeze or shut down. Replace if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor furnace/air-handler breaker and the outdoor AC breaker in your main panel. Reset once if tripped. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Visible debris:** With the system **off at the breaker**, look through the top grille for leaves or sticks touching the fan blade. Remove anything obvious by hand.
- **Vents & registers:** Make sure supply and return vents inside are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate:** Check that the drain line isn't overflowing, which can trigger a safety float switch.

**Do not** open the electrical access panel on the outdoor unit. The capacitor stores a dangerous charge even with power off, and the terminals carry 240V.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the flow helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm power** — Verifies 240V is reaching the condenser and 24V is reaching the contactor coil.
2. **Test the capacitor** — With power off and the capacitor safely discharged, they measure its microfarad rating against the label. A weak cap is the #1 find.
3. **Check the contactor** — Looks for pitted or welded contacts and confirms it pulls in on a call for cooling.
4. **Test the motor** — Checks windings for resistance/short, spins the blade by hand to feel for seized bearings, and looks for burnt smell or discoloration.
5. **Inspect wiring** — Traces for loose, corroded, or rodent-damaged connections.
6. **Board/module check (variable-speed only)** — On XV18/XV20i units, they'll read fault codes and test the ECM fan module.

A good tech won't just replace the fan motor without testing the capacitor first — a $15 cap is a far more common culprit than a $400 motor.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None (high-voltage) | Test & replace capacitor |
| Fan won't start; can push-start with a stick | Weak capacitor or tired motor | None | Replace cap, test motor |
| No power to outdoor unit at all | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor / trace wiring |
| Fan blade physically stuck | Debris or ice jam | Remove debris (power off) | Inspect motor after clearing |
| Burning smell, discolored motor | Burnt-out fan motor | Shut system off | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Fault code on variable-speed unit | Control board / ECM module | Note the code | Test & replace board/module |
| Buzzing at contactor, no fan | Failing contactor | None | Replace contactor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included unless noted:

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $300 (the part is cheap; a diagnostic/service call makes up most of it)
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC/standard):** $350 – $550
- **Variable-speed ECM fan motor or module:** $500 – $650+
- **Control board (variable-speed units):** $400 – $700
- **Debris removal only:** $0 DIY, or a single service-call fee (~$90–$150) if a tech clears it
- **Wiring repair:** $100 – $250 depending on extent

If a tech finds the compressor was damaged from running without the fan, that's a much larger repair ($1,500+) and may make replacing an aging unit the smarter call.
