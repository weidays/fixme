## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a numeric fault code — it's a physical symptom on the outdoor condenser unit. On a running Trane air conditioner, the outdoor fan should turn any time the compressor is energized. When the compressor hums or the unit calls for cooling but the top fan sits still, the unit is not rejecting heat and cooling will drop off or stop entirely.

On variable-speed Trane units (XV20i, XV18, XV17) an ECM condenser fan motor may report a fault to the control board or app. On single- and two-stage units (XR13/14/16, XL16i and older 2TTX/4TTX), there is no code — the fan simply doesn't spin, and diagnosis is by measurement.

This is a **pro-level** issue: the most common fixes involve a capacitor that stores a dangerous charge and 208/240V line voltage.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed dual run capacitor** — by far the most common cause. The capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque. A bulged, leaking or out-of-spec capacitor leaves the fan unable to start (often it will spin if nudged).
2. **Debris jamming the fan** — a stick, leaves, ice or a bent blade physically blocking rotation.
3. **Burned-out condenser fan motor** — bearings seize or windings fail, sometimes after running against a bad capacitor for weeks.
4. **Contactor not pulling in** — a pitted or failed contactor doesn't send line voltage to the fan and compressor.
5. **Tripped breaker or lost line voltage** to the outdoor unit.
6. **Control board or ECM module fault** (variable-speed models) — the board isn't commanding the fan, or the ECM motor's electronics have failed.
7. **Wiring damage** — rodent chews, corroded connections or a loose fan lead.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do — no panels off the electrical compartment.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temp. Replace batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Air filter:** A clogged indoor filter can cause the system to short-cycle or freeze; swap it if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check the indoor and outdoor breakers, plus any outdoor disconnect. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Visible obstruction:** With the system **off at the breaker**, look through the top grille for leaves, a stick, or an obvious blockage you can remove by hand. Do not reach in while power is on.
- **Ice check:** If the outdoor coil or lines are iced, turn the system to OFF and let it thaw before running again.
- **Vents:** Make sure indoor supply and return vents aren't blocked.

If the fan still won't spin after these steps, shut the system off and call a technician — do not keep running the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech will typically:

1. Confirm 208/240V line voltage and 24V control voltage at the outdoor unit.
2. Verify the thermostat call reaches the contactor and the contactor pulls in.
3. **Discharge and test the run capacitor** with a meter, comparing microfarads to the rating printed on it.
4. Check the fan motor for continuity, shorts to ground and free rotation; measure amp draw against the nameplate.
5. On variable-speed units, read board/ECM fault data and confirm the fan command signal.
6. Inspect wiring for burned terminals, chew damage and loose connections.

A quote that jumps straight to "new motor" without a capacitor test is a red flag — the capacitor is cheaper and far more common.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan won't start; spins if nudged | Failed run capacitor | Shut off; note capacitor rating on unit | Discharge & replace capacitor |
| Fan blocked, won't turn by hand | Debris or bent blade | Power off, remove visible debris | Straighten/replace blade, check motor |
| No hum, no fan, no compressor | Tripped breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor, check voltage |
| Fan ran, then quit; hot or noisy motor | Seized/burned fan motor | Shut system off | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Ice on coil, fan stalled | Airflow/refrigerant issue | Set to OFF, let thaw | Diagnose freeze cause |
| Variable-speed unit shows fan fault | ECM motor/board fault | Shut off, note code | Read data, replace ECM/board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts plus labor:

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC, standard units):** $350 – $650+
- **ECM/variable-speed fan motor (XV series):** $600 – $1,200+
- **Control board (variable-speed):** $500 – $1,000+
- **Clearing debris / breaker reset:** $0 DIY (or a service-call minimum, ~$90–$150, if you call someone)
- **Wiring repair:** $150 – $400

Prices vary by region, unit age and parts availability. Trane variable-speed components cost significantly more than standard PSC parts.
