## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a numbered Trane fault code — it's a symptom you observe at the condenser (the outdoor unit). The large fan on top of the outdoor unit should spin whenever the compressor is running to blow heat out of the refrigerant. When it stops, the compressor quickly overheats and either trips on its internal overload or gets damaged over time.

On most Trane units the outdoor fan and compressor are powered through the same contactor and share a **dual run capacitor**. Because of that, the fan and compressor problems are closely linked. Variable-speed models (like the XV20i) use an **ECM fan motor** with its own control module, so their failure behavior differs — see "Applies to" above.

This is a **pro-level** repair for most homeowners because it involves high-voltage components and, sometimes, a stored capacitor charge.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor** — the single most common cause. The fan side of the dual capacitor loses its charge, so the motor hums or won't start (it may spin if you nudge it).
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor** — bearings fail, or the windings burn out from age, heat, or debris. Often smells hot or shows scorching.
3. **Bad contactor** — pitted or stuck contacts fail to pass power to the fan and compressor. Sometimes the fan runs but the compressor doesn't, or nothing runs.
4. **Tripped safety / high-pressure switch or thermal overload** — the unit shut the fan (and compressor) down to protect itself, often from a dirty coil or low airflow.
5. **Blocked or jammed fan blade** — debris, ice, or a bent blade physically stops rotation.
6. **Loss of 24V control signal or blown low-voltage fuse** — the contactor never pulls in, so nothing outside runs.
7. **ECM fan motor or module failure** (variable-speed models only) — the motor's electronics fail; diagnosis differs from PSC-motor units.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the electrical panel on the condenser or touch the capacitor.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Indoor air filter:** A clogged filter can cause low airflow and trigger outdoor shutdowns. Replace if dirty.
- **Breakers:** Check the breaker for the outdoor unit and the indoor air handler/furnace. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Make sure the pull-out disconnect box near the condenser is fully seated.
- **Visible obstructions:** With the system OFF, look through the fan grille for leaves, sticks, or ice on the fan blade. Clear loose debris only — don't reach in with power on.
- **Airflow around the unit:** Make sure grass, mulch, or shrubs aren't choking the coil.
- **Condensate:** A full condensate pan/float switch can shut the whole system down indoors — check for standing water and clogs.

If the fan still won't spin after these checks, **turn the system off** and call a technician. Running the compressor with a dead fan risks expensive damage.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** — confirm 24V is reaching the contactor coil.
2. **Check the contactor** — look for pitting; measure voltage in and out with the unit calling.
3. **Test the capacitor** — measure microfarads against the printed rating (e.g., 45+5 µF). A reading well below spec confirms a bad cap.
4. **Test the fan motor** — check windings for continuity/resistance and confirm it isn't seized. On ECM units, they'll check the motor module and control signals.
5. **Check safeties** — high-pressure switch, thermal overload, and low-voltage fuse.
6. **Inspect the coil and blade** — a dirty coil raises head pressure and trips safeties; a bent blade stops rotation.

Ask to see the capacitor reading or the failed part. That's how you sanity-check a quote.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin; starts if nudged | Weak/failed run capacitor | Turn unit off; don't keep nudging it | Test & replace dual capacitor |
| Compressor runs, fan dead | Bad capacitor or fan motor | Shut off to protect compressor | Diagnose cap vs. motor, replace |
| Nothing outside runs, no hum | Contactor, 24V signal, or fuse | Check breaker/disconnect once | Test contactor, control voltage, fuse |
| Fan blade physically stuck | Debris or bent blade | Power off; remove loose debris | Clear jam / replace blade |
| Fan runs then shuts off repeatedly | Overheating motor or tripped safety | Replace dirty filter; clear coil area | Find root cause (coil, pressure, motor) |
| Burning smell, scorched wires | Burned-out motor or contactor | Turn off at breaker immediately | Replace failed component |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your area and model may vary.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $400 (part is cheap; most cost is labor and the service call)
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Low-voltage fuse / minor wiring:** $100 – $250
- **Condenser fan motor (standard PSC):** $300 – $650 installed
- **ECM fan motor / module (variable-speed models):** $500 – $1,000+ installed
- **Coil cleaning (if it triggered a safety trip):** $150 – $400

A weak capacitor is often the cheapest and most common fix — always worth confirming before agreeing to a motor replacement.
