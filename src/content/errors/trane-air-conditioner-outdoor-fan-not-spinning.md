## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a blink code — it's a physical symptom you observe at the outdoor condenser unit. On a Trane air conditioner, the large fan on top of the outdoor unit should spin whenever the compressor runs. It pulls air across the condenser coil to reject heat from your home.

When that fan stops but the compressor keeps humming, heat has nowhere to go. Pressure and temperature climb fast inside the unit, which can trip a high-pressure switch, a thermal overload, or in the worst case damage the compressor. Because of that risk, this is a **pro-level** repair once basic power checks fail — most causes involve line-voltage components inside the sealed electrical panel.

Note: on variable-speed Trane models (XV20i, XV18) the fan is driven by an ECM controlled by the outdoor board, so a stopped fan may point to a board or motor module fault rather than a simple capacitor.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — By far the most common cause on XR/XL units with PSC motors. The fan side of the dual capacitor weakens, so the motor can't start turning. You'll often hear a hum.
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor** — Bearings fail, or the winding burns out. The motor may be hot, smell burnt, or not respond even with a good capacitor.
3. **Failed contactor** — The relay that sends power to the fan and compressor has pitted or stuck contacts, so voltage never reaches the motor.
4. **Tripped high-pressure or thermal safety** — A dirty coil or a prior overheat event locks out the unit; the fan won't run until the fault is cleared.
5. **Loose, corroded, or chewed wiring** — Rodents, vibration, or moisture break a connection at the motor or capacitor.
6. **ECM motor or control board fault (variable-speed models)** — On XV-series units, a failed ECM module or outdoor board can stop the fan even when everything else looks fine.
7. **Debris jamming the blade** — Sticks, ice, or a bent blade physically blocks rotation.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Anything past this involves line voltage or the sealed panel — leave it to a pro.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Air filter:** A badly clogged indoor filter can cause the system to trip safeties that affect outdoor operation. Replace if dirty.
- **Breakers:** Check the main panel for a tripped AC breaker and the outdoor disconnect near the unit. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Indoor blower:** Make sure the indoor unit is running. If nothing at all runs, the problem may be a common power fault, not just the fan.
- **Visible debris:** With power OFF at the disconnect, look for leaves, sticks, or ice blocking the fan blade. Do not reach in with power on.
- **Coil airflow:** Clear tall grass, leaves, or clutter within about 2 feet of the unit so it can breathe.

Do **not** spin the fan blade with a stick, open the electrical panel, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. Kill power at the disconnect and verify it's dead before opening the panel.
2. Test the **run capacitor** with a meter — comparing microfarad reading to the rating printed on the cap. A weak fan side confirms the most common failure.
3. Check the **contactor** for pitted contacts and confirm 24V and line voltage across it when energized.
4. Verify **voltage at the fan motor** — if power arrives but the motor won't turn, the motor is bad.
5. Test the fan motor **windings for continuity and ground faults**.
6. Inspect **high-pressure/thermal switches** and check refrigerant pressures and coil cleanliness.
7. On XV variable-speed units, read fault data from the **outdoor control board** and test the ECM module.

If a tech quotes a compressor or full unit replacement for a fan that won't spin, ask them to first confirm the capacitor and motor were tested — those are far cheaper and far more likely.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Shut off unit to protect compressor | Test and replace dual capacitor |
| Fan silent, motor hot/burnt smell | Seized/burned fan motor | Turn off power, don't force blade | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Nothing outdoors runs | Failed contactor or power fault | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, 24V, and line voltage |
| Unit runs then shuts off quickly | Tripped high-pressure/thermal safety | Clean around unit, replace filter | Check pressures, clean coil, clear lockout |
| Fan blade won't turn by hand (power off) | Debris or seized bearing | Clear visible debris | Free or replace motor |
| XV model fan dead, board lights odd | ECM or board fault | Note any fault indicators | Read board codes, test ECM module |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC, XR/XL):** $350 – $650
- **ECM fan motor / module (XV variable-speed):** $600 – $1,200+
- **Wiring repair:** $120 – $300
- **High-pressure switch or safety:** $150 – $400
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $75 – $200 (often credited toward the repair)

DIY cost is effectively $0 if a breaker reset or clearing debris solves it. Capacitor and motor work involves stored charge and line voltage — we recommend a pro.
