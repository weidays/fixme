## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a stored fault code — it's a physical symptom of your Trane condenser (the outdoor unit). The large fan on top of the unit pulls air through the coil to reject heat that the compressor has pumped outside. When that fan stops, the compressor loses its only way to cool itself.

Trane residential condensers generally don't display a numeric fault for a stalled fan. Instead you'll notice the compressor humming, warm air from the vents, or the unit tripping off on internal overload. Because a running compressor with a dead fan can destroy itself quickly, this is a **pro-level** repair once basic checks fail.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — By far the most common cause. The dual run capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. When it weakens, the motor hums but won't spin, or won't start at all.
2. **Burned or stuck contactor** — The 24V contactor sends power to the fan and compressor. Pitted, welded, or non-pulling contacts leave the fan without power.
3. **Failed condenser fan motor** — Worn bearings, a shorted winding, or a tripped thermal overload inside the motor.
4. **Seized or obstructed fan blade** — Debris, ice, or a bent blade physically blocking rotation. Bad motor bearings can also lock the shaft.
5. **Control board or relay fault** — On two-stage (XL16i) and variable-speed (XV18, XV20i) units, an outdoor control board or ECM module drives the fan. A failed board or communication fault can stop it.
6. **Low-voltage / thermostat call issue** — A blown 24V fuse, broken low-voltage wire, or no "Y" call means the outdoor unit never energizes.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do — no panels off the electrical box, no touching the capacitor.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to Cool and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace weak batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor furnace/air-handler breaker and the outdoor AC breaker (and any outdoor disconnect box). Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause the indoor coil to ice up and shut things down. Replace if dirty.
- **Indoor vents:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate:** A full condensate drain pan or tripped float switch can stop the whole system. Check for standing water at the indoor unit.
- **Look and listen (power OFF):** After shutting the unit off at the breaker, look for obvious debris, leaves, or ice around the fan blade and coil. Do **not** reach into the unit or attempt to spin the fan with power on.

If the unit is humming or the breaker won't stay set, turn it off and leave it off until a technician arrives.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the call:** Confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor unit and the thermostat is calling for cooling.
2. **Discharge and test the capacitor:** The tech safely discharges the dual run capacitor and measures its microfarad (µF) rating against the nameplate. A reading well below spec confirms a bad cap.
3. **Check the contactor:** They confirm 24V is pulling the contactor in and that line voltage is passing to the fan motor terminals.
4. **Test the fan motor:** With correct voltage present but no spin, they check motor windings for resistance and look for a tripped internal overload or seized bearings.
5. **Inspect the blade/shaft:** They spin the blade by hand (power off) to feel for drag or seized bearings.
6. **Board/communication check (staged & variable units):** On XL and XV models they read the outdoor control board or ECM diagnostics for fan or communication faults.

A good tech isolates the exact failed part before recommending a motor — motors are the most expensive item and are often blamed when a $30 capacitor is the real culprit.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan still | Failed run capacitor | Turn unit off; don't run it | Discharge & replace capacitor, verify µF |
| Nothing runs outdoors at all | Tripped breaker, bad contactor, or 24V fuse | Reset breaker once; check indoor float switch | Test contactor, 24V fuse, and control wiring |
| Fan starts if nudged, then runs | Weak capacitor | Note it; shut down | Replace capacitor |
| Loud grinding then fan stops | Seized bearings / bad motor | Shut off at breaker | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Fan blocked by debris or ice | Obstruction | Power off, clear visible debris | Inspect blade, motor, and coil airflow |
| Fan intermittent on staged/variable unit | Control board or ECM fault | Cycle power once | Read board diagnostics, replace board/module |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts plus labor. Rates vary by region and by model complexity.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180 (often applied to the repair)
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor replacement:** $350 – $650 (universal motor); Trane OEM or ECM motors on variable-speed units can run higher
- **Outdoor control board / ECM module (XL16i, XV18, XV20i):** $450 – $900+ depending on part availability
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $300

If a tech quotes a full motor without first showing you a failed capacitor reading, ask them to test the capacitor first — it's the cheapest and most common fix.
