## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a blink-code on Trane equipment — it is a symptom. The condenser fan is the propeller-style fan on top of your outdoor unit. Its only job is to pull air across the condenser coil so the refrigerant can shed heat.

When it stops, the compressor may still be running (you'll hear a hum or the unit will short-cycle), but with no airflow the system builds up high head pressure, overheats, and either trips an internal safety or damages the compressor. That's why this is rated a **pro** repair: the most common causes involve a capacitor that holds a lethal charge, live 240V terminals, or a failed motor.

On variable-speed **XV** units the fan is an ECM motor commanded by the inverter/control board — there is no separate run capacitor, and a non-spinning fan there usually points to the board or the motor module rather than a capacitor.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed dual run capacitor.** The single most common cause on PSC-motor units (most XR/XL and older models). The capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque; when it weakens the fan won't start or needs a "push."
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor.** Worn bearings, water intrusion, or age. Motor may hum, get hot, or do nothing.
3. **Stuck or pitted contactor.** The relay that sends 240V to the outdoor unit can weld shut or fail to close, cutting power to the fan and compressor.
4. **Obstruction in the fan blade.** Leaves, twigs, a warped blade, or ice buildup physically blocking rotation.
5. **Blown fuse or tripped breaker on the outdoor disconnect.** Loss of power to the whole condenser.
6. **Control-board or inverter fault (XV variable-speed units).** The board that drives the ECM fan has failed or thrown a fault.
7. **Loss of 24V control signal.** A bad thermostat wire, transformer, or low-voltage fuse means the contactor never energizes.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the outdoor unit's electrical panel — the capacitor can hold a dangerous charge even with power off.

- **Thermostat:** Set it to COOL and lower the setpoint several degrees. Confirm it isn't in FAN-only or OFF. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim.
- **Indoor air filter:** A clogged filter can cause freeze-ups that ice the outdoor system. Replace if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Visible vents/registers:** Make sure indoor supply and return vents are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate:** Check for a full condensate pan or a tripped float switch that shuts the system down.
- **Look (don't touch) at the outdoor unit:** From outside, check for obvious leaves, sticks, or ice on the fan blade. Turn the system OFF at the thermostat before clearing debris, and never reach in with power connected.

If the fan still won't spin after these checks, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify power and 24V signal** — confirms the disconnect fuse, breaker, transformer, and low-voltage fuse are good.
2. **Test the run capacitor with a meter** — reads microfarads against the rated value; anything significantly low means replacement. This is often the fix.
3. **Check the contactor** — inspects for pitted/welded contacts and confirms the coil pulls in when the thermostat calls.
4. **Test the fan motor** — checks windings for open/short, spins the blade by hand for a seized bearing, and confirms voltage is reaching the motor.
5. **On XV/variable-speed units** — reads inverter/control-board fault codes and checks the ECM motor module before condemning either part.
6. **Check pressures if needed** — a tech may confirm the system isn't tripping on high pressure from a coil or airflow problem.

A good tech tests the capacitor and contactor first because they're cheap and common. Be cautious of a quote to replace the whole motor without a capacitor test.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan doesn't turn | Failed run capacitor | Turn system off at thermostat | Test & replace dual run capacitor |
| Fan spins with a push, then stalls | Weak capacitor | None — stop running it | Confirm µF and replace capacitor |
| Nothing runs outdoors, no hum | Tripped breaker / blown fuse / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test disconnect fuse & contactor |
| Fan motor hot, buzzing, no spin | Seized/burned fan motor | Shut off, don't force it | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Blade physically blocked | Debris or ice | Power off, clear visible debris | Inspect blade, address freeze-up |
| Indoor unit runs, outdoor dead | No 24V signal / transformer / thermostat wire | Check thermostat settings | Trace low-voltage fault, replace fuse/transformer |
| XV unit shows a fault code | Inverter/control board or ECM fan | Note the code | Read board codes, replace board or motor module |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts plus labor, for common Trane condenser fan issues:

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80 – $180
- **Dual run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor replacement (PSC):** $350 – $650
- **Clearing debris / minor obstruction:** $0 DIY – $120 if part of a service visit
- **Low-voltage fuse or transformer:** $120 – $300
- **XV inverter/control-board repair:** $500 – $1,200+ (varies widely by model and part availability)

Prices vary by region, unit age, and part availability. Variable-speed XV components run noticeably higher than standard PSC parts.
