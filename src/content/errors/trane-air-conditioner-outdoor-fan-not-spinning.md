## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is not a Trane fault code — it's a physical symptom. Your outdoor unit (the condenser) has a large fan on top that pulls air across the coil to reject heat. When it stops turning while the compressor is trying to run, the system can't shed heat and cooling collapses.

This is a **pro-severity** situation for one reason: running the compressor with the outdoor fan stopped drives head pressure and temperature up fast, and can cook the compressor — by far the most expensive part in the system. If your fan isn't spinning, turn the system off at the thermostat and leave it off until the cause is found.

Note: standard Trane condensers (XR, XL series) use a run capacitor and contactor. Variable-speed models like the **XV20i and XV18** use an electronic drive/control module instead of a simple capacitor, so several checks below don't apply to them — we flag those cases.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor.** The single most common cause. The fan motor needs the capacitor for starting torque. A bad cap means the fan hums, stalls, or does nothing — but often starts if nudged.
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor.** Bearings fail or windings burn open. The fan won't spin even with a good capacitor, and may smell hot.
3. **Contactor not pulling in.** The 24V contactor sends power to the fan and compressor. A pitted, stuck, or unenergized contactor cuts power to both.
4. **Debris or ice physically jamming the blade.** Sticks, leaves, or a wasp nest can lock the blade. Ice buildup can also bind it.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown high-voltage fuse.** No power reaching the condenser at all — often the compressor won't run either.
6. **Failed control board / drive module (variable-speed units).** On XV18/XV20i, an electronic fault or bad module can stop the fan; these throw model-specific error codes on the thermostat.
7. **Low-voltage / thermostat control fault.** No 24V call reaching the contactor from the indoor board or thermostat.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps you should do yourself. Do **not** open the electrical panel of the condenser or touch the capacitor — it can hold a lethal charge even with power off.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to Cool and the temperature is below room temperature. Replace thermostat batteries if it uses them.
- **Air filter:** A clogged indoor filter can cause freeze-ups that mimic outdoor fan problems. Replace it if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor furnace/air-handler breaker and the outdoor A/C disconnect breaker. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Visible debris:** With the system **off at the breaker**, look through the top grille for obvious sticks, leaves, or a nest resting on the blade. Do not reach in unless power is fully off.
- **Ice:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or coil, turn the system off and let it thaw fully — running it iced up makes things worse.
- **Airflow:** Make sure the outdoor unit has clearance and isn't blocked by tall grass, mulch, or a cover left on.

If the fan still won't spin after these checks, shut the system off and call a technician. Do not keep resetting the breaker or "helping" the fan spin.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works from cheapest to most expensive. Here's the typical order so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Confirm the call and power.** Verify 24V control signal at the contactor and 240V line power at the disconnect.
2. **Test the contactor.** Check that it pulls in and passes voltage across the load side; inspect for pitted or welded contacts.
3. **Test the capacitor.** With power off and the cap discharged, they measure microfarads against the printed rating. A cap reading well below spec is replaced.
4. **Check the motor.** With a known-good capacitor, they test whether the motor spins and draws normal amperage. A seized, open-winding, or high-amp motor is condemned.
5. **On variable-speed units (XV18/XV20i):** they read fault codes at the thermostat and test the fan drive/control module rather than a simple capacitor.
6. **Verify after repair:** confirm normal fan rotation, correct amp draw, and healthy head pressure.

Expect a **service call / diagnostic fee** ($90–$200) that's often credited toward the repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't turn; starts if nudged | Failed run capacitor | Turn off system; don't keep pushing it | Discharge & test cap, replace with matching µF rating |
| Fan silent, compressor also silent | Tripped breaker or dead contactor | Reset breaker once; if it re-trips, stop | Test line voltage, contactor, and disconnect fuses |
| Fan won't spin even with good cap; hot smell | Burned-out / seized fan motor | Shut down to protect compressor | Test motor amps/windings, replace motor |
| Fan blade physically stuck | Debris or nest jamming blade | Power off at breaker, remove visible debris | Inspect blade, bearings, and shaft alignment |
| Ice on lines, fan struggling | Airflow/freeze issue | Turn off, replace filter, let thaw | Check charge, coil, and airflow |
| Fan dead + error code on thermostat (XV series) | Control/drive module fault | Note the code, shut down | Read code, test electronic fan drive module |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included unless noted:

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Condenser fan motor (standard PSC):** $300 – $650
- **Debris removal / blade freeing:** often folded into a $90–$200 service call
- **Blown high-voltage fuse at disconnect:** $75 – $150
- **Variable-speed fan drive / control module (XV18, XV20i):** $500 – $1,200+ — these modules are model-specific and far pricier than a standard cap
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90 – $200 (often credited toward the repair)

If a tech recommends a full condenser replacement over a bad fan motor, get a second opinion unless the compressor is also damaged from running hot.
