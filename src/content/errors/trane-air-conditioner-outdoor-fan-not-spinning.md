## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a blink-code on most Trane air conditioners — it's a physical symptom. The fan on top of your outdoor condensing unit is supposed to run whenever the compressor runs, pulling air across the coil to dump heat outside. When it won't spin, the system can't reject heat, indoor cooling drops off, and the compressor is at real risk of overheating.

On single-stage Trane units (XR13, XR14, XR16, 2TTR/4TTR series), the fan is a simple 240V motor driven through a run capacitor and a contactor. On variable-speed units (XV18, XV20i, some XL models), the fan is an ECM or inverter-driven motor controlled by a board, and a fault there will often show a diagnostic light or app message instead.

Because live 240V and a charged capacitor are involved, this is rated a **pro** repair. The safe checks below are limited to what you can do without opening the electrical panel.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — By far the most common cause on single-stage units. The capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque; when it weakens, the fan hums, stalls, or only starts if nudged.
2. **Seized or burned-out fan motor** — Worn bearings lock the shaft, or the windings fail. The motor may be hot, smell burnt, or not respond even with a good capacitor.
3. **Contactor not pulling in** — If the contactor doesn't close, neither the compressor nor the fan gets power. Often you'll hear no hum at all outside.
4. **Debris or ice jamming the blade** — Sticks, grass, or a bent blade can physically block rotation. Winter ice can also lock it.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuse** — No power reaches the outdoor unit, so nothing runs.
6. **Control/board fault (variable-speed units)** — On XV inverter units, the ECM driver or control board can fault out, disabling the fan and logging an error.
7. **Low-voltage / thermostat call issue** — No 24V "Y" signal means the contactor never energizes.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner — no panels off, no electrical work:

- **Thermostat:** Set to COOL and drop the setpoint 3–4°F below room temperature so there's a clear cooling call. Replace thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated tripping means a fault.
- **Air filter:** A filthy filter can cause the indoor system to freeze and shut things down. Replace if dirty.
- **Indoor vents/registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate:** Check for a full condensate pan or a tripped float switch — a full drain can stop the whole system from calling for cooling.
- **Look at the outdoor unit (power OFF at the thermostat):** Visually check for leaves, sticks, or ice jamming the fan blade. Clear obvious debris by hand only — do **not** open the electrical access panel or touch the capacitor.

If none of these solve it, shut the system off and call a technician. Do not keep running or repeatedly resetting a unit whose fan won't spin.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical path so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Confirms the call:** Verifies 24V "Y" signal is reaching the outdoor unit.
2. **Checks the contactor:** Looks for it pulling in and measures line voltage across it — pitted or stuck contacts get flagged.
3. **Tests the capacitor:** Discharges it safely, then measures microfarads against the nameplate rating (e.g., 5 µF fan side). Out-of-spec = replace.
4. **Checks the motor:** Measures winding resistance and ohms-to-ground, checks the bearings by hand, and confirms it spins freely.
5. **Inspects wiring and blade:** Looks for loose or burnt connections and a bent/bound blade.
6. **On variable-speed units:** Reads any board/app fault codes and tests the ECM/inverter drive per Trane service literature.

Ask to see the capacitor reading or motor test result — legitimate diagnoses come with a number, not just a guess.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin; starts if nudged | Weak/dead run capacitor | Shut off system; don't nudge repeatedly | Discharge & replace capacitor, verify µF |
| No hum at all outside, compressor silent too | Contactor not closing or no 24V | Check breaker/thermostat call | Test contactor, low-voltage circuit, transformer |
| Fan motor hot, burnt smell, won't turn | Seized/burned fan motor | Turn unit off immediately | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Blade physically jammed | Debris or bent blade | Power off, clear visible debris | Straighten/replace blade, inspect motor |
| Whole outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker or blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Find root cause of overload before re-powering |
| XV unit off with app/board fault | ECM/inverter drive fault | Note the code shown | Read fault, test drive & board per Trane docs |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices including parts and labor; your area and unit type vary.

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor (single-stage):** $350 – $700
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Low-voltage/thermostat wiring repair:** $150 – $400
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80 – $180 (often applied to the repair)
- **Variable-speed ECM/inverter board or drive (XV units):** $500 – $1,500+ — these are specialty parts and can be a large share of the unit's value.

DIY cost is effectively $0 if the fix is just clearing debris or resetting a breaker — but capacitor and motor work is not a safe DIY job for most homeowners.
