## What this code means

Most Trane air conditioners don't display a specific alphanumeric "code" for a stalled outdoor fan — the symptom is what you observe: the compressor and outdoor unit are energized (you hear a hum or the indoor blower runs), but the fan blade on top of the condenser doesn't turn.

On variable-speed and communicating Trane systems (XV18, XV20i and similar), the control board may log a fault or flash a diagnostic pattern when the fan motor fails to reach commanded RPM. The exact behavior varies by board, so read your unit's fault chart rather than assuming a universal code.

Either way, the outdoor fan's job is to pull air across the condenser coil to reject heat. If it isn't spinning, the system cannot cool and the compressor is at risk of overheating. **Turn the system off at the thermostat until it's fixed.**

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — The single most common cause. The dual run capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque. When it weakens or fails, the fan won't start (often with a humming sound) or spins only if pushed.
2. **Seized or worn fan motor** — Bearings wear out or the motor windings fail. A motor that's hot, buzzing, or won't turn freely by hand (power off) is likely done.
3. **Failed contactor** — The relay that sends power to the fan and compressor can pit, stick, or fail to pull in, cutting power to the fan.
4. **Debris jamming the blade** — Sticks, ice, or a bent blade can physically block the fan.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — Partial power can run the compressor circuit but not the fan on some wiring faults.
6. **Bad fan motor capacitor wiring or loose connection** — Corroded or loose spade terminals interrupt the circuit.
7. **Control board / ECM module fault** — On variable-speed Trane units, a failed fan module or board is possible but less common.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should perform. **Do not open the condenser electrical panel** — capacitors hold a lethal charge even with power off.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery model.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause the indoor system to freeze and shut down airflow; swap it if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check the outdoor unit's breaker in the main panel and any nearby disconnect. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Visible debris (power OFF):** Turn the unit off at the breaker, then look through the top grille for sticks, leaves, or ice on the blade. Clear loose debris only.
- **Return and supply vents:** Make sure indoor vents aren't blocked.
- **Condensate:** A full drain pan or clogged line can trip a safety switch and shut the system down.

Do **not** spin the fan blade with a stick to "help it start" while powered — that's a sign the capacitor is failing and needs professional replacement.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified HVAC tech will:

1. **Verify incoming voltage** at the disconnect and contactor (240V line, 24V control).
2. **Test the run capacitor** with a meter for its microfarad (µF) rating against the nameplate — a weak cap is the usual culprit.
3. **Check the contactor** for pitting and confirm it pulls in and passes voltage to the fan.
4. **Test the fan motor** windings for continuity and shorts, and spin the blade by hand (power off) to feel for seized bearings.
5. **Inspect wiring and terminals** for corrosion, loose spades, or burnt connections.
6. **On variable-speed units,** read board fault codes and test the ECM fan module.
7. **Check refrigerant/pressures** if the compressor cycled on a high-pressure trip.

A fair quote should identify the failed part specifically — be wary of a full "board or motor" replacement before the capacitor is even tested.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit hums, fan won't turn | Failed run capacitor | Shut off system | Test & replace dual run capacitor |
| Fan won't turn, no hum | Bad contactor or no power | Reset breaker once | Test contactor & voltage, replace as needed |
| Fan hot, buzzing, hard to turn | Seized fan motor | Shut off system | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Blade visibly blocked | Debris or ice jam | Power off, clear loose debris | Inspect blade & motor for damage |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Short in motor or wiring | Stop resetting, call pro | Locate fault, replace faulty component |
| Fault code on display | Board/ECM module (variable-speed) | Note the code | Diagnose per Trane fault chart |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your area and unit age will vary.

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $300 (part is cheap; you're paying for a qualified tech and testing)
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor replacement:** $350 – $650 (universal motor) to more for an OEM Trane-specific motor
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $300
- **ECM/variable-speed fan module or control board:** $400 – $900+
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80 – $180 (often applied to the repair)

If the compressor overheated from running without the fan, damage costs climb sharply — another reason to shut the unit off immediately.
