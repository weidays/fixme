## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a blinking fault code — it's a symptom you can see and hear at the condenser (the outdoor unit). The fan on top of the unit pulls air across the condenser coil to dump the heat your AC removed from your home. When it stops, the system either won't cool at all or cools weakly, then trips on high pressure or overheats.

On most Trane units there is no display telling you why the fan stopped. Instead, a technician diagnoses it by measuring voltage, capacitance, and motor resistance. On variable-speed models (XV20i, XV18, XV17) the outdoor ECM motor and control board can log faults, so diagnosis differs from a standard single-stage unit.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — The most common cause by far. The capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. When it weakens or fails, the fan hums or sits still while the compressor may keep running.
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor** — Bearings fail, or the windings burn out after years of heat. The fan won't spin even with a good capacitor.
3. **Faulty contactor** — The relay that sends power to the outdoor unit. Pitted or stuck contacts can cut power to the fan.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — No power reaching the outdoor unit means no fan and no compressor.
5. **Obstruction or debris** — A branch, ice, or accumulated leaves physically jamming the blade.
6. **Failed control board or ECM motor (variable-speed models only)** — On XV-series units, the outdoor board or ECM fan motor can fail and log a fault.
7. **Wiring or low-voltage problem** — A broken wire, bad splice, or failed transformer stops the 24V signal that energizes the contactor.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the system off first.** Set the thermostat to off. If the compressor is running while the fan is dead, running it longer can destroy it.
- **Check your thermostat** — confirm it's set to Cool and the temperature is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim.
- **Check the breaker** — look for a tripped AC breaker in your main panel and the outdoor disconnect box near the unit. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Check the air filter** — a badly clogged filter can cause pressure and airflow problems that mimic outdoor faults. Replace if dirty.
- **Look for obvious obstructions** — with the power OFF at the breaker, look through the fan grille for leaves, sticks, or ice on the blade. Clear anything loose. Do not reach in with power on.
- **Check indoor vents and drain** — make sure supply vents are open and the condensate drain isn't overflowing (a full drain pan safety switch can shut the system down).

Do **not** open the electrical panel on the condenser, discharge or replace the capacitor, or nudge the fan blade with a tool unless you're comfortable working around stored electrical charge. Capacitors hold a dangerous charge even with power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirms power** at the disconnect and measures voltage at the contactor.
2. **Tests the capacitor** with a meter, comparing measured microfarads (µF) to the rating printed on the can. A reading well below spec confirms a bad capacitor.
3. **Checks the contactor** for proper pull-in and clean contacts; measures voltage across the load side.
4. **Tests the fan motor** — resistance across windings, checks for an open or shorted winding, and spins the blade to feel for seized bearings.
5. **Inspects wiring and the 24V control circuit**, including the transformer.
6. **On variable-speed models**, pulls fault codes from the outdoor control board and tests the ECM motor per Trane's diagnostic chart.

A capacitor test takes minutes. If a tech quotes a full motor replacement without testing the capacitor first, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan still, compressor humming | Failed run capacitor | Turn unit off to protect compressor | Test & replace capacitor |
| Fan hums but won't turn | Weak capacitor or seized motor | Turn off; don't force blade | Test capacitor; replace motor if bearings seized |
| No fan, no compressor, no hum | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor & power supply |
| Fan blade physically jammed | Debris or ice | Power off, clear loose debris | Inspect for damage, check for airflow issue |
| Fan runs then stops, cooling weak | Overheating motor or low airflow | Replace filter, clear coil area | Diagnose motor & system pressures |
| Fan dead on XV variable-speed unit | ECM motor or control board fault | Note thermostat/board messages | Pull board codes, test ECM per Trane chart |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices including parts and labor; your area and unit age affect the total.

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor (standard PSC):** $350 – $650
- **Clearing debris / minor blade or grille repair:** $0 – $150
- **Wiring or transformer repair:** $150 – $400
- **ECM fan motor (variable-speed XV models):** $600 – $1,200+
- **Outdoor control board (variable-speed models):** $500 – $900+

A capacitor is the cheapest and most common fix. If the fan motor is failing, replacing the capacitor at the same time is smart since it's cheap and often the culprit. Get a second opinion before replacing a control board on a variable-speed unit — those are expensive and sometimes misdiagnosed.
