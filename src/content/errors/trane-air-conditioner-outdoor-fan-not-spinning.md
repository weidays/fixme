## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a numeric fault code — it's a physical symptom. Your Trane outdoor unit (the condenser) has a fan that pulls air across the coil to reject heat. When the compressor runs but that fan sits still, the system can't shed heat, pressures climb fast, and the compressor will overheat and shut down.

Note that behavior varies by model. On many Trane single-stage units the fan and compressor start together off the same contactor. On some variable-speed or two-stage units (XV series), the fan motor is an ECM driven by the outdoor control board, so a "no fan" condition may be logged differently or accompanied by a board-level fault. Check your unit's control board LED and service label for model-specific diagnostics.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — The single most common cause. The dual-run capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque. When it weakens or fails, the fan hums but won't spin, or won't start at all.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor** — Worn bearings, burnt windings, or an open thermal overload. Sometimes the motor is hot to the touch and cuts out.
3. **Debris or ice jamming the blade** — Sticks, leaves, or a bird's nest physically blocking the fan blade; occasionally a bent blade rubbing the shroud.
4. **Bad contactor** — Pitted or stuck contacts interrupt power to the fan and compressor.
5. **Seized or dry motor bearings** — The shaft won't turn freely; the fan may spin briefly if pushed, then stall.
6. **Control board or relay fault** — More common on ECM/variable-speed Trane units where the board commands fan speed.
7. **Blown fuse or tripped high-pressure switch** — Loss of 24V control power or a safety lockout preventing startup.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe with the unit **powered off**:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to Cool and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace dead batteries if applicable.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor and outdoor breakers, plus the outdoor disconnect box near the condenser. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Air filter:** A filthy filter can cause the whole system to short-cycle or lock out. Replace if dirty.
- **Look for debris:** With the unit fully off, look through the top grille for leaves, sticks, or a nest around the fan blade. Clear anything visible.
- **Blade by hand:** With power off, spin the blade with a stick. It should turn freely and coast. If it's stiff or grinding, the motor bearings are likely bad.
- **Supply vents:** Make sure indoor vents aren't blocked so airflow issues don't compound the problem.
- **Condensate:** Check that the drain isn't full and triggering a float safety switch that shuts the system down.

Do **not** open the electrical access panel, touch the capacitor, or bypass any safety switch. The capacitor stores a dangerous charge even with power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A pro will typically:

1. Kill power at the disconnect and confirm zero voltage.
2. **Discharge and test the capacitor** with a meter — comparing the reading to the microfarad rating printed on it.
3. **Check the contactor** for pitted contacts and verify 24V is reaching the coil.
4. **Test the fan motor** — resistance across windings, continuity to ground, and whether the thermal overload has opened.
5. **Spin the blade** by hand to feel for bearing wear or a seized shaft.
6. On ECM/variable-speed units, **read the control board LEDs/fault history** and confirm the board is commanding the fan.
7. Verify **line voltage** and check fuses in the disconnect.

A sound quote will name the specific failed part and its measured reading — not just "the fan's bad."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Turn system off to protect compressor | Discharge, test & replace capacitor |
| Fan totally dead, compressor also silent | Contactor, fuse, or lost 24V | Check breakers/disconnect | Test contactor coil & control power |
| Blade won't turn by hand | Seized motor bearings | Leave off, don't force | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Fan blade blocked/rubbing | Debris or bent blade | Clear debris with power off | Straighten/replace blade or motor |
| Fan starts then stops after minutes | Motor overheating on thermal | Turn off, let cool | Replace failing fan motor |
| No fan on variable-speed unit | Control board/relay fault | Note any board LED codes | Diagnose board, replace if faulted |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts + labor:

- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor replacement:** $350 – $650 (higher for ECM/variable-speed motors)
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $300
- **Control board (ECM/variable-speed units):** $400 – $900+
- **Debris cleared / no parts:** $0 DIY, or a $75 – $150 service call minimum if you call someone

Capacitor and contactor jobs are the most common and cheapest. A motor swap is the big-ticket item. If a tech recommends replacing the whole condenser over a fan motor on an older unit, get a second opinion.
