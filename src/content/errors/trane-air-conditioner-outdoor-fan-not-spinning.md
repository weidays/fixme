## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a numeric fault code — it's a physical symptom on your Trane outdoor unit (the condenser). The fan on top of the condenser pulls air across the coil to reject the heat your system removes from your home. When the compressor is running but that fan sits still, heat has nowhere to go.

This is a **pro-level** problem for one important reason: continuing to run the compressor without the fan will overheat it and can trigger an expensive compressor failure. Most causes involve high-voltage components — a run capacitor, contactor, or fan motor — that store or carry lethal voltage and should be handled by a qualified technician.

On single-stage Trane units (XR/XL series) the fan is usually a PSC motor driven by a dual run capacitor. On variable-speed units (XV18, XV20i) the fan is a communicating ECM motor controlled by the board, so diagnosis differs — say so to any tech who quotes you.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor** — the single most common cause. The capacitor gives the fan motor the jolt it needs to start. A weak one causes hum-but-no-spin or a fan that only turns after a push.
2. **Seized or burned-out condenser fan motor** — bearings fail or windings burn out, especially on older units. The motor won't turn even with a good capacitor.
3. **Contactor not pulling in** — the relay that sends power to the outdoor unit can fail or have pitted contacts, so the fan (and often the compressor) never gets power.
4. **Debris jamming the fan blade** — leaves, sticks, or a bent blade physically blocking rotation.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown disconnect fuse** — no power reaching the outdoor unit at all.
6. **Control board / ECM fault (variable-speed units)** — on XV-series units, a communicating fan module or board fault can stop the ECM motor.
7. **Low-voltage / thermostat call issue** — the unit isn't getting the signal to start in the first place.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do **not** open the electrical panel on the condenser or touch the capacitor — it can hold a dangerous charge even with power off.

- **Thermostat:** Set it to Cool and lower the target several degrees so there's a clear call for cooling. Replace thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause the indoor coil to freeze and confuse symptoms. Replace if dirty.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor and outdoor AC breakers in your main panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure they're open and unblocked.
- **Look (don't touch) at the outdoor unit:** With the system OFF, look through the top grille for obvious debris on the fan blade. Clear leaves and sticks from around the unit.
- **Condensate:** Check that the indoor drain line isn't overflowing, which can trip a safety float switch and shut the system down.

If the fan still won't spin after these checks, turn the system off at the thermostat and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical high-voltage checklist — this helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirms the call and power** — verifies 24V call at the contactor and 208/230V line voltage into the outdoor unit.
2. **Tests the run capacitor** — measures microfarad (µF) rating against the label spec. A capacitor reading well below spec is replaced.
3. **Checks the contactor** — looks for pitted contacts and confirms it pulls in and passes voltage to the fan and compressor.
4. **Tests the fan motor** — checks windings for continuity/shorts and spins the blade by hand (power off) to feel for seized bearings.
5. **Inspects wiring** — for burnt, loose, or rodent-damaged connections at the motor and contactor.
6. **On variable-speed (XV) units** — reads fault codes from the board/communicating thermostat and tests the ECM fan module, which is a different (and pricier) part than a PSC motor.

Beware a quote that jumps straight to a full motor replacement without first testing the much cheaper capacitor.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan doesn't spin | Failed run capacitor | Shut off AC to protect compressor | Test µF, replace capacitor |
| Fan only spins after a push | Weak run capacitor | Stop running the unit | Replace capacitor |
| No sound at all from unit | Contactor not engaging or no power | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor, check voltage |
| Fan blade won't turn by hand | Seized fan motor or debris | Clear visible debris (power off) | Replace fan motor |
| Breaker trips when AC runs | Shorted motor or wiring fault | Do not keep resetting | Diagnose short, replace faulty part |
| Fault code on XV thermostat | ECM/board communication fault | Note the code | Read code, test ECM module/board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included. Prices vary by region and by whether you have a standard or variable-speed Trane.

- **Run capacitor:** $150–$300
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC, standard units):** $350–$650
- **ECM fan motor / module (variable-speed XV units):** $600–$1,200+
- **Control board (variable-speed):** $500–$1,000+
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90–$200 (often applied to the repair)
- **DIY:** $0 for clearing debris or resetting a breaker

If your unit is 12+ years old and needs a motor or board, ask your tech to weigh repair cost against replacement.
