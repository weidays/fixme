## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a blink code — it's a physical symptom on your Trane condenser (the outdoor unit). When your AC calls for cooling, the outdoor unit should power up the compressor **and** the outdoor fan together. That fan pulls air across the condenser coil to reject the heat your system pulled out of your house.

If the fan blade sits still while the unit hums, buzzes, or the compressor runs, the system can't shed heat. Pressures and temperatures climb quickly, and Trane's compressor overload or high-pressure protection will shut the unit down — sometimes before you notice anything but weak or no cooling indoors.

On variable-speed **XV** models, the fan is driven by an ECM/inverter board and may report a fault differently than the simpler PSC-motor units in the **XR** and **XL** lines. Because a non-spinning fan risks compressor damage, this is a **pro-level** repair for most homeowners.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor.** The single most common cause. The fan (and often compressor) share a dual run capacitor. When the fan side weakens or fails, the motor hums but won't spin up.
2. **Condenser fan motor failure.** Worn bearings, an open winding, or a burned-out motor. Sometimes the motor is hot to the touch or spins roughly by feel (power off).
3. **Contactor not pulling in or burned contacts.** Pitted or stuck contacts can drop power to the fan and compressor circuit.
4. **Obstructed or seized blade.** Debris, ice, a bent blade, or a rusted-out motor shaft physically blocks rotation.
5. **Control board or relay fault.** More common on **XV** variable-speed units with an inverter/ECM board; the board may command the fan and not receive feedback.
6. **Loose, corroded, or chewed wiring.** Rodent damage or a loose fan lead at the capacitor or motor.
7. **Blown low-voltage fuse or failed thermostat call** — less likely if the compressor itself is running.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do only these. Everything inside the condenser is line-voltage and dangerous — leave it for a technician.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause freeze-ups and odd shutdowns. Replace if dirty.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor furnace/air-handler breaker and the outdoor condenser breaker/disconnect. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure they're open and unblocked indoors.
- **Look (don't touch) at the outdoor unit:** With the system OFF, look for obvious debris, leaves, or an object jamming the fan blade, or ice on the coil.
- **Condensate:** Check that the indoor condensate drain isn't overflowing and tripping a safety float switch.

If the fan still won't spin after these, shut the system off at the thermostat and call a technician. Running it risks compressor damage.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

- Confirm the 24V call is reaching the outdoor unit and the **contactor** pulls in.
- Kill power and **test the run capacitor** with a meter against its rated microfarads (the printed uF value); a reading well below spec explains a humming fan.
- Check the **fan motor windings** for continuity/resistance and test for an open or shorted winding.
- Manually check the blade for a **seized bearing** or bent blade (power off).
- Inspect the **contactor** contacts for pitting and verify voltage on both sides.
- On **XV/variable-speed** units, read fault codes at the inverter/control board and check ECM feedback.
- Verify wiring, low-voltage fusing, and that the compressor and fan share/receive proper voltage.

Sanity check a quote: they should tell you *what* they measured (capacitor uF, motor amps/ohms) — not just "the fan's bad."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit hums, fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Shut off, don't hand-start | Test & replace capacitor |
| No hum, compressor also silent | Contactor or no 24V call | Reset breaker once, check thermostat | Test contactor & control circuit |
| Fan spins slow, stalls, or hot motor | Failing fan motor/bearings | Turn system off | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Blade physically jammed | Debris or bent/seized blade | Remove visible debris (power off) | Free/replace blade or motor |
| Fan dead on XV/variable unit | ECM/inverter board fault | Note any board codes | Read codes, test board/motor |
| Breaker trips when fan should run | Shorted motor or wiring fault | Don't keep resetting | Locate short, replace component |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Run capacitor:** $150–$300
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor (PSC, XR/XL):** $300–$650
- **Fan blade replacement:** $100–$250
- **ECM/inverter or control board (XV units):** $400–$900+
- **Wiring/fuse repair:** $100–$300
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$180 (often credited toward the repair)

Prices vary by region, motor horsepower, and whether your model is under Trane's parts warranty. A capacitor is the cheapest and most common fix — always worth ruling out first.
