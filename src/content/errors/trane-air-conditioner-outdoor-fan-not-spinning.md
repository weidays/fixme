## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom rather than a specific Trane fault code — most single-stage Trane condensers have no display and simply fail to run the fan. The outdoor (condenser) fan pulls air across the coil to reject heat picked up indoors. When it stops, the compressor overheats fast, the coil pressure spikes, and the unit will eventually trip on its internal high-pressure or thermal limit.

On two-stage and variable-speed Trane systems (XL and XV series with a communicating board or ECM fan motor), a fan fault may show up as a diagnostic light pattern or a message at the indoor control. **The exact code varies by board and model**, so read your unit's service label or the fault list in the installer manual rather than assuming a generic meaning.

The bottom line: if the compressor hums or runs but the top fan blade sits still, turn the system off and treat this as a pro repair.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run/dual capacitor** — by far the most common cause. The capacitor gives the fan motor its starting torque. When it weakens, the fan won't start (or only starts with a push).
2. **Burned-out condenser fan motor** — bearings seize or windings fail, especially on older units or after a capacitor has run weak for a while.
3. **Contactor not pulling in or welded** — the relay that sends power to the fan and compressor can fail open or stick.
4. **Debris or ice jamming the blade** — sticks, leaves, or a bent blade physically block rotation.
5. **Blown fuse, tripped breaker, or lost 240V leg** — the fan gets no power.
6. **Failed control/fan board or bad ECM module** (two-stage/variable-speed units) — the board isn't commanding the fan.
7. **Low-voltage or thermostat wiring fault** — the contactor never receives the 24V call.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do. **Do not open the condenser electrical panel** — capacitors store a lethal charge even with power off.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor air-handler breaker and the outdoor condenser breaker/disconnect. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause the indoor unit to freeze and shut things down. Replace if dirty.
- **Visible obstructions:** With the system OFF at the thermostat, look through the top grille for leaves, sticks, or a bird nest. Clear only what you can reach without removing panels.
- **Ice:** If the outdoor unit or refrigerant lines are iced over, turn the AC off and set the fan to ON at the thermostat to thaw it. Persistent icing points to airflow or refrigerant problems needing a pro.
- **Condensate:** Check the indoor drain pan/line isn't overflowing and tripping a float switch.

If the fan still won't spin after these checks, shut the system off and schedule service.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. **Confirm the call for cooling** and verify 24V is reaching the contactor.
2. **Discharge and test the capacitor** with a meter, comparing measured microfarads to the rating printed on the can (e.g., 45+5 µF). A reading well below spec means replacement.
3. **Check the contactor** — measuring whether it pulls in and passes line voltage to the fan and compressor; inspecting for pitted or welded contacts.
4. **Test the fan motor** — checking winding resistance, amp draw, and whether it spins freely by hand (power off).
5. **On two-stage/variable-speed units**, retrieve fault codes from the board and check the ECM module and communication.
6. **Verify incoming power** — both 240V legs and any line fuses in the disconnect.

Sanity check: a good tech confirms the capacitor and contactor before condemning the more expensive motor. Being told you need a new motor without a capacitor test is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums, fan sits still | Dead/weak run capacitor | Turn AC off; don't push blade | Discharge & replace capacitor, test motor |
| Fan starts only if nudged | Weak capacitor | Turn off; schedule service | Replace dual capacitor |
| Nothing runs outdoors, no hum | No power / tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test disconnect, fuses, contactor |
| Blade physically stuck | Debris or seized bearing | Clear visible debris (power off) | Replace motor if bearings seized |
| Fan and unit iced over | Airflow/refrigerant issue | Filter change, thaw with fan ON | Check charge, coil, airflow |
| Two-stage unit shows fault light | Control/ECM board fault | Note the light pattern | Read code, test board/ECM |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your area and unit's age affect the final number.

- **Run/dual capacitor replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Condenser fan motor replacement:** $350 – $650 (more for OEM ECM/variable-speed motors)
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Control/fan board (two-stage/variable-speed):** $400 – $900+
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80 – $180 (often credited toward the repair)

If the unit is old (12–15+ years) and the failure is a costly motor or board, ask whether repair still makes sense versus replacement.
