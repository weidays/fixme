## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a blink-code — it's a physical symptom on your Trane condenser (the outdoor unit). The compressor may still be running while the top fan sits still, or the whole unit may be silent. Either way, the fan that pulls heat out of the refrigerant has stopped moving air across the coil.

This matters because the outdoor fan is what rejects the heat your AC pulls out of your house. Without it, head pressure and compressor temperature spike within minutes. A stopped fan is a **stop-using-it** situation until diagnosed.

On single-stage Trane units (XR, XL series), the fan is a PSC motor driven by a run capacitor. On variable-speed units (XV18, XV20i), the fan is an ECM motor controlled by an inverter/control board, so the failure points and diagnostics differ — we note this where it matters.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor** — By far the most common. The capacitor gives the fan motor the phase kick it needs to start. When it weakens, the fan hums but won't spin, or won't start at all. Affects PSC-motor XR/XL units most.
2. **Seized or failed fan motor** — Worn bearings, a burned winding, or an overheated motor that's tripped its internal overload. Sometimes a nudge starts it (a classic sign of a weak capacitor or dying motor).
3. **Bad contactor** — The relay that sends power to the outdoor unit can pit, stick, or fail to pull in, cutting power to the fan (and often the compressor too).
4. **Blade physically jammed** — Debris, a warped blade, or ice/mud lodged against the fan can stop it mechanically.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — No power to the condenser at all.
6. **Control/inverter board fault** — On XV variable-speed units, an ECM fan that won't spin often points to the board or motor module, not a simple capacitor.
7. **Overheated compressor lockout** — Some units cut power broadly on a high-limit trip, and the fan stops with everything else.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe with no tools beyond a screwdriver — **do not** open the electrical panel on the condenser or touch the capacitor.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause freeze-ups and odd shutdowns. Replace it if dirty.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor furnace/air handler breaker and the outdoor AC breaker. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** The pull-out box near the condenser should be fully seated.
- **Visible debris:** With the unit **off at the breaker**, look through the top grille for leaves, sticks, or a bird's nest jamming the blade. Clear anything obvious — do not force the blade or reach past it while powered.
- **Vents:** Make sure indoor supply and return vents aren't blocked.

If the fan hums, buzzes, or the unit clicks but the fan won't turn — turn the system off at the thermostat and call a technician. Running it damages the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

1. **Verify power and voltage** at the disconnect and contactor (line and 24V control).
2. **Test the run capacitor** with a meter — a weak reading (mfd well below the rating stamped on the case) confirms the most common cause.
3. **Check the contactor** for pitted or stuck contacts and confirm the coil pulls in.
4. **Test the fan motor** — resistance across windings, spin-by-hand feel for seized bearings, and whether the internal overload has tripped.
5. **On XV variable-speed units,** read fault data from the inverter/control board and test the ECM motor module rather than looking for a capacitor.
6. **Check head pressure and compressor amps** to confirm no secondary damage occurred.

A reasonable quote names the specific failed part (capacitor, motor, or contactor) and the tested value. Be cautious of a "replace the whole unit" quote without a capacitor test first — that's the cheapest, most common fix.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums, won't spin (nudge starts it) | Weak/failed run capacitor | Shut off; don't run it | Test & replace capacitor |
| Fan silent, compressor also silent | Contactor, breaker, or lost power | Reset breaker once; check disconnect | Test contactor & control voltage |
| Fan tries to start, then trips overload | Failing fan motor | Turn off to avoid damage | Replace condenser fan motor |
| Blade visibly jammed by debris | Mechanical obstruction | Power off, clear obvious debris | Inspect blade/bearing for damage |
| XV unit fan won't run, board light odd | Inverter/control board or ECM motor | Note any fault lights | Read board faults, test ECM module |
| Breaker trips every time | Short in motor or wiring | Do not keep resetting | Locate short, replace faulty part |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts + labor. Regional and after-hours rates vary.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $75 – $200
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $400 (part is ~$15–$40; most cost is labor and trip)
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Condenser fan motor replacement:** $300 – $650 (PSC motor on XR/XL units)
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150 – $350
- **ECM motor / inverter board (XV variable-speed):** $600 – $1,500+ — significantly more than single-stage parts
- **Clearing a jam only:** $0 DIY if you catch obvious debris with the power off

If your unit is out of warranty and the fan motor plus other components are failing, get a second opinion before agreeing to major work.
