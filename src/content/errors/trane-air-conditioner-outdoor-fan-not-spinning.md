## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" isn't a Trane fault code — it's a symptom. On most Trane split systems, the outdoor unit (the condenser) has a fan that pulls air across the coil to dump heat outside. If the compressor is running but that top-mounted fan sits still, the system can't reject heat and pressures spike quickly.

On single-stage Trane units (many XR and XL models), the fan is a PSC motor driven directly through a contactor and capacitor. On two-stage and variable-speed units (XV, some XL), the fan is often an ECM motor controlled by a communicating board — so a "not spinning" fan there may point to a board or communication fault rather than a simple capacitor.

**Turn the system off at the thermostat now.** Running the compressor with a dead fan is the fastest way to a compressor failure.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run/start capacitor** — By far the most common. The dual capacitor serves both compressor and fan; a weak fan section leaves the motor humming but not starting.
2. **Seized or failed condenser fan motor** — Worn bearings or a burned winding. Often smells hot or the motor is too hot to touch.
3. **Contactor not pulling in** — A pitted, stuck, or non-energizing contactor cuts power to the fan and compressor together.
4. **Debris jamming the fan blade** — Leaves, a stick, ice, or a bird's nest physically blocking the blade.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — No power to the outdoor unit at all.
6. **Failed control board / ECM module (variable-speed units)** — On XV and communicating systems, the board or motor module can fault out.
7. **Loose or corroded wiring** — Especially the fan motor leads at the capacitor terminals.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner:

- **Thermostat:** Set it to Cool, several degrees below room temp, and confirm the display is on (replace batteries if it's a battery model).
- **Breakers:** Check the indoor air-handler breaker and the outdoor disconnect/breaker. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Air filter:** A clogged filter can cause the indoor system to freeze and mask outdoor behavior — replace if dirty.
- **Look for obvious debris:** With the system **off at the breaker**, look through the fan grille for leaves, sticks, or a nest. Remove visible debris only — do not reach into or force the blade with power on.
- **Ice:** If the outdoor coil or lines are iced, turn the system off and let it thaw; run the indoor fan only.

**Do not** open the electrical panel of the condenser, discharge or touch a capacitor, or bypass the contactor. Capacitors hold a dangerous charge even with power off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A licensed tech will typically:

1. Confirm 24V control signal is reaching the outdoor unit and the contactor is pulling in.
2. Verify high-voltage (208–240V) is present at the contactor load side.
3. **Test the capacitor** with a meter against its rated microfarads (µF) — the number one culprit.
4. Perform the "spin test": if the fan starts when nudged, it confirms a start/capacitor issue rather than a dead motor.
5. Check motor windings for continuity and a short to ground; feel for seized bearings.
6. On variable-speed/communicating units, read fault history from the control board and check the ECM motor module and comm wiring.
7. Inspect wiring, terminals, and the contactor contacts for pitting or corrosion.

A quote that jumps straight to a motor or compressor without a capacitor test is a red flag on a single-stage unit.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan hums but doesn't spin; starts if nudged | Failed capacitor | Shut off, don't nudge repeatedly | Test µF, replace dual run capacitor |
| No hum, fan and compressor both dead | Contactor or power loss | Reset breaker once; check disconnect | Test contactor coil/contacts, replace if bad |
| Fan grinds, hot smell, or won't turn by hand | Seized fan motor | Turn off system | Replace condenser fan motor & capacitor |
| Blade physically blocked | Debris/nest/ice | Power off, remove visible debris | Clear jam, inspect blade balance |
| Whole outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker / blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Find cause of trip, replace 3A/5A fuse |
| Variable-speed unit faults, fan dead | ECM/board fault | Note any indoor error display | Read board codes, test ECM module/board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Dual run capacitor:** $150–$350
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor (single-stage PSC):** $400–$700+
- **ECM fan motor / module (variable-speed):** $600–$1,200+
- **Fan blade replacement:** $150–$350
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80–$180 (often applied to repair)
- **Low-voltage fuse or wiring repair:** $100–$250

Prices vary by region, unit age, and part availability. If your Trane is under 10 years old, check whether the part is still under Trane's parts warranty — you may pay labor only.
