## What this code means

"Won't turn on" is not a stored fault code. It describes a Goodman heat pump that shows no response when the thermostat calls for heating or cooling. You hear no indoor blower and no outdoor unit, and the thermostat may be blank.

On a Goodman split system, the indoor air handler holds the 24-volt transformer. That transformer powers the thermostat and the outdoor unit's contactor. So a no-start condition almost always traces back to one of these:

- Line-voltage power
- The 24V control circuit
- A safety switch that interrupts that circuit
- The thermostat

How the system recovers depends on the cause:

- **Condensate float switches** reset automatically once the water drains.
- **Blown fuses and failed transformers** stay dead until a technician repairs them.
- **Communicating systems** (ComfortBridge/ComfortNet) may show a fault on the thermostat or on the board's LED/7-segment display. What those displays show varies by model, so check the wiring diagram label inside your unit's panel.

If only the outdoor unit is dead and the indoor fan runs, the problem is narrower. See "Goodman Heat Pump Compressor Not Running."

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat problem.** The batteries are dead, the thermostat is set to the wrong mode, the setpoint isn't calling, or a wire has come loose.
2. **Tripped breaker or off disconnect.** This can be the air handler breaker, the outdoor unit breaker, the indoor service switch, or the outdoor pull-out disconnect.
3. **Condensate overflow/float switch tripped.** A clogged drain backs water up and the float switch opens the 24V circuit.
4. **Blown 3- or 5-amp low-voltage fuse on the air handler control board.** This is often caused by a shorted thermostat wire, for example a chewed or nicked wire running to the outdoor unit.
5. **Failed 24V transformer.**
6. **Blower door switch open.** This happens when the air handler panel isn't fully seated.
7. **Failed air handler control board, or a communication fault** on ComfortBridge/ComfortNet systems.
8. **Loose or burned high-voltage wiring connections.**

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Thermostat:**
   - Replace the batteries.
   - Confirm the mode is HEAT or COOL and the fan is set to AUTO.
   - Set the temperature at least 3°F beyond room temperature.
   - Wait 5 minutes, because many thermostats and boards have a built-in compressor delay.
2. **Breakers:**
   - Find the breakers for both the air handler and the outdoor heat pump.
   - Reset any tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
3. **Switches:**
   - Make sure the light-style service switch near the indoor unit is ON.
   - Make sure the outdoor disconnect is fully inserted in the ON position.
4. **Air filter:** Replace it if it's dirty. A badly clogged filter won't usually cause a dead system, but it adds stress to every part.
5. **Condensate drain:**
   - Look for water in the secondary pan or for a tripped float switch.
   - Clear the drain line at its accessible cleanout, using a wet/dry vacuum at the outside drain end.
6. **Panels:** Make sure the air handler's front panels are fully seated so the door switch closes. Do not open the cabinet.
7. **Vents:** Confirm the supply and return registers are open and unblocked.

If none of these checks restores operation, call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Line voltage:** Measures incoming power (about 240V) at the air handler and the outdoor unit.
- **24V circuit:** Checks the transformer's 24V output and the low-voltage fuse on the board.
- **Short hunting:** If the fuse is blown, finds the short before replacing the fuse. That means checking the thermostat cable and the outdoor low-voltage wiring.
- **Safety switches:** Tests the door switch and the float switch for continuity.
- **Thermostat:** Jumpers the R-G/R-Y terminals at the board to separate a thermostat fault from an equipment fault.
- **Control board:** Reads the board's LED fault indicators and inspects the board.
- **Communicating systems:** On ComfortBridge/ComfortNet units, checks the data wires and fault history.
- **Connections:** Inspects the contactor and wiring for burned connections.

A good quote names the specific failed part and the test that proved it failed. "Needs a new board" is not enough without a stated test.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat screen blank | Dead batteries, blown fuse or bad transformer | Replace batteries, check breaker | Test transformer and fuse, find short |
| Thermostat on, nothing runs | Float switch, door switch or thermostat fault | Clear condensate drain, seat panels | Test switches, jumper thermostat terminals |
| Breaker tripped | Electrical short or overload | Reset once only | Find shorted component or wiring |
| Works briefly, then dies again | Intermittent short blowing the fuse | Note when it happens | Trace and repair wiring, replace fuse |
| Water in pan, system off | Clogged condensate line | Clear drain line | Clean drain, verify float switch |
| Error on communicating thermostat | Board or communication fault | Note the code, power cycle once | Diagnose board and data wiring |
