## What this code means

A blinking light on a Trane mini-split indoor unit is the system's way of reporting a status or a fault. Depending on the model, you may see:

- the run, timer or heat LED flashing, or
- an alphanumeric code on the display panel.

Trane ductless lines have been built on different platforms over the years. As a result, the LED behavior and code tables are **not uniform across series**. The same blink count can mean different things on different models. Your exact model number, found on the label on the side of the indoor unit, and its service manual are the only reliable decoder.

Broadly, blinking falls into two groups:

1. **Normal status indication.** Examples are defrost, cold-air prevention during heat-mode warm-up, and mode conflict on multi-zone systems.
2. **Protection or fault codes.** These cover indoor-to-outdoor communication loss, failed temperature sensors, fan motor faults, and high or low pressure or temperature protection.

How the light resets depends on the type:

- **Self-clearing:** many protection modes reset once conditions normalize.
- **Hold until repaired:** communication, sensor and motor faults generally stay active until fixed. Some boards also need a power cycle.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost or warm-up (heat mode).** The heat or run light blinks while the outdoor coil defrosts, or while the indoor coil warms before the fan starts. It clears within minutes.
2. **Mode conflict (multi-zone only).** Indoor heads are set to opposing modes, heat versus cool or dry. The head that loses the conflict blinks and stands by.
3. **Communication error between the indoor and outdoor units.** Common causes are:
   - loose or miswired interconnect wiring,
   - a corroded terminal,
   - a power interruption, or
   - a failing control board.

   This is one of the most frequent true faults.
4. **Temperature sensor (thermistor) fault.** An indoor room, indoor coil, outdoor coil or discharge sensor is open or shorted.
5. **Protection trip from airflow or heat-transfer problems.** A clogged indoor filter or dirty coil can cause freeze protection in cooling or overheat protection in heating.
6. **Indoor or outdoor fan motor fault.** The board detects no fan feedback or an abnormal fan speed.
7. **Refrigerant or pressure-related protection.** Low charge from a leak or a high-pressure trip causes the unit to shut down.
8. **Voltage or current protection.** Low or high supply voltage, or an inverter or compressor overcurrent, triggers protection.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Wait 15 minutes in heat mode.** If the blinking stops and warm air comes out, it was defrost or warm-up.
- **Check mode settings on every head (multi-zone).** Set all zones to the same mode, either all heat or all cool.
- **Replace the remote batteries** and confirm the mode, setpoint and fan settings are what you intended.
- **Clean the indoor unit's washable filters.**
  1. Lift the front panel.
  2. Slide the filters out.
  3. Rinse them, let them dry fully, and reinstall them.
- **Check the breaker and outdoor disconnect.** If either has tripped, do **one** reset by switching the breaker off, waiting 5 minutes, then switching it back on. If the blinking returns, stop and call a technician.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit and indoor head.** Remove snow, leaves and furniture that block airflow.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Make sure the drain line exits freely and isn't kinked or clogged at the outlet.

Write down the blink count or display code before you call. It speeds up diagnosis and helps you check the quote you get.

## How a technician will diagnose it

1. Read the blink count or display code and match it to the error table for your specific model. A good tech may also pull fault history through the board's diagnostic mode.
2. Verify the supply voltage at the outdoor unit and the communication voltage across the interconnect terminals.
3. Inspect the interconnect wiring for loose, corroded or miswired terminals.
4. Measure the thermistors' resistance against the manufacturer's temperature/resistance chart.
5. Check the indoor and outdoor fan motors for proper operation and speed feedback.
6. Inspect the coils for dirt and measure pressures and temperatures if a refrigerant-related code is present. Any leak repair requires EPA-certified work.
7. Replace a control board only after the wiring, sensors and power supply check out. Be cautious of a quote that jumps straight to a board without these tests.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heat light blinks, no airflow for a few minutes | Defrost or cold-air prevention | Wait 10–15 minutes | None if it clears |
| One multi-zone head blinks, others run | Mode conflict | Set all heads to the same mode | Check zone wiring if it persists |
| Blinking with code, unit won't start at all | Indoor/outdoor communication error | One breaker reset | Test interconnect wiring, voltage and boards |
| Runs briefly, then stops and blinks | Freeze or overheat protection | Clean filters, clear airflow | Clean coils, check charge |
| Blinking with sensor-type code | Failed thermistor | None | Test and replace the sensor |
| Fan doesn't spin, light blinks | Fan motor fault | Confirm power, one reset | Test and replace the fan motor or board |
| Blinks with weak cooling or heating, ice on lines | Low refrigerant or pressure protection | Turn off and call | Leak search, repair, recharge |
