## What this code means

American Standard does not use a single, universal "blinking light" code across its mini-splits. When an LED on the indoor head flashes in a repeating pattern, the unit's self-diagnostics have usually detected a fault and stopped or limited operation to protect itself.

What the blinks mean depends on two things:

- **The model:** which LED is flashing (operation, timer or run light) and how many times it blinks between pauses.
- **The display:** many heads also show an alphanumeric code (such as E-series or P-series) on the display or the remote.

Model-specific tables are in the service manual for your exact model number. Use the steps below to separate normal behavior from a real fault.

**Normal blinking you can ignore:**
- In heat mode, a slow blink often means **defrost or anti-cold-air pre-heat**. The fan stays off until the coil warms up, and the light stops blinking when normal heating resumes.

**Reset behavior:**
- Temporary protection trips (high pressure, high or low temperature, over-current) often clear automatically once conditions normalize.
- Sensor, communication and board faults usually persist or return after a power cycle until they are repaired.

Because the causes are mostly electronic or refrigerant-related, this is a **professional repair** in nearly all cases.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

This ranking reflects typical service-manual troubleshooting order and how often each fault shows up in the field. Exact codes vary by model.

1. **Normal defrost or pre-heat indication.** A slow blink in heating mode with no fan is not a fault.
2. **Indoor–outdoor communication error.** This is caused by a loose or damaged interconnect wire, poor terminal connections, a power interruption at the outdoor unit, or a failed board.
3. **Temperature sensor (thermistor) fault.** The room, indoor coil, outdoor coil, discharge or ambient sensor is open or shorted.
4. **Protection trip.** This can be high-pressure or overheat protection (often from very dirty filters or coils, or a blocked outdoor unit), over-current, or compressor discharge temperature protection.
5. **Indoor fan motor fault.** The control board is not receiving speed feedback from the blower motor.
6. **Condensate or float-switch fault.** This applies only to models with a float switch or pump, which are more common on ceiling cassettes and ducted heads than on wall units.
7. **Refrigerant-related protection.** Low charge from a leak can trigger temperature or pressure protection.
8. **Failed indoor or outdoor control or inverter board,** including the IPM module.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Is it defrost?** In heat mode, wait 10–15 minutes. If the blinking stops and warm air starts, the unit is working normally.
- **Remote and mode:** Replace the remote batteries. Confirm the mode (cool, heat, auto) and the set temperature are what you intend.
- **Filters:** Lift the front panel of the indoor head, slide out the mesh filters, then rinse, dry and reinstall them. Very dirty filters can cause overheat protection trips.
- **Breaker and disconnect:** Make sure the breaker isn't tripped and the outdoor disconnect switch is on. If the breaker trips again right away, stop and call a technician.
- **One reset only:** Turn the unit off at the remote, cut power at the breaker for about 5 minutes, restore it and restart. If the blink pattern returns, write down the count and which LED is flashing. Don't reset it again.
- **Outdoor unit clearance:** Remove leaves, snow, ice buildup or debris around the outdoor unit. Keep about 2 ft clear on all sides, and don't spray or open the unit.
- **Condensate:** Check that the drain hose outlet outside is not crushed, kinked or clogged with debris, and that water isn't dripping from the indoor head.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified technician will typically:

- Read the blink count or display code, plus the outdoor board's LED, against the model's service manual.
- Check supply voltage at the outdoor unit and the indoor–outdoor communication voltage at the terminal block.
- Inspect the interconnect wiring for loose, corroded or miswired terminals.
- Unplug the suspected thermistors and measure their resistance against the manufacturer's temperature-resistance chart.
- Check indoor fan motor operation and its feedback signal.
- Measure system pressures and temperatures, and leak-check if refrigerant protection is suspected.
- Test the compressor windings and the IPM or inverter board before condemning major parts.

**Sanity-checking a quote:** a good quote names the specific code or blink pattern and the confirmed failed part. Be wary of a "replace the board" quote with no wiring or sensor testing first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slow blink in heat mode, no fan, then warm air | Defrost or pre-heat (normal) | Wait 10–15 minutes | None needed |
| Blinking right after power-up, outdoor unit never starts | Communication error | Check the breaker and disconnect; one power reset | Test interconnect wiring and terminals, check comm voltage, replace a board if needed |
| Repeating blink count, unit stops quickly | Thermistor fault | One power reset; record the pattern | Measure and replace the sensor |
| Runs, then shuts off with blinking in hot or cold weather | High-pressure or overheat protection | Clean filters; clear around the outdoor unit | Clean coils, check the fan, check the charge |
| Blinking, no indoor airflow | Indoor fan motor or feedback fault | One power reset | Test and replace the fan motor or board |
| Blinking plus water at the indoor head | Drain or float-switch fault | Check the outside drain outlet for blockage | Clear the drain or pump, test the float switch |
| Weak performance, then blinking, possible ice | Low refrigerant / leak | Turn the unit off; don't reset repeatedly | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Blinking persists after every check | Control or inverter board failure | Call for service | Diagnose and replace the board or IPM |
