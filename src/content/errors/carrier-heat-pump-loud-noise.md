## What this code means

"Loud noise" is not a Carrier fault code. It is a symptom, and the sound itself is your best clue.

**Sounds that are normal by design:**

- **Defrost whoosh.** In winter, the reversing valve shifts to defrost the outdoor coil. You may hear a whoosh or hiss and a brief rattle while the fan stops. Steam may rise from the unit. This lasts a few minutes and then the unit returns to heating.
- **Startup hum.** A short hum when the compressor starts is expected.

**Sounds that are not normal:**

- Grinding, screeching or squealing
- Banging or rattling that continues
- A loud buzz with nothing turning
- Constant shrieking

How loud is "normal" varies by model:

- **Variable-speed Infinity and Greenspeed units** ramp up gently and tend to be quieter.
- **Single-stage Comfort and Performance units** start and stop more abruptly and can sound harsher.

There is nothing to reset for noise. Infinity systems may store related faults, such as outdoor fan, compressor or pressure faults, on the thermostat or outdoor control board.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost cycle.** Whoosh, hiss and steam lasting a few minutes in cold, damp weather. No repair needed.
2. **Debris or ice in the outdoor unit.** Sticks, leaves or ice can hit the fan blade and cause rattling or ticking.
3. **Failing outdoor fan motor bearings or a bent or loose fan blade.** Causes squealing, grinding or wobbling noise.
4. **Loose panels, fasteners, or a refrigerant line vibrating against the cabinet or wall.** Causes rattling or buzzing.
5. **Worn or failing contactor.** Causes a loud buzz or chatter at the outdoor unit.
6. **Weak or failed run capacitor.** Causes a loud hum or buzz, with the fan or compressor struggling to start or not starting.
7. **Compressor problems.** These include broken internal mounts, worn bearings, hard starting, or a slug of liquid refrigerant. Sounds include banging, clanking, knocking, or a constant rattle or growl. Hard starting may be linked to refrigerant charge or a failed TXV.
8. **Reversing valve problems.** Abnormal, constant hissing or shrieking outside of defrost.
9. **Indoor blower problems.** A failing indoor blower can screech or rattle at the air handler, even when the outdoor unit sounds fine.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

**First, identify the sound.**

- If it is a whoosh or hiss lasting under about 10 minutes in winter, with steam, it is likely defrost. Watch whether heating resumes afterward.
- If you hear grinding, screeching, banging or constant buzzing, set the thermostat to **OFF** and leave the system off.

**Then work through these checks:**

1. **Thermostat.** Confirm the mode and setpoint are correct. If it uses batteries, replace them. Erratic cycling can cause frequent startup noise.
2. **Air filter.** Replace a dirty filter. A clogged filter can make the indoor blower roar or whistle.
3. **Vents and registers.** Open closed supply registers and clear blocked return grilles. Restricted airflow causes whistling.
4. **Look at the outdoor unit from outside the cabinet.**
   - Clear leaves, sticks and snow from around and on top of the unit.
   - Keep about 2 feet of clearance around it.
   - Do not reach into the fan grille.
5. **Exterior panels.** If an access panel is visibly loose, press it back into place so it is properly seated. Do not remove panels.
6. **Breaker.** If the unit stopped and a breaker tripped, you may reset it **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician should do most of the following. Use this list to sanity-check a quote.

- **Listen with the panels on and off** to locate the source: fan, compressor, contactor, lines or reversing valve.
- **Check the outdoor fan motor.** Spin it by hand with power off to feel for bearing drag. Inspect the blade and set screw.
- **Test the run capacitor** with a meter and compare it to its rated microfarad value.
- **Inspect the contactor** for pitting and chatter.
- **Measure compressor amperage** and compare it to the nameplate RLA and LRA. They may also check for hard starting.
- **Read refrigerant pressures, superheat and subcooling.** Noise from liquid slugging or an overcharge shows up here.
- **Watch a defrost cycle** to confirm the reversing valve shifts cleanly.
- **Pull stored fault codes** from the Infinity thermostat or the outdoor board's LED history.
- **Check refrigerant lines and mounts** for rub points and vibration.

Be cautious if a technician recommends a compressor replacement without amp readings and pressure data.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whoosh or hiss for a few minutes in winter, with steam | Normal defrost | None; confirm heating resumes | None needed |
| Rattle or ticking at the outdoor fan | Debris or ice touching the blade | Clear debris from outside; turn off if it continues | Remove debris, inspect and straighten the blade |
| Squeal or grinding from the top of the outdoor unit | Fan motor bearings | Turn the system off | Replace the fan motor (and capacitor if weak) |
| Loud buzz, nothing spinning | Failed capacitor or contactor | Turn off at the thermostat | Test and replace the capacitor or contactor |
| Banging or clanking from the compressor | Broken mounts, slugging, internal wear | Turn the system off | Check amps and pressures; repair or replace the compressor |
| Constant rattle or vibration | Loose panel, screws, or lines rubbing | Seat loose exterior panels | Tighten hardware, isolate lines, add pads |
| Shriek or hiss outside of defrost | Reversing valve or refrigerant issue | Turn off | Diagnose the valve and charge; repair the leak |
| Roar or whistle indoors | Dirty filter or closed registers | Replace the filter, open registers | Inspect the blower wheel and motor |

## Repair costs

These are typical US price ranges:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service call and diagnosis | $90–$200 |
| Debris removal or tightening panels and lines | $90–$250 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Contactor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Outdoor fan motor replacement | $350–$800 |
| Fan blade replacement | $150–$350 |
| Reversing valve replacement (includes refrigerant recovery and recharge) | $1,000–$2,500 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800–$3,500+ |

Parts and labor costs vary by region and by system tonnage. Carrier's parts warranty, typically 10 years with timely registration, may cover components but usually not labor.
