## What this code means

"Wi-Fi not connecting" is not a fault code from your heating or cooling equipment. It means the thermostat cannot join your home network or cannot reach Honeywell's cloud service. Depending on the model, you may see one of these:

- A crossed-out or blinking Wi-Fi icon
- A "Wi-Fi Setup" or "Not Connected" message
- The device shown as "Offline" in the Resideo / Honeywell Home app or the Total Connect Comfort app

Your HVAC system keeps running on the thermostat's local schedule. You only lose app control, remote access, and smart features.

**The condition clears on its own.** Once the network problem is fixed, the thermostat reconnects without a power cycle.

**Behavior varies by series:**

- **Newer Smart models (T5, T6, T9, T10, Lyric Round):** These use the Resideo app. Many are set up over Bluetooth or a temporary hotspot.
- **Older Wi-Fi 9000 and 7-day Wi-Fi models:** These use Total Connect Comfort and a temporary hotspot. The hotspot is named something like "NewThermostat_XXXXXX".

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Router changed.** A new router, a new network name, a new password, or an internet provider swap means the thermostat's saved credentials no longer match.
2. **Router outage or glitch.** The router or internet service went down and the thermostat didn't rejoin automatically.
3. **5 GHz-only or combined-band network.** Most Honeywell Wi-Fi thermostats require 2.4 GHz. Mesh systems and "smart connect" routers that steer devices toward 5 GHz commonly break setup.
4. **Password entered wrong during setup.** Passwords are case-sensitive, and some special characters cause problems on older models.
5. **Weak signal at the thermostat.** Distance, walls, or metal ductwork between the router and the thermostat can weaken the signal too much.
6. **App or account issue.** Common examples are using the wrong app for your model (Resideo vs. Total Connect Comfort), an expired login, or the device being removed from your account.
7. **Router security or compatibility settings.** These include WPA3-only mode, MAC filtering, hidden network names, or captive-portal networks. Captive portals are the login pages used by some apartment and dorm Wi-Fi.
8. **Unstable power on battery-only or no-C-wire installs.** This can cause the radio to drop off the network repeatedly.
9. **Failed Wi-Fi module.** This is rare and usually means replacing the thermostat.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Restart your router and modem.** Unplug them for 30 seconds, then wait about 2 minutes after plugging back in. Confirm other devices can get online.
- **Check for a 2.4 GHz network.** In your router's app, enable a separate 2.4 GHz network name, or temporarily turn off band steering.
- **Re-run Wi-Fi setup from the thermostat menu.**
  - On T5/T6 models, look under Menu → Wi-Fi.
  - On Wi-Fi 9000 models, look under Menu → Wi-Fi Setup.
  - Then re-enter the password carefully.
  - Use your model's Wi-Fi reset if one is offered. This keeps your schedule, unlike a full factory reset.
- **Use the correct app** for your model, sign out, and sign back in. Keep your phone near the thermostat with Bluetooth on during setup.
- **Replace the batteries** if your model uses them. Low batteries can cause dropouts.
- **Check the signal.** Stand at the thermostat with your phone. If the Wi-Fi is weak there, add a range extender or a mesh node that broadcasts 2.4 GHz.
- **Power-cycle the thermostat once.** Pull the faceplate straight off its wall plate, wait 30 seconds, and press it back on. Alternatively, switch off the HVAC breaker for 30 seconds, then back on.
- **Factory reset only as a last resort.** If nothing else works, follow the reset steps in your model's manual, then set the thermostat up as new.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Most HVAC technicians treat a Wi-Fi problem as a network issue, not an HVAC repair. Expect them to:

- Confirm the thermostat powers up steadily and controls heating and cooling normally
- Check whether a C-wire is present and supplying steady 24V, using a meter at the wall plate
- Install a C-wire or a Honeywell C-wire adapter if power is the cause
- Confirm the thermostat's firmware is current and that your model supports your router's security mode
- Test the thermostat against a phone hotspot running at 2.4 GHz to separate a router problem from a thermostat problem

If the thermostat connects to the hotspot but not to your router, the router needs reconfiguring. Your internet provider or an IT helper can do that. If it connects to neither, the Wi-Fi module has likely failed and the thermostat needs replacing.

Be skeptical of a quote for furnace or air conditioner repair to fix Wi-Fi alone.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Went offline right after a router or provider change | Saved credentials no longer match | Re-run Wi-Fi setup with the new network and password | None usually needed |
| Setup can't find your network | 5 GHz-only, combined-band, or hidden network | Enable a visible 2.4 GHz network | None usually needed |
| "Incorrect password" or authentication failure | Typo, special characters, or WPA3-only mode | Re-enter the password; set the router to WPA2/WPA3 mixed mode | None usually needed |
| Connects, then drops repeatedly | Weak signal or unstable power | Add an extender; replace batteries | Install a C-wire or C-wire adapter |
| Shows connected but app says offline | App or account problem, or cloud outage | Sign out and back in; check you're using the correct app; wait out any service outage | None |
| Won't connect even to a phone hotspot | Failed Wi-Fi module | Replace the thermostat (DIY possible with existing wiring) | Replace thermostat; verify wiring |
