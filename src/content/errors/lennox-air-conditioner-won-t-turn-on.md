## What this code means

"Won't turn on" is a symptom, not a fault code. It means the system shows no response to a cooling call: the outdoor condenser stays silent, and often the indoor blower does too.

This page covers the **complete no-start**. Other situations have their own pages:

- If the outdoor fan runs but the compressor doesn't, see Compressor not running.
- If the indoor fan blows warm air, see Fan runs but no cold air.
- If the breaker trips repeatedly, see Breaker keeps tripping.

On a Lennox split system, the indoor furnace or air handler supplies the 24-volt control power. A fault on the indoor side can therefore keep the outdoor unit from starting, even though the outdoor unit has its own 240V power.

How the system resets depends on the model:

- **Conventional 24V systems** usually restart automatically once power and the thermostat call are restored. A built-in anti-short-cycle delay of roughly 5 minutes is normal.
- **Variable-capacity and communicating models** (XC21, XC25, EL18XCV and iComfort-paired units) can hold some lockouts until power is cycled. They log alerts on the thermostat.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat issue.** The display may be blank from dead batteries, the mode may not be set to Cool, the setpoint may be above room temperature, or a schedule or hold may be overriding the call.
2. **Tripped breaker or switch.** This could be the indoor unit breaker, the outdoor breaker, the outdoor disconnect, or the furnace service switch (which looks like a light switch).
3. **Condensate float or safety switch tripped.** A clogged drain backs up water and opens the switch, which cuts the cooling call.
4. **Blown 3- or 5-amp low-voltage fuse on the indoor control board.** This is usually caused by a shorted thermostat wire, often outdoors where wire was nicked by a trimmer or damaged by pests.
5. **Indoor blower door safety switch open.** The furnace or air handler panel is not fully seated.
6. **Failed 24V transformer or control board,** or loose or corroded control wiring.
7. **Outdoor unit faults.** These include a failed contactor coil, or a lockout from the high- or low-pressure switch, which is common on Lennox units with these safeties. If the outdoor unit is the only dead part, the fault often sits here.
8. **Communication fault on iComfort/S30/S40 systems.** The equipment has lost its data link to the thermostat or the outdoor control.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Set it to Cool and lower the setpoint 3–5°F below room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is blank or dim. Check for an active vacation hold or schedule.
- **Breakers and switches:** Check the indoor unit breaker and the outdoor AC breaker in the panel. Reset any tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop. Make sure the furnace service switch and the outdoor disconnect are on.
- **Wait out the delay:** After any reset, give the system 5–10 minutes. Lennox anti-short-cycle timers delay compressor start.
- **One power reset:** Thermostat Off, both breakers off for 5 minutes, then restore power and call for Cool. Do this one time only.
- **Condensate:** Look at the drain pan and the condensate line near the indoor unit. If water is standing, or there is a float switch on the pan or line, clear the drain line opening. A wet/dry vac on the outdoor end works well. The float should then drop and restore operation.
- **Panels:** Confirm the furnace or air handler front panel is fully seated, so the door switch is pressed. Do not remove panels to look inside.
- **Filter:** Replace the filter if it is dirty. A filter alone rarely causes a no-start, but it contributes to freeze-ups and float trips.
- **Communicating thermostats:** Note any alert code shown before you reset anything.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A technician should work through these steps:

- **Confirm 24VAC at the transformer secondary,** then confirm R-to-C voltage at the thermostat and Y-to-C voltage at the outdoor contactor during a call.
- **Inspect and test the control board fuse.** If it is blown, they should hunt for the short in the thermostat wiring or the outdoor low-voltage wiring before replacing it. Simply swapping the fuse is not a fix.
- **Check the safety circuits:** the door switch, the condensate float switch, and the high/low-pressure switches. They may check refrigerant pressures with gauges if a pressure switch is open.
- **Measure line voltage at the outdoor disconnect and contactor,** and inspect the contactor coil and contacts.
- **On communicating systems,** read the active and stored alerts in the thermostat's advanced menu and verify the communication wiring.
- **On the outdoor control,** read the status LEDs, where the model has them. Flash patterns vary by model and board, so the technician should use the unit's installation and service literature.

A good quote names the failed component and the measurement that proved it, for example "0V at the transformer secondary with 120V at the primary."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostat display blank | Dead batteries or no 24V | Replace batteries; check indoor breaker and service switch | Test transformer, fuse and wiring |
| Display normal, nothing runs | Mode or setpoint wrong, or open safety switch | Set to Cool and lower setpoint; seat the blower door | Trace the open safety circuit |
| Water in drain pan, unit dead | Float switch tripped | Clear the condensate line | Clean the pan and trap; repair the drain |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor silent | Outdoor breaker or disconnect off, contactor, or pressure switch | Reset the outdoor breaker once | Test the contactor and pressure switches; check charge |
| Worked, then went dead after yard work | Damaged thermostat wire blew the board fuse | None beyond one reset | Repair the wire; replace the fuse |
| Alert code on iComfort/S30 | Communication or component fault | Record the code; reset once | Diagnose per the alert code |
| Breaker trips immediately | Electrical short or compressor fault | Do not keep resetting | Electrical diagnosis (see Breaker keeps tripping) |

These are typical US ranges, including the service call.

|---|---|
| Thermostat batteries or settings | $0–$10 |
| Clear condensate line (pro) | $100–$250 |
| Float switch replacement | $120–$250 |
| Low-voltage fuse and thermostat wire repair | $100–$300 |
| Door switch | $120–$225 |
| 24V transformer | $150–$350 |
| Contactor | $150–$350 |
