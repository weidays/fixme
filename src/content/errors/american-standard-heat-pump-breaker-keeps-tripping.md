## What this code means

"Breaker keeps tripping" is not a code on the American Standard control board. It is a symptom: a circuit breaker in your electrical panel is cutting power because it sees too much current or a short circuit.

A heat pump differs from a cooling-only AC in an important way. It runs year-round. It reverses refrigerant flow through a reversing valve, and it usually relies on **electric auxiliary heat strips** in the indoor air handler. Those strips are often the largest electrical load in the house.

This page covers what is distinct about heat pumps. Most often that means trips that happen in heat mode or during defrost, and trips on the air handler or heat strip breaker. For causes shared with cooling-only systems, see the American Standard AC Breaker Keeps Tripping page.

**Reset behavior:** a breaker does not reset on its own. You can reset it **once**. If it trips again, immediately or within minutes, a fault is present. Leave it off and call a licensed HVAC technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or failing auxiliary heat strip, sequencer, or relay (heat mode trips).** A heat strip element that is shorted or grounded trips the air handler breaker as soon as aux or emergency heat engages. A welded relay or sequencer can also cause a trip. This is the most common heat-pump-specific cause in winter.
2. **Loose or burned connections at the heat strip or breaker lugs.** High-amperage strip circuits heat up their connections. A burned lug or an overheated breaker causes nuisance trips and is a fire hazard.
3. **Failing run or dual capacitor in the outdoor unit.** A weak capacitor makes the compressor draw high starting current. Because a heat pump runs all year, its capacitors see more hours than those in a cooling-only unit.
4. **Compressor electrical fault.** A shorted or grounded winding, or a locked rotor, trips the outdoor breaker within seconds of startup. A hard-starting compressor also causes trips.
5. **Outdoor fan motor shorted or seized.** This trips the outdoor breaker. It may coincide with the fan not spinning.
6. **Reversing valve or defrost-cycle related high load.** Trips that happen right at defrost changeover can point to a high head pressure condition or a sticking valve.
7. **Shorted wiring or contactor.** Rodent damage, chafed wiring in the outdoor unit, or a pitted contactor can cause this.
8. **Restricted airflow (dirty filter or coil).** This raises system pressures and current draw. It is a contributing factor more often than a sole cause.
9. **Worn or undersized breaker.** Breakers weaken with age and repeated trips. Only an electrician or technician should confirm and replace one.

American Standard does not publish a single ranked breaker-trip procedure. The order above follows standard heat pump service practice, and the most likely cause varies by model, installation, and season.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Note which breaker tripped.** Is it the outdoor unit breaker or the air handler / heat strip breaker? Write it down.
- **Note when it trips.** Does it trip at startup, during heat mode only, during cooling, during defrost (the outdoor fan stops and the unit steams), or randomly?
- **Reset the breaker once.** Switch it fully OFF, then ON. If it trips again, leave it off. Do not keep resetting it.
- **Check for burning smells or scorch marks** at the panel or the unit. If you find any, leave everything off and call a professional.
- **Replace the air filter** if it is dirty.
- **Check the thermostat.** Make sure it is not stuck in Emergency Heat. Set a moderate temperature and replace the thermostat batteries if it has them.
- **Make sure supply and return registers are open and unblocked.**
- **Clear snow, ice, and debris** from around the outdoor unit. Do not chip ice off the coil.
- **Confirm exterior access panels are seated.** Do not open them.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Inspects the breaker, panel lugs, and disconnect for heat damage. Verifies the breaker size matches the unit's nameplate maximum breaker size (MOCP).
- Measures amp draw on the heat strips and checks each element for resistance and shorts to ground.
- Tests the heat strip sequencers or relays and the limit switches.
- Tests the capacitor in microfarads against its rated value.
- Megohm or ohm tests the compressor windings to ground and checks for a locked rotor. May try a hard-start kit.
- Checks the outdoor fan motor windings and the contactor.
- Observes a defrost cycle and checks refrigerant pressures and reversing valve operation.
- Inspects wiring for rodent damage or chafing.

**A good quote names the specific failed part and the measurement that proved it** (for example, "heat strip element shorted to ground"). Be cautious of a compressor or system replacement recommendation that comes without winding test results.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air handler breaker trips when aux/emergency heat comes on | Shorted heat strip, bad sequencer or relay | Reset once; note thermostat mode | Test and replace the strip element, sequencer, or relay |
| Breaker warm, buzzing, or scorched | Loose or burned lug, failing breaker | Leave off; call promptly | Repair connections, replace breaker |
| Outdoor breaker trips seconds after startup | Shorted or grounded compressor, bad capacitor | Reset once only | Capacitor test and replacement, compressor winding test |
| Trips during defrost | High head pressure, reversing valve issue | Clear snow and debris around the unit | Check pressures and the reversing valve |
| Trips plus outdoor fan not spinning | Shorted fan motor or capacitor | Clear debris from the unit | Replace motor or capacitor |
| Trips intermittently in both modes | Wiring damage, pitted contactor, weak breaker | Replace filter; log the times it trips | Inspect wiring, replace contactor or breaker |
