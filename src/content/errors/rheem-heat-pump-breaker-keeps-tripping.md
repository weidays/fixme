## What this code means

"Breaker keeps tripping" is not a code shown on a Rheem display. It is a symptom: the circuit breaker feeding the outdoor unit or the indoor air handler shuts off power repeatedly. A breaker trips when it sees more current than it is rated for, or a short circuit.

This page covers circuit breakers at your electrical panel tripping. It does not cover a fault light on the unit, which is a separate page. If the outdoor unit simply stops while the breaker stays on, see the other Rheem heat pump pages instead.

**How it behaves:**
- A tripped breaker does not reset itself. It stays off until someone flips it back.
- When it trips matters for diagnosis:
  - Immediately on reset usually means a dead short.
  - At compressor start usually means a hard-start or locked-rotor condition.
  - After running a while usually means overheating or a marginal component.
  - Only in cold weather usually points to heat strips.

Because the most common causes are electrical failures inside the cabinet, this is professional work.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor (outdoor unit).** A failing capacitor makes the compressor or fan motor draw excessive current at startup. This is the most common cause of trips that happen as the outdoor unit starts.
2. **Compressor electrical fault.** A shorted or grounded compressor winding, or a locked rotor, draws very high current. A breaker that trips instantly at the outdoor unit and won't hold often means a grounded compressor.
3. **Shorted or failing outdoor fan motor.** A seized or shorted condenser fan motor can overload the circuit. It also causes high head pressure, which raises compressor current draw.
4. **Electric heat-strip fault (air handler breaker).** A shorted heat-strip element, a failed sequencer or relay, or a damaged heat kit can trip the air-handler breaker. This usually happens in cold weather, when auxiliary or emergency heat comes on.
5. **Wiring problems.**
   - Loose, burned or corroded lugs at the contactor, disconnect or breaker
   - Rodent-damaged wiring
   - Water entering the disconnect box
6. **Failed contactor.** Pitted or welded contacts can arc or cause erratic current draw.
7. **Undersized or worn breaker.** The breaker may not match the maximum overcurrent protection rating on the Rheem data plate, or an aging breaker may trip below its rating.
8. **High-pressure operating conditions.** A very dirty outdoor coil, airflow problems or a refrigerant issue raise compressor amps until the breaker trips. These conditions usually trip the unit's internal protection first.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Reset once only.** Turn the thermostat to OFF. Then switch the breaker fully OFF and back ON. Wait 5 minutes, then restore heating or cooling. If it trips again, leave it off and call a professional.
- **Note the details.** Write down:
  - Which breaker tripped (outdoor unit or air handler)
  - When it tripped (instantly, at startup, after running, only on aux heat)
  - Any buzzing, humming or burning smell
- **Check the air filter.** Replace a clogged filter. Restricted airflow raises system load, especially with heat strips running.
- **Check thermostat settings.** Avoid running Emergency Heat unnecessarily. Heat strips draw the most current in the system.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove leaves, snow and debris from the outside of the unit, keeping 2 feet of clearance. Do not open the panels.
- **Make sure exterior access panels are seated** and that the disconnect box cover is closed.
- **Stop immediately** if you smell burning, see scorch marks or hear loud humming. Leave the breaker off and call a professional.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent technician should do the following:

- **Inspect the panel and disconnect** for burned lugs or a heat-damaged breaker. Then confirm that the breaker size matches the data plate's MCA and MOP ratings.
- **Megohm or ohm test the compressor windings.** This checks for a short to ground and for open or shorted windings between the common, start and run terminals.
- **Test the capacitor** with a capacitance meter against its µF rating.
- **Measure amp draw** on the compressor, fan motor and heat strips with a clamp meter, and compare against the rated RLA, FLA and LRA values.
- **Test heat-strip elements and sequencers or relays** in the air handler for shorts and proper staging.
- **Inspect the contactor and all wiring** for pitting, arcing or chafing.
- **Check operating pressures and coil cleanliness** if amp draw is high but the electrical parts test good.

**Be cautious with these quotes:**
- A compressor replacement quote that doesn't include a documented ground or winding test.
- A whole-system replacement quote when only a capacitor or contactor has been tested as bad.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outdoor breaker trips instantly on reset | Grounded compressor or wiring short | Leave breaker off | Megohm test compressor, inspect wiring |
| Trips as outdoor unit starts, with a hum | Failed capacitor or locked compressor | One reset only, then call | Test and replace capacitor, add hard-start kit |
| Trips after running 10 minutes or more | Fan motor failing, dirty coil, high pressure | Clear debris around unit, replace filter | Amp-draw test, motor replacement, coil cleaning |
| Air-handler breaker trips in cold weather | Shorted heat strip or sequencer | Avoid Emergency Heat, replace filter | Test elements and relays, replace heat kit parts |
| Burning smell or scorched breaker | Loose or burned lug, failing breaker | Leave off, call promptly | Repair terminations, replace breaker |
| Trips randomly, worse in rain | Moisture in disconnect or damaged wiring | Confirm disconnect cover is closed | Replace disconnect, repair wiring |

## Repair costs

These are typical US installed prices. Costs vary by region and by model.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic visit | $90–$200 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Hard-start kit | $200–$450 |
| Contactor replacement | $150–$400 |
| Wiring or lug repair, disconnect replacement | $150–$500 |
| Breaker replacement (electrician or HVAC) | $150–$350 |
| Outdoor fan motor replacement | $350–$800 |
| Heat-strip element or sequencer/relay | $200–$700 |
| Complete heat kit replacement | $400–$1,200 |
| Compressor replacement (labor and refrigerant even if parts are under warranty) | $1,500–$2,500 |
| Compressor replacement out of warranty | $2,000–$4,000 |

On older units, a grounded compressor out of warranty often makes replacing the whole system the better value.
