## What this code means

"Frozen evaporator coil" is not a blinking fault code on most York air conditioners. It is a condition you can see. Ice or frost forms on the indoor A-coil above or beside your furnace or air handler. You may also see it on the copper suction line (the larger, insulated line) running to the outdoor unit.

The coil freezes when its surface drops below 32°F. That happens in two main situations:

- **Too little warm air moves across the coil.** This is an airflow problem.
- **The refrigerant pressure is too low.** This is usually caused by a leak.

Typical signs include:

- Weak or warm airflow from the vents.
- The system runs constantly.
- Water appears around the indoor unit as the ice melts.
- Frost shows up on the outdoor service valves.

**Reset behavior:** No code needs resetting. The ice stays until the coil thaws. If you only thaw the coil and fix nothing, it usually refreezes within hours to days.

Some York systems with communicating or variable-speed equipment can log related alerts, such as low pressure or airflow faults, on the thermostat or board. Check your model's installation manual for those.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter.** This is the most common cause. Restricted return air starves the coil of heat.
2. **Closed or blocked supply registers or return grilles.** Furniture, rugs, or closing too many vents to "save energy" all reduce total airflow.
3. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Evaporator coils, braze joints and Schrader valves are common leak points. Low charge lowers coil pressure and temperature.
4. **Dirty evaporator coil.** Dust and biofilm on the coil fins block airflow. This often happens in systems that ran with poor filtration.
5. **Blower problems.** Possible faults include:
   - A failing blower motor or capacitor.
   - A dirty blower wheel.
   - The blower set to a speed tap too low for the AC tonnage.
   - A variable-speed (ECM) motor not getting the correct cooling airflow setting.
6. **Running the AC in cool outdoor weather.** Operating below roughly 60–65°F outdoors can drop coil pressure enough to ice up. This is especially likely on systems without low-ambient controls.
7. **Metering device problems.** A restricted or failing TXV, a clogged piston/orifice, or a refrigerant restriction such as a plugged filter-drier can starve the coil.
8. **Undersized or collapsed ductwork.** Return duct problems are the usual culprit. They are often present from installation but can worsen over time.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Stop cooling and thaw the coil.** Set the thermostat to **Off** and the fan to **On**. This moves warm house air across the coil and thaws it faster. Put towels around the indoor unit. Never chip or scrape the ice.
- **Replace the air filter.** Use the size printed on the filter frame. A filter that looks gray or dusty is overdue. Pleated MERV 8–11 filters work well for most homes. Very restrictive filters can reduce airflow on some systems.
- **Open all supply registers and clear return grilles.** Aim to keep at least 80% of the vents open.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it is not set extremely low, such as 62°F, for long periods. Make sure the fan is not stuck in an unusual mode, and replace weak batteries.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Once the ice is melting, make sure water flows out of the drain line or condensate pump. Do not let the pan overflow. If the drain line has an accessible cleanout, you can flush it with a cup of water or a mild vinegar solution.
- **Check the breaker and service switch.** Make sure the furnace or air handler is getting power. If the blower doesn't run at all with the fan set to On, call a technician.
- **Seat the panels.** Make sure the blower compartment door is fully closed. An unseated door switch can stop the blower.
- **Test after thawing.** Wait until the coil is fully thawed and dry. Then run cooling and check after 1–2 hours. If you see ice again on the suction line or coil, shut the system off and call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician works through these steps in roughly this order. You can use the list to check whether a quote makes sense.

1. **Confirms the coil is fully thawed.** Readings taken while the coil is iced are not reliable.
2. **Measures airflow.** This includes:
   - Checking the filter and the blower wheel condition.
   - Confirming the blower speed or ECM airflow setting matches the condenser tonnage. The target is roughly 350–400 CFM per ton.
   - Measuring total external static pressure across the system.
3. **Inspects the evaporator coil face for dirt.** This may mean removing a coil access panel. Cleaning uses coil cleaner, never high-pressure water.
4. **Tests the blower motor and capacitor.** The tech measures amp draw and capacitance (µF) against the ratings.
5. **Connects gauges and measures refrigerant pressures.** The tech compares superheat or subcooling against the York charging chart. TXV systems use subcooling. Piston systems use superheat.
6. **Searches for the leak if the charge is low.** Methods include an electronic detector, bubble solution or UV dye. Common leak spots are the evaporator coil, flare/braze joints and service valves. A reputable tech finds the leak instead of just "topping off."
7. **Evaluates the metering device and filter-drier.** This is done if pressures point to a restriction rather than low charge.
8. **Reviews the ductwork and operating conditions.** The tech looks for undersized returns or low-ambient use and suggests a low-ambient kit or freeze stat where it fits.

**Red flag:** Be wary if a tech adds refrigerant without checking airflow first, or quotes repeated recharges without a leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on the coil, filter very dirty | Restricted airflow | Thaw with fan On, replace the filter | Only if ice returns |
| Ice returns within a day despite a clean filter | Low refrigerant (leak) | Shut cooling off, call for service | Gauge test, leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Weak airflow from all vents | Blower motor/capacitor fault, dirty wheel, wrong speed tap | Check filter and registers, confirm the blower door is seated | Test the motor and capacitor, clean the wheel, set the correct airflow |
| Frost on the outdoor suction line and service valve | Low charge or restriction | Turn cooling off and thaw | Check superheat/subcooling, test the TXV, check for a filter-drier restriction |
| Freezes only on cool nights | Running in low ambient temperatures | Raise the setpoint, avoid cooling below about 60°F outdoors | Install a low-ambient kit or freeze thermostat |
| Water on the floor near the indoor unit | Ice melting plus a clogged drain | Clear or flush the condensate line, use towels | Clean the pan, fix the pump or float switch |
| Coil looks matted or dirty | Fouled evaporator coil | Keep up regular filter changes | Chemically clean the coil |
| Some rooms hot, coil iced, many vents closed | Restricted supply airflow | Open the registers | Assess duct static pressure |
