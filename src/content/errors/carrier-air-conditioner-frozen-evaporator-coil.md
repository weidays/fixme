## What this code means

A frozen evaporator coil isn't a specific Carrier fault code — it's a physical condition your AC develops when the indoor coil gets so cold that condensation on it freezes into ice. Most basic Carrier thermostats won't display anything; you'll just notice weak or no cooling, ice on the copper lines, or water pooling near the indoor unit.

On Infinity systems with the Ion / SYSTXCCITC control, the equipment may log a related fault (low airflow, low suction pressure, or a charge issue) rather than "frozen coil" directly. Behavior varies by control board and model, so treat any logged code as a clue, not the whole story.

The core problem is always one of two things: **not enough warm air moving across the coil**, or **not enough refrigerant** in the system. Both drop the coil temperature below freezing.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow lets the coil get too cold.
2. **Closed, blocked, or too-few supply/return vents** — furniture, rugs, or shut registers starve the coil of airflow.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm insulate the coil and reduce heat pickup.
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak** — lower pressure means a colder coil that ices over.
5. **Failing or slow blower motor / wheel** — reduced CFM across the coil (Infinity ECM motors may log this).
6. **Running the AC when it's too cool outside** (below ~62°F) — the coil runs colder than designed.
7. **Clogged condensate drain** causing water to back up and freeze in some configurations.
8. **Undersized ductwork or a stuck damper** on zoned systems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn cooling OFF, set the fan to ON.** This is the most important step — it stops making ice and thaws the coil safely.
- **Replace the air filter** if it's gray, matted, or more than 60–90 days old. This alone fixes many frozen coils.
- **Open and unblock all supply registers and return grilles.** Move furniture, rugs, and drapes away from vents.
- **Check the thermostat** — make sure it's set to COOL with a reasonable setpoint (not, say, 60°F on a cool day).
- **Look at the breaker** for the air handler/furnace and condenser; a tripped breaker can leave the fan off while the coil freezes on restart.
- **Check the condensate drain and pan** for standing water; clear a visible clog at the drain line exit if you can reach it safely.
- **Let all ice melt completely** (1–24 hours) before restarting in COOL, and place towels to catch meltwater.

If the coil re-freezes after a clean filter, open vents, and a full thaw, stop and call a pro — the cause is likely refrigerant or blower related.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Confirms the coil is fully thawed, then runs the system and measures **temperature split** (return vs. supply air) — typically ~16–22°F on a healthy system.
- Checks **airflow / static pressure** across the coil and inspects the blower wheel and motor speed (on Infinity, reads ECM data and logged faults).
- Inspects the **evaporator coil** for dirt and the filter/ductwork for restrictions.
- Connects gauges to measure **suction and head pressure** and calculates **superheat/subcooling** to confirm proper refrigerant charge.
- If charge is low, performs a **leak search** (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test) rather than just topping off.
- Verifies the metering device (TXV/piston) is operating correctly.

A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without checking airflow or searching for a leak deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on copper line / indoor coil, weak cooling | Dirty filter or blocked vents | Replace filter, open all vents, thaw fully | Verify airflow and temperature split |
| Coil re-freezes after clean filter | Low refrigerant (leak) | Stop running; call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge to spec |
| Water around air handler | Clogged condensate drain | Clear visible drain clog | Flush drain, check pan and float switch |
| Weak airflow at every vent | Failing/slow blower motor | Confirm fan runs on FAN ON | Test/replace ECM or PSC motor |
| Freezes on cool days/nights | Low charge or running too cold out | Avoid AC below ~62°F outdoors | Check charge, add low-ambient controls |
| Dirty, fuzzy coil visible | Dirty evaporator coil | None (coil is delicate) | Professional coil cleaning |

## Repair costs

- **New air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250 by a tech; near $0 DIY at the drain exit.
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$450 depending on access.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900 (ECM motors on Infinity systems cost more).
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$450.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older R-22 is far more expensive).
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500+ including labor and refrigerant.

Prices vary by region, system size, and refrigerant. Get an itemized quote before authorizing refrigerant work.
