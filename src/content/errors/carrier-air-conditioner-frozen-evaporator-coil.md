## What this code means

"Frozen evaporator coil" is not a Carrier fault code in the traditional sense — most Carrier air conditioners (Comfort and Performance series) have no display to show it. On Infinity systems with the wall control, you may see airflow or refrigerant-related faults logged, but the freeze itself is a physical condition you *see and feel*: a sheet of ice on the indoor coil (inside the furnace or air handler) and often frost creeping down the copper refrigerant line to the outdoor unit.

The coil freezes when its surface drops below 32°F while humidity condenses on it. Two root causes drive this: **not enough warm air moving across the coil** (airflow problem) or **not enough refrigerant** (leak). Either way, the fix is to thaw first, then find the cause — running a frozen system risks compressor damage.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. A restricted filter starves the coil of warm return air, so it ices over.
2. **Blocked return or supply airflow** — closed vents, crushed flex duct, or too many registers shut.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm insulate the coil and reduce heat exchange.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — low charge lowers coil pressure and temperature, freezing it. This is a pro repair, not a "top-off."
5. **Failing or slow blower motor / bad capacitor** — reduced fan speed cuts airflow.
6. **Thermostat set too low or fan set to a very low speed** overnight in cool weather.
7. **Stuck or failed expansion valve (TXV) / metering device** — improper refrigerant metering starves or floods the coil.
8. **Running AC in cold outdoor temps** (below ~60°F) — outdoor conditions push the coil below freezing.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn cooling OFF, fan to ON.** At the thermostat, switch the system to *Off* for cooling and set the fan to *On* (or continuous). This melts the ice safely. Give it up to 24 hours.
- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or older than 60–90 days, swap it. This alone fixes many freeze-ups.
- **Open and unblock vents.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and not covered by furniture or rugs.
- **Check the condensate drain and pan.** As ice melts, it can overflow the pan and drip. Confirm the drain line is clear and the pan isn't full. Wet-vac the line if needed.
- **Check the breaker.** If the indoor blower breaker tripped, the fan can't move air — reset once.
- **Confirm thermostat settings.** Cooling not set unreasonably low; fan not stuck on a very low speed.
- **Do NOT** chip, scrape, or hair-dryer the ice, add refrigerant, or open the sealed system.

If the coil freezes again within a day or two of thawing and a fresh filter, you have a deeper cause — call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified HVAC tech will typically:

- Confirm the coil is fully thawed before testing (a frozen coil gives false readings).
- Measure **static pressure** and airflow across the coil to rule out duct or blower restriction.
- Inspect and clean the evaporator coil and blower wheel.
- Check blower motor speed and capacitor.
- Attach gauges to read **superheat and subcooling** — this tells them if the charge is low (leak) or the metering device (TXV) is faulty.
- If low on refrigerant, perform a **leak search** (electronic detector or dye) rather than just adding charge, since a leak will reoccur.
- On Infinity systems, pull stored fault history from the wall control.

A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search or airflow check is a red flag — ask them to justify it.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on indoor coil, weak airflow | Dirty filter / blocked vents | Replace filter, open vents, thaw with fan ON | Check static pressure, clean coil |
| Frost on outdoor copper line, warm air inside | Low refrigerant (leak) | Turn off, call pro — no DIY | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Coil refreezes after new filter | Dirty coil or slow blower | Thaw again, note recurrence | Clean coil, test blower/capacitor |
| Water leaking near air handler | Melted ice overflowing pan | Clear drain line, wet-vac | Clear drain, check float switch |
| Freezes only in cool weather / overnight | Low outdoor temp operation | Avoid running AC below ~60°F | Verify low-ambient limits |
| Uneven cooling, some rooms hot | Restricted ducts / metering issue | Open registers | Inspect ducts, test TXV |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional variation applies):

- **New air filter (DIY):** $10–$40
- **Thaw + reset (DIY):** $0
- **Professional coil cleaning:** $150–$450
- **Blower capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Blower motor (ECM/PSC):** $400–$900
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$400
- **Leak repair + recharge (R-410A):** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant amount
- **Expansion valve (TXV) replacement:** $500–$900
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (coil is a sealed component; often driven by an unrepairable leak)

If your Carrier system is under **10 years old**, the coil and compressor may still carry a limited parts warranty — have the tech check the serial number before quoting a full coil.
