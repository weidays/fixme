## What this code means

"Frozen evaporator coil" isn't a numeric fault code — it's a physical condition where the indoor coil (inside your air handler or furnace cabinet) drops below freezing and ice builds up on it. On most Carrier ACs there is no dedicated error code for this; you notice it as **weak or no cold air, ice on the copper lines, or water around the furnace** once it thaws.

The coil freezes when it gets **too cold relative to how much air is moving across it**. That happens either because airflow is restricted or because the refrigerant charge/pressure is wrong. Once ice forms, it blocks airflow even more, which makes the freezing worse — a snowball effect.

Higher-end **Carrier Infinity** systems may log low-airflow or system-fault data at the smart thermostat, but they won't specifically announce "frozen coil." Basic Comfort and Performance units give you no on-board indication at all.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow lets the coil get too cold.
2. **Closed, blocked, or too few supply/return vents** — reduces airflow the same way a dirty filter does.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm insulate the coil and choke airflow.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — lowers coil pressure and temperature, causing ice even with good airflow. Requires a licensed tech.
5. **Weak or failing blower motor / wrong fan speed** — not enough air across the coil.
6. **Running the AC in cold weather** — operating below roughly 60°F outdoor temperature can freeze the coil.
7. **Restricted metering device (TXV/orifice) or plugged filter drier** — a refrigerant-side restriction. Pro diagnosis only.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do these first — they resolve a large share of frozen-coil calls:

- **Turn the system OFF and let it thaw.** Set the thermostat to OFF and, if available, set the **fan to ON** to speed thawing. Wait until all ice is gone (1–24 hours) before running cooling again.
- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, swap it. Note the size printed on the frame.
- **Open and unblock vents.** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and not covered by rugs, furniture, or closed doors.
- **Check the thermostat setting.** Don't run cooling if it's cold outside (below ~60°F).
- **Check the breaker.** If the indoor blower or condenser tripped, that can starve airflow — reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Check the condensate drain and pan.** A thawed coil dumps a lot of water. Make sure the drain line isn't clogged and the pan isn't overflowing; clear standing water with a wet/dry vac and use drain tablets to keep it clear.

If the coil freezes again within a day or two after a fresh filter and clear vents, the cause is deeper — stop running it and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Carrier tech will:

- **Fully thaw the coil** before testing — you can't read pressures accurately on an iced system.
- **Measure airflow** and inspect the blower motor, capacitor, and fan speed settings.
- **Inspect the evaporator coil and blower wheel** for dirt buildup.
- **Attach gauges and read superheat/subcooling** to determine whether the charge is correct or the system is low on refrigerant.
- **Leak-search** (dye, electronic detector, or nitrogen pressure test) if the charge is low — recharging without fixing the leak is a temporary, wasteful fix.
- **Check the metering device and filter drier** for restriction if pressures point that way.

Sanity check: if a tech says "just needs more refrigerant" without mentioning a **leak search**, get a second opinion. AC refrigerant doesn't get "used up" — if it's low, it leaked.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on indoor coil/lines, weak airflow | Dirty filter / blocked vents | Turn off, thaw, replace filter, open vents | Confirm airflow, inspect blower |
| Freezes again days after filter change | Dirty coil or low refrigerant | Thaw and stop running it | Clean coil or leak-search & recharge |
| Water pooling near furnace after thaw | Coil thawed + clogged drain | Clear drain, vacuum pan, add tablets | Clear/replace drain line, check pan |
| Cold air weak, coil freezes fast | Low refrigerant (leak) | None — call a pro | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Blower barely moving air | Failing blower/capacitor | Check breaker once | Replace motor/capacitor, set speed |
| Freezes only in cool weather | Running AC below ~60°F | Don't cool in cold weather | Verify no low-ambient control issue |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional and Infinity-vs-basic differences apply):

- **Filter replacement / thaw:** $0–$30 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$450
- **Blower motor capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900 (ECM/variable-speed motors cost more, especially on Infinity systems)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,600, depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ if the coil itself is leaking

If your Carrier system is out of warranty and the coil or compressor is leaking, ask your tech to price the repair **against a full system replacement** — sometimes it's the smarter spend on older units.
