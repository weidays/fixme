## What this code means

A frozen evaporator coil is a condition, not a Carrier fault code. It means the indoor coil above your furnace or inside the fan coil has dropped below 32°F, and moisture from the air has frozen on it instead of draining away.

Common signs:
- Ice or frost on the copper suction line (the larger, insulated line) at the indoor unit or outdoor unit
- Weak airflow from the vents
- Warm or barely cool air
- Water around the furnace once the ice melts

**Reset behavior:** there is nothing to reset. The ice must melt completely, and the underlying cause must be fixed, or the coil will refreeze.

**Communicating systems:** Infinity and some Performance systems may record a low-suction-pressure or airflow event in service history. Some outdoor units also have a low-pressure switch that can shut down the compressor. Whether yours does depends on the model.

**Overlap with other pages:** if your AC is simply not cooling and you see no ice, see the separate "Not cooling" page. This page covers only the icing problem.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Restricted airflow from a dirty filter.** This is the most common cause by far. Low airflow across the coil drops its temperature below freezing.
2. **Blocked or closed returns and supply registers.** Furniture over the returns, or many supply registers shut, reduces total airflow.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil.** Dust packed onto the coil fins blocks airflow even when the filter is clean. This is common when filters were skipped or poorly fitted.
4. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Low charge causes low suction pressure, which drops the coil below freezing. Ice often starts at the coil inlet or on the suction line.
5. **Blower problems.**
   - On PSC motors: a weak motor or a failing capacitor.
   - On ECM or variable-speed blowers: incorrect airflow (CFM) or a dehumidify setting that is too aggressive.
   - On Infinity systems: the airflow setup is configured at the wall control.
6. **Collapsed or disconnected ductwork,** or an undersized return duct.
7. **Running the AC in cool outdoor temperatures** (below about 60–65°F) or with a very low setpoint for long periods. Low load lets the coil temperature fall below freezing.
8. **Metering device restriction.** A clogged TXV, piston or filter-drier starves the coil. This is less common.
9. **Contactor stuck closed.** The compressor runs nonstop even when the thermostat is satisfied.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Stop the freeze.** Set the thermostat to OFF (cooling off) and the fan to ON. Let the coil thaw fully, typically 1–3 hours. Put towels near the furnace for the meltwater.
2. **Check the filter.** Replace it if it is gray or clogged. Confirm the airflow arrow points toward the furnace. Do not run the system without a filter.
3. **Check the thermostat.**
   - Confirm it is set to COOL with a reasonable setpoint (not 60°F).
   - Replace the batteries if the display is dim.
   - Avoid running AC on cool days.
   - On Infinity controls, note any service alerts so you can tell your technician.
4. **Open registers and clear returns.** Keep most supply registers open. Make sure nothing blocks the return grilles.
5. **Check the condensate drain.** During the thaw, confirm water is draining from the condensate line or pump and not overflowing the pan. If the drain is clogged, clear its accessible outside end or cleanout. Do not open the cabinet to do this.
6. **Check the blower door.** Make sure the furnace or fan coil blower door is fully seated. An unseated door lets in unfiltered air, and on most units it also stops the blower.
7. **Check the breaker.** If anything tripped, reset the breaker once. If it trips again, call a technician.
8. **Test the result.** After a full thaw, run cooling and check again in a few hours. If ice returns, shut the system off and call a technician.

Do not chip ice off the coil, open the coil cabinet, or add refrigerant.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Use this to sanity-check a quote. A technician will typically:

- Confirm the system is fully thawed. Readings taken on an iced coil are meaningless.
- Inspect the filter, the coil face (through an access panel, if one exists) and the blower wheel.
- Measure **static pressure** and **temperature split** to judge airflow.
- Check blower settings:
  - PSC motor: speed tap and capacitor reading.
  - ECM motor: dip switch or Infinity airflow settings.
- Connect gauges or probes to read **suction pressure, superheat and subcooling**, compared against Carrier's charging chart for your outdoor unit. Low suction pressure with normal airflow points to low charge or a restriction.
- **If charge is low:** perform leak detection (electronic sniffer, bubbles or a nitrogen pressure test). Simply topping off without finding the leak is a red flag.
- Check the contactor and thermostat wiring for a compressor that runs continuously.
- On Infinity systems, review fault history at the wall control.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on coil, weak airflow, dirty filter | Restricted airflow | Thaw (fan ON, cool OFF), replace filter | None if it doesn't refreeze |
| Refreezes with a clean filter | Low refrigerant or leak | Shut off, call a technician | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge to spec |
| Refreezes, dusty coil visible | Dirty evaporator coil | Keep a fresh filter installed | Clean the coil (in place or pulled) |
| Airflow weak even after thaw | Blower capacitor, motor or speed setting | Check the filter and registers | Test the capacitor and motor, set CFM or speed tap |
| Freezes on cool days or at a low setpoint | Low load | Raise the setpoint, avoid AC under about 60°F | Verify airflow and charge |
| Compressor won't stop, coil iced | Stuck contactor or wiring | Turn off at the thermostat and breaker | Replace the contactor, check wiring |
| Water around furnace after thaw | Pan or drain overwhelmed | Clear the condensate line's outside end | Clean the pan and drain, check the float switch |
| Ice with normal airflow, low suction | Metering device or drier restriction | Shut off, call a technician | Replace the TXV, piston or filter-drier |
