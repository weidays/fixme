## What this code means

"Frozen evaporator coil" isn't a fault code Carrier displays — it's a physical condition you'll see or feel. The indoor evaporator coil (inside your air handler or above your furnace) gets covered in frost or a solid block of ice. On Infinity systems you may see related airflow or charge-related messages at the thermostat, but most Carrier units simply stop cooling well and you find ice on the copper lines or coil.

The coil freezes when it gets colder than it should. That happens for two root reasons: **not enough warm indoor air moving across the coil**, or **too little refrigerant** in the system. Both drop the coil's surface temperature below freezing, and humidity condensing on it turns to ice instead of draining away.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the most common cause. Restricted airflow drops coil temperature and it ices over.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers, blocked returns, or crushed ductwork starve the coil of airflow.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and grime insulate the coil and reduce heat transfer, mimicking low airflow.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — a genuine leak lowers pressure and coil temperature. This requires a licensed tech; refrigerant is not a DIY item.
5. **Weak or failing blower motor / capacitor** — the blower isn't moving enough air across the coil.
6. **Running AC in cold weather** — operating cooling below ~60°F outdoors can freeze the coil.
7. **Clogged condensate drain** — standing water and high humidity around the coil can contribute to icing in some setups.
8. **Faulty thermostat or stuck contactor** — the system runs continuously and overcools the coil.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn cooling OFF, fan to ON.** Set the thermostat to fan-only (or "On") with cooling off. This melts the ice safely. Do **not** keep running cooling.
- **Check the air filter.** Pull it out. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many frozen coils.
- **Walk the house and open vents.** Make sure supply registers are open and returns aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or curtains.
- **Check the breaker.** If the air handler/furnace blower breaker is tripped, the fan can't run — reset it once.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's not set absurdly low and that batteries (if any) are fresh.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Look for standing water in the drain pan or a backed-up drain line. A shop vac on the outdoor drain outlet can clear a simple clog.
- **Let it fully thaw** — 3 to 24 hours. Place towels to catch melting water. Only restart cooling once all ice is gone.

If the coil freezes again within a day or two of thawing and a fresh filter, stop and call a pro — you likely have a refrigerant or blower problem.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Carrier tech will:

- Confirm the coil has fully thawed before testing (a frozen coil can't be measured accurately).
- Measure airflow — checking blower speed, static pressure, and coil/filter condition.
- Attach gauges and measure **superheat and subcooling** to determine whether the charge is correct. Abnormal readings point to a refrigerant issue.
- Inspect the evaporator coil for dirt and the blower motor/capacitor for weak operation.
- Perform a **leak search** (electronic detector, dye, or soap bubbles) if refrigerant is low — a proper repair fixes the leak, not just "tops off" the system.
- On Infinity systems, read any logged thermostat faults for airflow or charge hints.

Be wary of a quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search or airflow check. Adding refrigerant to a leaking system is a temporary patch, not a repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on coil, weak/warm airflow | Dirty filter, closed vents | Replace filter, open vents, thaw | Verify airflow, static pressure |
| Coil re-freezes after clean filter | Low refrigerant (leak) | Thaw, then call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Ice plus dirty visible coil | Dirty evaporator coil | Thaw only; leave cleaning to pro | Deep coil cleaning |
| Weak airflow even with clean filter | Failing blower/capacitor | Thaw, note fan behavior | Test/replace blower motor or cap |
| Water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain with shop vac | Clear line, add tablets, check pan |
| Freezing in cool weather | Running AC below ~60°F | Switch off cooling | Confirm no other fault |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Filter replacement:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150–$300 professionally.
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$400.
- **Blower capacitor replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $450–$900 (ECM motors on higher-end Carrier units cost more).
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $100–$350.
- **Leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (major leaks or corroded coils; higher for R-410A or newer refrigerant systems).

Thawing and a new filter are free-to-cheap. If the coil keeps freezing, the diagnostic fee (often $80–$180, sometimes credited toward repair) is money well spent.
