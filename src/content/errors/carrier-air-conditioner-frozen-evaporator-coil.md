## What this code means

A frozen evaporator coil isn't a numeric fault code on most Carrier equipment — it's a physical condition you'll see or feel: ice or frost on the indoor coil, the refrigerant lines, or weak, warm airflow from the vents even though the unit is running.

The evaporator coil (inside your air handler or furnace cabinet) gets cold as refrigerant absorbs heat from your indoor air. If something reduces airflow across that coil, or drops the refrigerant charge, the coil surface falls below freezing and condensation turns to ice. Ice then insulates the coil, cooling gets worse, and more ice forms — a runaway cycle.

Carrier **Infinity** systems with a SAM or Infinity thermostat may log a related airflow or low-suction fault, but the base **Comfort** and **Performance** units display nothing. In all cases you diagnose the freeze by symptom, not by a code on a board.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow drops coil temperature below freezing.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or a crushed return duct starves the coil of airflow.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm insulate the coil and block airflow, same effect as a dirty filter.
4. **Low refrigerant charge (leak)** — a low charge drops coil pressure and temperature. This is the leading *repeat* cause after filters are ruled out.
5. **Failing or slow blower motor / bad capacitor** — reduced fan speed means not enough warm air moves across the coil.
6. **Running AC in cold weather** — outdoor temps below ~62°F can cause freezing on many systems.
7. **Stuck metering device (TXV or piston)** — floods or starves the coil, causing localized icing. Requires a tech.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the system to fan-only.** Set the thermostat mode to OFF (cooling) and the fan to ON. This thaws the coil safely without running the compressor.
- **Replace the air filter.** If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, swap it. This alone fixes many freezes.
- **Open and unblock vents.** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't covered by furniture or rugs. Don't run with more than a couple of registers closed.
- **Check the thermostat setting.** Don't set cooling below ~68°F, and don't run AC when it's cold outside.
- **Inspect the condensate drain/pan.** A thawing coil dumps a lot of water. Confirm the pan isn't overflowing and the drain line is clear. Treatment tablets help prevent clogs.
- **Check the breaker.** If the indoor fan won't run at all to thaw the coil, verify the air-handler/furnace breaker hasn't tripped.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bend/pry ice off the coil. Let it melt.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works the airflow-then-refrigerant path so you can sanity-check the quote:

- Confirms the coil is fully thawed before testing (a frozen coil gives false readings).
- Inspects filter, coil cleanliness, and blower operation; measures **static pressure** to find airflow restrictions.
- Checks blower motor speed and capacitor.
- Connects gauges to read **suction pressure and superheat/subcooling**, and takes temperature splits across the coil to judge the charge.
- If the charge is low, performs a **leak search** (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test) — they should find and fix the leak, not just top off.
- Verifies the metering device (TXV/piston) is feeding correctly.

Be cautious of any quote that adds refrigerant without mentioning a leak search — recharging a leaking system is a temporary, repeat-freeze fix.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on indoor coil, warm air at vents | Dirty filter / low airflow | Fan ON to thaw, replace filter | Measure static pressure, clean coil |
| Freeze returns days after new filter | Low refrigerant (leak) | Thaw, then call a pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Weak airflow, fan sounds slow | Failing blower / bad capacitor | Thaw, verify breaker on | Test motor, replace capacitor/motor |
| Coil freezes only in cool weather | Running AC below ~62°F outside | Switch to fan or heat mode | Confirm low-ambient limits |
| Localized frost on one coil section | Stuck TXV / metering device | Thaw only | Test/replace metering device |
| Water on floor near air handler | Overflowing condensate pan | Clear drain, add tablets | Clear/replace drain, flush line |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges — your area and system size will vary:

- **New air filter (DIY):** $10–$40
- **Blower capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Blower motor replacement:** $450–$900 (ECM motors run higher)
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$450
- **Leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs older)
- **TXV / metering device replacement:** $500–$1,000
- **Full evaporator coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (matched coil, labor, refrigerant)

If your system is out of warranty and needs a coil plus a leak repair, weigh the repair against age — units over ~12–15 years may justify replacement.
