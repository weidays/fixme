## What this code means

"Frozen evaporator coil" is not a numeric Carrier fault code — it's a physical condition your indoor coil enters when it drops below freezing and moisture in the air turns to ice. Carrier air conditioners don't display a dedicated "frozen coil" code, so you'll usually notice it by weak or no cold air, ice on the copper lines or coil, and water pooling near the air handler as it thaws.

The evaporator coil is designed to run cold but stay above freezing. When airflow across it drops too low, or refrigerant pressure drops too far, the coil surface falls below 32°F and frost builds until it becomes a solid block of ice. Ice insulates the coil, so cooling collapses further — a cycle that gets worse the longer it runs.

Because a frozen coil is almost always a **symptom** of another problem (airflow or refrigerant), fixing it means finding that root cause. This is rated **pro** severity: some causes are DIY, but a recurring freeze points to refrigerant work, which requires EPA certification.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — the single most common cause. A clogged filter starves the coil of airflow and it freezes.
2. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers or blocked returns reduce airflow the same way.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm on the coil fins block airflow even with a clean filter.
4. **Failing or dirty blower wheel / weak blower motor** — reduced CFM across the coil.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak** — lower pressure means a colder coil that ices up. This is a repair, not a "top-off."
6. **Running the AC in cold weather** — using cooling when it's below ~62°F outside can freeze the coil.
7. **Clogged condensate drain backing up** — can contribute in some systems and signals a coil/moisture problem.
8. **Stuck metering device (TXV/orifice)** — less common, causes uneven or excessive coil cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the system OFF or to FAN ONLY** and let the ice thaw completely before doing anything else. Set the thermostat fan to ON to speed thawing.
- **Check and replace the air filter** if it's gray, matted, or overdue. This alone fixes a large share of freezes.
- **Open and unblock vents:** make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or curtains.
- **Check the thermostat:** confirm it's set to COOL with a reasonable setpoint, and don't run cooling when it's cold outside.
- **Check the breaker:** if the unit tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Check the condensate drain and pan:** clear a visibly clogged drain line and mop up standing water so it doesn't overflow as the coil thaws.
- **Do not** chip at ice, open the sealed refrigerant system, or add refrigerant yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified Carrier technician will:

1. **Confirm the coil is fully thawed** before testing — you can't diagnose airflow or pressures through a block of ice.
2. **Measure airflow / static pressure** and inspect the filter, coil, and blower wheel to rule out restriction.
3. **Test blower motor operation and speed tap** (or ECM programming) for correct CFM per ton.
4. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and check pressures, superheat, and subcooling to see whether the charge is correct.
5. **Leak-check** with an electronic detector or dye if the charge is low — a low charge means a leak, not normal loss.
6. **Inspect the metering device (TXV/orifice)** if pressures suggest it's stuck or restricted.

If a quote jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a filter/coil check or a leak search, ask why — that's a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on coil, weak airflow, gray filter | Dirty air filter | Replace filter, thaw fully | Verify airflow, inspect coil |
| Some rooms have no air, coil freezes | Closed/blocked vents or returns | Open registers, clear returns | Measure static pressure |
| Clean filter but coil still freezes | Dirty evaporator coil | None — leave sealed system | Professional coil cleaning |
| Ice plus weak air, blower sounds off | Failing/dirty blower motor or wheel | None | Clean/replace blower, check speed |
| Coil re-freezes after thaw, no dirt found | Low refrigerant from a leak | None | Leak-find, repair, recharge |
| Water overflowing near air handler | Clogged condensate drain | Clear visible drain line | Clear/flush drain, check pan |
| Freezes only in cool weather | Running AC below ~62°F outside | Don't run cooling when cold | Confirm no other cause |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US pricing for Carrier systems; your market and tonnage vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 DIY.
- **Coil thaw:** $0 (time only).
- **Professional evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$450.
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100–$250.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900 (more for Infinity/ECM variable-speed).
- **Refrigerant leak diagnosis:** $100–$350.
- **Leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,600+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,500+ (higher on R-410A/newer refrigerant systems and matched coils).

A simple filter fix costs almost nothing; a leaking coil is the expensive end. Get the root cause identified before approving refrigerant work.
