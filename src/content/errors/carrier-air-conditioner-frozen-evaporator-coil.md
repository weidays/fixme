## What this code means

A "frozen evaporator coil" isn't a numeric fault code — Carrier systems typically show it as **no cooling, weak airflow, ice on the copper lines, or water around the indoor unit**. The evaporator coil is the indoor part of your AC that absorbs heat from your home's air. When something disrupts the balance between airflow and refrigerant, the coil surface drops below freezing and condensation turns to ice.

Once ice forms, it insulates the coil and blocks airflow, so cooling gets worse and worse until the coil is a solid block of frost. On Infinity and some Performance systems, the control may throw a related low-suction or airflow fault, but the freeze itself is a physical condition, not a stored code.

The two big root causes are **not enough airflow across the coil** or **not enough refrigerant in the system**. One is often a cheap DIY fix; the other needs a licensed tech.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — the single most common cause. A clogged filter starves the coil of warm return air and it freezes.
2. **Dirty or blocked evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm on the coil fins restrict airflow even with a clean filter.
3. **Closed, blocked, or too few supply/return vents** — furniture, rugs, or closed registers reduce airflow across the coil.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — low charge drops coil pressure and temperature below freezing. Requires a technician; adding refrigerant without fixing the leak is a temporary band-aid.
5. **Failing blower motor or wrong blower speed** — a weak or miswired ECM/blower can't move enough air (varies by model and board).
6. **Running the AC in cool weather** — operating cooling when outdoor temps are below ~60°F can cause freezing on many systems.
7. **Blocked condensate drain** causing a safety float switch or standing water to interfere with normal operation (secondary contributor).

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn cooling OFF at the thermostat, but set the fan to ON.** Running the blower with the compressor off speeds thawing and protects the compressor.
- **Check and replace the air filter.** If it's gray or you can't see light through it, swap it. This alone fixes many freeze-ups.
- **Let the coil thaw completely** (1–24 hours). Do not turn cooling back on while ice or heavy frost is present.
- **Open and clear all supply registers and return grilles.** Move furniture, rugs, and boxes blocking airflow.
- **Check the thermostat setting** — make sure it isn't set unusually low or running in cool mode on a cold day.
- **Check the condensate drain and pan.** If water is pooling, gently clear the drain line with a wet/dry vac at the outdoor termination. Do not disable any float switch.
- **Verify the breaker and outdoor disconnect** are on (a frozen coil sometimes trips a float switch that cuts power).

If the coil thaws and the system cools normally after a filter change, you've solved it. **If it refreezes within a day, stop and call a pro** — you have an airflow or refrigerant fault that needs diagnosis.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified Carrier tech will typically:

- Confirm the coil is fully thawed before testing (a frozen system gives false readings).
- **Measure airflow** — check filter, blower speed/CFM, and duct static pressure.
- **Inspect the evaporator coil** for dirt, biofilm, or bent fins restricting air.
- **Connect gauges and check superheat/subcooling** to determine if refrigerant charge is correct — low readings point to a leak.
- **Leak-search** with electronic detectors or dye if charge is low, then quote the repair.
- Verify blower motor operation and control-board settings (fan speed programming varies across Infinity/Performance/Comfort boards).

A good quote should identify the *root cause*, not just "add refrigerant." If a tech tops off refrigerant without mentioning a leak search, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air, ice on indoor coil | Dirty filter | Replace filter, thaw coil | None if resolved |
| Refreezes after filter change | Dirty evaporator coil | Turn off, call pro | Professional coil cleaning |
| Ice on outdoor copper lines, poor cooling | Low refrigerant / leak | Turn off, call pro | Leak search + repair + recharge |
| Water pooling around indoor unit | Blocked condensate drain | Clear line with wet/dry vac | Clear drain, check float switch |
| Weak airflow with clean filter & open vents | Failing/misset blower | Turn off, call pro | Test blower, correct board settings |
| Freezes only on cool days | Low outdoor temp operation | Avoid cooling below ~60°F | Confirm no other fault |

## Repair costs

- **Filter replacement (DIY):** $10–$40.
- **Condensate drain clearing (DIY):** $0–$25; **pro:** $75–$250.
- **Professional evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$450 depending on access.
- **Refrigerant leak diagnosis:** $100–$350.
- **Leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+, depending on leak location and refrigerant type.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900 (more for variable-speed ECM on Infinity systems).
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500+ if the coil itself is leaking or corroded.

Costs vary by region, refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older systems), and how much refrigerant has been lost. Get an itemized quote that names the root cause.
