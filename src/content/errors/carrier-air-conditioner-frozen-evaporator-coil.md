## What this code means

A frozen evaporator coil is not a diagnostic fault code — it's a physical condition Carrier air conditioners develop when the indoor coil (inside your air handler or furnace cabinet) drops below 32°F and moisture from the air freezes on it. On Infinity systems, this may show up indirectly as poor cooling or an airflow-related fault at the thermostat, but most Comfort and Performance units simply stop cooling well and you'll notice ice, water, or weak airflow.

The coil normally runs cold but above freezing. Anything that reduces airflow across the coil or lowers refrigerant pressure can push it below freezing, and once ice forms it insulates the coil, making cooling collapse and the problem worse. The two root categories are **airflow problems** (common, often DIY) and **refrigerant problems** (a pro job).

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the #1 cause. Restricts airflow so the coil overcools and freezes.
2. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust buildup on the coil itself insulates it and blocks airflow.
3. **Blocked or closed supply/return vents** — too many closed registers starve the coil of airflow.
4. **Failing or slow blower motor / bad capacitor** — reduced fan speed means not enough warm air over the coil.
5. **Low refrigerant charge (leak)** — low pressure lowers the coil's boiling temperature below freezing. Requires a pro.
6. **Running the AC in cool weather** — operating below ~60°F outdoors can freeze the coil.
7. **Clogged condensate drain causing a safety trip**, or a stuck/failed metering device (TXV) — pro diagnosis.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the system to cool-Off but leave the fan set to On.** This thaws the coil safely — you cannot properly diagnose anything while it's iced.
- **Replace the air filter** if it's gray, matted, or more than 60–90 days old. This alone fixes many cases.
- **Check the thermostat** — make sure it's set to Cool and a reasonable temperature, not stuck in a mode that short-cycles.
- **Check the breaker** for the air handler/furnace and the outdoor unit.
- **Open all supply and return vents** and remove furniture, rugs, or boxes blocking them. Keep most registers open.
- **Check the condensate drain and pan** for standing water or overflow near the indoor unit — a clogged drain can trip a safety switch. Clear a visible clog if you safely can.
- **Do not** chip ice off the coil, open the refrigerant lines, or repeatedly reset a unit that won't cool. Never add refrigerant yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech will first let the coil fully thaw, then:

- Measure **static pressure and airflow (CFM)** to confirm the blower is moving enough air.
- Inspect and test the **blower motor and run capacitor** for correct speed and amp draw.
- Check the **filter and evaporator coil** for cleanliness.
- Connect gauges to read **suction and head pressure**, and measure **superheat and subcooling** to confirm the refrigerant charge.
- If charge is low, perform a **leak search** (electronic detector, UV dye, or nitrogen pressure test) — because simply "topping off" a leaking system is a temporary and improper fix.
- Inspect the **metering device (TXV/piston)** and the condensate safety switch.

If a quote jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on copper lines/coil, warm air from vents | Restricted airflow | Fan On to thaw, replace filter, open vents | Measure airflow, clean coil |
| Coil refreezes after clean filter | Dirty coil or weak blower | Inspect for visible dust | Clean coil, test blower & capacitor |
| Weak airflow at all vents | Slow/failing blower or bad capacitor | Confirm filter is clean | Replace capacitor or blower motor |
| Hissing/bubbling, gradual cooling loss | Refrigerant leak | None — call a pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Water around indoor unit, AC quits | Clogged condensate drain / safety trip | Clear visible drain clog | Clear drain, test float switch |
| Freezes only in mild weather | Running AC below ~60°F outdoors | Avoid cooling in cool weather | Verify no other fault |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor, vary by region and model):

- **Air filter replacement:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $75–$250.
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$400.
- **Blower motor capacitor:** $150–$350.
- **Blower motor replacement:** $400–$900 (ECM/variable-speed motors on Infinity systems run higher).
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $150–$450.
- **Leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500+, more if the coil is part of a matched Infinity system.

R-410A and older R-22 pricing differs significantly; R-22 systems are often more economical to replace than repair.
