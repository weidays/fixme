## What this code means

"Frozen evaporator coil" is not a numbered fault code on most Carrier systems—it is a physical condition. The indoor evaporator coil (inside your air handler or on top of your furnace) drops below freezing and ice builds up on it. Once iced over, the coil can no longer absorb heat, so your Carrier AC blows warm or weak air even while the outdoor unit runs.

Carrier communicating systems (Infinity/Greenspeed) may log a low-suction-pressure or airflow fault, but many single-stage systems show no code at all. You'll simply see ice on the copper lines, water pooling near the indoor unit, and poor cooling. The underlying problem is almost always **too little heat reaching the coil**—either from restricted airflow or low refrigerant.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Starves the coil of warm return air, so it freezes.
2. **Blocked airflow** — closed/blocked supply vents, closed dampers, or a collapsed/disconnected duct.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil** — dust and biofilm insulate the coil and choke airflow across the fins.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — lower pressure drops the coil temperature below freezing. Requires a pro.
5. **Failing or slow blower motor** — a weak ECM/PSC motor or bad capacitor reduces airflow volume.
6. **Running the AC in cool weather** — operating below roughly 60°F outdoors can freeze the coil.
7. **Stuck/failed metering device (TXV or piston)** — overfeeds or restricts refrigerant into the coil.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn cooling OFF, fan ON.** At the thermostat set the system to OFF and the fan to ON to thaw the coil safely. Do this first—don't keep running cooling against ice.
- **Check and replace the air filter.** A gray, matted filter is the #1 cause. Swap in a clean MERV 8–11 filter.
- **Open and unblock all supply vents.** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed dampers aren't choking airflow. Leave return grilles clear.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to COOL and a reasonable temperature; a very low setpoint on a mild day encourages freezing.
- **Look at the condensate line and pan.** A thawing coil dumps a lot of water. Make sure the drain isn't overflowing and the pump (if fitted) runs.
- **Check the breaker.** If the indoor blower breaker tripped, the coil freezes because no air moves across it. Reset once; if it trips again, stop and call a pro.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or scrape ice off the coil. If cooling still fails after a clean filter and a full thaw, it's a pro job.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Carrier technician will confirm the coil is fully thawed first, then:

- **Measure airflow** across the coil (temperature split, static pressure) to rule out ducts, blower, or a dirty coil.
- **Inspect the evaporator coil and blower wheel** for dirt buildup.
- **Attach refrigerant gauges** and check superheat/subcooling against Carrier's charging chart for your model.
- **Leak-test** the coil, line set, and connections with an electronic detector or dye if the charge is low.
- **Test the blower motor and capacitor** for correct amp draw and speed.
- **Check the metering device (TXV/piston)** operation.

Be wary of any tech who tops off refrigerant without leak-testing—low charge means a leak that will just freeze again.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on indoor coil/copper line, warm air | Dirty filter or blocked vents | Fan ON to thaw, replace filter, open vents | Airflow/static pressure test |
| Freezes again days after new filter | Dirty evaporator coil | Thaw fully; inspect if accessible | Professional coil cleaning |
| Weak cooling + ice + hissing/bubbling | Low refrigerant / leak | Thaw and shut off | Leak test, repair, recharge |
| Ice + weak/uneven airflow | Failing blower or bad capacitor | Confirm fan runs on ON | Test/replace motor or capacitor |
| Water overflowing near unit | Condensate drain blocked | Clear drain, check pump | Clear line, replace pump |
| Freezes on cool days | Running AC below ~60°F outside | Avoid cooling in mild weather | Confirm charge is correct |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor). Your Carrier model and region change these.

- **Air filter (DIY):** $10–$40
- **Thaw + diagnostic service call:** $80–$200
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $150–$450
- **Blower capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Blower motor (ECM/variable-speed):** $500–$1,200
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500+ depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500 (higher if under warranty labor only)
- **TXV/metering device replacement:** $400–$900

Refrigerant work must be done by an EPA-certified technician—it's illegal and unsafe for homeowners to handle refrigerant.
