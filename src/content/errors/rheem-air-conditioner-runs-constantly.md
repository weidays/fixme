## What this code means

"Runs constantly" is a symptom, not a fault code, so a Rheem air conditioner won't display it. It means the system either never reaches the thermostat setpoint or keeps running after the setpoint is satisfied. These two cases point to different causes:

- **Running but losing the battle.** The house stays warm and the system never shuts off. Something is reducing capacity, such as low airflow, dirty coils or low refrigerant, or the heat load is beyond the system's design.
- **Running with no call for cooling.** The outdoor unit keeps going even when the thermostat is satisfied or turned off. This is an electrical control problem, most often a stuck contactor or shorted thermostat wiring.

There is nothing to reset. The symptom continues until its cause is fixed. If the air coming out is warm, see the related pages on blowing warm air and not cooling. This page covers units that are cooling but can't keep up or won't stop.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Extreme heat or normal design behavior.** In very hot weather, even a correctly sized single-stage unit may run nonstop. Two-stage and variable-speed Rheem models are designed to run long low-speed cycles.
2. **Dirty or restricted air filter.** Reduced airflow lowers capacity and stretches runtime. This is the most common fixable cause.
3. **Thermostat setting or placement problems.** Common examples include:
   - The fan is set to ON instead of AUTO, so the blower runs constantly.
   - The setpoint is unrealistically low.
   - The thermostat sits in sun or near a heat source.
   - The thermostat has weak batteries or is miscalibrated.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil.** Debris, cottonwood fluff or grass clippings block heat rejection, which reduces capacity and raises head pressure.
5. **Dirty indoor evaporator coil or weak blower performance.** This cuts airflow and heat absorption. A dirty coil can progress to a frozen coil.
6. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Capacity drops gradually. You may see ice on the suction line or poor temperature split.
7. **Stuck or welded contactor.** The outdoor unit runs even when the thermostat isn't calling for cooling.
8. **Shorted or damaged thermostat wire.** A short between R and Y keeps the call for cooling active. This can be caused by wire damaged by mowers or pests near the outdoor unit.
9. **Duct leakage or disconnected ducts.** Cooled air is lost into an attic or crawlspace.
10. **Undersized system or added heat load.** An addition, finished attic or lost insulation can exceed the unit's capacity.
11. **Weak compressor.** Worn valves reduce pumping capacity. A technician confirms this only after ruling out charge and airflow problems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the thermostat.**
  - Confirm it is set to COOL with the fan on AUTO, not ON.
  - Raise the setpoint a few degrees and watch whether the system cycles off once it is satisfied.
  - Replace the batteries if the thermostat uses them.
  - Make sure no direct sun, lamp or appliance is heating the thermostat.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it looks gray or clogged, or if it's past its interval (typically 1–3 months for 1-inch filters).
- **Check the vents and registers.** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from outside.** Keep about 2 feet of clearance around it. Clear leaves and debris from the outside of the fins by hand. Do not open the unit or spray into it.
- **Test whether the unit stops with no call for cooling.** Set the thermostat to OFF.
  - If the outdoor unit keeps running after a few minutes, shut it off at the outdoor disconnect or breaker and call a technician. Don't leave it running.
- **Check the condensate drain.** Make sure the line is draining and the safety float switch in the pan hasn't tripped.
- **Check the exterior panels.** Confirm the indoor unit's access panels are fully seated. A loose panel lets the system pull in unconditioned attic air.

If the system still runs nonstop on a mild day after these checks, call a professional.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent technician should do the following:

- **Measure the temperature split.** They check return versus supply air across the indoor coil. Roughly 16–22°F is typical, depending on humidity.
- **Check airflow.** This includes total external static pressure, blower speed settings and the evaporator coil condition.
- **Inspect and clean the condenser coil** and confirm the outdoor fan motor is turning at proper speed.
- **Check the refrigerant charge** using superheat (on piston systems) or subcooling (on TXV systems), compared against the Rheem charging chart on the unit.
  - If the charge is low, they should leak-search before adding refrigerant.
- **Test the control circuit.**
  - With the thermostat satisfied, they confirm there is no 24V on Y at the outdoor unit.
  - They inspect the contactor for pitted or welded contacts.
  - They check the thermostat wire for shorts.
- **Check compressor performance**, including amperage draw against the rated load amps and suction and discharge pressures.
- **Inspect ductwork** for disconnects or major leakage. They may perform a load calculation (Manual J) if they suspect sizing problems.

**Red flags in a quote:**
- Recommending a full system replacement before checking charge, airflow and coils.
- Adding refrigerant without mentioning a leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs nonstop only on very hot days, air is cold | Load exceeds design capacity | Close blinds and raise the setpoint slightly | Load calculation if the problem persists on mild days |
| Long runs, weak airflow at vents | Clogged filter or blocked returns | Replace the filter and clear the returns | Static pressure test, blower check |
| Blower never stops | Thermostat fan set to ON | Set the fan to AUTO | Check the fan relay or control board if the setting doesn't fix it |
| Outdoor unit runs with thermostat OFF | Welded contactor or shorted thermostat wire | Shut off at the disconnect or breaker | Replace the contactor and repair the wiring |
| Runs constantly, ice on the copper line | Low refrigerant or dirty evaporator coil | Turn cooling off and run the fan only to thaw | Leak search and repair, recharge, coil cleaning |
| Outdoor coil matted with debris | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris from outside the unit | Chemical coil cleaning |
| Some rooms hot, attic feels cool | Duct leak or disconnected duct | Check that the registers are open | Duct repair or sealing |
| Gradual capacity loss with normal charge and airflow | Weak compressor | None | Compressor diagnosis and replacement |
