## What this code means

"Loud noise" is not a fault code. Your Rheem heat pump will not show a flash code for noise, and nothing needs to be reset. It is a symptom, and the type of sound is the best clue to the cause.

Some loud sounds are normal on a heat pump:

- **Defrost.** In heating mode, a loud whoosh or hiss happens when the reversing valve shifts. The outdoor fan stops, and steam may rise from the coil.
- **Startup and shutdown.** A short clunk can occur as the compressor starts or stops.
- **Cold-weather operation.** Variable-speed and two-stage models ramp up to higher speeds when it is cold, so they get louder.

Abnormal sounds usually point to a mechanical or electrical problem in the outdoor unit:

- Grinding
- Banging
- Rattling
- Continuous screeching
- Buzzing
- Humming with nothing moving

These are technician repairs.

Furnace-side bangs on gas systems are covered separately, under Loud bang on startup.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost cycle (whoosh, hiss, steam, fan stops).** This is by far the most common "loud noise" call in winter. The reversing valve shifts the refrigerant flow, which makes a loud rush of sound. A short pause or clunk follows when the cycle ends. It is not a fault.
2. **Debris or loose panels (rattling, ticking, slapping).** Common sources are:
   - Sticks, leaves or ice hitting the fan blade
   - Loose service panels or screws
   - Vibrating refrigerant lines touching the cabinet or the house
3. **Failing outdoor fan motor or bent fan blade (squeal, grinding, wobble, scraping).** Worn bearings make a squeal or grinding sound. A blade damaged by ice or debris strikes the shroud or wobbles. Ice buildup on the fan or guard is a related cause, covered under Ice on outdoor unit.
4. **Bad capacitor or contactor (buzzing, humming, chattering).** Each sounds a little different:
   - A weak run capacitor makes the motor or compressor hum as it struggles to start.
   - A pitted or failing contactor buzzes loudly or chatters.
5. **Compressor problems (loud clanking, banging, rattling at startup, or continuous screaming).** Possible causes include:
   - Worn or broken compressor mounts
   - Hard starting
   - Internal wear
   - High-pressure conditions

   A continuous high-pitched scream needs prompt attention. Some scroll compressors briefly "run backward" with a short scream at shutdown. That can be normal on certain models, which is why a technician should judge it.
6. **Refrigerant issues (gurgling, hissing outside of defrost, bubbling).** An undercharge or restriction can cause unusual flow noises. Refrigerant work is always technician-only.
7. **Indoor air handler noise (whistling, rattling, booming ducts).** A restricted filter or closed registers can make the blower whistle or the ducts pop. A loose blower wheel or housing can rattle.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Wait out a defrost cycle.** If the sound is a whoosh with steam and the fan stopped, wait up to 10 minutes. If the unit returns to normal heating, no action is needed.
- **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it is dirty. A clogged filter causes whistling and makes the blower strain.
- **Open the supply and return registers.** Make sure they are open and not blocked by furniture. Closed vents raise duct pressure and cause whistling and booming.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from outside the cabinet.** Look only; do not reach in.
  - Clear leaves, sticks and snow from around the unit, keeping roughly 2 feet of clearance.
  - Check that the top fan guard is not blocked.
  - Do not reach into the fan guard or remove the top.
- **Check that the exterior panels are seated.** If a service panel is visibly loose and rattling, press it back into place and snug the existing screws by hand. Don't go further than that.
- **Check the thermostat.** Make sure the mode and settings are what you expect. Replace the batteries if the low-battery indicator is showing.
- **Shut it off if the noise is harsh.** Grinding, banging, screaming, or a hum with no fan spinning all mean you should stop the system:
  1. Set the thermostat to off.
  2. Turn off the outdoor disconnect or breaker.
  3. Call a technician.

  Do not keep restarting the unit to "test" it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will:

1. **Identify the sound and when it happens.** They will note whether it occurs at startup, during the run, at shutdown or during defrost. They may run a forced defrost to confirm normal reversing valve operation.
2. **Kill power and inspect the fan.** They will check the blade for damage and the motor bearings for play. They will also check the fan's clearance to the shroud.
3. **Test the run capacitor.** This is done with a meter, comparing the reading to the rated microfarads.
4. **Inspect the contactor.** They will look for pitting or burning and check for chatter.
5. **Measure motor and compressor amp draw.** A hard start, locked rotor, or high current points to a capacitor or compressor fault.
6. **Check the compressor.** They will look at the mounts and listen for internal knocking.
7. **Look for loose or rubbing parts.** They will check for line-set rubbing and loose tubing clamps.
8. **Connect gauges if needed.** This checks refrigerant pressures and superheat/subcooling when the noise suggests a charge or restriction problem.
9. **Check the indoor unit.** They will inspect the blower wheel, motor mounts and duct static pressure for indoor noise.

**Red flags on a quote:**

- A compressor or full system replacement recommended without amp-draw, winding or pressure readings.
- "Recharge" quoted without a leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whoosh/hiss, steam, fan stops in winter | Normal defrost | Wait 2-10 minutes; no action | None unless it is constant (see Stuck in defrost mode) |
| Rattling or ticking outdoors | Debris, loose panel, line set vibration | Clear debris around the unit; seat panels by hand | Secure the line set, replace mounting hardware |
| Squealing or grinding from the top fan | Worn fan motor bearings | Shut off the system | Replace the condenser fan motor (and capacitor if needed) |
| Scraping or thumping fan | Bent blade or ice contact | Clear snow around the unit; shut off | Replace the blade, address the cause of ice |
| Loud hum, fan or compressor not turning | Failed capacitor or seized motor | Turn off at the thermostat and disconnect | Test and replace the capacitor or motor |
| Buzzing or chattering at the unit | Failing contactor or low voltage | Shut off | Replace the contactor, check control voltage |
| Clanking or banging at compressor start | Bad mounts, hard start, internal wear | Shut off | Check mounts and amp draw, add a hard-start kit, or replace the compressor |
| Continuous screaming | High pressure or compressor fault | Shut off immediately | Check pressures, diagnose the compressor |
| Gurgling or hissing outside defrost | Refrigerant charge or restriction | None | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Whistling at registers or indoor unit | Dirty filter, closed vents | Replace the filter, open registers | Measure static pressure, correct the ductwork |
| Rattle inside the air handler | Loose blower wheel or panel | Seat the air handler door | Tighten or replace the blower wheel or mounts |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Service call / diagnostic | $80 – $200 |
| Normal defrost noise | $0 (or only a diagnostic fee if you call) |
| Securing panels, line set or fan guard | $100 – $250 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $120 – $350 |
| Contactor replacement | $120 – $350 |
| Hard-start kit | $150 – $400 |
| Condenser fan blade | $150 – $350 |
| Outdoor fan motor (PSC) | $300 – $650 |
| Outdoor fan motor (ECM / variable-speed) | $500 – $1,200 |
| Compressor mount replacement | $200 – $500 |
| Refrigerant leak repair and recharge | $300 – $1,500+ (depends on leak location and refrigerant type) |
| Indoor blower wheel or motor | $300 – $1,200 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800 – $4,500+ |

If your heat pump is still under Rheem's limited warranty, parts may be covered. The compressor typically carries 10 years with registration. Labor usually is not covered unless you have an extended plan.
