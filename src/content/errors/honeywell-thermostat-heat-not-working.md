## What this code means

"Heat not working" is not an error code your Honeywell thermostat displays. It is a symptom: you set the thermostat to heat, and the house stays cold.

The problem sits in one of three places:

1. **The thermostat is not calling for heat.** This can be caused by the wrong mode, the setpoint, a schedule override, low batteries, or a setup error.
2. **The thermostat calls for heat, but the call is not reaching the equipment.** This points to a wiring fault or a tripped breaker.
3. **The equipment gets the call but will not run.** This can be a furnace safety lockout, an open door switch, or a heat pump fault.

**How to tell which one you have.** Most Honeywell models show "Heat On", or a flame icon, whenever they are actively calling for heat.

- **No heat call shown:** the thermostat side is the suspect.
- **Heat call shown, but no warm air after about 5 minutes:** the problem is downstream at the furnace or heat pump.

**A built-in delay can be normal.** Many Honeywell models include a compressor protection delay of up to about 5 minutes. It mainly applies to heat pump heating and to air conditioner compressors. During that time the display may show a flashing icon or "Wait". This is not a fault. On a gas furnace system, "Wait" may not appear at all. Whether and when the delay shows depends on your model and equipment type.

**There is no lockout to clear.** The thermostat itself does not hold a lockout. Once the cause is corrected, it resumes calling for heat on its own.

Furnace control boards are different. Some lock out after repeated failed ignitions, and many auto-retry after about an hour. That behavior varies by furnace brand, not by thermostat.

**If your display is blank,** see the separate blank-screen page instead.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Wrong system mode or setpoint.** The thermostat is set to Cool, Off, or Auto with a deadband. Or the heat setpoint is below the room temperature.
2. **Schedule or hold overriding your setting.** A programmed period has lowered the setpoint. Or a "Permanent Hold" or vacation hold is active at a low temperature.
3. **Compressor protection delay.** On heat pump systems especially, if you changed settings within the last few minutes, the thermostat may be waiting before it energizes the equipment. This varies by model and equipment type.
4. **Low or dead batteries.** On battery-powered models, the display may still show, but the thermostat will not switch reliably. Many models show a low-battery warning first.
5. **Furnace, air handler or outdoor unit has no power.** The breaker is tripped, the furnace service switch (it looks like a light switch) is off, the outdoor heat pump disconnect is off, or the blower door is not seated. An unseated door means the safety door switch keeps the unit off.
6. **Clogged air filter.** Restricted airflow can trip the furnace's high-limit switch. Burners then shut off, and only cool air comes out, or nothing at all.
7. **Incorrect installer setup.** This happens most often after a new install or a factory reset. Typical mistakes are:
   - System type set wrong (conventional versus heat pump).
   - Wrong O/B reversing valve setting.
   - Wrong number of heat stages.
   - On Wi-Fi models, the app configured differently from the actual equipment.
8. **Furnace safety lockout or equipment fault.** This includes ignition failure, a flame sensor problem, or a pressure switch fault. The furnace's LED shows a blink code. This is equipment-side and technician work.
9. **Wiring fault.** A loose W (heat) wire, a broken thermostat cable, a missing C wire on models that need it, or a blown low-voltage fuse on the furnace board. All of these are technician work.
10. **Failed thermostat.** This is rare, but it happens: relays or the circuit board stop sending a heat call. It is confirmed when the furnace runs with the thermostat bypassed during a technician's test.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

1. **Confirm mode and setpoint.** Set the system to **Heat** (not Auto, Cool, or Off). Set the temperature at least 3°F above the current room reading.
2. **Cancel holds and schedules.**
   - Press **Cancel** or **Run Schedule**. Or set a temporary hold to a warmer temperature.
   - On Wi-Fi models, also check the Honeywell Home or Resideo app. A schedule or geofence setting there can override the wall unit.
3. **Wait 5 minutes.** If your model uses a protection delay, let it finish. Watch for "Heat On" or a steady flame icon. If you have a gas furnace and see no "Wait" message, that can be normal too.
4. **Replace the batteries.** Use fresh alkaline batteries of the type your model takes, usually AA or AAA. Do this even if the screen is still lit.
5. **Check the breaker and furnace switch.**
   - Reset a tripped breaker for the furnace or air handler **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
   - Make sure the service switch near the furnace is **On**.
6. **Heat pump owners: check outdoor power.** Make sure the breaker for the outdoor unit is not tripped, and reset it **once** if it is. Check that the outdoor disconnect near the unit is in the On position. Look only at the outside of the disconnect box, and do not open or work inside it.
7. **Heat pump owners: look at the outdoor unit.** Check it from a safe distance. A unit encased in thick ice that does not clear needs a technician. Do not chip, pick at, or pour hot water on the ice.
8. **Heat pump owners: note Aux or Em Heat on the display.** Write down whether the thermostat shows "Aux Heat" or "Em Heat", and tell the technician. What these indicators mean, and how they behave, varies by model, so check your manual.
9. **Seat the blower door.** Push the furnace's exterior front panel fully into place so it latches. Do not tape or hold down the switch behind it.
10. **Replace a dirty filter.** If you cannot see light through the filter, replace it with the same size.
11. **Open vents.** Make sure supply registers are open, and return grilles are not blocked by furniture or rugs.
12. **Check the condensate drain on high-efficiency furnaces.** If the drain line or pan is visibly overflowing, or a float switch shuts the unit down, clear any visible clog at the drain line's outlet. Only do this if it is accessible outside the cabinet.
13. **Do one furnace reset.** If the furnace is locked out, turn its power off for 30 seconds, then back on. Do this **only once**. If it fails to heat again, write down the LED blink code if you can see it through a viewport in the blower door without removing panels. Then call a technician.

If heat still does not come on, and the thermostat shows a heat call, the problem is not something to fix with settings.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will usually work through these steps:

1. **Verify the call.** They confirm the thermostat shows a heat call and review the installer setup: equipment type, heat stages, O/B setting, and fuel type on dual-fuel systems.
2. **Measure 24V.** At the furnace or air handler, they check for 24VAC between R and C. When heat is called, they check for 24V on W (or Y and O/B for a heat pump).
3. **Jump R to W at the furnace board.** If the furnace fires with the thermostat bypassed, the problem is the thermostat or its wiring. If it does not fire, the furnace is at fault.
4. **Read the furnace blink code.** They interpret it per the furnace manufacturer, then test the relevant part: pressure switch, igniter, flame sensor, or limit switch.
5. **Check the low-voltage fuse and transformer.** If a fuse blew, they look for a shorted wire before replacing it.
6. **Check thermostat wiring.** They inspect both ends for loose or broken conductors, and confirm a C wire if the model requires one.

A thermostat replacement should only be quoted after step 3 shows the thermostat is at fault.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display says Off or Cool | Wrong mode | Set to Heat and raise the setpoint | None |
| Setpoint keeps dropping back | Schedule or hold active | Cancel the hold and edit the schedule in the app or on the unit | None |
| Flame icon or "Wait" flashing | Protection delay (varies by model and equipment type) | Wait 5 minutes | None |
| Low-battery warning or erratic behavior | Weak batteries | Replace with fresh alkaline batteries | None |
| "Heat On" shown, furnace silent | Breaker, service switch, or door not seated | Reset breaker once, switch on, latch the panel | Check for a short or an open door switch |
| Heat pump outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or outdoor disconnect off | Reset breaker once, confirm the disconnect is On | Diagnose the heat pump |
| Outdoor unit encased in ice that does not clear | Heat pump fault | Do not chip the ice; note any Aux or Em Heat display | Diagnose the heat pump |
| Furnace starts, then blows cool air | Dirty filter or limit trip | Replace the filter, open vents | Test the limit switch, check airflow |
| Problem began after a new thermostat or reset | Installer setup wrong | Compare settings to your manual | Correct the system type and O/B setting |
| Heat call shown, furnace LED blinking | Furnace lockout | One power cycle, then note the code | Repair the igniter, sensor, or pressure switch |
| Furnace runs when R and W are jumped, not from the thermostat | Wiring or failed thermostat | Replace the thermostat if confirmed | Repair the wiring or install a new thermostat |

These are typical US price ranges.

| Fix | Typical cost |
|---|---|

| Batteries | $5–$10 |
