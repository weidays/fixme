## What this code means

"Leaking water" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. It means condensate from the indoor head of your Rheem ductless system is not leaving through its drain path.

During cooling and dry modes, the indoor coil pulls moisture out of the air. That water collects in a drain pan inside the head. From there it flows by gravity, or through a condensate pump, to the outside or to a drain.

When that path is blocked, poorly sloped or not pumping, the pan overflows. Water then drips from the bottom or front of the unit, or runs down the wall.

Some Rheem models with a condensate pump or float switch display an error code, or shut off cooling, when the water level gets too high. Many wall-mount heads give no code at all and simply drip. Whether a float-switch shutdown clears itself once the water drains depends on the model. Check your manual.

This page covers the ductless indoor head. Central systems are covered in "Rheem AC Leaking Water."

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged condensate drain line or pan.** Algae, slime, dust or insect nests block the drain hose or the pan outlet. This is the most common cause on any ductless head.
2. **Dirty indoor filters.** Restricted airflow can let the coil get too cold and frost over. When the ice melts, it can produce more water than the pan handles. Dirty filters also let debris reach the pan and drain.
3. **Improper drain slope or a kinked or sagging drain hose.** The line must fall continuously from the head. A sag, an uphill run, or a kink at the wall penetration traps water.
4. **Indoor head not level, or tilted the wrong way.** Wall-mount heads should be level or pitched slightly toward the drain side. A head tipped away from the drain overflows at the other end. This is often an installation issue.
5. **Failed or clogged condensate pump or float switch.** This applies where the drain can't run by gravity. The pump may have failed, have a clogged inlet, or have lost power.
6. **Frozen indoor coil.** Low refrigerant charge or airflow problems freeze the coil, and meltwater overwhelms the drain. See "Rheem AC Frozen Evaporator Coil" for that issue.
7. **Cracked drain pan, loose drain fitting, or insulation sweating.** Damaged parts or poorly insulated lines and fittings drip inside or behind the head.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Turn the unit off** with the remote. If water is near outlets or wiring, also switch off the breaker or disconnect.
- **Clean the filters.** Lift the front panel as your manual describes and slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them in lukewarm water, let them dry completely, and reinstall them.
- **Inspect the outside drain end.** Find where the drain hose exits outdoors, often next to the line set. Make sure it isn't buried, crushed, kinked, submerged or plugged with mud or insects. Gently clear any debris from the opening.
- **Look along the visible drain hose and line-set cover** for obvious kinks or sags you can see without removing covers.
- **Check whether a condensate pump is plugged in,** if your installer used a pump with a visible plug. Plug it back in if it came loose. Don't open the pump.
- **Keep the outdoor-unit and indoor-head airflow clear.** Nothing should block the indoor louvers.
- **If the unit locked out or shows a code, reset it once** by cycling the breaker. If it leaks or faults again, stop and call.

Do not remove the head's cover or the drain pan, pour chemicals into the head, or push wire into the unit's drain outlet. These parts are fragile and sit next to electronics.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Confirm the mode and runtime when the leak happens, and check for an error or float code.
- Remove the indoor head's cover and inspect the drain pan, pan outlet and drain hose connection.
- Flush the drain line, often with nitrogen, a pump or a vacuum, and test the flow by pouring water into the pan.
- Check that the head is level and the drain slopes properly along its full run. They may remount the head or reroute the line.
- Test the condensate pump, float switch and the wiring to them.
- Check for coil icing, and measure airflow and refrigerant pressures if freezing is suspected. Refrigerant work is licensed work only.
- Check insulation on the line set and fittings for sweating.

A credible quote names the specific failed part or condition, such as "clogged pan outlet" or "pump failed." A vague "full service" with no finding is not a diagnosis.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady drip from bottom of head in cooling | Clogged drain line or pan | Clean filters; clear the outdoor drain end | Flush drain, clean pan and outlet |
| Water drips from one end only | Head not level or pitched away from drain | Note which side drips | Re-level the head, correct the drain pitch |
| Leaks after long runs, then stops | Coil freezing then thawing | Clean filters; run fan-only to thaw | Check airflow and refrigerant charge, repair leak |
| Unit stops cooling and shows a code, with water present | Pump or float switch tripped | Confirm the pump is plugged in; reset once | Test or replace pump and float switch |
| Water running down wall behind head | Drain fitting loose, hose kinked behind head | Turn off and dry the area | Reseal the fitting, reroute the hose |
| Drips along line-set cover | Sweating, poorly insulated lines | None beyond reporting | Re-insulate lines and fittings |
| Outdoor drain end dry while indoors leaks | Blockage between head and exit | Check for kinks you can see | Clear or replace drain hose |

## Repair costs

| Repair | Typical US cost |
|---|---|
| Filter cleaning (DIY) | $0 |
| Replacement filters (DIY) | $15–$40 |
| Service call / diagnosis | $90–$200 |
| Drain line and pan clearing | $100–$250 |
| Deep coil and head cleaning (bag/flush method) | $150–$400 per head |
| Condensate pump replacement | $200–$450 |
| Re-level head or reroute drain | $250–$600+ |
| Drain pan replacement | $200–$500 |
| Refrigerant leak repair and recharge (if the coil freezes) | $300–$1,500+ |

Prices vary by region, by how hard the head is to reach, and by whether parts are under warranty. Rheem parts warranties often require registration, but labor usually isn't covered.
