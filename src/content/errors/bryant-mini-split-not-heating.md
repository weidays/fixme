## What this code means

"Not heating" is a symptom, not a single fault code. Your Bryant mini-split is in heat mode, but it blows cool or lukewarm air, doesn't run at all, or can't keep the room warm.

Some causes are normal behavior that only looks like a fault:

- **Defrost cycles.** In cold weather the indoor fan pauses for several minutes while the outdoor coil defrosts.
- **Cold-air prevention.** At startup the indoor fan holds off until the coil warms up.

Other causes are real failures in the refrigerant circuit, the reversing valve, sensors, or the inverter electronics.

If the indoor unit shows an error code or blinking LEDs, write it down. Code meanings differ between Bryant indoor-unit families, so look up the exact code in your model's manual. Whether a fault clears on its own or holds until power is cycled also depends on the model. Many protection stops clear automatically once conditions return to normal. Some faults latch and need a power cycle.

If you have a ducted Bryant heat pump rather than a ductless unit, see the Bryant Heat Pump Not Heating page instead.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Wrong mode, setpoint or remote settings.** Common culprits:
   - The unit is set to Cool, Dry, Fan or Auto with a low setpoint.
   - The remote batteries are dead.
   - A timer or schedule has switched the unit off.
2. **Normal defrost or cold-air prevention.** The unit stops blowing warm air for a few minutes, then resumes.
3. **Dirty indoor filters or a blocked outdoor unit.** Clogged washable filters reduce airflow. Snow, ice or debris around the outdoor coil starves the system.
4. **Power interruption or a tripped breaker.** This often affects the outdoor unit only, while the indoor fan still runs.
5. **Outdoor temperature below the unit's heating range.** Capacity drops, especially on non-low-ambient models.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** The coil may ice up or protection shutdowns may follow.
7. **Faulty sensor or reversing valve problem.**
   - A thermistor (room, coil or outdoor sensor) gives the control board false readings.
   - The reversing valve or its coil sticks, so the unit stays in cooling.
8. **Communication, inverter board or compressor fault.** These usually come with a displayed error code.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the remote.** Confirm it is set to Heat (the sun icon), with a setpoint a few degrees above room temperature. Make sure no timer or sleep mode is active.
- **Replace the remote batteries.** If the display is dim or the unit doesn't beep when you press a button, put in fresh batteries.
- **Wait out a defrost.** If the outdoor unit is steaming or quiet and the indoor fan has paused, wait 10–15 minutes before deciding the unit has failed.
- **Clean the indoor filters.** Lift the front panel, slide the mesh filters out, rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry fully, and reinstall them.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Gently brush away snow, leaves and debris. Don't chip at ice on the coil with tools. Keep about 2 feet of clearance around it.
- **Check the breaker and disconnect.** Look at both the breaker in the panel and the outdoor disconnect switch. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a pro.
- **Power-cycle the unit once.** Switch it off at the breaker, wait five minutes, then restore power. If the error code returns, stop and call a technician.
- **Check the condensate drain.** In heat mode, water drains from the outdoor unit during defrost. Make sure the drain path isn't frozen into a block of ice that touches the coil.
- **Seat the panels.** Confirm the indoor front panel and the outdoor service panel are fully closed.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically work through these steps:

1. **Read the codes.** Retrieve the error code or LED blink pattern and any fault history from the indoor and outdoor boards.
2. **Check line voltage and communication.** Verify supply voltage at the outdoor unit and confirm the communication wiring between the indoor and outdoor units.
3. **Measure temperatures.** Check the temperature difference between return and supply air at the indoor head, and the line-set temperatures.
4. **Check refrigerant pressures.** Connect gauges and compare pressures to the manufacturer's charging data. If pressures are low, perform leak detection, often at the flare connections.
5. **Test the sensors.** Measure each thermistor's resistance against the temperature chart in the service manual.
6. **Test the reversing valve.** Confirm the reversing-valve coil gets voltage in heat mode and that the valve actually shifts.
7. **Check the inverter and compressor.** Run the inverter board's diagnostics and check compressor windings.

**When reviewing a quote:** a refrigerant recharge with no leak search or repair is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit blows room-temperature air | Wrong mode or setpoint | Set Heat mode and raise the setpoint | None if this fixes it |
| Fan stops for 5–15 minutes and the outdoor unit steams | Normal defrost | Wait | None |
| Weak, barely warm airflow | Clogged filters | Wash the filters | Clean the coil and blower wheel if heavily soiled |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, comms or board fault | Reset the breaker once | Diagnose power, communication wiring and inverter board |
| Outdoor coil iced solid, no heat | Blocked drainage, low charge, sensor fault | Clear snow and debris; don't chip ice | Leak search and repair, recharge, sensor test |
| Cold air in heat mode continuously | Stuck reversing valve or its coil | Power-cycle once | Test and replace the valve coil or valve |
| Error code shown on the display | Sensor, comms, inverter or compressor fault | Record the code, power-cycle once | Diagnose using the service manual |
| Heat fades in deep cold | Below the unit's rated range | Use backup heat | Confirm sizing; consider a low-ambient model |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Settings, filters, batteries | $0–$15 |
| Diagnostic visit | $100–$250 |
| Thermistor (sensor) replacement | $150–$350 |
| Reversing-valve coil | $150–$350 |
| Reversing valve replacement (includes refrigerant recovery and recharge) | $800–$1,500 |
| Leak repair plus recharge | $300–$1,200, depending on leak location and refrigerant |
| Indoor or outdoor control/inverter board | $400–$1,200 |
| Indoor fan motor | $300–$700 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500–$3,000+ |

If the system is older and out of warranty, a compressor failure often makes replacement the better value.
