## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you notice when a Lennox air conditioner runs (or tries to run) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On newer Lennox systems paired with an **iComfort** or **S30/S40** smart thermostat, you may see an actual alert or error code at the thermostat that points to the underlying cause. Older units with conventional thermostats give you no code at all — just warm air.

Because "not cooling" can stem from anything from a $15 filter to a failed compressor, this page walks you through the safe checks you can do yourself and what a technician should be doing before you approve an expensive repair.

If your Lennox thermostat is showing a **specific numbered code**, look that code up directly — this page covers the general no-cooling condition.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — Restricted airflow reduces cooling capacity and can freeze the indoor coil, which then blocks airflow entirely.
2. **Thermostat problem** — Wrong mode (set to "heat" or "fan only"), dead batteries, or an incorrect schedule/setpoint.
3. **Tripped breaker or disconnect** — The outdoor condenser has its own breaker and often an outdoor disconnect; if it's off, the compressor can't run.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — Grass clippings, dust, and debris insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — A very common failure. The fan or compressor may hum, struggle to start, or not start at all.
6. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — Caused by low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — AC systems don't consume refrigerant; low charge means a leak that must be found and repaired, not just "topped off."
8. **Failed contactor** — The electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit can pit or stick and stop the compressor from engaging.
9. **Blocked condensate drain** — A full drain pan trips the safety float switch and shuts cooling down on many Lennox installs.
10. **Failed compressor or fan motor** — The most expensive causes, usually a last resort after other faults are ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the electrical compartment, handle refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

- **Air filter** — Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A dirty filter is the #1 no-cooling cause.
- **Thermostat** — Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers** — Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers in your electrical panel. Reset one that has tripped **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor disconnect** — The box near the outdoor unit should be in the "on" position (don't open sealed compartments).
- **Vents and registers** — Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit airflow** — Clear leaves, grass, and debris away from the condenser and gently rinse the outside fins with a garden hose (power off first).
- **Condensate drain** — If you see water pooling or the unit shut off after a rain, a full pan or clogged drain may have tripped the float switch. Clearing the drain line is sometimes DIY-able; otherwise call a tech.
- **Ice on the lines** — If you see ice on the copper refrigerant lines or indoor coil, **turn the system off and set the fan to On** to let it thaw before running again. Persistent freezing means a real fault.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified Lennox technician should work methodically — this helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the symptom and read any codes** at the iComfort/S30 thermostat or control board.
2. **Confirm call for cooling** — measure 24V at the contactor to confirm the thermostat is sending the signal.
3. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower operation, and indoor coil for ice or dirt.
4. **Measure capacitor** — test the run/start capacitor against its rated microfarads.
5. **Test the contactor** — check for pitting, chatter, or failure to pull in.
6. **Check electrical draw** — compare compressor and fan amps to rated values (RLA/FLA).
7. **Take refrigerant readings** — measure superheat/subcooling and pressures to determine if the charge is correct and whether there's a leak.
8. **Leak search** — if charge is low, use electronic or dye leak detection rather than just adding refrigerant.
9. **Inspect the compressor** — check windings and starting components if it won't run.

Be wary of any tech who wants to "add a couple pounds of refrigerant" without doing a leak search — that's treating the symptom, not the cause.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, indoor fan running | Dirty filter / thermostat in wrong mode | Replace filter, set to Cool | Check airflow, blower, controls |
| Outdoor unit completely dead | Tripped breaker / disconnect off | Reset breaker once, check disconnect | Diagnose electrical fault if it re-trips |
| Outdoor fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — do not open panel | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on copper lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Diagnose airflow / refrigerant leak |
| House never reaches setpoint | Dirty condenser coil / low charge | Rinse outdoor coil, replace filter | Measure charge, clean coil, leak search |
| Unit shut off, water in pan | Clogged condensate drain, float tripped | Clear drain line, drain tablets | Clear line, verify float switch |
| Compressor tries then clicks off | Failing capacitor / hard-starting compressor | None | Test start components / compressor |
| Breaker trips every time | Shorted compressor or wiring fault | Stop resetting — call a pro | Isolate short, test windings |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor; varies by region and model):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300–$1,500 (depends on leak location; evaporator coil replacement runs higher)
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth weighing against full system replacement on older units)
- **Diagnostic service call:** $75–$200 (frequently credited toward the repair)

Prices vary by market, unit age, and whether your Lennox system is still under parts warranty — original Lennox warranties often cover parts but not labor, so ask.
