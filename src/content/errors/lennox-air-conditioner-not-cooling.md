## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may be running, blowing air, and showing no error at the thermostat, yet the house never reaches the setpoint. On communicating **iComfort** systems you may see a comfort-alert or a specific fault stored in the thermostat's service menu; on older non-communicating units (Merit, many Elite models) there is no display and you diagnose by behavior.

"Not cooling" covers everything from a dirty filter choking airflow to a leaked-out refrigerant charge or a dead compressor. The severity is rated **pro** because the most damaging causes — refrigerant loss and compressor failure — legally and practically require a licensed HVAC technician. The good news: several common causes are safe for a homeowner to check first.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the evaporator coil, and kills cooling capacity. The single most common cause.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, cottonwood, and dirt block heat rejection, so the system can't dump heat outside.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow (filter, blower, closed vents) or low refrigerant. Ice blocks all cooling.
4. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (Fan "On" vs "Auto"), or a miswired/failed thermostat.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced capacity, warm air, hissing, or ice on the lineset. Requires leak repair, not a top-off.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — a cheap part that can stop the compressor or fan from starting; outdoor unit hums but won't run.
7. **Contactor failure** — the outdoor unit gets no signal to energize; often the fan and compressor are both dead.
8. **Blower motor or fan issue** — indoor blower or outdoor fan not moving air, so heat exchange fails.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome. Unit runs, breaker may trip, but no cooling.
10. **Tripped breaker or blown control fuse** — outdoor disconnect or main breaker tripped, or the blown 3/5-amp board fuse on the indoor control.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are genuinely safe for any homeowner. Stop at anything requiring tools, refrigerant, or opening the sealed system.

- **Filter:** Pull and inspect it. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Set to **Cool**, fan to **Auto**, and drop the setpoint 5°F below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery model and the display is dim or blank.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor and outdoor breakers, plus the outdoor disconnect box near the condenser. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — don't keep resetting.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs. Closing too many vents can freeze the coil.
- **Condensate drain:** A full drain pan or clogged drain line can trip a safety float switch and shut the system down. If you see standing water, clear the line and dry the pan.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, **turn the system to Off but leave the fan On** for a few hours to thaw before running it again. Running a frozen unit strains the compressor.

If none of these restore cooling, the fault is likely in the refrigerant circuit or electrical components — that's technician territory.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified Lennox tech will typically:

- Verify thermostat call and confirm 24V signal reaches the outdoor contactor.
- Measure temperature split across the indoor coil (return vs supply air) — a healthy system usually shows about 15–20°F.
- Attach gauges to read **suction and liquid pressures** and calculate superheat/subcooling to judge the refrigerant charge.
- Check the run capacitor with a meter (microfarad reading vs the rating stamped on it).
- Test the contactor, compressor windings, and fan motor for continuity and amp draw.
- On iComfort systems, pull stored fault codes and comfort-alert data from the thermostat service menu.
- Leak-search with an electronic detector or dye if the charge is low.

Ask to see the pressure and temperature-split readings. A tech who claims "you're low on refrigerant" without finding and quoting the leak repair is selling you a temporary fix.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Check blower, ductwork |
| Ice on lines/indoor coil | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, run fan to thaw, replace filter | Diagnose airflow vs refrigerant, repair leak |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None (needs meter) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit totally dead | Contactor, breaker, or fuse | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor or blown control fuse |
| Warm air, hissing, low capacity | Refrigerant leak | None (regulated) | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor fan doesn't | Fan motor or capacitor | Check breaker/disconnect | Replace fan motor or capacitor |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Failed compressor or major leak | None | Diagnose compressor, quote replacement |
| System shuts off shortly after starting | Full condensate pan / float switch | Clear drain, dry pan | Clear blockage, check float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included. Actual quotes vary by region and model.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 pro
- **Run/dual capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Blower or condenser fan motor:** $400–$900
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,600 (depends on leak location; evaporator coil leaks cost more)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against full system replacement on older units)

If your Lennox condenser is over 12–15 years old and uses R-22 refrigerant, a major refrigerant or compressor repair may not be cost-effective versus replacement — ask for both quotes.
