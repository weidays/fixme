## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may be running, blowing air, and drawing power, but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

Newer Lennox systems with an **iComfort** communicating thermostat may display an actual alert or error code when a fault is detected, and the exact codes and wording **vary by control board and model**. Older units with a conventional 24V thermostat give you no code at all — just warm air. This page covers the full range of causes behind a Lennox AC that runs but won't cool, from simple DIY fixes to problems that require a licensed HVAC pro.

Because refrigerant and compressor issues are common culprits here — and both require certification and specialized tools — this is rated a **pro** repair.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, reduces cooling, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a failed thermostat that isn't calling for cooling.
3. **Tripped condensate float switch** — a full drain pan or clogged line shuts down cooling to prevent water damage (common on Lennox systems with a safety float).
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor and/or condenser fan won't start, so no cooling.
5. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked with dirt, grass, or cottonwood, it can't reject heat.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system can't absorb heat; this is a sealed-system repair, never a simple "top-off."
8. **Failed contactor** — the electrical switch that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
9. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cold air.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop before anything involving refrigerant, capacitors, or opening the electrical panel of the unit.

- **Check the air filter.** A dirty filter is the #1 cause of poor cooling and frozen coils. Replace it if it's gray or clogged.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on AUTO. Replace the **batteries** if it's battery-powered.
- **Check the breakers.** Look for a tripped breaker for both the indoor air handler/furnace and the outdoor condenser. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Check the condensate drain.** If your system has a float switch and the drain pan is full or the line is clogged, the unit won't cool. You can carefully clear a visible clog and pour water/vinegar down an accessible drain line.
- **Check the vents and registers.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Look for ice.** If the indoor coil or the refrigerant line at the outdoor unit is iced over, turn the system to **OFF** and run the fan only to let it thaw. Do not run cooling on a frozen coil.

If none of these restores cooling, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Lennox tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verifies the complaint** and checks thermostat call, mode, and any iComfort error codes on communicating systems.
2. **Measures airflow** and inspects the filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Checks electrical components** — capacitor microfarad reading, contactor condition, and voltage/amp draw at the compressor and fan motor.
4. **Reads refrigerant pressures** with gauges on both the high and low side, and calculates superheat/subcooling to judge charge and system performance.
5. **Leak-searches** if the charge is low, using electronic detectors or dye — a proper repair finds and fixes the leak, not just refills.
6. **Tests the compressor** for windings, amp draw, and starting ability if all else checks out.

Expect a diagnostic/service call fee of **$75–$200**, often credited toward the repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, house won't cool | Dirty air filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and ductwork |
| No cold air, thermostat blank/unresponsive | Dead batteries or failed thermostat | Replace batteries | Test/replace thermostat, check 24V |
| Indoor unit runs, outdoor unit silent | Failed capacitor or contactor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor/contactor |
| Ice on coil or refrigerant lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw with fan | Find restriction or leak, recharge |
| Warm air, hissing, oily residue at lines | Refrigerant leak | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, evacuate & recharge |
| Outdoor coil caked in dirt/grass | Dirty condenser coil | Gently rinse fins with hose | Deep coil cleaning, check pressures |
| Unit shut off, water in/around pan | Tripped condensate float switch | Clear visible drain clog | Clear line, test float, add tablets |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Electrical short or failing compressor | Reset once only | Diagnose short/compressor draw |
| Unit runs constantly, never cold | Failed compressor | None — call pro | Test compressor, quote replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Prices vary by region and model.

| Thermostat batteries | $5 (DIY) |
| Diagnostic / service call | $75 – $200 |
| Run capacitor replacement | $150 – $400 |
