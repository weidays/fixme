## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single Lennox error code — it's the symptom homeowners see when the system runs but the house never reaches setpoint, or air from the vents feels warm.

How your Lennox reports the problem depends on the equipment:

- **Single-stage Merit and Elite units** (ML14, EL16 and similar) don't display fault codes at all. You diagnose by symptom.
- **Variable-capacity Signature units** (SL25XCV, XC25) paired with an **iComfort thermostat** *can* store fault codes and alerts, viewable on the thermostat screen. If yours shows a numbered alert, note it — it narrows the cause considerably.

In all cases, "not cooling" points to one of four buckets: **airflow restriction**, **refrigerant loss**, **electrical/component failure**, or **thermostat/control error**. This page walks each one from most to least likely.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow across the evaporator coil, drops capacity, and can freeze the coil solid so no cold air moves. Most common and fully DIY.
2. **Dirty condenser coil (outdoor unit)** — grass, dust and cottonwood block heat rejection. The unit runs but can't shed heat, so cooling suffers.
3. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or an iComfort thermostat that lost its link to the outdoor unit.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** to the outdoor condenser — the indoor blower runs and moves air, but that air is never cooled.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor and/or condenser fan can't start. You may hear humming or clicking with no fan spin.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck open; the outdoor unit never energizes.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, ice on the copper lines, longer run times. Requires a licensed tech; never a simple "top-off."
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — caused by low airflow or low refrigerant. The unit blows warm and may drip.
9. **Failing compressor** — the heart of the system. Symptoms include tripping breakers, loud hard-start noises, or no cooling at all. Most expensive outcome.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should perform. None involve refrigerant, opening the sealed system, or bypassing a safety.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of "warm air."
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the fan to **AUTO**, and setpoint below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor air-handler/furnace breaker and the **outdoor condenser breaker/disconnect**. If a breaker has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop — call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for the fan spinning and airflow out the top. Clear leaves, grass and debris from the coil fins. Never insert tools or fingers while power is on.
- **Condensate:** Check the drain pan and line. A backed-up drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Clear standing water and add drain tablets.
- **Iced-up unit:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or copper lines, turn the system OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw for several hours before running again. If it re-freezes, you have a refrigerant or airflow fault for a tech.

If none of these restore cooling, the remaining causes require a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Check airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Verify power and controls** — reads the outdoor disconnect, contactor, capacitor, and thermostat call. On iComfort systems they'll read stored **alert codes** at the thermostat or control board.
4. **Test electrical components** — capacitor microfarad reading, contactor condition, compressor amp draw against nameplate.
5. **Connect refrigerant gauges** — checks pressures and superheat/subcooling. Abnormal readings point to undercharge (leak), overcharge, or a metering/compressor issue.
6. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — electronic detector or dye. A reputable tech repairs the leak, not just recharges.

Be cautious of any quote that recharges refrigerant without a leak search — the charge will just leak out again.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but is warm | Outdoor unit not running | Check outdoor breaker/disconnect; reset once | Test capacitor, contactor, compressor |
| Weak airflow, house never cools | Dirty filter / dirty coil | Replace filter; rinse outdoor coil gently | Deep coil cleaning, blower check |
| Ice on copper lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Find/repair leak, recharge, verify charge |
| Humming, no fan spin outside | Failed run capacitor | None — power hazard | Replace capacitor, verify amps |
| Outdoor unit dead, indoor fine | Blown fuse or bad contactor | Check disconnect fuse/breaker | Replace contactor or fuse |
| Breaker trips when AC starts | Failing compressor or short | Do NOT keep resetting | Diagnose compressor/wiring |
| Thermostat blank or offline | Dead batteries / lost link | Replace batteries; re-pair | Control board or wiring check |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged condensate drain | Clear drain, add tablets | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$650
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,200+ (often makes an older system worth replacing)
- **Full system diagnostic/service call:** $80–$200 (often credited toward the repair)

Prices vary by region, refrigerant type (older R-410A vs. newer systems), and whether the unit is still under Lennox parts warranty — check your registration, as many Lennox parts carry a 5–10 year limited warranty.
