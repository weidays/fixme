## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the most common complaint homeowners bring to a Lennox air conditioner. The system may be running (fan spinning, air moving) but the air is warm or only mildly cool, or the outdoor unit may not be starting at all.

Unlike a furnace lockout, a Lennox AC that fails to cool usually gives no blinking-light error unless you have a communicating **iComfort** system, which can display fault messages on the thermostat (for example, a call for cooling with no compressor response). Older single-stage Merit and Elite units give no code at all — you diagnose by symptom.

This page covers the mechanical and electrical reasons a Lennox split system stops cooling, what you can safely check, and what a technician should charge.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil. The single most common cause.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant. You'll see ice on the indoor coil or refrigerant lines and weak/warm airflow.
3. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system never gets cold enough. Requires EPA-certified diagnosis; refrigerant does not "run out" normally.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common outdoor-unit failure. The compressor or fan motor hums or won't start.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so cooling fades, especially on hot afternoons.
6. **Condenser fan motor failure** — outdoor fan not spinning causes high-pressure shutdown and warm air.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown control fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
8. **Thermostat, wiring, or contactor fault** — the call for cooling never reaches the compressor. On iComfort systems a communication fault can also block cooling.
9. **Compressor failure** — least common but most expensive. The unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe. Do **not** open the outdoor unit's electrical panel, touch the capacitor, or add refrigerant.

- **Thermostat**: Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. On iComfort, note any on-screen alert message.
- **Air filter**: Pull it and inspect. If it's gray or clogged, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Breakers**: Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor AC breakers. If one is tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents**: Make sure they're open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Frozen coil**: If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF (leave the fan ON) and let it thaw for a few hours before restarting.
- **Condensate drain**: If a safety float switch tripped due to a clogged drain, the unit may refuse to cool. Check for standing water in the drain pan.
- **Outdoor unit**: Look for debris, tall grass, or leaves blocking the condenser. Clear the area around it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox technician should:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** at the thermostat and confirm 24V reaches the outdoor contactor.
2. **Check the run capacitor** with a meter — a cheap, common failure they should test early.
3. **Inspect the contactor and compressor/fan amp draw** to see whether motors are starting and pulling normal current.
4. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and read suction/head pressures and superheat/subcooling to judge the charge. Low readings point to a leak.
5. **Run a leak search** (electronic detector, dye, or bubble test) if refrigerant is low — a recharge without finding the leak is a red flag.
6. **Inspect the evaporator and condenser coils** for dirt or ice, and measure temperature split across the indoor coil (typically 16–22°F on a healthy system).
7. On **iComfort** systems, read stored fault history at the thermostat or with a service tool.

Ask for the actual pressure and temperature readings. A quote for "add refrigerant" with no leak-search line item deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, low airflow | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check coil/blower if unresolved |
| Ice on coil or lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, run fan | Find airflow issue or leak |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Bad capacitor or fan motor | None — call pro | Test/replace capacitor or motor |
| Unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Replace dual run capacitor |
| Cooling fades on hot days | Dirty condenser coil | Clear debris around unit | Professional coil cleaning |
| Warm air, normal airflow | Low refrigerant (leak) | None — call pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker/contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor/fuse, check wiring |
| Runs constantly, no cooling | Compressor failure | None — call pro | Compressor test/replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor). Regional labor rates and your Lennox model tier affect the total.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Air filter (DIY) | $0–$30 |
| Condensate drain clearing | $75–$250 |
| Run capacitor | $150–$400 |
| Contactor replacement | $150–$350 |
| Condenser fan motor | $300–$650 |
| Condenser coil cleaning | $150–$400 |
| Refrigerant leak repair + recharge | $600–$1,600 |
| Evaporator coil replacement | $1,000–$2,500 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500–$2,500+ |

Note: If your Lennox uses R-22 refrigerant (older units), recharge costs are far higher and often make replacement the smarter choice. Confirm your refrigerant type before approving a recharge.
