## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on your Lennox equipment — it's the
symptom homeowners describe when the system runs but the house won't reach the
thermostat setpoint, or the air from the vents isn't cold. On communicating
Lennox systems (iComfort thermostats paired with SL, XC, or variable-capacity
condensers), the thermostat may show a specific alert or error number instead.
On older single-stage Merit and Elite units there's no display at all, so you're
working from symptoms.

The problem falls into three buckets: **airflow** (filter, blower, coil),
**the refrigerant circuit** (charge, compressor, coils), or **electrical/controls**
(capacitor, contactor, thermostat, float switch). This page walks all three so
you can rule out the cheap, safe stuff before paying for a diagnostic visit.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the indoor coil, and kills cooling. Most common and fully DIY.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, cottonwood, and dust blanket the coil so it can't reject heat.
3. **Thermostat issue** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), or a miswired/failing communicating stat.
4. **Tripped condensate float switch** — a full or clogged drain line trips a safety switch and shuts cooling down.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan won't start; you may hear humming or see the fan not spinning.
6. **Frozen indoor evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — weak cooling, long run times, ice on the copper line. Requires a certified pro.
8. **Failed contactor or start components** — outdoor unit won't energize even when calling for cool.
9. **Compressor failure** — the outdoor unit hums but the compressor won't run, or it trips the breaker. The costliest fix.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Air filter:** Pull it. If light won't pass through it, replace it. A dirty filter is the #1 cause of poor cooling and coil freeze-ups.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, setpoint is below room temperature, and fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's a battery-powered model.
- **Breaker:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler **and** the outdoor condenser (they're often separate). Reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor unit:** Make sure the condenser coil (the fins wrapping the outdoor unit) isn't packed with grass, leaves, or debris. Clear vegetation back at least 2 feet. With power **off** at the disconnect, gently rinse fins with a garden hose from the inside out.
- **Vents and registers:** Confirm supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate:** Look for a full drain pan or a small float switch on the drain line. If the pan is full, the system may have shut off on purpose. Clear standing water and check the drain line for clogs.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and set the fan to **On** for a few hours to thaw before restarting. Persistent freezing means a real problem — call a pro.

Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, bypass the float switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech will:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** — confirm the thermostat is sending 24V and the low-voltage wiring is intact.
2. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower, and indoor coil; measure temperature split across the coil (typically 16–22°F on a healthy system).
3. **Read the communicating board** — on iComfort/XC/SL systems, pull active and stored alert codes from the thermostat or control board.
4. **Test electrical components** — measure the run capacitor's microfarad value, test the contactor, and confirm the compressor and fan motor are drawing correct amps.
5. **Take refrigerant readings** — connect gauges, measure suction/liquid pressures and superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct or if there's a leak.
6. **Leak search** — if the charge is low, use an electronic detector or dye rather than just topping off.

If a quote jumps straight to "add refrigerant" with no leak search, that's a red flag — a properly charged system doesn't lose refrigerant unless there's a leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak or no cold air, dirty filter | Clogged filter restricting airflow | Replace filter, thaw coil if iced | Inspect coil/blower if it recurs |
| Outdoor unit runs but house won't cool | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse fins with power off | Deep coil cleaning, check charge |
| Vents blow room-temp air, fan runs | Wrong thermostat setting or dead batteries | Set to Cool/Auto, replace batteries | Test/replace thermostat, check wiring |
| System off, water in drain pan | Tripped condensate float switch | Clear drain line and pan | Clear/repair drain, replace switch |
| Outdoor fan not spinning, humming | Failed run capacitor | None — call a pro | Test and replace capacitor/contactor |
| Ice on copper lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw with fan | Diagnose airflow vs. refrigerant leak |
| Long run times, warm air, some ice | Low refrigerant from a leak | None — call a certified pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor unit hums, trips breaker | Compressor or hard-start failure | Shut off, don't reset repeatedly | Test compressor, quote replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor). Actual pricing varies by region and by whether your unit is single-stage or a communicating variable-capacity system, which uses more expensive controls.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$10 (DIY).
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250.
- **Condensate float switch replacement:** $100–$250.
- **Run/dual capacitor replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$700.
- **Coil cleaning (deep/professional):** $150–$400.
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair + recharge:** $500–$1,800 depending on leak location.
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ — often the tipping point toward replacing the whole condenser, especially on units 10+ years old.

Get a written diagnosis with measured pressures and part readings before authorizing any repair over a few hundred dollars.
