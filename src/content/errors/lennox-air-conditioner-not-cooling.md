## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific Lennox error code — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may be running (fan spinning, outdoor unit humming) but the air coming out of the vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On newer **iComfort** and **variable-capacity** Lennox systems (like the SL28V or XC21), you may see an actual fault code on the thermostat display or the outdoor control board, which narrows the cause considerably.

Cooling depends on three things working together: airflow (blower + clean coils + clear filter), the refrigerant circuit (correct charge, working compressor), and the controls (thermostat, contactor, capacitor, safeties). A failure in any one of these produces "not cooling." Because several of the likely causes involve refrigerant or the compressor, this is rated **pro** — real diagnosis usually needs gauges and a certified technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return** — Restricts airflow, causes weak/warm air and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — From low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so no cold air comes out.
3. **Clogged condensate drain / tripped float switch** — A safety float shuts off cooling when the drain backs up. Very common in humid climates.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — Grass clippings, cottonwood, and dirt insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — The compressor or outdoor fan won't start; you may hear a hum or clicking.
6. **Bad contactor** — Pitted or stuck contacts prevent the outdoor unit from energizing.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — Undercharge means little cooling and often a frozen coil. Requires leak repair, not just a "top-off."
8. **Thermostat problem or wiring fault** — Wrong mode, dead batteries, or a broken 24V wire.
9. **Failed compressor** — The most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cooling, or trips the breaker.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do — no panels off, no refrigerant, no bypassing safeties:

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of weak cooling and frozen coils.
- **Breakers:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker on the air handler and outdoor unit. Reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers are open and returns aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain:** If the indoor unit stopped and there's water near the air handler, the float switch likely tripped. Check for a clogged drain line; you can pour drain tablets in the pan.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** but leave the fan **ON** for a few hours to thaw before running cooling again.
- **Outdoor unit:** Gently clear leaves, grass, and debris from around and off the condenser (power off first). Do not bend the fins or spray high-pressure water.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Lennox tech works from cheap to expensive:

1. **Reads any fault codes** on the iComfort thermostat or outdoor board (variable-capacity units log specific faults).
2. **Checks airflow** — filter, blower operation, and static pressure.
3. **Inspects the coils** — indoor and outdoor for dirt, ice, or restriction.
4. **Tests electrical** — measures capacitor microfarads, checks the contactor, and confirms 24V control voltage.
5. **Connects refrigerant gauges** and takes temperature readings to calculate **superheat and subcooling** — this tells them if the charge is correct or if there's a leak or restriction.
6. **Leak search** if the charge is low — electronic sniffer, bubbles, or dye. A legitimate shop repairs the leak; it doesn't just add refrigerant and leave.
7. **Compressor test** — checks windings, amp draw, and whether it's grounded or seized.

If a quote jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without any of steps 1–5, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm air from vents | Dirty filter or frozen coil | Replace filter; turn to fan-only to thaw | Check airflow, charge, coil condition |
| Indoor unit stopped, water present | Clogged drain / tripped float | Clear drain, add tablets | Flush drain line, test/replace float switch |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None (capacitor holds charge) | Test and replace run/start capacitor |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Bad contactor or low refrigerant | Check breaker only | Test contactor; gauge charge, find leak |
| Ice on refrigerant lines | Low airflow or low charge | Thaw with fan-only, new filter | Diagnose airflow vs. undercharge |
| Outdoor coil caked with dirt | Dirty condenser | Rinse gently, power off | Deep coil cleaning, verify charge |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Compressor or electrical fault | Do not keep resetting | Test compressor windings and amps |
| House never reaches setpoint | Undercharge or undersized/aging unit | Verify filter and vents | Full charge and capacity check |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor; regional and unit-dependent):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (higher if using R-410A on newer units or a hard-to-reach coil leak)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800–$2,500+ (often the point where replacing the whole condenser or system is worth pricing)

If your Lennox unit is 12+ years old and facing a compressor or coil, ask your tech to price full replacement — sometimes the repair is close enough that a new system makes more sense.
