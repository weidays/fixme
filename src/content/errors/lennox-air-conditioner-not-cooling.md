## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a fault code Lennox logs on a display — it's the symptom you're living with. Your Lennox air conditioner may be running, cycling, or dead silent, but the air coming out of your vents is warm or barely cool.

On communicating Lennox systems (iComfort-equipped Signature series), the indoor board or thermostat may store an actual numeric fault code that points to the cause. On Merit and most Elite single-stage units, there's no display at all — you diagnose by symptom, not by code.

This page walks through the real reasons a Lennox central AC stops cooling, what you can safely check, and what a technician should charge to fix it.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the #1 cause. Restricted airflow drops coil temperature, ices up the evaporator, and kills cooling.
2. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse to the outdoor unit** — if only the indoor fan runs and the outdoor condenser is dead, suspect power.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure, especially in summer heat. The compressor or fan motor hums but won't start.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — Lennox systems don't "use up" refrigerant; low charge always means a leak. Causes weak cooling and frozen coils.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so the system runs constantly without cooling.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit gets pitted or stuck, cutting power to the compressor.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — usually a downstream symptom of low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks the coil entirely.
8. **Thermostat problem or dead batteries** — a miscommunicating or mis-set thermostat never calls for cooling.
9. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; the outdoor unit tries to start, trips, or is silent, and the system won't cool at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Everything else involves electrical, refrigerant, or sealed components — leave those to a pro.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on AUTO. Replace the batteries if it's a battery-powered model.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker. Lennox systems typically have two — one for the indoor air handler/furnace and one for the outdoor condenser. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated trips signal a short.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed registers aren't choking airflow. Keep return grilles clear.
- **Outdoor unit:** Check that the condenser isn't buried in leaves, grass clippings, or cottonwood fluff. Gently rinse the exterior fins with a garden hose (power off first) — do not use a pressure washer.
- **Condensate:** If your system has a float safety switch and the drain is clogged, the unit may refuse to cool. Look for standing water near the indoor unit; a clogged drain is a common shutdown trigger.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn cooling OFF and set the fan to ON to thaw it (can take 1–3 hours), then replace the filter before restarting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech should work systematically — this is how you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** at the thermostat and confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor unit.
2. **Check line voltage and the contactor** — measuring whether the contactor pulls in and passes power to the compressor and fan.
3. **Test the run/start capacitor** with a meter against its rated microfarads. A weak capacitor reads low.
4. **Measure refrigerant pressures** with gauges and take superheat/subcooling readings to judge charge and airflow.
5. **Inspect the coils** — condenser and evaporator — for dirt and ice, and check the blower for proper airflow.
6. **Read stored fault codes** on iComfort/communicating systems via the thermostat or control board.
7. **Test the compressor** windings and amp draw if the outdoor unit won't start.

Ask the tech to show you the capacitor reading or refrigerant pressures. A legitimate diagnosis is measurable, not a guess.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm vents | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check coil and blower if it recurs |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker or blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Find the cause of the trip; replace fuse |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace run capacitor |
| Runs constantly, barely cools | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Rinse coil exterior | Clean coil; check refrigerant, find leak |
| Ice on coil or copper lines | Frozen evaporator | Turn off, fan ON to thaw, new filter | Diagnose airflow or refrigerant cause |
| Clicking, no power to condenser | Failed contactor | None — call pro | Replace 24V contactor |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries or wiring | Replace batteries | Repair wiring / replace thermostat |
| Outdoor unit trips breaker repeatedly | Failing compressor or short | Stop resetting | Test compressor windings and amp draw |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices including parts and labor. Regional rates and system size vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 (more for iComfort communicating models)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,200+ (often worth weighing against full system replacement on older units)

If your Lennox is under 10 years old, check whether the sealed system (compressor/coil) is still under the manufacturer parts warranty — you may owe only labor.
