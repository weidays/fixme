## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific numeric fault code — it's the symptom you notice: the Lennox system is running (or trying to run) but your home isn't getting cold. On newer Lennox systems with an **iComfort** or S30/S40 thermostat, you may see plain-language alerts or numeric codes on the display or app instead of a blinking LED. Older Merit and Elite units often communicate faults through an LED on the outdoor control board.

Because "not cooling" covers everything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, the goal here is to narrow it down. Some causes are safe for a homeowner to check; others require a licensed HVAC tech, especially anything involving refrigerant or high-voltage components.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — restricts airflow across the indoor coil, dropping capacity and eventually freezing the coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection so the system can't cool effectively.
3. **Thermostat setting or wiring issue** — set to "fan on" instead of "cool," wrong mode, dead batteries, or a lost signal on communicating iComfort systems.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the outdoor fan or compressor hums but won't start; very common failure in hot climates.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak** — gradual loss of cooling, longer run times, and sometimes ice on the copper lines.
6. **Failed contactor** — the 24V relay that powers the outdoor unit gets pitted or stuck and won't close.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — the result of low airflow or low charge; you'll see ice on the indoor coil or lineset.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — no power to the outdoor unit.
9. **Failed compressor or fan motor** — the most expensive outcome, often after long-term neglect of the causes above.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should perform. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

- **Filter:** Pull the indoor air filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it and run the system for an hour to see if cooling recovers.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery-powered model. On iComfort/S30/S40 units, check the screen for alerts and confirm Wi‑Fi/comfort settings.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor breakers. Reset **once** if tripped. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs. Too many closed vents can freeze the coil.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Make sure the fan is spinning when the system calls for cooling.
- **Condensate:** If the indoor unit has a float switch and the drain is clogged, the system may shut off cooling. A visibly full drain pan or standing water near the air handler points to this. You can carefully clear a visible drain line, but leave wiring alone.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** but leave the **fan On** to thaw it. Never chip at ice. If it refreezes after thawing, you need a tech.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Lennox tech works from cheap-and-likely to expensive-and-rare, roughly in this order:

1. **Confirms the symptom** and reads any thermostat/board codes (iComfort alerts or outdoor LED flash patterns).
2. **Checks airflow** — filter, blower operation, and coil condition.
3. **Measures electrical values** — verifies 24V control voltage, tests the capacitor with a meter, and inspects the contactor for pitting.
4. **Puts gauges on the system** to read suction and liquid pressures and calculates **superheat and subcooling** — this tells them whether the charge is correct or the system is low.
5. **Performs a leak search** if the charge is low (electronic sniffer, dye, or nitrogen pressure test).
6. **Tests the compressor and fan motor** windings and amp draw if the unit still won't cool.

If a tech recommends adding refrigerant without also looking for a leak, get a second opinion — refrigerant doesn't get "used up," so a low charge always means a leak somewhere.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Inspect coil and blower if unchanged |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None (high voltage) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Gradual loss of cooling, longer runtimes | Low refrigerant (leak) | None | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low charge | Turn to Off, fan On to thaw | Diagnose airflow/charge root cause |
| Nothing runs outside | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| System short-cycles, then stops cooling | Clogged condensate/float switch | Clear visible drain | Clear drain, verify float switch |
| Loud hum, then breaker trips | Failing compressor | Stop resetting | Test windings, replace compressor |
| Thermostat blank or no cool call | Dead batteries or control fault | Replace batteries | Check 24V, board, or comm wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor). Prices vary by region and by whether your unit is still under Lennox warranty.

- **Air filter:** $15 – $40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $75 – $250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400 – $1,500 (depends on leak location; evaporator or condenser coil leaks are at the high end)
- **Condenser fan motor:** $400 – $700
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $900 – $2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800 – $3,000+ (often worth comparing against full system replacement on older units)

A diagnostic/service call fee of **$85 – $200** is typical and is often applied toward the repair.
