## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific error code — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may be running (or trying to run) but the air coming from your vents is warm, weak, or barely below room temperature.

How your system reports the problem depends on the model:

- **Single-stage Merit series** (e.g. ML14XC1): usually no code at all. The unit just runs and fails to cool, so you diagnose by symptoms.
- **Variable-capacity Elite/Signature models** with an iComfort thermostat (XC21, XC25, SL28XCV): the thermostat or the outdoor control board may display an alert or fault message pointing to a specific component (compressor, sensor, communication error). Always read the exact wording on your thermostat — it narrows the cause considerably.

"Not cooling" spans everything from a $15 filter to a failed compressor, so the goal below is to help you rule out the cheap, safe stuff before paying for a diagnostic.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the #1 cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from a dirty filter, blocked return vents, or low refrigerant. A block of ice means zero cooling.
3. **Thermostat issue** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setpoint, or a wiring/communication fault (common on iComfort systems).
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling and coil freeze-up. Requires a certified tech; never just a "top-off."
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the outdoor fan or compressor hums but won't start, or trips off quickly.
6. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked with dirt, grass, or debris, the unit can't reject heat.
7. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
9. **Blower motor failure** — indoor fan doesn't move air across the coil.
10. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive cause; usually a last resort after everything else checks out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps shutting down.

- **Filter:** Pull the return-air filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a huge share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **AUTO**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker (both the indoor air handler/furnace and the outdoor condenser). Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents & registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs. Blocked airflow causes freeze-ups.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, switch the system to **FAN ONLY** (or OFF) for a few hours to let it thaw completely before running cooling again. Running a frozen unit can damage the compressor.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. If you have an accessible drain line, clear standing water and pour in drain tablets. Do not disable the float switch.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the outside of the coil with a garden hose (power off) if it's visibly dirty.

If cooling doesn't return after these checks, the problem is likely refrigerant, electrical, or mechanical — call a licensed HVAC technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Read the fault/alert** on the iComfort thermostat or outdoor board (variable models) and check any stored history.
2. **Verify airflow** — inspect filter, blower operation, and coil for ice.
3. **Measure electrical values** — checks voltage at the contactor, tests the capacitor with a meter (microfarad reading vs. spec), and confirms the contactor pulls in.
4. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and measure suction/liquid pressures, superheat, and subcooling to determine if the charge is correct.
5. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — electronic sniffer, UV dye, or soap bubbles to find the leak location.
6. **Check the compressor** — measures amp draw and windings if it isn't starting or is short-cycling.
7. **Confirm the fix** — verifies proper temperature split (typically 16–22°F difference between return and supply air) after repair.

A good tech won't just "add a pound of Freon" and leave. If refrigerant is low, they should find and quote the leak. A top-off without a leak search is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm airflow, dirty filter | Clogged filter restricting air | Replace filter | None needed if that fixes it |
| Ice on coil or refrigerant lines | Airflow restriction or low charge | Set to FAN only to thaw; replace filter | Find cause; repair leak / recharge |
| Outdoor unit hums but fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — stop and call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit dead, no power | Tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor; check wiring |
| Runs constantly, never reaches setpoint | Low refrigerant leak or dirty coils | Clean outdoor coil area | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Indoor fan not running | Blower motor / control fault | Check breaker & filter | Test/replace blower or board |
| Thermostat blank or wrong mode | Dead batteries / settings | Replace batteries; set COOL | Check thermostat wiring/comm |
| Cools poorly, high energy bills | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse coil exterior (power off) | Professional coil cleaning |
| Short-cycles then quits | Failing compressor / overload | None — call pro | Diagnose compressor; quote replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor, typical 2026 pricing). Your area and unit size affect these:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$200 professional; ~$15 DIY with tablets
- **Capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Professional coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type; R-410A vs. older R-22 costs vary widely)
- **Blower motor replacement:** $450–$1,200
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often the point where replacing the whole system is worth considering)
- **Full outdoor unit / system replacement:** $5,000–$12,000+

A diagnostic service call typically runs **$90–$200**, often credited toward the repair.
