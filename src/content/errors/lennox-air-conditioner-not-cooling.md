## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's the most common symptom homeowners report with Lennox air conditioners. On standard 24V systems there may be **no error code at all**, just warm air. On Lennox communicating systems with an **iComfort** thermostat, you may see a specific alert or fault number on the display that points to a component or communication problem.

In every case, "not cooling" means the refrigeration cycle isn't moving enough heat out of your home. That can be an airflow problem, an electrical problem, a refrigerant problem, or a mechanical failure of the compressor. The good news: several causes are cheap DIY checks. The bad news: refrigerant and compressor issues are pro-only and can be expensive.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat issue** — wrong mode (heat vs. cool), a too-high setpoint, dead batteries, or on iComfort systems a lost connection to the equipment.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser and indoor blower are often on separate circuits; one can trip without the other.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant. You'll see ice on the indoor coil or the copper line, and little to no airflow.
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common outdoor-unit failure. The fan or compressor hums but won't start.
6. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil caked in grass, dust, or cottonwood can't reject heat, so cooling drops off on hot days.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, longer run times, possible icing. Requires a licensed tech.
8. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or burns out.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — the outdoor fan doesn't spin, the unit overheats and shuts down.
10. **Failed compressor** — the worst case. The unit runs but produces no cooling, or trips the breaker on startup.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here if a check requires opening the sealed refrigerant system or the outdoor electrical panel.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and look. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the fan is on **AUTO**, and the setpoint is at least a few degrees below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. On iComfort, check that it shows a live connection to the equipment.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler/furnace **and** the outdoor condenser. Reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure vents aren't closed, blocked by furniture, or shut in too many rooms — this starves the system of airflow.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper line or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** but leave the fan on **ON** for a few hours to thaw, then replace the filter and try cooling again. If it re-freezes, you likely have a refrigerant or airflow problem for a tech.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a safety float switch and stop cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan and pour drain treatment down the line.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical path, and knowing it helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply and return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower, and coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Confirm the call for cooling** — verify the thermostat is sending 24V, and on iComfort, read any stored fault codes at the control board.
4. **Test electrical components** — measure the capacitor's microfarads, check the contactor, and confirm the compressor and condenser fan draw proper amperage.
5. **Read refrigerant pressures** — attach gauges to measure suction and liquid pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling. This reveals a low charge, a leak, or a restriction.
6. **Leak search** — if the charge is low, they use an electronic detector or dye to locate the leak before recharging.
7. **Compressor test** — if pressures and electricals are normal but no cooling, they test the compressor's windings and mechanical function.

Expect a **diagnostic fee of $80–$150**, usually credited toward the repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and ductwork |
| Ice on coil or copper line | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Thaw with fan ON, replace filter | Find airflow/refrigerant cause, recharge |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — capacitors hold a charge | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit dead, indoor runs | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check for cause |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed condenser fan motor | Turn unit off to prevent damage | Replace fan motor |
| Long run times, poor cooling on hot days | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Rinse outdoor coil gently with hose | Deep-clean coil, check refrigerant |
| Runs constantly, no cold air | Low refrigerant or failed compressor | None | Leak repair, recharge, or compressor replacement |
| No cooling, water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate float switch | Clear drain, add treatment | Clear line, verify float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges. Actual prices vary by region, unit size, and refrigerant type (older R-410A vs. newer refrigerants).

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Diagnostic fee:** $80–$150
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650 installed
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $200–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant amount
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against full unit replacement)

If your Lennox is under the original parts warranty, the compressor part may be covered — you'd typically still pay labor and refrigerant. Keep your registration and model/serial handy.
