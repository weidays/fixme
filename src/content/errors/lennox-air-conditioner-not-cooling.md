## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your Lennox air conditioner runs but the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint, or the air from the vents isn't cold. On connected Lennox systems (iComfort, S30/S40 thermostats), you may see a related alert or a specific equipment error in the app, but many cooling failures produce no code at all because the problem is mechanical (airflow, refrigerant, capacitor) rather than a board-detected electrical fault.

Because effective cooling depends on correct refrigerant charge, clean coils, adequate airflow and a healthy compressor, "not cooling" covers a wide range of root causes. Some are safe DIY checks; several require an EPA-certified technician.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow drops cooling capacity and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat setting or wiring issue** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a schedule override. On iComfort systems, a lost equipment connection can stop cooling calls.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser and indoor blower are often on separate circuits; one can trip and leave the fan running with no cooling.
4. **Dirty condenser coil / blocked outdoor unit** — grass, cottonwood, or debris packed in the outdoor coil kills heat rejection.
5. **Failed run capacitor or contactor** — a very common outdoor-unit failure that stops the compressor and/or fan from starting.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling, ice on the lines, and eventually no cooling. Requires leak repair, not just a "top-off."
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — a symptom of low airflow or low refrigerant; the coil turns to a block of ice and blocks all airflow.
8. **Clogged condensate drain triggering a float switch** — a full drain pan can shut down cooling to prevent water damage.
9. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit hums or trips but won't produce cold air.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here and call a pro for anything else.

- **Filter:** Locate and inspect your air filter. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is set to Auto. Replace batteries if it uses them. On iComfort/S30 units, check for alerts on the screen or in the app.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker to both the air handler/furnace and the outdoor unit. Reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — don't keep resetting it.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers are open and return-air grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** With the system off, clear leaves, grass clippings and debris away from the outdoor condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance around it.
- **Condensate drain:** Check for standing water near the indoor unit. If your drain has a visible cleanout, you can pour a cup of water through to confirm it flows. A clogged drain that tripped a float switch will stop cooling.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, remove electrical panels on the outdoor unit, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will typically:

1. **Verify the thermostat call** for cooling reaches the equipment and confirm 24V control voltage.
2. **Check airflow** — filter, blower operation, and evaporator coil condition; measure temperature split across the coil (typically ~14–20°F when healthy).
3. **Inspect the outdoor unit** — test the run capacitor with a meter, check the contactor, and confirm the compressor and condenser fan energize.
4. **Attach refrigerant gauges** to read suction and liquid pressures, and measure superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct or if there's a leak.
5. **Leak-search** with electronic detectors or dye if refrigerant is low.
6. **Test the compressor** electrically (windings, amp draw) if it won't start.

Sanity check: a legitimate quote should name the specific failed part (capacitor, contactor, coil, compressor) and, for low refrigerant, describe finding and fixing the leak — not just recharging.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but isn't cold | Dirty filter, low refrigerant, dirty condenser | Replace filter, clear outdoor unit | Check charge, clean coil, leak search |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Failed compressor or capacitor | None — call a pro | Test capacitor/compressor, replace |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Set fan to On to thaw, swap filter | Diagnose airflow vs. charge, repair |
| Nothing runs at all | Tripped breaker, blown fuse, thermostat | Reset breaker once, check t-stat | Trace power/control fault |
| Water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain/float switch | Clear visible drain, use tablets | Clear line, test float switch |
| Weak cooling on hot days | Undercharge or dirty condenser coil | Clean debris from outdoor unit | Measure subcooling, clean coil |
| App shows equipment alert | iComfort comm/equipment fault | Note the message, restart t-stat | Diagnose per specific alert |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US retail; your area, model and warranty status will vary. Many Lennox parts carry a 5–10 year parts warranty if registered — confirm before paying for parts.

- **Air filter (DIY):** $10–$40
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80–$180
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Condenser or evaporator coil cleaning:** $100–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $200–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $600–$2,000+
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,800+ (often near the cost of replacing the outdoor unit — get a full comparison)

If the unit is old (12+ years) and facing a compressor or coil replacement, ask for a repair-vs-replace comparison.
