## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific error number on a Lennox control board — it's the symptom homeowners describe when the air conditioner runs but the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint, or when the system does nothing at all on a cooling call.

On Lennox units with a communicating iComfort or S30/S40 thermostat, you may see an actual alert code or a message like "system fault" instead of a silent no-cool. On older non-communicating systems (legacy thermostats and Merit-series equipment), there is often no code at all — just warm air. Because the cause ranges from a $10 filter to a $2,500 compressor, this page walks you from cheapest to most serious.

If you have a Lennox **furnace** (heating) fault instead, see the related code below — this page is for the outdoor air conditioner and indoor cooling coil only.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged or collapsed air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the evaporator coil, and kills cooling capacity. The single most common cause.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, cottonwood, and dirt block heat rejection so the system can't dump heat outside.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (Heat/Off instead of Cool), or a miswired/failing thermostat that never calls for cooling.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler loses power while the thermostat still shows "cooling."
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or fan hums but won't start, or the outdoor fan spins but the compressor doesn't.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow (see #1) or low refrigerant; ice blocks the coil and cooling stops entirely.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — long cooling cycles, warm air, ice on the copper lines. Requires a certified tech to find and repair.
8. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit fails, so the compressor never energizes.
9. **Failing compressor** — grinding, tripping breakers, or no start even with a good capacitor. The most expensive outcome.
10. **Blocked condensate drain (on some units)** — a safety float switch shuts down cooling to prevent water damage on systems equipped with one.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, remove electrical panels, or bypass any safety switch.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is at least 3–5°F below room temperature. On non-communicating stats, replace the batteries.
- **Air filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of no-cool calls.
- **Breakers:** Check the main panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler/furnace **and** the outdoor condenser (often a separate double-pole breaker). Reset once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear grass, leaves, and debris from around the condenser. Make sure nothing blocks airflow within 2 feet.
- **Supply and return vents:** Open closed registers and make sure furniture or rugs aren't blocking return grilles. Restricted airflow freezes the coil.
- **Condensate drain / safety switch:** If your indoor unit has a drain pan with standing water or a float switch, a clog may be shutting the system off. You can pour a cup of vinegar or a drain tablet into an accessible cleanout, but don't force anything.
- **Check for ice:** If you see frost or ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** (fan **On** is OK) and let it thaw fully for a few hours before running again. Running an iced unit can damage the compressor.

If the house still won't cool after a clean filter, good batteries, correct settings, and a reset breaker — it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the cooling call** at the thermostat and confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor contactor.
2. **Read any iComfort alert codes** on communicating systems (S30/S40 thermostats log fault history).
3. **Check the capacitor and contactor** with a meter — cheap, common failures ruled out first.
4. **Measure amp draw** on the compressor and fan motor to spot a seizing compressor or weak motor.
5. **Take temperature-split readings** (return vs. supply air) — a healthy split is roughly 14–20°F.
6. **Attach gauges** to read suction/head pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling — this reveals low refrigerant or a restriction.
7. **Leak search** with an electronic detector or dye if refrigerant is low.
8. **Inspect coils** for dirt and the evaporator for ice.

A good tech won't jump straight to "you need a new compressor" or "add refrigerant" without pressure readings and a leak check. Be cautious of any quote to "just top off the refrigerant" with no leak repair — that's a temporary fix that leaks out again.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak airflow | Clogged filter / frozen coil | Replace filter; thaw coil with system off | Confirm airflow, check blower and coil |
| No air, thermostat blank | Dead batteries / no power | Replace batteries; check breakers | Trace 24V and transformer fault |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Bad capacitor / compressor | None — call a pro | Test/replace capacitor or diagnose compressor |
| Humming outdoor unit, no start | Failed capacitor or contactor | Cut power; do not repeatedly reset | Replace capacitor/contactor, verify amps |
| Ice on copper lines | Low refrigerant or low airflow | Turn off, thaw, check filter | Pressure test, leak search, recharge |
| Breaker trips on cooling call | Short, seized compressor | Reset once only; then stop | Electrical/compressor diagnosis |
| Long cycles, house stays warm | Dirty condenser or low charge | Rinse outdoor coil gently | Clean coil, verify charge and split |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear accessible drain | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included where noted:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Service/diagnostic call:** $80–$180
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650 installed
- **Coil cleaning (outdoor or indoor):** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A pricing varies)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,200
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth weighing against full system replacement on older units)

Costs vary by region, Lennox series (Signature variable-capacity systems cost more to service than Merit single-stage), and whether the part is under Lennox's parts warranty — always check your warranty registration first.
