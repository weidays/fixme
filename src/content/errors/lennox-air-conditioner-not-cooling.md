## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific fault code — it's a symptom. Most Lennox central air conditioners don't display an alphanumeric error for weak or no cooling; instead you notice the house won't reach setpoint, air from the vents feels warm, or the outdoor unit won't start.

Some higher-end Lennox systems with communicating controls (like the iComfort thermostat paired with SL/XC-series equipment) *can* log fault codes for specific failures such as compressor or sensor faults. If your thermostat shows a numeric code, look that up specifically. This page covers the general "it's not cooling" complaint that applies across nearly all Lennox split-system AC units.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the coil. The single most common cause.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; blocks all cooling until it thaws.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), or a miscalibrated/failed thermostat.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser gets no power, so only the indoor fan runs.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor and/or fan motor won't start; you may hear humming from the outdoor unit.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so the compressor never energizes.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling and often freezes the coil. Requires a licensed tech.
8. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so the system runs constantly with poor cooling.
9. **Failing compressor or fan motor** — the most expensive causes, usually a last diagnosis after the above are ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything is unclear.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Do this first.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, set below room temperature, and on **Auto** (not "On," which runs the fan without cooling). If it's a battery model, replace the batteries.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker (indoor **and** the separate outdoor circuit). Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Make sure the pull-out disconnect near the condenser is fully seated (it can be bumped loose during yard work).
- **Vents and returns:** Confirm supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Ice check:** Look at the copper lines and indoor coil. If you see frost or ice, **turn cooling off, set the fan to On** to thaw it (this can take a few hours), replace the filter, then try cooling again.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and cut cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan and clear the visible drain line if you can safely reach it.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the workflow helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply/return temperature split (a healthy system drops air ~16–22°F).
2. **Checks airflow** — filter, blower operation, and coil condition.
3. **Electrical test at the condenser** — confirms 24V call reaches the contactor, tests the capacitor with a meter, and inspects the contactor for pitting.
4. **Refrigerant readings** — attaches gauges to check pressures and superheat/subcooling. Low readings point to a leak, which they'll then locate with an electronic detector or dye.
5. **Component tests** — compressor windings and fan motor amperage if power is present but the unit won't run.

A good tech shows you readings, not just a part swap. If someone quotes refrigerant with no leak search, ask why.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, bad capacitor or contactor | Reset breaker once; check disconnect | Test/replace capacitor or contactor |
| Ice on lines or coil | Dirty filter or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw, replace filter | Find/repair refrigerant leak |
| Runs constantly, weak cooling | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Gently rinse outdoor coil with hose | Clean coil, check charge, find leak |
| Nothing runs at all | Thermostat, breaker, or float switch | Check batteries, mode, breaker, drain | Trace control wiring / low-voltage fault |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — needs a meter | Replace capacitor, confirm compressor |
| Reaches temp very slowly on hot days | Undersized/dirty system or aging compressor | Replace filter, clear vents | Measure capacity, assess compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries:** under $10 (DIY).
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400 depending on model (communicating iComfort thermostats cost more).
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$350.
- **Contactor:** $150–$350.
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$300.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type (older R-22 systems cost far more).
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,500+; on an older unit, replacing the whole system is often more cost-effective.

Prices vary by region and by Lennox model series — Signature (SL/XC) equipment with variable-speed components runs higher than Merit-series parts.
