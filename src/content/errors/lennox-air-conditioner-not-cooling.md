## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific fault code on your Lennox equipment — it's a symptom. Your air conditioner may be running but blowing warm or lukewarm air, short-cycling, or the outdoor condenser may not start at all. On systems with an iComfort thermostat you may see an alert or a stored error, but many Lennox AC problems produce no code and simply fail to bring the house down to setpoint.

Because "not cooling" covers everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, this page walks you from the cheapest, safest checks up to the pro-level failures — so you can tell whether you have a five-minute fix or a service call.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow across the indoor coil, cutting cooling and eventually freezing the coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so nothing cools.
3. **Failed run/dual capacitor** — the compressor or fan hums or won't start; a very common, inexpensive part failure.
4. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a miscommunicating iComfort thermostat.
5. **Tripped breaker or outdoor disconnect** — the outdoor unit gets no power.
6. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is caked with dirt/grass, so it can't reject heat.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced or no cooling, possible hissing or icing; requires EPA-certified repair.
8. **Failed condenser fan motor** — outdoor fan doesn't spin, unit overheats and trips.
9. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the compressor doesn't pull in.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to COOL and the temperature is below room temp. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. On iComfort, check for a Wi-Fi/comms alert.
- **Breaker** — check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated tripping means a fault.
- **Outdoor disconnect** — the pull-out box near the condenser should be fully seated.
- **Vents and registers** — make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Frozen coil / condensate** — if you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system OFF and set the fan to ON to thaw it (can take a few hours). Check that the condensate drain isn't backed up, which can trip a safety float switch and stop cooling.

Do **not** open the outdoor unit's electrical panel, touch the capacitor, or add refrigerant. Those steps require training and can cause serious injury.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verifies the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Checks airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and evaporator coil for icing or dirt.
3. **Tests electrical components** — reads the capacitor's microfarads against its rating, checks the contactor, and confirms voltage at the compressor and fan.
4. **Measures refrigerant** — attaches gauges to read pressures and calculates superheat/subcooling. Low readings point to a leak, which they'll locate with a detector or dye.
5. **Reads control/board data** — on iComfort or communicating systems, pulls stored alerts and status from the thermostat or control board.
6. **Confirms the compressor** — tests amp draw and windings if all else checks out.

Ask the tech to show you the capacitor reading or the refrigerant pressures. A trustworthy diagnosis is measured, not guessed.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air from vents, fan running | Dirty filter or frozen coil | Replace filter; thaw coil with fan ON | Diagnose airflow/refrigerant, clear ice |
| Ice on copper lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw, replace filter | Find leak, recharge, check blower |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — do not open unit | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit completely dead | Tripped breaker/disconnect | Reset breaker once, seat disconnect | Test contactor, transformer, wiring |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed fan motor or capacitor | Turn unit off to prevent damage | Replace fan motor/capacitor |
| Weak cooling, dirty outdoor coil | Clogged condenser coil | Gently rinse fins with garden hose | Deep coil clean, check charge |
| Runs constantly, never reaches setpoint | Low refrigerant or failing compressor | Verify filter and thermostat only | Pressure test, compressor amp test |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries or comms fault | Replace batteries, check settings | Diagnose thermostat/board comms |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Regional rates and warranty status vary widely:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$10 (DIY)
- **Capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $450–$900
- **Coil cleaning (condenser/evaporator):** $150–$450
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,600 depending on leak location and R-410A/R-454B cost
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement if the unit is 10+ years old)

Many Lennox systems carry a 5- or 10-year limited parts warranty if registered — confirm coverage before approving major parts, as it can save hundreds on a compressor or coil.
