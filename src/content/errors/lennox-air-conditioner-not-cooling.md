## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on Lennox equipment — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may be running (the outdoor fan spins, air moves from the vents) but the air isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On units with a communicating control board (many Signature-series iComfort systems), you may see an actual error code on the thermostat; on Merit and older single-stage units, you'll usually get no code at all — just warm air.

Because "not cooling" covers everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, this page walks through the causes from cheapest and most likely to most expensive. Some of these you can check yourself; several require a licensed HVAC tech with gauges and EPA certification.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil. The single most common cause.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant. Ice blocks heat transfer, so air blows but isn't cold.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a cheap part; when it fails, the compressor or fan motor won't start, so no cooling.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — a slow leak reduces cooling capacity over time. Requires leak repair, not a "top-off."
6. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit burns or sticks.
7. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a wiring/communication fault (more common on iComfort systems).
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; the unit runs but produces no cooling, often after other faults are ignored.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here if a repair requires opening the sealed refrigerant system, the electrical panel of the condenser, or handling refrigerant.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Air filter:** Pull it out and check it against a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of weak or no cooling.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure they're open and not blocked by furniture or rugs. Closed vents choke airflow and can freeze the coil.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the AC (there are usually two — one for the furnace/air handler, one for the outdoor unit). Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Condensate drain:** If cooling stopped and you see water near the indoor unit, a clogged condensate line may have triggered a safety float switch. Look for standing water in the drain pan.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for obvious debris, leaves, or tall grass blocking the condenser coil. Turn the unit off at the thermostat before clearing anything, and never insert tools into the fan.
- **Frozen coil check:** If you see ice on the copper refrigerant lines or the indoor coil, turn the system to OFF (or FAN ONLY) and let it thaw for a few hours before running it again — running a frozen unit can damage the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence, so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the complaint and airflow** — checks the filter, coil, and blower operation first.
2. **Measure electrical values** — tests the capacitor with a meter, checks the contactor, and confirms voltage at the outdoor unit.
3. **Reads any board/thermostat codes** — on iComfort/communicating systems, stored fault codes point to specific components.
4. **Connects refrigerant gauges** — measures suction and liquid pressures and calculates superheat/subcooling to determine if refrigerant charge is correct. Low readings indicate a leak.
5. **Leak search** — if refrigerant is low, uses an electronic detector or dye to locate the leak. A refill without finding the leak is a red flag.
6. **Checks the compressor** — measures amp draw and windings if the compressor won't start or is short-cycling.

Ask for the measured numbers (capacitor microfarads, refrigerant pressures, superheat). A tech who quotes a compressor or full recharge without showing readings should be questioned.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but isn't cold | Dirty filter, low refrigerant, or frozen coil | Replace filter; thaw coil with fan mode | Measure charge, find leak, check airflow |
| Outdoor unit fan runs, no cold air | Failed capacitor or compressor | None — safe to check breaker only | Test/replace capacitor; test compressor |
| Ice on refrigerant lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Diagnose airflow vs. charge; repair leak |
| Outdoor unit completely dead | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor; check wiring |
| House won't reach setpoint on hot days | Undersized/low charge or dirty condenser | Clear debris; rinse coil gently | Verify charge and capacity |
| Cooling stopped, water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain, float switch tripped | Clear visible clog; use drain tablets | Clear line, test float switch |
| No cooling, thermostat blank | Dead batteries or control fault | Replace batteries | Diagnose thermostat/board wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Prices vary by region and contractor.

- **Air filter replacement:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $75–$250 (or DIY with tablets)
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 (more for communicating iComfort models)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,200+ (often near the cost of a new condenser)

If the compressor or coil has failed on an older unit that's out of warranty, ask whether replacing the whole outdoor unit or system makes more financial sense. Lennox parts warranties may cover the component cost if the unit is registered and within term — labor is usually separate.
