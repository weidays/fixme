## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single error code — it's the most common complaint homeowners report about a Lennox air conditioner. Depending on your model, you may see nothing at all (older units have no display), or a fault code on an **iComfort S30/E30 thermostat** or on the outdoor unit's control board LED on higher-end Signature models.

The symptom breaks down into a few real situations: the system runs but blows warm or room-temperature air, the outdoor unit won't start, air barely moves from the vents, or the house never reaches the setpoint on hot days. Each points to a different root cause, from a clogged filter to a refrigerant leak or a failed compressor.

Because refrigerant handling, electrical repairs inside the condenser, and compressor work require tools and EPA certification, this is rated **pro** — but there are several safe checks you can do first that resolve many "not cooling" calls before a tech ever arrives.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat set wrong or dead batteries** — set to "fan/on" instead of "cool," wrong temperature, or a blank iComfort screen.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer so nothing cools.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit, indoor blower, or both lose power.
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure; the compressor or fan hums but won't spin up.
6. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked with grass, dust, and debris, the unit can't reject heat.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — undercharged systems cool weakly, then not at all, and often freeze up.
8. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the condenser is pitted or stuck open.
9. **Blower or condenser fan motor failure** — no air movement indoors or no heat rejection outdoors.
10. **Compressor failure** — the worst case; the unit runs but produces no cooling at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all homeowner-safe. Stop if anything requires opening the sealed refrigerant system or the electrical panel inside the condenser.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause and a $15 fix.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Set the fan to **Auto**, not On. For iComfort/S30 screens that are blank, check the C-wire connection and power.
- **Batteries:** If you have a battery-powered thermostat, swap in fresh batteries.
- **Breakers:** Check your main panel for a tripped breaker on the AC or air handler. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the exterior fins with a garden hose (power off) — never a pressure washer.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and set the fan to **On** to thaw it (can take several hours). Running a frozen unit makes damage worse.
- **Condensate:** A full drain pan or a tripped float switch can shut down cooling. Empty standing water and clear the drain line if you can safely reach it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — confirm airflow, check setpoint vs. actual temperature, and inspect the filter.
2. **Read fault codes** — on iComfort/Signature units, they'll pull codes from the thermostat or board LEDs.
3. **Electrical checks** — test the capacitor with a meter, inspect the contactor for pitting, and confirm voltage at the condenser.
4. **Airflow and coil inspection** — check for a frozen or dirty evaporator coil and a fouled condenser coil.
5. **Refrigerant readings** — connect gauges to measure suction/head pressure and calculate superheat/subcooling. Low charge triggers a **leak search** (dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen).
6. **Motor and compressor tests** — check amp draw on the blower, condenser fan, and compressor windings.

A legitimate diagnosis takes 30–60 minutes. Be cautious of any tech who quotes a compressor or "recharge" without measuring pressures and finding the leak first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blows warm air, unit runs | Low refrigerant / dirty filter | Replace filter; check setpoint | Find & fix leak, recharge to spec |
| Ice on lines or coil | Frozen evaporator (low airflow/charge) | Turn Off, fan On to thaw | Diagnose airflow or charge cause |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — do not open unit | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit fully dead | Tripped breaker / bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, wiring, board |
| Weak airflow at vents | Dirty filter / blower motor | Replace filter, open vents | Test blower motor, check ducts |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Undercharge or compressor | Check filter & condenser coil | Pressure test; compressor check |
| Blank iComfort screen | Power / C-wire / batteries | Swap batteries, check power | Trace 24V power, board check |
| System shuts off, water present | Full drain / float switch | Empty pan, clear drain | Clear line, replace pump/switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Regional pricing and unit age vary widely.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $80–$200
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $400–$700
- **Blower motor:** $450–$900
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 (depends on leak location and R-410A/R-454B cost)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500 (often worth weighing against full-system replacement on older units)

If your Lennox is more than 12–15 years old and facing a compressor or coil replacement, ask for a repair-vs-replace comparison — especially with the R-410A phase-down affecting refrigerant costs.
