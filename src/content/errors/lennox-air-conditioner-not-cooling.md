## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you're living with when a Lennox air conditioner runs (or tries to run) but the house never reaches temperature. On iComfort thermostats you may see the system "calling for cool" indefinitely, while standard 24V thermostats simply won't satisfy.

The underlying cause falls into a few buckets: **airflow restriction** (filter, coils, blower), **refrigerant problems** (low charge from a leak, restriction), **electrical failures** (capacitor, contactor, compressor), or a **control/thermostat issue** telling the unit to do the wrong thing. This page walks you through separating the safe DIY checks from the licensed-pro work.

Because Lennox spans single-stage Merit units up to variable-capacity Signature models (XC21, XC25), the exact diagnostic path varies — a variable-speed compressor reports faults through the iComfort controller, while an older single-stage unit gives you no electronic feedback at all. We note where this matters.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the evaporator coil, and kills cooling capacity. Most common and most overlooked.
2. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — grass, dust, and cottonwood block heat rejection so the system can't shed heat.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks the coil and cool air stops.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — you cannot "run low" without a leak. Requires EPA-certified repair.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common electrical failure; the compressor or fan hums but won't start.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
8. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, miswiring, or failed iComfort sensor.
9. **Blower motor or ECM fault** — indoor air isn't moving even if the outdoor unit runs.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; the unit won't cool at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything looks burnt, smells hot, or involves refrigerant lines.

- **Air filter:** Pull it. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is below room temperature. For non-iComfort models, try fresh batteries.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel. Both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) circuits should be on. If a breaker is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated trips mean a fault.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for tall grass, leaves, or debris packed against the condenser fins. Gently clear it. Never poke tools between the fins.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or coil, **turn the system to OFF and the fan to ON** to thaw it (can take a few hours). If it re-freezes, that's a low-airflow or refrigerant issue for a pro.
- **Condensate drain:** A full drain pan or clogged line can trip a float switch and stop the system. Check for standing water and clear a visible clog with drain tablets.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the call for cool** — confirms the thermostat is actually signaling and 24V is present at the outdoor unit.
2. **Check power and electrical** — tests the contactor, run capacitor (microfarad reading), and voltage at the compressor and fan.
3. **Measure airflow and temperature split** — the difference between return and supply air (typically 15–20°F). A low split points to airflow or refrigerant.
4. **Gauge the refrigerant** — connects manifold gauges to read suction and liquid pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling. This tells them if the charge is low, overcharged, or restricted.
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, they use electronic detectors or dye to find the leak. Simply recharging without fixing a leak is bad practice.
6. **Read controller faults** — on iComfort/Signature units, the technician pulls stored fault codes from the control board or thermostat for compressor, motor, or communication errors.
7. **Inspect coils and blower** — checks for dirty coils, a failed ECM/blower motor, or a seized compressor.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air from vents, outdoor unit running | Low refrigerant or dirty condenser coil | Clear debris from outdoor unit | Gauge charge, find/repair leak |
| No air or weak air from vents | Dirty filter or blower/ECM fault | Replace filter | Test blower motor and control board |
| Ice on coil or refrigerant lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Diagnose airflow vs. charge |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor blower runs | Contactor, capacitor, or breaker | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor/capacitor |
| Compressor hums but won't start | Failed run capacitor | None | Test and replace capacitor |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Short, failing compressor, or wiring | Stop — leave off | Electrical fault diagnosis |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries or wiring/float switch | Replace batteries, check drain | Test 24V, C-wire, float switch |
| House cools slowly, never satisfies | Undersized charge, dirty coils, or duct loss | Clean filter and coils | Full charge and airflow check |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges. Older units and variable-capacity Signature models cost more due to parts and labor.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$200
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$300
- **Blower motor / ECM module:** $400–$900
- **Thermostat replacement (iComfort):** $300–$700 installed
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$2,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **TXV / metering device:** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$3,000+ (often worth weighing against a new unit if out of warranty)

Get an itemized quote. If a company offers to "just add refrigerant" without a leak search, ask why — that's a temporary fix.
