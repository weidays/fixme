## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom homeowners describe most often. On older Lennox split systems there is no diagnostic display, so you only notice warm air or a system that runs constantly without dropping the indoor temperature. On communicating Lennox systems (iComfort, S30/S40 thermostats, some Signature-series equipment) the thermostat or control board may log an actual alert code that points to a specific problem.

In plain terms, "not cooling" means one of three things is happening:

- The system **isn't running** at all (no power, control, or safety lockout).
- The system **is running but can't move heat** (airflow restriction, dirty coils, frozen evaporator).
- The system **is running but can't reject heat** (low refrigerant charge, failed compressor, bad condenser fan).

Because refrigerant work and compressor diagnosis require EPA certification and gauges, this is rated **pro-level**. Homeowners can safely rule out the easy causes below, but the repair itself usually belongs to a licensed HVAC tech.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Thermostat setting or wiring** — set to "fan on" instead of "cool," wrong temperature, dead batteries, or a communication fault on iComfort systems.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs, so you feel warm air.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common outdoor failure. The compressor or condenser fan hums but won't start.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil caked with dirt, grass, or cottonwood can't reject heat, so the house never cools.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; ice blocks the coil and airflow drops to near zero.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — never a "top-off" fix; the leak must be found and sealed.
8. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor relay that supplies the compressor. Pitted or stuck contacts prevent startup.
9. **Bad condenser fan motor** — the compressor may run but overheat and shut down without airflow across the coil.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome, usually the last thing confirmed after everything else is ruled out.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. If any of them doesn't resolve it, stop and call a pro.

- **Filter:** Pull the filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it and give the system an hour to recover.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the target temp is several degrees below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (disconnect near the condenser) breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call a tech.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance. **Do not** open the electrical panel or touch the capacitor.
- **Condensate:** A full drain pan or clogged condensate line can trip a float switch and shut the system down. Empty a full pan and check for a tripped safety switch, but don't disable it.
- **Ice:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Fan Only** for a few hours to thaw it, then run cooling again. Persistent icing means a real problem — call a pro.

Never add refrigerant, open sealed panels, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping or locking out.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — confirms the system is calling for cool and measures supply/return air temperature split (typically 16–22°F on a healthy system).
2. **Check airflow** — inspects filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Confirm power and controls** — tests voltage at the disconnect, contactor, and low-voltage circuit; checks the fuse on the control board.
4. **Test electrical components** — measures the capacitor's microfarad rating and checks the contactor for pitting and pull-in.
5. **Read refrigerant pressures** — connects gauges to check suction and liquid pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling. Low readings point to a leak.
6. **Leak search** — if charge is low, uses electronic detector or dye to locate the leak rather than just adding refrigerant.
7. **Compressor and fan test** — checks amp draw, windings, and the condenser fan motor.
8. **Communicating systems** — pulls stored fault codes from the iComfort/S30 thermostat and control board to speed diagnosis.

A good tech won't just "add a pound of Freon" and leave. If they recommend recharging, ask what caused the low charge and whether the leak was located.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air from vents, blower runs | Dirty filter or thermostat set wrong | Replace filter, set to Cool/Auto | Verify airflow and controls |
| Indoor blower runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Test disconnect, fuse, contactor |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Failed capacitor | None — do not open panel | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on copper lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Run Fan Only to thaw, replace filter | Check charge, find leak, verify airflow |
| Runs constantly, house won't cool | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Clear debris around unit | Clean coil, check pressures |
| Compressor runs, fan blade not spinning | Bad condenser fan motor | None | Replace fan motor/capacitor |
| System short-cycles then stops | Overheating compressor or float switch | Check condensate pan | Diagnose overheating, safeties |
| No cool at all, thermostat error/alert | Control or communication fault (iComfort) | Note the displayed code | Read board codes, test wiring |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your region, warranty, and unit age all affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat battery / setting:** $0–$15.
- **Capacitor replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$700.
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$300.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location; evaporator coil leaks run higher.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+; often makes replacing the whole condenser worthwhile on older units.
- **Full outdoor unit / system replacement:** $4,500–$9,000+.

If your Lennox unit is under its parts warranty (typically 5–10 years registered), you may pay only labor on major components — always ask the tech to check the serial-number warranty status.
