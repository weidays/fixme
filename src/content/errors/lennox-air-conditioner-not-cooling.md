## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's a symptom. Most single-stage Lennox condensers (Merit and many Elite units) have no display and throw no code; they simply run without cooling, short-cycle, or won't start. Higher-end variable-capacity **Signature** units (XC21, SL18XC1, and similar) paired with an **iComfort** thermostat can report specific system alerts or communication faults on the thermostat screen.

If your thermostat shows a numbered alert, look that up specifically. If not, this page walks you through why a Lennox AC blows warm air or fails to keep up on a hot day, what you can safely check, and what a technician should be doing to earn a repair quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil into a block of ice.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs. "auto"), or a miscalibrated or failed iComfort thermostat.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs, so you feel air but it's not cold.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — debris, grass clippings, and dust block heat rejection, killing efficiency on hot days.
5. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
6. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan can't start; you may hear a hum or clicking.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — gradual loss of cooling, longer run times, ice on the lineset. Requires a licensed tech.
8. **Failed contactor or condenser fan motor** — outdoor unit won't energize or the fan won't spin.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit runs but produces no cooling, or trips the breaker.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Don't open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and inspect. If it's gray or clogged, replace it. A dirty filter is the #1 cause of weak cooling and frozen coils.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to **Cool** and the fan to **Auto**. Set the temperature at least 3–5°F below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear at least 2 feet of space around the condenser. Gently rinse the outer coil fins from the outside with a garden hose (power off) if they're caked with dirt or grass.
- **Frozen coil / lineset:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** (fan **On**) and let it thaw fully — often several hours — before running again. Persistent icing means low refrigerant or airflow issues; call a tech.
- **Condensate:** A clogged drain or full pan can trip a safety float switch and stop cooling. Clear a visible clog only if you can reach it safely.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should work in this order — use it to sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the complaint:** Measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F).
2. **Checks airflow:** Inspects filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Electrical checks:** Tests the capacitor, contactor, and voltage at the condenser; confirms the compressor and fan motor draw correct amps.
4. **Refrigerant check:** Attaches gauges and measures superheat/subcooling to determine if charge is correct. Low charge points to a leak — a reputable tech will locate and repair the leak, not just top off.
5. **Component testing:** Confirms compressor windings, reversing valve (heat pump models), and control board or iComfort communication faults on Signature units.

Ask any tech to show you the measured temperature split and pressures. "It's low on Freon" without a leak search is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but not cold | Low refrigerant, bad compressor, or outdoor unit off | Check outdoor breaker; replace filter | Gauge charge, find leak, test compressor |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, blown fuse, bad contactor/capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test capacitor, contactor, board fuse |
| Ice on coil or copper lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Replace filter; thaw with fan On | Correct charge, clear airflow restriction |
| Unit hums but won't start | Failed run/start capacitor | None (electrical) | Replace capacitor, verify motor/compressor |
| Short-cycling, weak cooling on hot days | Dirty condenser coil, undercharge | Rinse outdoor coil, clear debris | Deep coil clean, verify charge |
| Warm air + thermostat blank | Dead batteries, thermostat fault | Replace batteries | Test/replace thermostat, check wiring |
| Breaker keeps tripping | Shorted compressor or wiring fault | Stop resetting; shut off | Diagnose short, test compressor windings |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor). Regional rates and Lennox part pricing vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY).
- **Capacitor replacement:** $150–$400.
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350.
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$700.
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400.
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,600, depending on leak location and refrigerant type (older R-22 systems cost more).
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000.
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,200+; on an aging unit, weigh this against full system replacement.
- **iComfort thermostat / control board:** $300–$700 on Signature communicating systems.
