## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code — it's the symptom Lennox owners search for most in summer. Your system may be running (thermostat calling, air moving from vents) but the air isn't cold, or the house temperature keeps climbing. On communicating **iComfort** systems you may see an alert or reduced-capacity message instead of a hard lockout.

Cooling requires three things working together: airflow (blower + clean filter + open coil), a working refrigerant circuit (compressor, capacitor, correct charge), and a controls chain (thermostat, contactor, board) that tells the compressor to run. A failure anywhere in that chain shows up as "not cooling."

This page covers Lennox **air conditioner** cooling loss. If your **furnace** or air handler is the issue and you're seeing a numeric fault, that's a separate diagnosis — see Related codes.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the most common and cheapest cause. Restricted airflow ices the indoor coil, which stops cooling entirely.
2. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a bulged or dead dual-run capacitor stops the compressor and/or outdoor fan. Very common in units 5+ years old.
3. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — caked with grass, dust, or cottonwood, the unit can't reject heat and cooling drops off, especially on hot afternoons.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak or no cooling and often coil icing. Requires a licensed tech; never just "topped off."
5. **Tripped breaker or failed contactor** — the outdoor unit gets no power, so the compressor never starts even though the blower runs.
6. **Frozen indoor evaporator coil** — the result of #1, #3, or #4; ice blocks airflow entirely.
7. **Thermostat or wiring fault** — wrong setting, dead batteries, or a broken low-voltage wire so the system never gets the cooling call.
8. **Control board or compressor failure** — least common but most expensive; typical of older Lennox condensers.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything is beyond them.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, fan **AUTO**, and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of no cooling.
- **Breaker:** Check the panel for a tripped AC breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — that's an electrical fault.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear grass, leaves, and debris at least 2 feet around the condenser. Gently rinse visible dirt off the coil fins with a garden hose (power off).
- **Condensate drain:** A full drain pan or clogged line can trip a safety float switch and stop cooling. Clear standing water and pour drain tablets down the line.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the indoor unit or refrigerant lines, turn the system to **OFF** and the fan to **ON** for a few hours to thaw before running cooling again.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, remove electrical panels on the live condenser, bypass the float switch, or add refrigerant.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech works the chain in order, and you can sanity-check their steps against a quote:

1. **Verify the call:** Confirm the thermostat is sending a cooling signal (24V at the Y terminal) and the contactor pulls in.
2. **Check power and capacitor:** Measure voltage at the contactor and test the run capacitor's microfarad rating against its rating.
3. **Measure airflow and temperature split:** A properly running system drops supply-air temp roughly 15–20°F below return air. A small split points to airflow or refrigerant problems.
4. **Gauge the refrigerant:** Attach gauges to read pressures and superheat/subcooling. Low readings mean a leak — they should offer to locate it, not just add refrigerant.
5. **Inspect coils and the compressor:** Check for dirty coils, a compressor that hums but won't start (bad capacitor or windings), and control-board faults on communicating units.
6. **Read iComfort data:** On communicating systems, the tech pulls fault/alert history from the thermostat or board to confirm the culprit.

A good quote names the specific failed part and its cost — be wary of "just add refrigerant" with no leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but not cold | Bad capacitor, low charge, or dirty coil | Replace filter, rinse outdoor coil, reset breaker once | Test capacitor, gauge refrigerant, find leak |
| No air from vents at all | Frozen coil or blower fault | Turn to OFF, run fan to thaw ice | Diagnose airflow/refrigerant cause of icing |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Failed compressor or capacitor | Check breaker | Test compressor windings and capacitor |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or dead contactor | Reset breaker once; clear debris | Replace contactor, check for shorts |
| Weak cooling on hot days only | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Clean coil, clear 2 ft around unit | Measure subcooling, check charge |
| House warm, thermostat blank | Dead batteries or wiring fault | Replace thermostat batteries | Trace low-voltage wiring |
| Water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Clear line, add drain tablets | Clear/repair drain, check float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Your quote varies by region and Lennox model tier.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY).
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY).
- **Run/dual-run capacitor:** $150–$400 installed.
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed.
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250.
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300.
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair + recharge:** $600–$1,600 depending on leak location.
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500 (higher if under refrigerant-line rework).
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ — often the point where replacing the condenser or system is worth pricing.
- **Control/communicating board (iComfort):** $400–$900.

Diagnostic/service-call fees typically run $80–$180 and are often credited toward the repair.
