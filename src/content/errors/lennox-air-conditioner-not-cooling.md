## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific fault code on most Lennox systems — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may power on, the thermostat may call for cooling, and air may even move through the vents, but the air isn't getting cold (or isn't getting cold enough to satisfy the setpoint).

How this shows up depends on your model:

- **Single-stage units** (13ACX, 14ACX, XC13, XC14, XC16) usually give **no code at all** — you just notice warm air. Diagnosis is done with gauges and meters, not a display.
- **Variable-capacity units** (XC20, XC21, XC25) paired with an **iComfort / S30 thermostat** may log an alert or communication fault at the thermostat, which points a technician toward the specific subsystem.

"Not cooling" almost always traces back to one of three things: **airflow**, **refrigerant charge**, or an **electrical/component failure** (capacitor, contactor, compressor, or fan motor).

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the most common and most overlooked. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Thermostat issue** — wrong mode, dead batteries, or a miscalibrated/failed iComfort thermostat not sending a proper cooling call.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit may be off while the indoor blower still runs, so you feel airflow but no cold.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure. The compressor or condenser fan won't start; you may hear humming or a clicking contactor.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is caked with dirt, grass or cottonwood, so it can't reject heat.
6. **Low refrigerant / leak** — undercharge from a slow leak. Often causes a frozen coil and gradually declining cooling.
7. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — a downstream result of low airflow or low charge; ice blocks cooling entirely.
8. **Failed contactor** — worn or pitted contacts prevent the outdoor unit from energizing.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor or blower motor** — the system runs but can't move heat or air.
10. **Failed compressor** — the least common but most expensive; the "heart" of the system no longer pumps refrigerant.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. **Do not** open the sealed refrigerant system, remove electrical panels, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Set the fan to **AUTO**. Replace the batteries if it uses them.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter alone can stop cooling and freeze the coil.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel. Central AC uses **two** circuits — one for the indoor air handler/furnace and one for the outdoor unit. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit clearance:** Clear leaves, grass and debris from around the condenser and gently rinse the outside of the coil with a garden hose (low pressure, top to bottom) if it looks dirty.
- **Frozen coil / ice:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, **turn the system OFF at the thermostat** and set the fan to **ON** to thaw it (can take a few hours). Running a frozen system can damage the compressor.
- **Condensate:** Check that the condensate drain isn't clogged and overflowing — a full pan can trip a float switch and shut the system down.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** — confirms the thermostat is actually signaling the equipment (measures 24V at the contactor).
2. **Measure airflow and inspect the coils** — checks filter, blower, and indoor coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Test electrical components** — measures the **run/start capacitor** with a meter, inspects the **contactor** for pitting, and confirms the **condenser fan** and **blower** spin freely.
4. **Attach gauges and take temperatures** — reads suction and liquid pressures, then calculates **superheat and subcooling** to judge refrigerant charge.
5. **Leak search if undercharged** — uses an electronic detector or dye; a proper tech finds and repairs the leak rather than just topping off.
6. **Compressor test** — checks amp draw, windings and whether it's pumping, if all else checks out.

A trustworthy quote names the failed part and the test result behind it (e.g. "capacitor reading 12µF, rated 45µF"). Be cautious of a "just add refrigerant" quote with no leak search — that's a temporary fix on a leaking system.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air flows but isn't cold; weak airflow | Dirty filter / restricted airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Check blower, static pressure, coil |
| Warm air; outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or failed contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor, trace fault |
| Outdoor unit hums or clicks, won't start | Failed run/start capacitor | None — call a pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on coil or refrigerant lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw with fan ON | Find restriction or leak, recharge |
| Cooling slowly worsened over weeks | Refrigerant leak / undercharge | None — call a pro | Leak search, repair, recharge to spec |
| Outdoor fan not spinning, unit hot | Failed fan motor or capacitor | Shut unit off | Replace fan motor or capacitor |
| Outdoor unit runs but no cold at all | Failed compressor | Shut unit off | Compressor test / replacement |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries / thermostat fault | Replace batteries | Replace/reconfigure thermostat |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Your price varies by region and unit.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$200
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location and refrigerant type; R-410A pricing varies)
- **Coil replacement (evaporator or condenser):** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against full unit replacement on older systems)

**Note:** If your Lennox unit is under 10 years old, check for a **parts warranty** — many Lennox compressors and coils carry 5–10 year limited coverage, which can cut the part cost significantly (you'll still pay labor).
