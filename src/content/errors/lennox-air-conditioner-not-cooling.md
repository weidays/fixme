## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single blinking error code — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may be running, blowing air, or showing no obvious fault, yet the house never reaches the setpoint. On systems paired with an **iComfort** or **ComfortSense** thermostat, you may see a specific alert or code number; on older mechanical thermostats you'll just feel warm air.

Because "not cooling" can stem from anything from a $10 filter to a $2,500 compressor, the goal of this page is to help you triage the likely cause before you pay for a diagnostic. Exact behavior and any displayed codes vary by your thermostat model and the control board in your specific condenser, so treat the rankings below as probabilities, not guarantees.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or restricted airflow** — the single most common cause. Low airflow across the indoor coil drops capacity and can freeze the coil solid.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant. A block of ice on the coil means almost no cooling.
3. **Dirty condenser coil** (the outdoor unit) — caked with grass, dust or cottonwood, it can't reject heat.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common electrical failure. The fan or compressor hums, struggles to start, or won't start.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — you'll often see ice, hear hissing, or notice weak, gradually worsening cooling over a season.
6. **Bad contactor** — the outdoor unit doesn't kick on at all, or chatters.
7. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a wiring/communication fault on iComfort systems.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit is completely dead.
9. **Failed compressor** — least common but most expensive; the unit tries to start but can't build pressure.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. **Do not** open the electrical panel on the condenser, touch capacitors, or handle refrigerant lines.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs, and the return grille isn't clogged.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for grass, leaves, or debris smothering the condenser. Gently rinse the exterior fins with a garden hose (unit off) if they're dirty.
- **Condensate:** A full condensate pan or clogged drain can trip a safety float switch and shut the system down. Check for standing water near the indoor unit.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, **turn the system to OFF and the fan to ON** for a few hours to thaw it, then replace the filter. If it re-freezes, you need a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should work methodically — this list helps you sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** and check thermostat settings and call for cooling.
2. **Measure temperature split** across the indoor coil (return vs. supply air) — typically 15–20°F on a healthy system.
3. **Inspect airflow** — filter, blower, and coil condition; look for ice.
4. **Check electrical** — voltage at the contactor, capacitor microfarad reading against the rated value, contactor condition.
5. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and read pressures, superheat and subcooling to judge charge. Low readings point to a leak, not just "needs a top-off."
6. **Leak search** if charge is low — with electronic detector or dye, then a repair estimate.
7. **Compressor test** — amp draw and start behavior if the compressor won't run.

Be wary of any tech who adds refrigerant without leak-checking, or quotes a compressor without measuring the capacitor and contactor first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but house won't cool | Dirty filter / low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Check coil, blower, temp split |
| Ice on indoor coil or lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, change filter | Diagnose charge, find leak |
| Outdoor unit dead, indoor fan runs | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, test circuit |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None (electrical) | Test & replace capacitor |
| Weak cooling worsening over weeks | Refrigerant leak | None | Leak search & repair, recharge |
| Warm air, thermostat blank | Dead batteries / thermostat fault | Replace batteries | Check wiring / iComfort comms |
| Outdoor fins caked with debris | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse gently with hose | Deep coil cleaning |
| Unit starts then trips breaker | Compressor or electrical fault | Stop resetting, call pro | Amp test compressor & wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Your area and unit age affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $10–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** ~$5 (DIY)
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location; a coil replacement pushes higher)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800–$2,500+ (on older units, often worth comparing to a full system replacement)
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$200, often credited toward the repair

If your Lennox condenser is 12+ years old and needs a compressor or coil, ask for a repair-vs-replace analysis before committing.
