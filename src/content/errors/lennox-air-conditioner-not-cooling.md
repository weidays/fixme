## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single Lennox fault code — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may be running, blowing air, and even showing no error at the thermostat, yet the house never reaches setpoint. Depending on your setup, you might see nothing at all, or an iComfort/S30/E30 thermostat may display an alert like a "communication fault" or "outdoor unit not responding" message.

Because behavior varies widely by model and board, the underlying issue could be anything from a clogged filter to a refrigerant leak or a dead compressor. This page walks through the likely causes in order, the safe checks you can do yourself, and how a tech should diagnose it — so you can sanity-check any quote.

> Note: Lennox communicating systems (iComfort with S30/E30 thermostats) surface specific numbered alerts. If you have a numbered code on the thermostat, note it down for your technician — it narrows the diagnosis considerably.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return** — the single most common cause of poor cooling. Restricted airflow drops capacity and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — a coil packed with grass, dust, or cottonwood can't reject heat, so the house won't cool even though the unit runs.
3. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a bulged or dead capacitor keeps the compressor or condenser fan from starting. Very common failure item.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling, long run times, and often ice on the copper line. Requires a licensed tech (EPA-certified) to repair.
5. **Bad contactor** — pitted or stuck contacts prevent the outdoor unit from energizing properly.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — usually a downstream symptom of low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks airflow and stops cooling.
7. **Thermostat or wiring issue** — dead batteries, wrong settings, or a loose low-voltage wire (including a bad C-wire) can stop a cooling call.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor disconnect or panel breaker tripped.
9. **Failed compressor** — seized or electrically failed compressor. Least common but most expensive.
10. **Control board or communication fault** — on iComfort systems, a comm error between indoor and outdoor units can halt cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening a sealed refrigerant system, touching capacitors, or bypassing a safety switch.

- **Filter:** Pull the air filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it and run the system. A clogged filter alone can stop cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered (a fading display often means dead batteries).
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker. Also check the outdoor disconnect box near the condenser. Reset a tripped breaker **once** — if it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and returns:** Make sure supply registers and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed doors.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and shut down cooling. Look for standing water at the indoor unit or a full drain pan. You can pour a cup of vinegar or drop a drain tablet into the cleanout, but don't disassemble anything.
- **Ice check:** Look at the copper lines and indoor coil area. If you see ice, turn the system to **Off** and set the fan to **On** for a few hours to thaw before a tech visits — and note it, because ice points to airflow or refrigerant problems.

Do **not** open the outdoor unit's electrical panel, discharge or replace capacitors, or add refrigerant yourself.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech should work methodically. Use this to sanity-check the visit:

1. **Verify the complaint** — confirm the thermostat is calling for cool and measure the temperature split (supply vs. return air, typically 15–20°F on a healthy system).
2. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Read any codes** — pull alert history from an iComfort/S30/E30 thermostat or inspect the outdoor board LED patterns.
4. **Test electrical components** — measure the run/start capacitor with a meter, check the contactor for continuity and pitting, and confirm proper voltage at the compressor terminals.
5. **Check the compressor and fan motor** — verify amp draw against the nameplate; a compressor pulling locked-rotor amps points to a start or mechanical failure.
6. **Gauge the refrigerant** — connect gauges to check pressures and superheat/subcooling. If low, they should search for the leak (dye, electronic sniffer, or bubble test) — **not just top it off**.
7. **Verify communication** (iComfort systems) — confirm the indoor and outdoor boards are talking.

A good quote names the failed part, the test that confirmed it, and — if refrigerant is low — where the leak is.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower & coil if unresolved |
| Indoor coil/lines iced over | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, fan on to thaw | Find cause; leak search & recharge |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Failed run capacitor | None (unsafe) | Test & replace capacitor |
| Unit runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant leak | None | Leak repair + EPA-certified recharge |
| Outdoor unit dead, no hum | Tripped breaker/disconnect | Reset breaker once | Check contactor, board, wiring |
| Humming but compressor won't start | Bad capacitor or contactor | None (unsafe) | Test & replace part |
| No cooling call at all | Dead thermostat batteries/settings | Replace batteries, set to Cool | Check low-voltage wiring & C-wire |
| Water at indoor unit, system off | Clogged condensate/float switch | Clear drain, add tablet | Clear drain, test float switch |
| Thermostat shows comm error | iComfort board/comm fault | Note the code | Diagnose board & communication wiring |
| Loud clunk, then no cooling | Failed/seized compressor | None | Compressor test & replacement |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your region, model, and warranty status change these.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 (more for communicating iComfort units)
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650 installed
- **Control board:** $400–$900 (varies by board and model)
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,500+; often worth comparing against a new unit if the system is aging or out of warranty

If your Lennox is under 10 years old, check parts warranty status — many Lennox components carry a 5–10 year limited parts warranty, though labor is usually separate.
