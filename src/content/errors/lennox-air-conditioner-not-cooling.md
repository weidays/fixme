## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your Lennox air conditioner runs but the house stays warm, or the system won't start a cooling cycle at all. Lennox split systems don't display a plain-English "not cooling" code; instead the cause shows up as a specific failure (a tripped float switch, a bad capacitor, low refrigerant, or a communicating fault on iComfort systems).

On **communicating Lennox systems** (iComfort S30/E30 thermostats with Signature-series equipment), the thermostat may show a numbered alert code pointing at a specific component. On **non-communicating systems** (most Merit and Elite units with a standard 24V thermostat), there's no code at all — you diagnose by symptom.

This page walks you through the realistic causes, what you can safely check, and what a tech should be doing so you can sanity-check the quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked return** — restricts airflow, the indoor coil ices over, and cold air stops reaching the vents. Cheapest and most common cause.
2. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — a coil packed with grass clippings, cottonwood, or dust can't reject heat, so the system runs but barely cools.
3. **Failed run capacitor** — a weak or bulged dual capacitor stops the compressor and/or fan motor from starting. Very common on units 6+ years old.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — you'll see weak cooling, ice on the copper lines, or long run times. Refrigerant doesn't get "used up," so low charge means a leak.
5. **Tripped condensate float switch** — a clogged drain fills the pan, the safety switch cuts cooling to prevent water damage. Common in humid climates.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit can pit or stick, leaving the condenser dead.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from airflow or refrigerant problems; the whole coil turns to ice and blocks the air path.
8. **Blower motor or control board fault** — indoor blower not running, or on iComfort systems, a communication/board error.
9. **Compressor failure** — the worst case; the outdoor unit hums or trips the breaker but won't run.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do — no panels off the electrical compartment, no refrigerant, no bypassing switches.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter causes more no-cool calls than anything else.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. On battery-powered thermostats, swap the batteries.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and the outdoor AC breakers. You can reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop — do not keep resetting.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure they're open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser (leave at least 2 ft of clearance). Gently rinse the outside of the coil fins with a garden hose (power off) if they're visibly dirty.
- **Condensate drain / float switch:** If your unit sits near a full drain pan or you see water, a float switch may have tripped. Clearing a clogged drain line is a reasonable DIY task; wet-vacuuming the drain outlet outside often restores flow.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or copper lines, turn the system to **Fan only** for a few hours to thaw it, then run cooling again. If it re-freezes, call a pro — that points to airflow or refrigerant.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech should work in this order, and a quote should reflect actual measurements — not guesses:

1. **Verify the symptom** and check thermostat call, filter, and airflow first.
2. **Measure supply/return temperature split** (a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F). A low split points to refrigerant or airflow issues.
3. **Check the capacitor** with a meter against its rated microfarads.
4. **Test the contactor** and confirm 24V control voltage is reaching the outdoor unit.
5. **Attach gauges and take refrigerant readings** (subcooling/superheat) — only an EPA-certified tech should do this. Low charge triggers a **leak search** (electronic sniffer or dye), because a recharge without a repair just leaks out again.
6. **Inspect the coils and blower** for airflow restriction and check the condensate/float circuit.
7. **On iComfort/communicating systems,** read the fault codes from the S30/E30 thermostat and check for communication errors between the thermostat, air handler, and outdoor board.

Be wary of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without a documented leak search or electrical test.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but not cold | Dirty filter or coil | Replace filter, rinse outdoor coil | Clean coils, measure temp split |
| Weak cooling, ice on lines | Low refrigerant (leak) | Turn to Fan to thaw; don't add refrigerant | Leak search, seal, recharge |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor fan runs | Bad capacitor or contactor | None (inside electrical panel) | Test/replace capacitor or contactor |
| System shuts off, water in pan | Tripped float switch / clogged drain | Clear drain line, wet-vac outlet | Clear drain, verify float switch |
| Whole coil iced over | Airflow or refrigerant fault | Fan-only to thaw, replace filter | Diagnose airflow vs. charge |
| Outdoor unit hums, trips breaker | Failing compressor | Reset breaker once only | Test compressor, hard-start kit or replace |
| Thermostat shows a code (iComfort) | Board/communication fault | Note the code number | Read code, test board wiring |
| Nothing runs at all | Breaker, control voltage, transformer | Reset breaker once, check batteries | Test 24V transformer and wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Prices vary by region and system age.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain cleaning:** $0 DIY – $150 pro
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Float switch replacement:** $100–$250
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ (depends on leak location and how much refrigerant is needed; older R-22 units cost far more)
- **Blower motor:** $400–$700
- **Control/communicating board:** $300–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$3,500+ — at this cost, weigh it against replacing the whole condenser or system, especially if the unit is 10+ years old or uses R-22.

Note: R-22 (older Lennox units) refrigerant is expensive and being phased out. If your unit uses R-22 and needs a recharge, replacement is often the smarter long-term choice.
