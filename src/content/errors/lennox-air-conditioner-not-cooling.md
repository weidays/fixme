## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom homeowners describe when a Lennox air conditioner is powered on and running but the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. Some Lennox units with communicating controls (iComfort thermostats and Signature-series equipment) may display a numeric code or alert on the thermostat screen, but many Merit and Elite single-stage systems have no display and simply blow warm or lukewarm air.

Because "not cooling" can stem from anything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, this page walks you from the cheapest, safest checks through the professional diagnostics that determine whether you're looking at a $20 fix or a $2,000 one.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — restricts airflow across the indoor coil, drops capacity, and can freeze the coil solid.
2. **Dirty condenser (outdoor) coil** — dust, grass clippings and cottonwood block heat rejection, so the system can't shed heat.
3. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode/setting, dead batteries, or a miscommunicating iComfort thermostat.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** at the outdoor disconnect — the indoor blower runs, but the compressor and condenser fan don't.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan motor hums but won't start.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; the system "runs" but no cold air comes out.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, longer run times, sometimes hissing or ice on the lineset. Requires an EPA-certified tech.
8. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — compressor tries to run but overheats and shuts off.
10. **Failing compressor** — the least common but most expensive; often the last-resort diagnosis.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Everything else involves electricity, refrigerant, or sealed components — leave those to a pro.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A dirty filter is the #1 cause of weak or no cooling and can freeze the coil.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. For iComfort thermostats, check for on-screen alerts.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker for the AC. There's usually one for the indoor air handler/furnace and a separate one for the outdoor unit. Flip it fully off, then on **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a tech.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** The metal box near the condenser may have a pull-out fuse block; make sure it's fully seated.
- **Vents and returns:** Confirm supply vents are open and unblocked by furniture/rugs, and return grilles aren't obstructed.
- **Condensate drain:** A full drain pan or clogged line can trigger a float switch that shuts down cooling. If your unit has an accessible drain line, clear standing water and add drain tablets.
- **Outdoor unit airflow:** Gently clear leaves, grass and debris from around and on top of the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.

⚠️ If you see ice on the copper lines or the indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** (leave the fan running to thaw it), and don't run it in Cool again until you've replaced the filter and called a tech if it refreezes.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work methodically — this list helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Confirm the complaint** and check thermostat call, mode and wiring.
2. **Measure temperature split** across the indoor coil (return vs. supply air) — a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F.
3. **Inspect airflow** — filter, blower operation, coil cleanliness.
4. **Check electrical** — voltage at the disconnect, contactor operation, and capacitor microfarad readings against the rating printed on the cap.
5. **Inspect the outdoor unit** — condenser fan motor, coil cleanliness, compressor amp draw.
6. **Connect refrigerant gauges** and take superheat/subcooling readings to judge the charge. On two-stage and variable-capacity Signature units, they may use Lennox's diagnostic tools or the iComfort interface to read stage/fault data.
7. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — electronic sniffer, dye, or nitrogen pressure test — before any recharge.

Be cautious of any tech who "just adds refrigerant" without finding the leak. That's a temporary fix that vents refrigerant into the atmosphere and violates EPA rules.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak flow at vents | Clogged filter / frozen coil | Replace filter; turn off to thaw | Clear coil, check airflow & charge |
| Indoor unit runs, outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker / blown disconnect fuse | Reset breaker once | Diagnose electrical fault, replace fuse |
| Outdoor unit humming, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None (call pro) | Test & replace capacitor |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Replace filter, thaw system | Find/repair leak, recharge, restore airflow |
| Longer run times, house won't cool | Dirty condenser coil / low charge | Rinse debris off outdoor unit | Deep-clean coil, verify refrigerant |
| Fan spins but no cold air | Contactor or compressor issue | None (call pro) | Test contactor, amp-check compressor |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Electrical fault / failing compressor | Stop resetting; call pro | Isolate short, test compressor |
| Water around indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain / float switch | Clear drain, add tablets | Clear line, verify float switch |

## Repair costs

Prices are typical US ranges including parts and labor; your region and unit size affect the total.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Air filter replacement (DIY) | $10 – $40 |
| Breaker reset / thermostat batteries | $0 |
| Thermostat replacement | $150 – $400 |
| Run capacitor | $150 – $350 |
| Contactor | $150 – $350 |
| Condenser coil cleaning | $100 – $300 |
| Condenser fan motor | $300 – $650 |
| Condensate drain clearing | $75 – $250 |
| Refrigerant leak repair + recharge | $600 – $1,600 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500 – $2,500+ |

If the compressor has failed and your unit is out of warranty, get a quote for full-system replacement too — on older units, replacing the whole condenser (or system) is often the smarter spend. Lennox parts warranties (typically 5–10 years when registered) may cover the compressor part cost, leaving you the labor.
