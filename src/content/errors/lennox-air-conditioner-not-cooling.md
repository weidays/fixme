## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single Lennox fault code — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may be running (indoor blower on, outdoor fan spinning) but the air coming from the vents is warm or barely cool, or the system runs constantly without hitting the thermostat setpoint.

On communicating Lennox systems with an **iComfort** thermostat, you may see an actual alert code (for example a compressor, pressure, or outdoor-unit fault) that points to a specific cause. On conventional 24V (non-communicating) setups there's no code — you just get warm air. Because the exact fault display **varies by outdoor control board and thermostat type**, this page covers the mechanical and electrical causes behind "not cooling" rather than one code number.

If you smell burning, hear loud grinding, or see the outdoor breaker tripping repeatedly, shut the system off at the breaker and call a pro before running it again.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or clogged evaporator coil** — restricts airflow, causing weak cooling and sometimes a frozen coil.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (set to "fan" or "heat"), or a schedule/setpoint issue.
3. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common failure. The compressor or outdoor fan can't start; you may hear humming.
4. **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil** — traps heat and cripples cooling capacity, especially in summer.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, ice on the lines, longer run times. Requires a licensed tech.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or burns out, so the condenser never starts.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; blows warm air and drips water.
8. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the outdoor unit hums or trips the breaker but won't run.
9. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse / control-board fault** — no power to the outdoor unit or a communicating board error.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Air filter** — pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of weak cooling.
- **Thermostat** — confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker** — check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor AC breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents** — make sure furniture, rugs, or closed registers aren't choking airflow. Keep at least 80% of vents open.
- **Outdoor unit** — clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Make sure the fan is spinning when the system calls for cooling.
- **Condensate drain** — if you see water pooling near the indoor unit or the system shut off, the drain may be clogged. You can clear a visible float switch or reset a full pan; drain-line clearing beyond that is a pro task.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, remove electrical panels on the outdoor unit, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work in this order — use it to sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** and check thermostat wiring and voltage.
2. **Check airflow** — filter, blower operation, and evaporator coil condition; look for icing.
3. **Read any iComfort/board fault codes** on communicating systems.
4. **Measure electrical components** — test the run capacitor with a meter, inspect the contactor, and check for proper voltage at the compressor.
5. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and take suction/liquid pressures plus superheat and subcooling to judge charge and airflow.
6. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen pressure test. A proper diagnosis finds the leak, not just adds gas.
7. **Test the compressor** — resistance and amp draw — if it won't start after the capacitor and contactor check out.

Be wary of any tech who wants to "just add a pound of Freon" without checking for a leak — that's a temporary fix on a system that will fail again.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak cooling, low airflow | Dirty filter / coil | Replace filter, clear vents | Deep-clean evaporator coil |
| Warm air, outdoor unit humming, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor runs | Bad contactor or breaker | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, verify voltage |
| Ice on refrigerant lines / coil | Low refrigerant or low airflow | Turn AC off, fan on to thaw; replace filter | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low charge, dirty condenser, undersized | Clean around condenser | Check charge, coil, load |
| Water near indoor unit, system off | Clogged condensate drain | Clear pan / reset float switch | Clear drain line, treat pan |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Compressor or electrical fault | Stop resetting; call pro | Test compressor, wiring |
| No cooling, iComfort shows alert code | Board-reported fault | Note the code | Diagnose per code |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges (parts + labor). Regional rates and Lennox model tier affect the total.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90 – $200
- **Air filter:** $15 – $40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0 – $10 (DIY)
- **Run capacitor:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor:** $150 – $350
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100 – $250
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150 – $350
- **Evaporator coil cleaning:** $200 – $500
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300 – $1,500 (depends on leak location; evaporator coil replacement is the high end)
- **Control board (communicating systems):** $400 – $900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500 – $2,500+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement on older units)

If your Lennox AC is under 10 years old, check warranty status — Lennox compressors and coils often carry 5–10 year parts warranties, which can dramatically cut the bill.
