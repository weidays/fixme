## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a specific error code on your Lennox unit — it is a symptom. Your system may be running normally at the thermostat, the outdoor unit may be humming, and air may even be blowing from your vents, but the air isn't cold or the house never reaches setpoint.

Some newer Lennox systems with **iComfort** or **S30/S40 smart thermostats** will display alerts or fault codes on the thermostat screen when a specific component fails. Older units with conventional thermostats give no code at all — you only notice the house getting warm. Because "not cooling" can stem from anything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, this page walks you from the cheap, safe checks up to the pro-level repairs so you can sanity-check any quote you're given.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — restricts airflow across the evaporator coil, cutting cooling and often causing the coil to freeze.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — a coil packed with grass, dust, or cottonwood can't reject heat, so cooling capacity drops.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (set to "fan" instead of "cool"), or a wiring/programming issue.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer entirely.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system runs but produces warm or weak air; requires a licensed tech.
6. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan won't start; a very common, relatively cheap failure.
7. **Bad contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
9. **Condensate overflow / float switch** — a full drain pan trips a safety switch that shuts the system down.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; unit hums, trips breakers, or won't start.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. If they don't restore cooling, stop and call a pro — don't keep resetting a unit that trips the breaker.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a huge share of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the fan to **Auto**, and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's a battery-powered model.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker feeding the indoor air handler/furnace and the outdoor condenser. Reset it once. If it trips again, stop — that's a fault, not a fluke.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Visually clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around and on top of the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate drain:** Look for a full drain pan or standing water near the indoor unit. A clogged drain can trip a float safety switch and stop cooling. If you see ice on the copper lines or coil, turn the system to **Off** and let it thaw fully (several hours) before restarting.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech works methodically — here's what a legitimate diagnosis looks like so you can spot shortcuts:

- **Confirms the complaint** and checks thermostat call, mode, and low-voltage signal to the outdoor unit.
- **Measures the temperature split** across the return and supply air (typically ~16-22°F on a healthy system) to gauge performance.
- **Inspects airflow** — filter, blower operation, and the evaporator coil for dirt or ice.
- **Checks the outdoor unit electrically** — capacitor microfarads, contactor condition, and voltage/amperage on the compressor and fan motor.
- **Gauges refrigerant pressures** and takes superheat/subcooling readings. Low charge means they'll search for a leak (dye, electronic detector, or soap) — a proper shop won't just "top off" without finding it.
- **Reads any thermostat/board codes** on iComfort-equipped systems to pinpoint component faults.

If someone quotes a compressor or a full recharge without gauges on the unit and airflow verified, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Verify blower, clean coil |
| Ice on refrigerant lines/coil | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, let thaw, replace filter | Diagnose leak, check charge |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor, capacitor |
| Fan on top not spinning | Failed capacitor or fan motor | None — shut off | Replace capacitor/fan motor |
| Water/leak near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Clear pan, drain tablets | Clear line, service float switch |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant (leak) | None | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Unit hums then trips breaker | Failing compressor or hard start | Shut off, don't reset repeatedly | Test compressor, replace |
| House warm, thermostat blank | Dead batteries, wiring | Replace batteries | Diagnose thermostat/wiring |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices including parts and labor. Your area, unit age, and warranty status will move these.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning / tune-up:** $75–$200
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing / new float switch:** $100–$300
- **Condenser fan motor:** $400–$750
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500 (higher if the leak is in the evaporator coil)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $2,000–$3,500+ (often worth weighing against a new unit if the system is 10+ years old)

A quick note on refrigerant: if your Lennox uses **R-22** (units made before ~2010), recharging is expensive and increasingly hard to source — a leak repair on an old R-22 system often tips the math toward replacement.
