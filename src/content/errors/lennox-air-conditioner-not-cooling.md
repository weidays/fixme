## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on Lennox equipment — it's the symptom homeowners report when the system runs (or tries to) but the house stays warm. Lennox communicating systems with **iComfort** thermostats may display an actual alert code or a message like "system fault" or "no cooling," while older non-communicating units simply run without cooling and show nothing.

Because "not cooling" covers everything from a clogged filter to a dead compressor, the goal here is to help you separate the safe, cheap DIY checks from the problems that legitimately require a licensed HVAC technician — and to help you judge whether a quote is fair.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, causing weak cooling and often a frozen coil.
2. **Thermostat set wrong or failing** — wrong mode, batteries dead, or a miscommunication on iComfort systems.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; the unit blows warm and you may see ice on the copper lines.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the most common "runs but won't cool" cause on units more than a few years old. Requires a licensed tech.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the outdoor fan or compressor hums but won't start.
6. **Bad contactor** — the outdoor unit won't energize at all.
7. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so cooling capacity drops in hot weather.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit is completely dead.
9. **Failed compressor** — the expensive worst case; the unit runs but produces no cold air and may trip breakers.
10. **Control board / communication fault** — on iComfort systems, a lost signal between thermostat, indoor unit and outdoor unit.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here if none of them solve it.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of weak cooling and frozen coils.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL, the temperature is below room temp, and the fan is on AUTO. Replace batteries if it's battery powered.
- **Breakers:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker for both the indoor air handler/furnace and the outdoor condenser. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure they're open and not blocked by furniture or rugs. Closing too many vents starves airflow.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch that shuts down cooling. If you have a visible drain line or pan overflowing, clear standing water and add drain tablets.
- **Outdoor unit:** Check that it's clear of leaves, grass and debris, and the coil fins aren't packed with dirt. Gently rinse with a garden hose (power off) if it's dirty.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to FAN ONLY for a few hours to thaw it, then run cooling again. If it re-freezes, you have an airflow or refrigerant problem — call a pro.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech will work systematically, and you can sanity-check their quote against these steps:

1. **Verify the complaint** and check thermostat settings and mode.
2. **Measure airflow and temperature split** across the indoor coil (typically 16–22°F drop). A low split points to airflow or charge issues.
3. **Inspect the filter and coils** for restriction and ice.
4. **Check electrical components** — measure the capacitor's microfarads, test the contactor, and verify voltage at the outdoor unit.
5. **Attach gauges and measure refrigerant pressures** (superheat/subcooling). This is the only reliable way to confirm charge — a good tech won't just "top it off" without finding the leak.
6. **Leak search** with an electronic detector or dye if charge is low.
7. **Test the compressor** for proper amp draw and windings if it won't start.
8. **On iComfort systems,** read stored fault codes and confirm communication between the thermostat, indoor unit and outdoor board.

Be wary of any tech who adds refrigerant without leak-testing, or condemns a compressor without measuring amp draw and windings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but not cold | Dirty filter or low refrigerant | Replace filter, check for ice | Measure charge, find and fix leak |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low charge | Set to FAN, thaw coil | Diagnose airflow/refrigerant cause |
| Outdoor fan runs, compressor won't start | Failed run capacitor | None (electrical) | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit completely dead | Tripped breaker, bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Runs constantly, weak cooling | Dirty condenser or low charge | Rinse outdoor coil | Verify charge and capacity |
| Warm air, high amp draw / trips breaker | Failing compressor | Shut off, call pro | Test and replace compressor |
| iComfort shows fault/no comms | Control or wiring fault | Cycle thermostat power once | Read codes, test communication bus |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including labor. Regional prices and unit tier vary widely.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$15 (DIY)
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair (minor) + recharge:** $500–$1,200
- **Major leak / coil replacement + recharge:** $1,200–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often not worth it on older units — weigh against replacement)
- **iComfort control board:** $400–$900

If the unit is more than 12–15 years old and needs a compressor or coil, ask your tech to compare repair cost against a new system.
