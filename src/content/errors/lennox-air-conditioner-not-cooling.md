## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code like the furnace **Code 200 (ignition lockout)** — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner is powered on and the blower or outdoor unit may be running, but the air coming from your vents isn't cold enough (or isn't cold at all).

On older single-stage Lennox units (Merit and many Elite models) there's usually no error display — you diagnose by observation. On newer **communicating** systems (Signature series with iComfort thermostats and variable-capacity XC/SL equipment), the thermostat or control board may log a specific alert code that points to the real problem. If your iComfort screen shows an active alert, note the number before troubleshooting.

The job of this page is to help you separate the cheap, do-it-yourself causes from the ones that need a licensed HVAC tech — and to sanity-check the quote you're given.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — Restricts airflow across the evaporator coil, which can cause weak cooling or a frozen coil. The single most common and cheapest cause.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — Caused by low airflow, low refrigerant, or running the AC in cold weather. Ice blocks heat transfer, so warm air blows.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — Grass clippings, dust, and cottonwood block the coil so it can't reject heat. Very common in mid-summer.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — The system can't move heat effectively. This is a repair, not a "top-off" — refrigerant doesn't get used up, so if it's low there's a leak.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — A weak or dead capacitor stops the compressor or condenser fan from starting. Common failure, especially in heat.
6. **Failed contactor** — The relay that sends power to the outdoor unit; pitted or stuck contacts stop the compressor from engaging.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — Outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs, so you get airflow but no cold.
8. **Thermostat problem** — Dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a wiring fault on communicating iComfort systems.
9. **Failed compressor** — The most expensive cause. The outdoor unit hums but won't start, or trips the breaker.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all homeowner-safe. If any require going beyond them, stop and call a pro.

- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is well below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Inspect and replace the air filter.** A clogged filter is the #1 cause. If it's gray and matted, swap it and give the system time to recover.
- **Check both breakers.** Central AC usually has two — one for the indoor air handler/furnace and one (often a 240V double-pole) for the outdoor condenser. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a tech.
- **Look for ice.** Check the copper lines near the indoor coil and the outdoor unit. If you see frost or ice, **turn the AC off at the thermostat and run the fan only** for a few hours to thaw it, then replace the filter.
- **Check the outdoor unit.** Make sure it's running and clear of leaves, grass, and debris. Keep at least 18–24 inches of clearance around it. Gently rinse visible dirt off the outside fins with a garden hose (unit powered off).
- **Verify vents and registers are open** in the rooms that feel warm.
- **Check the condensate drain.** A full drain pan or clogged line can trip a safety float switch that shuts the system down. Clear standing water and use drain tablets to prevent algae.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech should work through this order — use it to sanity-check the visit:

1. **Confirm the symptom and read any iComfort alert codes** on communicating systems.
2. **Check airflow** — filter, blower operation, and whether the evaporator coil is frozen.
3. **Measure electrical components** — test the capacitor with a meter, inspect the contactor for pitting, verify voltage to the outdoor unit.
4. **Attach refrigerant gauges** and read suction/liquid pressures and temperatures. Low or unusual pressures point to a leak or a metering/compressor issue.
5. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen pressure test. A good tech finds and fixes the leak rather than just recharging.
6. **Check the compressor** — measure amp draw and windings, verify it starts and runs.

Be cautious of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" without a leak search, or straight to "new compressor/new system" without electrical and airflow checks first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace the filter | Check blower speed and ductwork |
| Ice on indoor coil or lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw, swap filter | Find airflow/refrigerant cause and repair |
| Outdoor unit not running, indoor fan on | Tripped breaker or dead capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test and replace capacitor/contactor |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Failed capacitor or compressor | None — call a pro | Test capacitor, then compressor |
| Cooling that fades over weeks | Refrigerant leak | None — call a pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Warm air, dirty outdoor coil | Blocked condenser | Rinse fins, clear debris | Deep coil clean, verify performance |
| System off, water around unit | Clogged condensate line/float switch | Clear drain, use tablets | Clear line, test float switch |
| No response, blank thermostat | Dead batteries or thermostat fault | Replace batteries | Test thermostat and control wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Regional and system-type variation is significant, especially for communicating equipment.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 pro
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs older R-22 is far pricier)
- **Fan motor (condenser or blower):** $400–$900
- **TXV / metering device:** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,200+ — often the point where replacing the whole outdoor unit or system makes more sense

Get itemized quotes, and on compressor-level failures always ask whether the unit is under Lennox's parts warranty, which can cut the bill substantially.
