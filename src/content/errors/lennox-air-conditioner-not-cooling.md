## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific numeric fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your Lennox air conditioner runs but the house never reaches setpoint, or the air from your vents feels neutral or warm. On modern Lennox systems it can appear alongside a range of diagnostics: iComfort communicating thermostats may display alerts or codes, while older non-communicating systems show nothing at all and simply underperform.

Cooling failure means one of three things is happening: the system isn't getting the signal to run, the compressor circuit isn't engaging, or the refrigerant loop can't move heat (low charge, restriction, or airflow problem). Because several of these involve refrigerant and high-voltage components, this is rated a **pro** repair — but a handful of safe homeowner checks resolve a surprising number of cases before you pay for a visit.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. A clogged filter starves the indoor coil, drops capacity, and can freeze the coil so no cold air reaches you.
2. **Thermostat issue** — dead batteries, wrong mode (not on COOL), setpoint too high, or a loose/failed thermostat. On iComfort systems, a communication fault can stop the call for cooling.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor condenser and indoor air handler are often on separate circuits. One can trip and leave the blower running with no compressor.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common condenser failure. The fan or compressor won't start (or hums and stalls), so no heat is removed.
5. **Bad contactor** — the relay that sends power to the compressor and outdoor fan burns or sticks, cutting the condenser off.
6. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — caked with grass, dust, or debris, the unit can't reject heat and cooling collapses on hot days.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced cooling, longer run times, ice on the lines, or a low-pressure safety lockout. Always indicates a leak, never "using up" refrigerant.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low charge; the coil ices over and blocks air until it thaws.
9. **Clogged condensate line / tripped float switch** — a full drain pan trips the safety float and shuts cooling down to prevent water damage.
10. **Failed compressor** — the least common but most expensive; the unit runs but produces no cooling, or trips repeatedly.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should attempt. Do **not** open the electrical panel on the condenser, touch the capacitor, or add refrigerant.

- **Filter:** Pull the air filter. If it's gray, dusty, or you can't see light through it, replace it and run the system for an hour.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL**, the fan to **AUTO**, and the setpoint several degrees below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel. Reset any tripped breaker for the air handler or condenser **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris away from the condenser. Gently rinse the exterior fins with a garden hose (power off) if they look dirty.
- **Condensate:** Check for standing water near the indoor unit. A full drain pan or tripped float switch can shut cooling down. Clearing the visible drain outlet is fine; deeper clearing is a tech job.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or coil, turn the system OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw fully before running again — running iced-over damages the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work systematically. Use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Confirm the call for cooling** — verify 24V signal from thermostat to the outdoor unit and check for communication faults on iComfort boards.
2. **Check electrical** — measure incoming voltage, test the contactor, and read the run capacitor's microfarads against the label spec.
3. **Inspect airflow** — filter condition, blower operation, and whether the evaporator coil is frozen or dirty.
4. **Gauge the refrigerant** — connect manifold gauges to read suction and head pressures, calculate superheat/subcooling to determine if charge is correct.
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, they should find the leak (electronic detector, dye, or bubble test) rather than just "topping off."
6. **Compressor test** — measure amp draw and winding resistance if it isn't starting or is tripping.

A good tech tells you *why* a part failed. Be cautious of anyone who adds refrigerant without mentioning a leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air feels neutral, house won't cool | Dirty filter / low airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Clean coil, check blower |
| Blower runs, no cold air; outdoor unit dead | Tripped breaker | Reset breaker once | Find why it tripped |
| Outdoor fan won't start or hums | Failed run capacitor | None (high voltage) | Test & replace capacitor |
| Nothing runs outdoors, buzzing | Bad contactor | None | Replace contactor |
| Ice on lines/coil | Low airflow or low charge | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Long runtimes, weak cooling, hot days | Dirty condenser coil / low charge | Rinse outdoor fins | Coil clean or leak repair |
| Water near indoor unit, system stops | Tripped condensate float | Clear visible drain | Clear line, test float |
| Unit runs constantly, no cooling at all | Failed compressor | None | Compressor test/replace |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries / wiring | Replace batteries | Replace/rewire thermostat |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your region and system size will vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate line clearing:** $75–$250
- **Condensate float switch:** $80–$200 installed
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400 installed
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 installed
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair:** $300–$1,500+ depending on leak location
- **Refrigerant recharge:** $200–$700+ (R-410A pricing varies)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to full replacement on older units)

If your Lennox system is out of warranty and 12+ years old, weigh a major repair against replacement — Lennox parts warranties often cover the compressor but not labor.
