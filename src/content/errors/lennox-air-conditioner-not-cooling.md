## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may run but blow warm or room-temperature air, cool weakly, or short-cycle without reaching the thermostat setpoint.

On older Lennox systems with a dial or basic digital thermostat, there is no on-screen code — you just notice the house never cools. On newer systems paired with an **iComfort** or **iComfort S30** thermostat, you may see an equipment alert or code that points to a specific component (blower, coil temperature, communication fault). Behavior varies by model and control board, so treat any on-screen alert as a starting clue, not a diagnosis.

Because low cooling can be caused by anything from a $20 filter to a failed compressor, this page ranks the likely causes and separates the genuinely safe DIY checks from the work that needs a licensed HVAC pro.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops coil temperature, and can freeze the evaporator so no cold air moves.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil (indoor)** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; the coil turns to ice and cooling stops.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — leaves, grass, and dirt block heat rejection so the system can't dump heat.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan won't start reliably; you may hear a hum or clicking.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — cooling weakens gradually; requires certified diagnosis and repair.
6. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so the condenser never runs.
7. **Thermostat problem or wiring** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a loose wire stops the cooling call.
8. **Condensate safety switch tripped** — a clogged drain trips a float switch that cuts cooling to prevent overflow.
9. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive cause; the outdoor unit hums, trips the breaker, or won't start.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening the sealed refrigerant system or bypassing a safety device.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. On iComfort screens, note any alert message.
- **Air filter:** Pull it. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" calls.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker on the air handler and the condenser. Reset **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the fins with a garden hose (never a pressure washer) with power off.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain:** Check for water pooling near the indoor unit or a full drain pan. A clog can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Drain tablets help maintenance, but a real clog needs clearing.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the refrigerant lines, turn the system to OFF and set the fan to ON to thaw it (can take several hours), then replace the filter before restarting.

If the house still won't cool after these, the problem is inside the sealed system or electrical components — call a licensed technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the steps helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 15–20°F).
2. **Checks airflow** — filter, blower operation, and coil condition; a frozen coil gets thawed and investigated.
3. **Tests electrical** — capacitor microfarad reading, contactor condition, and voltage at the compressor and fan.
4. **Measures refrigerant** — connects gauges to read pressures and checks superheat/subcooling. Low charge means a leak search (dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen).
5. **Checks the compressor** — amp draw, windings, and start behavior if it won't run.
6. **Reads control data** — on iComfort/S30 systems, pulls equipment codes and history from the board.

A good tech explains what they found before replacing parts. Be cautious of anyone who "adds refrigerant" without finding the leak — that's a temporary and non-compliant fix.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs but blows warm air | Low refrigerant / bad capacitor | Check filter and breaker | Find leak, test capacitor, recharge |
| Indoor coil iced over | Dirty filter or low charge | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Diagnose airflow or refrigerant cause |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cooling | Failed compressor or capacitor | None — call pro | Test compressor, capacitor, contactor |
| Outdoor unit silent | Bad contactor / capacitor / breaker | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor or capacitor |
| Weak cooling, house never reaches setpoint | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse fins, clear debris | Deep coil clean, check charge |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged condensate drain, tripped float | Clear visible clog, check pan | Clear drain line, test float switch |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries / control fault | Replace batteries | Test wiring and control board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Regional rates vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 (more for iComfort)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,300–$2,200+ (often worth comparing to system replacement on older units)

If your Lennox AC is out of warranty and needs a compressor or coil, ask for a repair-vs-replace comparison — sometimes a new system is the better value.
