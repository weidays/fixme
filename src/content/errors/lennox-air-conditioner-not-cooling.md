## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific fault code. Many Lennox central air conditioners and heat pumps do not display a digital error code the way a furnace board does — the outdoor unit either runs and produces cool air, or it doesn't. Some higher-end variable-capacity Lennox systems (like the XC/XP21 and SL/S30 platforms with iComfort communicating controls) can report codes through the thermostat, but a large share of Merit and Elite series units communicate only through a simple control board and contactor.

So when your Lennox "isn't cooling," it falls into one of three buckets: **the system won't turn on at all**, **it runs but blows warm air**, or **it cools weakly / freezes up**. The checks below apply to all three.

This is rated a **pro** issue because the most likely underlying causes — low refrigerant, a failed compressor, or electrical faults — are not homeowner-serviceable. You can safely rule out the cheap causes, though.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter or blocked return** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil. The single most common cause.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, cottonwood, and dust insulate the coil so it can't reject heat. Very common in summer.
3. **Failed run capacitor** — a bulged or dead dual capacitor stops the compressor and/or condenser fan from starting. Common and cheap to fix.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces cooling and can ice the coil. Requires an EPA-certified tech; the leak must be found, not just topped off.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler gets no power.
6. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (HEAT instead of COOL), or a wiring/communication fault, especially on iComfort systems.
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — a downstream result of low airflow or low charge; the unit ices over and stops cooling.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — the compressor runs but the fan doesn't, causing high-pressure trips.
10. **Failed compressor** — least common but most expensive; often the end-of-life failure.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening a sealed electrical or refrigerant component.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the target temp is below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Lower the setpoint 3–4°F and listen for the outdoor unit to start.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and inspect. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. A dirty filter alone can cause warm air and coil icing.
- **Breakers:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and the outdoor AC breaker in your main panel. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting it.
- **Outdoor unit airflow:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the outside of the coil fins with a garden hose (water only, low pressure) with the power off at the disconnect.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure they're open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch that shuts down cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan and clear a visible clog if you can safely reach it.
- **Ice:** If the indoor coil or outdoor lines are frosted over, turn the system to OFF but leave the fan ON to thaw it, then replace the filter. If it re-freezes, you likely have low refrigerant — call a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a repeatable sequence. Knowing it helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** and check thermostat mode, call for cooling, and 24V signal at the outdoor unit.
2. **Measure electrical:** voltage at the disconnect, capacitor microfarads against the rated value, and contactor operation.
3. **Check airflow:** filter, blower operation, and coil cleanliness — a common miss on cheap quotes.
4. **Attach gauges and take temperatures:** superheat and subcooling readings tell them whether the system is properly charged, overcharged, or low. This is the key refrigerant diagnosis.
5. **If low on charge:** perform a leak search (electronic detector or dye) rather than just topping off. A tech who adds refrigerant without finding the leak is not fixing the problem.
6. **Test the compressor and condenser fan** windings and amp draw if the outdoor unit won't start.
7. **Read codes** on iComfort/communicating systems through the thermostat's advanced menu, if equipped.

Expect a diagnostic fee of roughly **$85–$175**, often credited toward the repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan blows but air isn't cold | Low refrigerant, failed compressor, or dirty condenser | Clean condenser, replace filter | Gauge charge, leak search, test compressor |
| Outdoor unit totally dead | Tripped breaker, blown fuse, bad contactor | Reset breaker once; check thermostat | Test contactor, low-voltage fuse, wiring |
| Compressor hums but won't start | Failed run/start capacitor | None (electrical) | Replace capacitor, test hard-start kit |
| Indoor coil iced over | Dirty filter, low airflow, low charge | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Verify charge, check blower and coil |
| Cools weakly on hot days | Dirty coil, low charge, undersized airflow | Clean coil, clear vents | Measure subcooling, static pressure |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed fan motor or capacitor | Power off; do not spin by hand repeatedly | Replace condenser fan motor/capacitor |
| No cooling, water in pan | Clogged condensate drain, float switch tripped | Clear visible drain clog | Flush drain line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included, for Lennox systems:

- **Air filter replacement:** $15–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$10 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$300
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$700
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$2,000+ depending on leak location; coil leaks are the costly end
- **TXV / metering device replacement:** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,900 (often makes full replacement more sensible on older units)
- **Full system replacement:** $6,000–$14,000+ depending on size and efficiency

Refrigerant work varies a lot by system age and refrigerant type (older R-410A vs. newer low-GWP blends), so get an itemized quote.
