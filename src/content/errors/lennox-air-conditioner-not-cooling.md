## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code — it's the symptom Lennox homeowners search most. Your system may be running but blowing warm or room-temperature air, short-cycling, or the outdoor unit may not be starting at all. On newer Lennox Signature (SL/XC-series) systems paired with an **iComfort** thermostat, you may see an actual alert or fault message at the thermostat. Older Merit and Elite units with a standard thermostat give no code at all — you diagnose by observing behavior.

A Lennox AC that won't cool is almost always caused by one of a handful of issues: restricted airflow, a frozen coil, a refrigerant problem (leak), a failed electrical component, or a control/thermostat fault. Some of these are safe homeowner checks; several require a licensed HVAC technician with gauges and meters.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops coil temperature, and can freeze the evaporator so no cold air moves.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (not set to Cool), or a setpoint above room temperature.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler loses power and can't run.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked with grass, dust, or cottonwood, so the system can't reject heat.
5. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the copper lines.
6. **Failed run/start capacitor** — fan or compressor hums but won't start; very common failure part.
7. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
8. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — system runs but never cools; requires leak repair, not just a "top-off."
9. **Failed compressor** — outdoor unit runs the fan but won't cool; the most expensive failure.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass safety switches, or handle refrigerant.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and check. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. This is the #1 cause and a free fix.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below the current room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor breakers in your panel. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Ice check:** Look at the copper refrigerant lines and indoor coil. If you see ice, turn the system **Off** but leave the fan **On** for a few hours to thaw. Running a frozen system damages the compressor.
- **Condensate drain:** Check for standing water near the indoor unit. A clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. You can pour a cup of distilled vinegar down an accessible drain line, but don't disassemble the unit.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser. Gently hose the fins from outside with the power off if they look dirty — never use a pressure washer.

If cooling doesn't return after a clean filter, correct thermostat settings, restored power, and a thawed coil, it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Lennox tech follows a logical sequence, so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Confirms the complaint** and checks thermostat call for cooling with a meter (24V present at the control board/contactor).
2. **Checks power** — voltage at the disconnect and low-voltage fuse on the board.
3. **Tests the capacitor** with a multimeter against its rated microfarads, and inspects the contactor for pitting.
4. **Measures temperature split** — the difference between return and supply air (should be roughly 14–22°F). A low split points to airflow or refrigerant issues.
5. **Connects refrigerant gauges** to read pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling. This tells whether the charge is low (leak) or the system is overcharged/restricted.
6. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — with electronic detector or dye. A reputable tech will find and repair the leak, not just add refrigerant.
7. **Checks the compressor** — amperage draw, windings, and whether it starts. On iComfort systems, they'll pull stored fault codes from the thermostat/control.

Be cautious of any tech who "adds a pound of Freon" without a leak search — that's a temporary fix and often a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, filter is filthy | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, wait 30 min | Verify airflow, check coil |
| No cold air, thermostat blank | Dead batteries / power | Replace batteries, check breaker | Test board, low-voltage fuse |
| Ice on copper lines/indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Check charge, find leak, clean coil |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor runs | Breaker, capacitor, contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace capacitor or contactor |
| Outdoor fan hums, won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Replace capacitor |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant / leak | None — call pro | Leak search & repair, recharge |
| Fan runs, no cooling, warm lines | Failed compressor | None — call pro | Compressor test/replacement |
| Water pooling, system stops | Clogged condensate drain | Clear accessible drain | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included. Regional and dealer pricing varies.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400+ (more for iComfort smart models)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Low-voltage fuse / minor control repair:** $75–$200
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location (a coil replacement pushes this higher)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$3,000+ (often worth comparing to full system replacement on older units)

If your Lennox unit is out of warranty and needs a compressor or coil, get a second quote and ask about the cost of a full replacement — the math sometimes favors a new system.
