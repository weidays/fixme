## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code on a Lennox air conditioner — it's a symptom. Your system may be running with warm air at the vents, cycling on and off, or not turning on at all. On basic Lennox units (Merit and many Elite models), there's no diagnostic display at all, so you're working from behavior. On variable-capacity Signature models (SL/XC25, XC21) paired with an **iComfort** thermostat, the thermostat itself will often display a specific alert or error code that points to the real problem.

Because "not cooling" can be caused by anything from a $20 filter to a failed compressor, the goal below is to help you separate the safe, homeowner-fixable causes from the ones that need a licensed HVAC technician — and to help you sanity-check any quote you're given.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged or dirty air filter** — restricts airflow, drops cooling capacity, and can freeze the evaporator coil. Most common cause overall.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (Heat vs. Cool), or a schedule/setback keeping the setpoint high.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler has lost power.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — matted with grass, dust, or cottonwood; the unit can't reject heat.
5. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; you'll see ice on the indoor coil or refrigerant line.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — warm air, long run times, hissing, or ice. Requires a licensed tech; refrigerant is never a DIY job.
7. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or condenser fan hums but won't start; a common, affordable pro repair.
8. **Failed condenser fan motor or contactor** — outdoor fan won't spin, unit overheats and shuts down.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; the unit runs but produces no cooling, or won't run at all.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Run the system for an hour afterward.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (disconnect near the condenser) breakers. Reset a tripped one **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance. Do **not** open the unit.
- **Condensate drain:** A full drain pan or a tripped float switch will shut cooling off. If you see standing water in the pan, clear the visible clog and empty it.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or copper lines, turn the system to **Off** and run the fan on **On** for a few hours to thaw it, then replace the filter. If it refreezes, you need a tech.

Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech should work in this order — use it to sanity-check the labor time you're billed:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply and return air temperatures. A healthy system shows roughly a 15–20°F drop (delta-T) across the coil.
2. **Check airflow** — inspect the filter, blower, and evaporator coil for restriction or ice.
3. **Read the thermostat/board** — on iComfort systems, pull active and stored fault codes from the thermostat menu.
4. **Test electrical** — measure the capacitor(s), contactor, and voltage at the compressor and fan motor.
5. **Check refrigerant** — connect gauges, read suction and liquid pressures, and calculate superheat/subcooling to confirm charge. If low, they should perform a **leak search**, not just top it off.
6. **Confirm the failed component** — capacitor, fan motor, contactor, TXV/metering device, or compressor.

Be cautious of any tech who adds refrigerant without discussing a leak, or quotes a compressor replacement without showing you failed electrical readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak airflow | Clogged filter | Replace filter, run 1 hour | Check blower & coil if it persists |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries / no power | Replace batteries, check breaker | Test transformer & low-voltage fuse |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor & disconnect |
| Ice on coil or lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Thaw with fan, replace filter | Leak search & recharge |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call a pro | Test & replace capacitor |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Fan motor or capacitor | None — call a pro | Replace fan motor/capacitor |
| Runs constantly, no cooling | Low refrigerant or bad compressor | None — call a pro | Diagnose charge & compressor |
| iComfost thermostat shows alert code | Board-reported fault | Note the code | Read code, follow Lennox diagnostics |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined. Regional labor rates and your model's parts availability move these numbers.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250 (pro) or DIY with tablets
- **Run/dual capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Coil cleaning (condenser or evaporator):** $150–$450
- **Refrigerant leak search:** $200–$600
- **Refrigerant recharge (after leak repair):** $250–$800+, depending on refrigerant type (R-410A vs. newer blends)
- **TXV / metering device:** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800; often the point to weigh replacing an older unit

If your Lennox is out of warranty and needs a compressor, ask whether full system replacement is more economical — especially on units 10+ years old still using older refrigerant.
