## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific numeric fault code — it's the symptom homeowners see most often with a Lennox air conditioner. Your thermostat calls for cooling, the system may hum or blow air, but the house never reaches setpoint.

On newer Lennox units with an **iComfort or S30/S40 communicating thermostat**, an actual alert or fault code is usually logged (for example, a low-pressure or high-pressure trip). On older single-stage Merit and Elite units with a standard 24V thermostat, there's **no code at all** — you're diagnosing purely from behavior. Because "not cooling" can stem from anything from a $15 filter to a failed compressor, this page ranks causes from most to least likely and flags what is safe to check yourself.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, ices the evaporator coil, and kills cooling capacity. The single most common cause.
2. **Dirty condenser coil / blocked outdoor unit** — leaves, grass clippings, or dryer lint smothering the outdoor coil so it can't reject heat.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), miswired, or an unconfigured communicating thermostat.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak/warm air and often ice on the copper line. This is a repair, not a top-off — a licensed tech only.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums but won't start; a very common, affordable failure.
6. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so the condenser never energizes.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from restricted airflow or low charge; you'll see ice on the indoor coil or refrigerant line.
8. **Tripped safety (high/low pressure switch)** — the board locks out cooling to protect the system; common on iComfort units.
9. **Failed compressor or fan motor** — the most expensive outcome, usually the last thing confirmed.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe. Do not open the sealed refrigerant system, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a locked-out unit.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Then let any coil ice fully thaw before restarting.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, setpoint is below room temperature, and fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser disconnect) breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Outdoor unit:** With the unit off, clear leaves, grass, and debris from around and on top of the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance on all sides.
- **Supply/return vents:** Make sure vents aren't closed, blocked by furniture, or covered by rugs.
- **Condensate:** A full drain pan or a tripped float switch can shut down cooling. Check for standing water at the indoor unit and clear an obvious clog if you can safely reach it.
- **Ice:** If you see ice on the coil or the copper line, turn the AC off and the fan on to thaw it before running again.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Lennox tech works from cheap to expensive, so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Reads any stored codes** on iComfort/S30 systems and verifies thermostat call for cooling.
2. **Confirms airflow** — filter, blower operation, and static pressure; checks the evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
3. **Checks electrical** — 24V control signal at the contactor, then contactor operation, and tests the run capacitor with a meter (microfarad reading).
4. **Measures refrigerant charge** with gauges — superheat/subcooling — to tell "undercharged from a leak" apart from an airflow problem.
5. **Leak search** if charge is low (dye, electronic sniffer, or nitrogen pressure test) before adding any refrigerant.
6. **Tests the compressor and fan motor** — windings, amp draw, and start capability — only after ruling out cheaper causes.

Be wary of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without measuring charge or checking the capacitor first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter / frozen coil | Replace filter, thaw coil | Check blower, static pressure |
| Outdoor fan runs, no cold air | Dirty condenser coil | Clean debris, rinse gently | Coil cleaning, charge check |
| Condenser hums but won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — leave sealed | Test & replace capacitor |
| Indoor blows, outdoor unit dead | Bad contactor or breaker | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, 24V signal |
| Ice on copper line / coil | Low refrigerant (leak) | Turn off, thaw | Leak search + recharge |
| Cools then shuts off, warm air returns | Pressure switch lockout | None | Diagnose high/low pressure fault |
| Nothing runs, no thermostat display | Dead batteries / no power | Replace batteries, check breaker | Verify wiring & transformer |
| Long runtime, never hits setpoint | Undercharge or failing compressor | Confirm filter/vents | Measure charge, test compressor |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Prices vary by region and by whether you're inside a Lennox warranty.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 installed
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A vs older R-22)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,800+ (often the point where replacing the whole condenser is the smarter spend)

If your unit is over ~12–15 years old and facing a compressor or coil, ask for a repair-vs-replace comparison before committing.
