## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on a Lennox display — it's the symptom homeowners report when the air conditioner is powered on but the house stays warm. On newer systems with an **iComfort** thermostat, you may see an active alert or a stored code that points to the real issue; on older units with a legacy thermostat, there's no code at all, just warm air.

This page covers the common mechanical and electrical reasons a Lennox central AC stops cooling. The exact diagnostic path varies by series and control board, so where behavior differs by model, we say so.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common reason. Restricted airflow reduces cooling and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — caked with dust, grass, or cottonwood, the unit can't reject heat.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a wiring/communication fault (more common on iComfort systems).
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling and often a frozen coil. Requires a licensed tech.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums but won't start reliably.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — a downstream result of low airflow or low refrigerant; blows warm despite running.
7. **Bad contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or fails to close.
8. **Failed condenser fan motor** — outdoor unit gets hot and trips on high pressure.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive outcome, often the endpoint of neglected electrical or refrigerant problems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should attempt. Do not open the outdoor unit's electrical panel or touch refrigerant lines.

- **Filter:** Pull the air filter. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. This alone fixes many "not cooling" complaints.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool** and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler or condenser. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — never repeatedly reset.
- **Vents & registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit clearance:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance on all sides.
- **Condensate drain:** If the indoor unit's drain pan is full or the float switch has tripped, the system may shut off. Clear the drain line and pan if you can reach it safely.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or coil, **turn the system off at the thermostat** and run the fan only to thaw it. Do not run cooling with ice present.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech should work through this sequence — use it to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** and check thermostat settings and call for cooling.
2. **Inspect airflow** — filter, blower operation, and whether the evaporator coil is frozen.
3. **Read stored codes** on iComfort systems or check indoor/outdoor control board status.
4. **Check electrical** — measure the run capacitor, test the contactor, and confirm voltage at the compressor and fan motor.
5. **Measure temperature split** across the evaporator (typically 15-22°F when working).
6. **Attach refrigerant gauges** to check pressures and superheat/subcooling. Low readings mean a leak — a leak search should follow, not just a "top-off."
7. **Isolate the failed component** and quote the repair. A good tech explains *why* the part failed, not just that it did.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower, ductwork |
| Ice on refrigerant lines | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, let thaw, run fan | Find airflow/refrigerant cause |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Outdoor unit dead, indoor runs | Bad contactor or tripped breaker | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant (leak) | None — call pro | Leak search + recharge |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed fan motor or capacitor | Clear debris | Replace motor/capacitor |
| Loud, then no cooling at all | Compressor failure | None — call pro | Compressor/system assessment |
| Blank thermostat | Dead batteries or power loss | Replace batteries, check breaker | Diagnose wiring/board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges, parts and labor. Your quote varies by region, unit age, and warranty status.

- **Air filter:** $15–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Service call / diagnosis:** $80–$150
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run/dual capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $300–$650
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $400–$1,500+ (depends on leak location; evaporator coil leaks cost more)
- **Blower motor:** $400–$800
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often makes replacing an older system more economical)

Refrigerant work and any repair opening the sealed system or electrical cabinet require a licensed HVAC tech. If your compressor or coil is still under Lennox's parts warranty, the part may be covered — labor usually isn't.
