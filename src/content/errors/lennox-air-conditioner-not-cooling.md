## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code on your Lennox — it's the symptom you notice: the system runs (or tries to), but the house doesn't get cold. On communicating **iComfort** systems you may also see a wrench icon or an alert on the thermostat that points to a specific subsystem, while older non-communicating Merit and Elite units simply blow warm or room-temperature air with no display message.

The cause almost always falls into one of three buckets: **airflow** (filter, coils, blower), **refrigerant** (leak, undercharge), or **electrical/control** (capacitor, contactor, thermostat, control board). This page walks through all three so you can tell a cheap fix from an expensive one before you pay for a service call.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter** — the #1 cause of weak or no cooling. A clogged filter starves the indoor coil of airflow and can cause it to freeze into a block of ice.
2. **Dirty or blocked outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, cottonwood, and dirt insulate the coil so it can't dump heat.
3. **Thermostat issue** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs. "auto"), incorrect setpoint, or lost communication on iComfort systems.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor hums but won't start, or trips the breaker. Very common in hot climates.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — gradual loss of cooling, ice on the copper line, hissing. Requires a licensed tech.
6. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor unit gets no power to start even though the thermostat is calling.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — usually a downstream symptom of a dirty filter or low refrigerant.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power at all.
9. **Failed compressor or control board** — the expensive, less common failures, typically diagnosed last.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are homeowner-safe. If a check requires opening the electrical panel of the AC, touching capacitors, or handling refrigerant, stop and call a pro.

- **Filter:** Replace it if it's gray or you can't see light through it. Then run the system 15 minutes and recheck airflow.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to **Cool**, fan to **Auto**, and set at least 5°F below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's a battery model. On iComfort screens, check for alert/wrench icons.
- **Breaker:** Look for a tripped breaker for the AC (there's usually one for the indoor blower and one for the outdoor unit). Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass, and debris at least 2 feet around the condenser. Gently rinse the outside coil fins with a garden hose (never a pressure washer) — power off first.
- **Condensate:** Check the drain pan and line. A full pan or clogged drain can trip a safety float switch and shut the system down.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper line at the outdoor unit, turn the system **off** and let it fully thaw (a few hours) before running again. Persistent icing means a real fault.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech should work systematically — this is what a fair diagnosis looks like:

1. **Confirm the complaint** and check thermostat call, mode, and (on iComfort) the alert history on the control board.
2. **Verify power** at the disconnect and contactor; test the capacitor with a meter (not by guessing).
3. **Measure airflow and temperature split** — the difference between return and supply air should typically be about 16–22°F. A low split points to airflow or refrigerant problems.
4. **Inspect coils** indoor and outdoor for dirt, and check for a frozen coil.
5. **Connect gauges** to read refrigerant pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling — this is how they confirm an undercharge or leak. Expect a leak search if refrigerant is low.
6. **Check the compressor and contactor** for correct amp draw and starting behavior.

If a tech quotes a compressor or refrigerant charge without ever putting gauges on the system or checking airflow, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, weak airflow | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Check blower, clean coils |
| Outdoor fan runs, indoor blows warm | Low refrigerant / leak | None — call pro | Leak search, seal, recharge |
| Compressor hums, won't start | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on coil or copper line | Airflow or low charge | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Diagnose airflow/refrigerant |
| Outdoor unit totally dead | Tripped breaker / contactor | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, test wiring |
| Thermostat blank or wrong mode | Dead battery / settings | Replace battery, set to Cool | Check thermostat wiring/comm |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Undercharge or dirty coils | Clean coil area, filter | Full refrigerant + coil check |
| Wrench/alert on iComfort screen | Control/sensor fault | Note the alert | Read board codes, replace part |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included where noted:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$15 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair:** $400–$1,500 depending on leak location
- **Refrigerant recharge:** $250–$800 (R‑410A; varies by amount and market)
- **Blower motor:** $450–$900
- **Control board (iComfort/communicating):** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against a new unit if out of warranty)

Prices vary by region, unit age, and whether your Lennox is still under its parts warranty — many Lennox compressors and coils carry 5–10 year limited warranties if registered, which can drastically cut parts cost.
