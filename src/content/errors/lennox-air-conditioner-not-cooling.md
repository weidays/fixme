## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numbered fault code — it's the symptom you're seeing when your Lennox air conditioner is running (or trying to) but the air coming from your vents isn't cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint. On iComfort thermostats you may see a specific alert or the outdoor unit may log an error; on conventional 24V systems there's no display, just warm air.

Lennox condensing units (the outdoor box) work with your indoor coil and blower to move heat out of your home. "Not cooling" breaks down into three failure zones: **airflow** (filter, blower, coil), **the refrigerant circuit** (charge, compressor, metering device), and **controls/electrical** (thermostat, contactor, capacitor, float switch). The checks below help you rule out the easy stuff before paying for a service call.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter or dirty indoor (evaporator) coil** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the coil into a block of ice.
2. **Tripped condensate float safety switch** — a full or clogged drain pan trips a safety that cuts cooling. Very common in humid climates.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, cottonwood, and dust insulate the coil so it can't reject heat.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a swollen or dead capacitor stops the compressor or fan from starting; you may hear a hum or clicking.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduced charge means weak or no cooling, often with ice on the copper lines. This is a repair, not a "top-off."
6. **Bad contactor** — pitted or stuck contacts prevent the outdoor unit from energizing.
7. **Thermostat fault or wiring issue** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a broken C-wire. iComfort thermostats can also lose communication with the unit.
8. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive outcome; the unit hums, trips the breaker, or won't start at all.
9. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — sometimes a symptom of a deeper electrical fault.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything smells like burning, sparks, or you'd have to open the sealed refrigerant system or bypass a safety.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. On iComfort, check for on-screen alerts or a "communication lost" message.
- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A blocked filter is the #1 cause of weak cooling.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed registers aren't choking airflow. Leave return grilles fully open.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. Reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated trips signal an electrical fault.
- **Condensate drain / float switch:** If the indoor unit shut off, look for a full drain pan or a float switch that's tripped. You can clear a visibly clogged drain line and use drain tablets, but don't defeat the switch.
- **Frozen coil / lines:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper line outside, turn the system to OFF (fan ON is okay) and let it thaw fully — several hours. Running a frozen unit can damage the compressor.
- **Outdoor unit:** Gently clear leaves and debris from around the condenser. Do not spray water into electrical components or bend the fins.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measures supply vs. return air temperature (a healthy split is roughly 16–22°F).
2. **Checks airflow** — filter, blower operation, and evaporator coil condition; a frozen or filthy coil is addressed first.
3. **Tests electrical** — reads the capacitor with a meter, inspects the contactor for pitting, and confirms 24V control voltage from the thermostat.
4. **Gauges the refrigerant circuit** — connects gauges to read pressures and superheat/subcooling. Abnormal readings point to charge, metering-device, or compressor problems.
5. **Leak search** — if charge is low, a good tech finds the leak (electronic detector, dye, or nitrogen pressure test) rather than just recharging.
6. **Compressor test** — checks amp draw and windings if the compressor won't start or trips the breaker.

A quote that recommends "just adding refrigerant" without a leak search is a red flag — insist on finding the leak.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm air, unit running | Dirty filter / airflow | Replace filter, open vents | Clean coil, check blower |
| Indoor unit off, drain pan full | Tripped float switch | Clear drain, add tablets | Clear line, verify switch/pump |
| Ice on coil or copper line | Low airflow or low charge | Turn to OFF, let thaw | Diagnose charge / airflow |
| Outdoor fan hums, won't spin | Failed capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Weak cooling, warm lines | Low refrigerant leak | None — call pro | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Nothing at outdoor unit | Contactor or breaker | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Compressor / short | Stop, leave off | Test compressor, wiring |
| No response, thermostat blank | Battery / C-wire fault | Replace batteries | Repair wiring, check board |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Regional rates and Lennox model tier affect pricing.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing / tablets:** $0–$25 DIY; $75–$200 for a pro service call
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Float switch replacement:** $75–$200
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$500 (more for iComfort communicating models)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $300–$1,500 depending on leak location; coil leaks run higher
- **TXV / metering device:** $500–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,200+ (often worth weighing against a new unit on older systems)

If your Lennox system is out of warranty and facing a compressor or coil replacement, get a second opinion and compare against full-system replacement costs.
