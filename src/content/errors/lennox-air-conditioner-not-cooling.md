## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code—it's the most common complaint reported for Lennox air conditioners. It means the system is powered on but the air coming from your vents is not cold, or the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

On communicating Lennox systems (Signature series with an **iComfort** thermostat), a genuine hardware failure often *does* post an alert or error code at the thermostat. On single-stage Merit and many Elite units with a conventional thermostat, there is no code—you diagnose by symptom.

Because most root causes (refrigerant, compressor, capacitor, sealed-system airflow) require gauges, meters, or EPA certification, this is rated a **pro** repair. The safe checks below can rule out the cheap causes first.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil. The single most common cause.
2. **Thermostat set wrong or failing** — set to "fan" instead of "cool," wrong programming, dead batteries, or a miscalibrated stat.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs, so you feel airflow but no cold.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common outdoor-unit failure; the fan or compressor hums but won't start.
5. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil caked with grass, dust, or cottonwood can't reject heat, killing cooling on hot days.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks all cooling until it thaws.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces or eliminates cooling; requires leak repair, not just a recharge.
8. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the compressor is pitted or stuck, so the outdoor unit never energizes.
9. **Clogged condensate drain / float switch tripped** — a safety float can shut the system down when the drain backs up.
10. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause; the outdoor unit won't cool at all despite power.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Check the filter.** Pull it and hold it to a light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Then let a frozen coil thaw before running again.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Check the breakers.** Look at your electrical panel for the AC breakers (often two: air handler and condenser). If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro—don't keep resetting.
- **Check the outdoor unit for debris.** With the unit off, look for grass clippings, leaves, or cottonwood matting the coil. You can gently rinse the *outside* of the coil fins with a garden hose (low pressure, top-down)—never a pressure washer.
- **Check the vents.** Make sure supply and return registers are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Check the condensate drain.** If you see water pooling near the indoor unit or a float switch, the drain may be clogged. Clearing it is a pro-level job on most systems.

Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, add refrigerant, remove electrical panels on the condenser, or bypass any safety switch.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified Lennox tech will typically:

1. **Verify the complaint** and read any iComfort thermostat alerts on communicating systems.
2. **Confirm airflow**—check the filter, blower operation, and inspect the evaporator coil for ice.
3. **Check power** at the disconnect, breaker, and 24V control circuit; test the low-voltage fuse.
4. **Test electrical components**—measure the run capacitor's microfarads against rating, and inspect the contactor for pitting.
5. **Connect refrigerant gauges** to read suction and liquid pressures, then calculate superheat/subcooling to judge the charge.
6. **Measure the temperature split** across the indoor coil (a healthy split is roughly 15–22°F).
7. **Leak-search** if refrigerant is low, using electronic detectors or dye.
8. **Test the compressor** windings and amp draw if the outdoor unit won't run.

A quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without gauge readings and a leak search is a red flag.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but isn't cold | Low refrigerant, bad capacitor, dirty condenser | Replace filter, rinse outdoor coil | Gauge charge, leak search, test capacitor |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor fan runs | Tripped breaker, blown fuse, bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, low-voltage fuse, wiring |
| Ice on copper line or indoor coil | Dirty filter or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw fully, replace filter | Check airflow and charge, leak search |
| Outdoor fan hums but won't spin | Failed run capacitor | None—shut it off | Test and replace capacitor |
| Warm air on hottest days only | Dirty condenser coil, marginal charge | Clean outdoor coil area | Verify subcooling, deep-clean coil |
| System short-cycles or shuts off | Tripped condensate float switch | Check for water near unit | Clear drain, test float switch |
| No cooling at all, unit clicks | Failed compressor or contactor | None | Test compressor windings and amps |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor included:

- **Air filter:** $10–$50 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$15 (DIY)
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $100–$300
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 (depends on leak location)
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800–$2,500+ (often worth weighing against full system replacement on older units)

Prices vary by region, unit age, and refrigerant type (R-410A vs. older R-22 is far costlier).
