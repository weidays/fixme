## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific Lennox fault code. Your air conditioner may be running (fan spinning, air moving) but the air isn't cold, or the outdoor unit isn't starting at all. Unlike Lennox furnaces, most residential Lennox condensers don't flash a numeric error code for cooling faults — the older split systems have no fault display, and only the newer iComfort/variable-capacity systems (with an S30/S40 thermostat) log codes you can read at the thermostat.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a dirty filter to a dead compressor, the goal below is to help you rule out the cheap, safe stuff first, then understand what a technician should be checking so you can sanity-check a quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter or blocked return** — restricts airflow, ices the evaporator coil, kills cooling capacity. Most common and free to fix.
2. **Thermostat set wrong or dead batteries** — set to "fan on" instead of "cool," wrong temperature, or a dead battery on battery-powered stats.
3. **Tripped condensate float switch** — a full drain pan trips the safety and cuts cooling to prevent water damage. Very common in summer.
4. **Dirty outdoor (condenser) coil** — grass, cottonwood, and dirt block heat rejection; the unit runs but can't cool.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a common $15 part that causes the compressor and/or fan motor to hum, buzz, or not start.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit gets pitted or stuck, so the condenser never energizes.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — weak cooling, ice on the copper lines, long run times. Requires a pro; needs leak repair, not just a "top-off."
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — result of low airflow or low refrigerant; the whole coil is a block of ice.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor or blower motor** — no airflow across a coil means no useful cooling.
10. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; the unit won't cool at all and may trip the breaker. Often near end-of-life or a warranty question.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A dirty filter is the #1 cause of poor cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's a battery-powered model and the display is dim or blank.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — don't keep resetting it.
- **Condensate drain:** Look for a full or overflowing drain pan and a float switch. A full pan trips the switch and stops cooling. Clearing a clog is DIY; a wet-vac on the drain line outlet often works.
- **Return and supply vents:** Make sure furniture, rugs, or closed dampers aren't blocking airflow. Open closed room vents.
- **Outdoor unit:** With the breaker on, make sure the fan and coil aren't buried in leaves, grass clippings, or cottonwood. Gently clear debris away from the outside — **do not** open the unit or spray water into electrical parts.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the copper lines or coil, turn the system to **Fan Only** (or Off) for a few hours to let it thaw before calling. Running a frozen system can damage the compressor.

If cooling doesn't return after these checks, it's a pro job.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply and return air temperature (a healthy split should show roughly a 16–22°F drop across the coil).
2. **Check airflow** — filter, blower operation, coil condition, and static pressure.
3. **Read the thermostat/control** — on iComfort S30/S40 systems, pull any logged fault codes; on older units, verify the 24V call for cooling reaches the outdoor contactor.
4. **Test electrical components** — capacitor microfarads, contactor operation, and voltage at the compressor and fan motor.
5. **Take refrigerant readings** — connect gauges, check pressures, superheat and subcooling against the Lennox charging chart for that model. This is how they confirm low refrigerant vs. an airflow problem.
6. **Leak search** — if refrigerant is low, they should find the leak (electronic detector, dye, or bubbles), not just add refrigerant and leave.
7. **Compressor test** — check windings and amp draw if the compressor hums but won't start.

Be wary of any quote that jumps straight to "add refrigerant" or "replace the compressor" without measuring airflow and pressures first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter / blocked returns | Replace filter, open vents | Check blower, static pressure |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn to Fan Only, thaw fully | Find airflow/refrigerant cause |
| Outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or bad contactor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor |
| Humming, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — high voltage | Test & replace capacitor |
| No cooling, water near air handler | Tripped condensate float switch | Clear drain line/pan | Clear clog, verify switch |
| Long run times, poor cooling | Low refrigerant (leak) | None | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Fan motor or capacitor failure | Verify breaker on | Replace motor/capacitor |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Shorted compressor or wiring | Stop resetting | Diagnose compressor/electrical |
| S30/S40 shows a fault code | Control-detected fault | Note the code | Interpret code, targeted repair |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US installed prices; your area, model, and warranty change them significantly.

- **Air filter:** $0–$30 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries / reset:** $0–$15 (DIY)
- **Clear condensate drain / reset float switch:** $0 DIY, or $100–$250 for a service call
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$350 replaced
- **Contactor:** $150–$350 replaced
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$650 replaced
- **Blower motor:** $450–$900 replaced (ECM motors cost more)
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,500+ depending on leak location; a coil replacement pushes this higher
- **Evaporator or condenser coil replacement:** $1,200–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,800–$2,200+ (often worth comparing to a full system replacement, especially on older units)
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90–$200, usually credited toward the repair

If a leak or compressor failure is involved and the unit is under 10 years old, ask whether the **Lennox parts warranty** covers the component — you may only owe labor.
