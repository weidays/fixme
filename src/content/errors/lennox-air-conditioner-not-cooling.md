## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a fault code that flashes on a board — it's the symptom you notice when your Lennox air conditioner runs but the house won't cool down, or the system won't start the cooling cycle at all. Depending on your equipment, the underlying problem may or may not trigger a diagnostic code.

- **Legacy Lennox units (Merit, older Elite):** These have no display. You diagnose by symptom, airflow, and electrical measurements.
- **Communicating iComfort systems (Signature series with an S30/S40 thermostat):** These *can* log alerts and error codes on the thermostat when a sensor, communication link, or outdoor unit reports a fault. Check the thermostat's alert/notification screen — the exact codes and wording vary by board and firmware, so read what your specific unit shows rather than assuming.

Either way, "not cooling" is a **pro-level** problem once you've ruled out the simple stuff, because the likely causes involve refrigerant, capacitors, or the compressor.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops efficiency, and can freeze the evaporator coil.
2. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — from restricted airflow or low refrigerant; the coil turns to a block of ice and stops absorbing heat.
3. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — you never "run low" normally; low charge means a leak somewhere in the system.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor and/or condenser fan won't start; you may hear a hum or clicking outside.
5. **Burned or stuck contactor** — the outdoor unit doesn't get power to start.
6. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat, so cooling capacity collapses on hot days.
7. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
8. **Thermostat problem or wiring fault** — no cooling call reaches the equipment.
9. **Failing compressor** — the heart of the system; expensive and usually the last suspect.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop here if the problem isn't obvious.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If it's gray and blocked, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to COOL, drop the target 3–5°F below room temp, and confirm the fan setting is AUTO or ON. If it's battery-powered and blank, swap the batteries.
- **Breaker:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker to the AC. Reset it once. If it trips again immediately, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting it.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked by furniture, rugs, or closed dampers.
- **Ice on the lines or coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF (or FAN ONLY) and let it thaw fully — several hours. Running a frozen coil damages the compressor.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float safety switch and stop cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan; clearing a visible clog is fine, but don't disable any safety switch.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris away from the condenser coil. Gently rinse it with a garden hose (power off) if it's caked with dirt.

**Do not** attempt to add refrigerant, open sealed refrigerant lines, bypass any safety switch, or repeatedly reset a unit that keeps tripping.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A quote should reflect a logical sequence. Here's what a competent tech does:

1. **Confirms the cooling call** at the thermostat and checks 24V control voltage.
2. **Reads any iComfort alerts** on communicating systems and checks control board status LEDs on legacy boards.
3. **Measures airflow and inspects the coil** for icing or dirt.
4. **Checks electrical components** — capacitor microfarads, contactor condition, and amp draw on the compressor and fan motor.
5. **Puts gauges on the system** to read suction and head pressures, then calculates superheat/subcooling to judge the refrigerant charge.
6. **Leak-searches** with an electronic detector or dye if the charge is low.
7. **Tests the compressor** windings and startup if pressures and electricals point to it.

If a tech quotes a compressor or "just add refrigerant" without gauges and a leak search, get a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and ductwork |
| Ice on coil or lines | Restricted airflow or low charge | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Verify airflow, check charge, leak search |
| Outdoor fan spins, no cooling | Low refrigerant leak | None — call pro | Find/repair leak, recharge |
| Humming outside, unit won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Indoor blower runs, outdoor dead | Contactor or breaker | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Outdoor unit caked in dirt | Dirty condenser coil | Rinse gently with hose | Deep-clean coil, check pressures |
| Water near indoor unit, no cooling | Clogged condensate / float switch | Clear visible clog | Clear line, reset/replace switch |
| Nothing runs at all | Thermostat or control fault | Check batteries and settings | Test 24V, control board, wiring |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condensate drain clearing / float switch:** $75–$250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type
- **Fan motor:** $300–$700
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth weighing against full unit replacement on older systems)
- **Diagnostic / service call:** $75–$200, often credited toward the repair

Costs vary by region, refrigerant type (older R-410A vs. legacy R-22 is far pricier), and whether the part is under Lennox warranty. Keep your registration info handy — many Lennox parts carry a 5–10 year limited warranty that covers the part but not labor.
