## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the symptom you notice when your Lennox air conditioner runs but the house stays warm, or the system won't start at all. Newer Lennox systems with **iComfort communicating controls** may show an actual alert or error number on the thermostat; older Merit and Elite units with standard contactors typically show nothing, leaving you to diagnose from behavior.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a $0 dirty filter to a $2,500 compressor replacement, the goal of this page is to help you rule out the cheap, safe causes yourself and understand what a technician should be checking so you can sanity-check any quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, freezes the indoor coil, and kills cooling capacity. The single most common cause.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass, cottonwood, and dust block heat rejection so the system can't shed heat.
3. **Thermostat problem** — wrong mode/setting, dead batteries, or a failed thermostat not calling for cooling.
4. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler has lost power.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a very common electrical failure; the fan or compressor hums but won't spin.
6. **Failed contactor** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling, ice on the lines, and eventually no cooling. Requires a pro.
8. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; blocks cooling until thawed.
9. **Clogged condensate drain** — a full safety float switch shuts the system off to prevent overflow.
10. **Failed compressor** — the worst case; the "heart" of the AC has failed and needs replacement.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or opening sealed equipment:

- **Filter:** Pull the air filter. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it. Then let the system run 30 minutes.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is several degrees below room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered. Make sure the fan is on AUTO, not just ON.
- **Breakers:** Check the electrical panel for the **air handler/furnace breaker** and the **outdoor AC breaker**. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Vents and registers:** Walk the house and confirm supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Check that the condenser coil (the fins around the outdoor cabinet) isn't packed with grass, leaves, or debris. Turn off power and gently rinse from the inside out with a garden hose if it's clearly dirty. Clear tall grass and weeds within 2 feet.
- **Condensate drain:** If your indoor unit has a shutoff float and the AC won't start, a full drain pan may be the cause. You can vacuum the drain line outlet with a wet/dry vac.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** to thaw it for a few hours, then replace the filter.

Do **not** open refrigerant lines, add refrigerant, bypass the float or safety switches, or open the sealed compressor compartment.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech should work through this order — use it to judge whether a quote is thorough or a guess:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** at the thermostat and confirm 24V is reaching the outdoor unit.
2. **Check power and electrical components** — measure the run capacitor's microfarad rating and inspect the contactor for pitting.
3. **Measure temperature split** — the difference between return and supply air should be roughly 15–20°F. Low split points to airflow or refrigerant issues.
4. **Attach gauges** to read suction and head pressures and calculate **superheat/subcooling** — this reveals low refrigerant, a leak, or a metering-device problem.
5. **Inspect coils and airflow** — evaluate the evaporator, condenser, and blower for restriction or freezing.
6. **On iComfort systems**, pull stored fault codes from the thermostat or control board.
7. **Leak search** if refrigerant is low — with electronic detector or dye — since topping off without a repair is a temporary and wasteful fix.

Ask for the measured numbers (capacitor reading, subcooling, temperature split). A pro who replaces a compressor without documenting refrigerant readings is skipping steps.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but isn't cold | Dirty filter or dirty coil | Replace filter; rinse condenser | Check refrigerant charge, coils |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, replace filter | Find airflow restriction or leak |
| Outdoor unit won't start, humming | Failed run capacitor | None — call pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| Whole system dead | Tripped breaker / blown fuse | Reset breaker once | Find cause of overload |
| Weak cooling, high energy bills | Low refrigerant from leak | None | Leak search and recharge |
| System shuts off, water near unit | Clogged condensate drain | Vacuum drain line | Clear drain, test float switch |
| Loud outdoor unit, no cooling, breaker trips | Failing compressor | Shut off — do not reset | Diagnose and replace compressor |
| Thermostat blank or unresponsive | Dead batteries or failed stat | Replace batteries | Replace/rewire thermostat |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Prices vary by region and unit age:

- **Diagnostic/service call:** $75–$200
- **Air filter:** $15–$40 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Thermostat replacement:** $150–$400
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500–$1,500+ depending on leak location
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900–$2,000+
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,500+ (often worth comparing to full unit replacement)
- **Full outdoor unit / system replacement:** $4,500–$9,000+

If your unit is over 12–15 years old and uses R-22 refrigerant, a major repair like a compressor or coil is often a poor investment — ask your tech to compare repair vs. replacement.
