## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a numeric fault code — it's the most common complaint homeowners bring about a Lennox air conditioner. The system may be running, blowing air, or even short-cycling, but the house never reaches the thermostat setpoint.

On variable-capacity Lennox systems paired with an **iComfort** thermostat, an underlying problem may display an actual alert or code number on the screen. On single-stage Merit and many Elite units, there is no code display at all — you diagnose by symptom.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a $10 filter to a $2,500 compressor, this page walks you from the cheap, safe fixes to the pro-level diagnosis so you can sanity-check any quote.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops capacity, and can freeze the indoor coil.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, dust, or shrubs blocking heat rejection.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan "on" vs "auto"), or a miswired/failing stat.
4. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks cooling entirely.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system can't absorb heat; almost always a leak, never "normal loss."
6. **Failed run capacitor** — a common, cheap part that stops the compressor or fan motor from starting.
7. **Contactor failure** — the outdoor relay that switches power to the compressor.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power.
9. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive cause; the unit hums or trips but won't cool.
10. **Undersized or blocked ductwork** — the system runs fine but can't keep up on the hottest days.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks you should do yourself. Do **not** open the sealed refrigerant system, remove electrical panels on the outdoor unit, or add refrigerant.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This alone fixes a surprising number of "not cooling" calls.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Set the fan to **AUTO**. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check both the indoor (air handler/furnace) and outdoor (condenser) breakers. If one is tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated resets can be dangerous.
- **Supply and return vents:** Make sure they're open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Check that the coil (the fins around the outside) isn't buried in grass, leaves, or shrubs. Clear at least 2 feet of space. You can gently rinse the fins with a garden hose from the outside **with the unit powered off at the breaker**.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper refrigerant line, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** to thaw it. Never chip ice off.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain can trip a float switch and stop cooling. Check for standing water in the drain pan.

## How a technician will diagnose it

Knowing the sequence helps you judge whether a quote is fair:

1. **Visual and airflow check** — filter, coils, blower, and any iComfort alerts.
2. **Electrical checks** — voltage at the contactor, capacitor microfarad reading, and control board or low-voltage fuse.
3. **Refrigerant pressures** — gauges on the service ports to read suction and liquid pressure, plus superheat and subcooling to judge charge and airflow.
4. **Leak search** — if the charge is low, an electronic detector or dye is used to find the leak before any refrigerant is added.
5. **Compressor test** — amperage draw and winding resistance to confirm whether the compressor is failing.
6. **iComfort diagnostics** — on variable-capacity units, the tech pulls stored fault history from the thermostat or outdoor board.

A good tech will show you the readings. Be cautious of anyone who "adds a couple pounds" of refrigerant without looking for a leak — that's a temporary and non-compliant fix.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but isn't cold | Dirty filter or low refrigerant | Replace filter | Check charge, find/repair leak |
| Ice on indoor coil or line | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Diagnose airflow vs. charge |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed capacitor or contactor | Confirm breaker is on | Test/replace capacitor or contactor |
| Unit hums but won't start | Failed capacitor or compressor | None — call a pro | Test compressor, replace part |
| Warm air from all vents | Wrong thermostat mode or dirty coil | Set to COOL/AUTO, clear coil | Verify wiring, clean coil |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant or undersized system | Shut off to protect compressor | Leak repair or load evaluation |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Electrical fault or shorted compressor | Do not keep resetting | Locate short, test compressor |
| iComfort shows an alert | Board-reported fault | Note the code number | Pull code, follow Lennox procedure |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Prices vary by region and model tier (Merit vs. Signature).

- **Air filter:** $10 – $40 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75 – $250
- **Thermostat batteries or replacement:** $0 – $350
- **Run capacitor:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor:** $150 – $350
- **Low-voltage fuse or minor control repair:** $75 – $250
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $500 – $1,500 (higher if a coil must be replaced)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000 – $2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500 – $2,500+ (often worth comparing to a full system replacement on older units)

If your unit is 12–15+ years old and needs a compressor or coil, ask for a repair-vs-replace comparison — Lennox variable-capacity models can be costly to keep patching.
