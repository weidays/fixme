## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a specific fault code. Your Lennox air conditioner may power on, the indoor blower may run, and the thermostat may call for cooling — but the air coming from your vents is warm, weak, or the house never reaches the setpoint.

Communicating Lennox systems (iComfort S30/E30/M30 thermostats paired with variable-capacity condensers) often *do* store specific alert codes on the thermostat screen. If yours shows a numeric or lettered alert, write it down — it narrows the diagnosis considerably. Basic single-stage Merit and Elite systems with a non-communicating thermostat typically show no code at all; you just get warm air.

This page covers the general "not cooling" condition across Lennox split-system central air conditioners. Because the causes span everything from a $20 filter to a $2,500 compressor, the severity here is rated **pro** — some checks are safe for homeowners, but refrigerant and electrical diagnosis are not.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow across the indoor coil, causing it to freeze into a block of ice. Airflow stops and cooling drops to nothing. The single most common cause.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, cottonwood, and dust coat the fins so the unit can't dump heat outside. Common after a mowing season or spring pollen.
3. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode (fan vs. cool), or a setpoint issue. Simple but frequently overlooked.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system can't absorb heat. Symptoms: weak cooling, longer runtimes, ice on the copper lines, or a hissing sound. Always caused by a leak, never "normal use."
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or outdoor fan motor won't start. You may hear a hum or clicking, or the outdoor fan sits still. A very common wear item.
6. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck. The outdoor unit won't energize even with a cooling call.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — the downstream result of #1, #2, or #4. Ice blocks all airflow. The unit must be shut off to thaw before further diagnosis.
8. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit is dead. Sometimes a symptom of a deeper electrical fault.
9. **Failed compressor** — the heart of the system. Warm air despite everything else running. The most expensive outcome.
10. **Control board or communication fault** — on iComfort systems, a lost signal between thermostat, indoor, and outdoor units. Often shows a stored alert code.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop at anything involving refrigerant, capacitors, or opening the sealed system.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it up to a light. If you can't see light through it, replace it. Check monthly in cooling season.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** (not just FAN), the setpoint is below room temperature, and — if it's battery powered — swap in fresh batteries.
- **Breaker:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker to the air handler and the outdoor unit. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — do not keep resetting.
- **Outdoor unit:** Make sure it's not smothered by leaves, tall grass, or a fence too close. Clear at least 2 feet of space around it. Gently rinse the outside of the coil fins with a garden hose (low pressure, top to bottom) if they look dirty.
- **Vents and registers:** Confirm supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Condensate drain:** Look for standing water in the drain pan or an overflowing safety switch — a clogged drain can shut the system down. A wet/dry vac on the outdoor drain end can clear a simple clog.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the indoor coil or the copper lines, turn the system OFF (set to FAN only) and let it thaw for a few hours before restarting. Running a frozen unit damages the compressor.

If none of these restores cooling, the problem is likely electrical or refrigerant-related — both require a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should work in this order — use it to sanity-check a quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — measure supply and return air temperature. A healthy system shows a 16–22°F split.
2. **Read stored codes** — on iComfort systems they'll pull alert history from the thermostat.
3. **Check airflow** — inspect filter, blower operation, and evaporator coil for ice or dirt.
4. **Electrical checks** — measure the capacitor with a meter, test the contactor, and confirm 24V control signal and line voltage at the outdoor unit.
5. **Refrigerant gauges** — connect manifold gauges to read suction and head pressures and calculate superheat/subcooling. This tells them if the charge is low (a leak) or if the compressor isn't pumping.
6. **Leak search** — if the charge is low, they should find the leak (electronic detector or dye), not just "top it off."
7. **Compressor test** — check amp draw and windings if pressures point to a failed compressor.

Be cautious of any tech who adds refrigerant without searching for a leak, or quotes a compressor without measuring pressures and electrical values first.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm air, blower runs | Dirty filter or frozen coil | Replace filter; thaw coil (fan only) | Check charge, airflow, coil |
| Ice on copper lines or coil | Low refrigerant or airflow restriction | Shut off, thaw, replace filter | Find/repair leak, recharge |
| Outdoor fan not spinning | Failed capacitor or motor | None — leave off | Test/replace capacitor or fan motor |
| Outdoor unit totally dead | Tripped breaker or contactor | Reset breaker once only | Replace contactor, check wiring |
| Humming, no start | Bad capacitor or seized compressor | None | Test capacitor, then compressor |
| Long runtimes, high bills | Dirty condenser coil or low charge | Rinse outdoor coil | Clean coil, verify charge |
| Alert code on iComfort screen | Communication or component fault | Note the code | Pull code, follow Lennox flowchart |
| Warm air, everything else runs | Failed compressor | None | Confirm and quote replacement |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Regional and system-type variation is significant.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $0 DIY – $150 pro
- **Outdoor coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$700
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $200–$1,500 (depends on leak location; evaporator coil leaks are the high end)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,800+ (often makes replacing the whole condenser worth considering)
- **Control board (iComfort/communicating):** $400–$900

If your Lennox system is out of warranty and the compressor or coil has failed, get a quote for a full condenser or system replacement to compare — repairs on aging R-410A or older R-22 systems can approach replacement cost.
