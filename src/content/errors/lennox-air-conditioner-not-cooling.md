## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a single Lennox fault code — it's a symptom. Depending on your equipment, it may show up as an alert on an iComfort or S30/E30 thermostat, or as no code at all on a Merit or Elite unit paired with a basic thermostat.

It means the system is failing to remove heat from your home. The outdoor unit may or may not be running, the indoor blower may be moving air, but the air coming from your registers isn't cold — or the house temperature keeps rising despite the thermostat calling for cooling.

Because "not cooling" spans everything from a $10 filter to a failed compressor, the goal below is to help you rule out the easy stuff and understand what a technician *should* check before they hand you a quote.

**Important:** Do not add refrigerant, open the sealed system, or handle any refrigerant lines yourself. That work is EPA-regulated and requires certification.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow starves the evaporator coil, drops capacity, and can freeze the coil into a block of ice.
2. **Thermostat misconfiguration** — set to "fan" instead of "cool," wrong schedule, dead batteries, or (on iComfort systems) a lost connection to the equipment.
3. **Frozen evaporator coil** — caused by low airflow or low refrigerant; the coil ices over and blocks all cooling until it thaws.
4. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — a coil packed with grass, cottonwood, or dust can't reject heat, so the system runs but barely cools.
5. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a common electrical failure. The compressor or condenser fan hums but won't start, so no heat is rejected outside.
6. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — reduces capacity and eventually freezes the coil. Always indicates a leak that must be repaired, not just "topped off."
7. **Tripped breaker or blown fuse** — the outdoor unit gets no power while the indoor blower still runs, so you get room-temperature air.
8. **Contactor failure** — the relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or burns, leaving the condenser dead.
9. **Failed compressor** — the most expensive cause. The system runs but produces no cooling; often the last thing checked.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do without tools or training:

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the top cause of no cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace the batteries if it's a battery model. On iComfort/S30 screens, check for any onscreen alert or "no communication" message.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped AC breaker (indoor **and** outdoor circuits — they're often separate). Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Registers and returns:** Make sure supply vents are open and return grilles aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for tall grass, leaves, or debris packed against the condenser fins and clear the area. Confirm the fan spins when cooling is called for.
- **Frozen coil / condensate:** If you see ice on the copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **Off** and run the **fan only** for a few hours to thaw it, and check that the condensate drain isn't clogged or backing up.

If none of these restores cooling — especially if the coil keeps freezing or the breaker keeps tripping — it's time for a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech should work through a logical sequence, not jump straight to "add refrigerant":

1. **Verify the call for cooling** and confirm the thermostat is sending 24V to the equipment.
2. **Check airflow** — filter, blower operation, and static pressure to rule out an airflow problem before touching refrigerant.
3. **Inspect the coils** — indoor evaporator and outdoor condenser for dirt, ice, or damage.
4. **Measure electrical components** — test the capacitor with a meter, check the contactor, and confirm the compressor and fan motor draw proper amperage.
5. **Attach gauges and read pressures** — measure superheat and subcooling to determine if the charge is correct, low (a leak), or overcharged.
6. **Leak search** — if refrigerant is low, they should locate the leak (electronic detector or dye) before recharging. Topping off without finding the leak is a red flag.
7. **Compressor test** — checked last, since it's the costliest component.

A quote that lists exactly *which* component failed and the measured readings is more trustworthy than a vague "needs freon."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air from vents isn't cold, outdoor fan runs | Dirty filter or coil, low charge | Replace filter, clear condenser | Check airflow, measure charge, find leak |
| Ice on refrigerant lines or indoor coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw, replace filter | Diagnose airflow/charge, repair leak |
| Outdoor unit silent, indoor blower runs | Tripped breaker, capacitor, contactor | Reset breaker once | Test capacitor/contactor, restore power |
| Outdoor unit hums but won't start | Failed start/run capacitor | None | Replace capacitor, verify amp draw |
| House never reaches setpoint | Undersized charge, dirty coils, worn compressor | Clear condenser, replace filter | Measure superheat/subcooling, test compressor |
| iComfort screen shows alert or "no comm" | Wiring/communication fault | Note the exact message | Check thermostat wiring and control board |
| Warm air only, thermostat blank | Dead batteries or thermostat power loss | Replace batteries, check float switch | Trace 24V power, replace thermostat if failed |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor. Prices vary by region and by whether you have a Merit, Elite, or Signature system.

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Air filter (DIY) | $10 – $40 |
| Thermostat batteries (DIY) | $0 – $10 |
| Condenser coil cleaning | $75 – $200 |
| Run/start capacitor replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150 – $350 |
| Refrigerant leak repair + recharge | $200 – $1,500 (depends on leak location) |
| Evaporator coil replacement | $1,000 – $2,500 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500 – $2,800+ (often near cost of new outdoor unit) |
| New thermostat (basic to iComfort) | $150 – $700 installed |

If a compressor or coil fails on an older system, get a quote comparing repair vs. replacement — repairs approaching half the cost of a new unit often aren't worth it.
