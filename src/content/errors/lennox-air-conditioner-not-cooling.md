## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific fault code — it's the most common complaint homeowners report about a Lennox air conditioner. Your system may be running, blowing air, or completely silent, but the house isn't getting cooler.

Lennox units span simple single-stage condensers (Merit series) up to variable-capacity communicating systems (Signature series with iComfort). On communicating systems, an actual alphanumeric error code may appear on the iComfort thermostat or the outdoor control board — if you see one, look that specific code up, because it will point you to the exact fault far faster than a general "not cooling" symptom.

On non-communicating and older units, there's no display, so diagnosis relies on observing symptoms: whether the outdoor unit runs, whether the indoor blower runs, and what the air coming out of the vents feels like.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. Restricted airflow can freeze the indoor coil, which then blocks cooling entirely.
2. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode/setting, or a failed thermostat not calling for cooling. Common on communicating systems after a power event.
3. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — the outdoor unit or air handler has lost power.
4. **Failed run/start capacitor** — a very common failure. The compressor or condenser fan hums, buzzes, or won't start.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak or no cooling, ice on the lines, and long run times. Always indicates a leak.
6. **Failed contactor** — the electrical relay that powers the outdoor unit is pitted or stuck, so the condenser won't energize.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — from low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer.
8. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor coil is caked with dirt/grass, so it can't reject heat.
9. **Failed condenser fan motor** — outdoor unit runs but overheats and shuts down.
10. **Failed compressor** — the least common but most expensive. The unit won't cool at all despite calling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop at anything that requires opening the sealed refrigerant system, touching capacitors, or bypassing a safety switch.

- **Filter:** Pull the air filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause and a free fix.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to COOL and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered. On iComfort, check for a code or a "Waiting" message.
- **Breaker:** Check the electrical panel for a tripped breaker to the AC or air handler. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents aren't closed, blocked by furniture, or covered.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for grass, leaves, or debris packed against the condenser coil. Gently clear away vegetation and lint. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
- **Condensate:** Check the condensate drain pan and line. A full pan or clogged drain can trigger a float switch that shuts the system down. If the pan is overflowing, you likely have a clog.
- **Ice:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn the system to OFF (fan ON) and let it thaw fully before running again — then replace that filter.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence, and knowing it helps you spot a padded quote:

1. **Verify the call for cooling** — confirms the thermostat is sending 24V and the system is trying to run.
2. **Check power and low voltage** — measures voltage at the contactor and the control board fuse.
3. **Test the capacitor and contactor** — a capacitor is measured with a meter against its rated microfarads; a burned contactor is inspected visually.
4. **Measure temperature split** — checks the difference between return and supply air. A healthy split is roughly 15–20°F; low split points to airflow or refrigerant issues.
5. **Attach refrigerant gauges** — reads system pressures and superheat/subcooling to determine if the charge is correct. Low pressures indicate a leak.
6. **Leak search** — if low on charge, they use electronic detectors or dye to find the leak before recharging.
7. **Inspect motors and coils** — condenser fan motor amperage, coil cleanliness, and compressor operation.

On communicating Signature/iComfort systems, the tech will also pull stored fault codes from the thermostat and control board, which speeds this up considerably.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Dirty filter / blocked returns | Replace filter, open vents | Check blower, static pressure |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, thaw, change filter | Diagnose airflow/refrigerant leak |
| Outdoor unit silent | Breaker, contactor, or capacitor | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor or capacitor |
| Humming, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — do not open panel | Test and replace capacitor |
| Runs constantly, warm vents | Low refrigerant / leak | None | Find leak, repair, recharge |
| Outdoor unit hot, fan not spinning | Failed condenser fan motor | Shut off to prevent damage | Replace fan motor/capacitor |
| Dirty outdoor coil | Debris on condenser | Clear vegetation, rinse gently | Chemical coil cleaning |
| No response, no display | Thermostat / low-voltage fuse | Replace batteries | Replace fuse, trace short |
| Nothing works, breaker trips | Compressor or electrical fault | Stop resetting; call pro | Diagnose compressor/wiring |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US pricing including parts and labor; your area and model tier will vary.

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** under $10 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Run/start capacitor:** $150–$400
- **Contactor:** $150–$350
- **Condenser fan motor:** $350–$700
- **Coil cleaning (condenser or evaporator):** $150–$500
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $600–$1,600 (higher for R-410A/newer refrigerants and buried leaks)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $1,000–$2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,200+ (often makes replacing an older unit more economical)

If your unit is out of warranty, over 12–15 years old, and needs a compressor or coil, ask your tech to price a full system replacement for comparison.
