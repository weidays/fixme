## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a specific error code — it's the most common complaint Lennox homeowners report. Your air conditioner may be running, the thermostat may show a call for cooling, but the air coming from your vents is warm, weak, or the house simply won't reach the setpoint.

On newer **iComfort / SunSource variable-capacity systems**, the thermostat may display an actual alert code or a "system fault" message. On older single-stage Merit and Elite units, there's no display — you just get warm air. Because the root cause ranges from a $15 filter to a $2,000 compressor, this is rated **pro**: several likely causes involve refrigerant or high-voltage components that are not DIY.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow** — the single most common cause. A clogged filter starves the indoor coil, which can freeze into a block of ice and stop cooling entirely.
2. **Dirty condenser coil** — the outdoor unit can't reject heat when the coil is packed with grass, dust, and pollen. Very common after a spring/summer of neglect.
3. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — causes weak cooling, ice on the copper lines, and rising bills. Refrigerant does not get "used up"; low charge means a leak.
4. **Failed run capacitor** — a cheap part that starts the compressor and fan. When it dies, you often hear a hum but the fan won't spin.
5. **Tripped breaker or blown low-voltage fuse** — outdoor unit gets no power, so it never runs even though the indoor blower does.
6. **Contactor failure** — the relay that sends power to the compressor is pitted or stuck; the outdoor unit stays silent.
7. **Frozen evaporator coil** — a symptom of low airflow or low refrigerant; the ice must fully thaw before cooling resumes.
8. **Failed compressor** — the worst case. The unit runs but produces no cooling because the "heart" of the system is dead.
9. **Thermostat or control board fault** — a miswired, failing, or misconfigured thermostat; on iComfort systems, a communication fault between components.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only checks a homeowner should do. **Do not** open the electrical panel of the condenser, touch the capacitor, or add refrigerant.

- **Filter:** Pull it and hold it to the light. If you can't see through it, replace it. Run the system 15–30 minutes afterward.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **COOL** and the setpoint is below room temperature. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank.
- **Breakers:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker for the AC/condenser. Reset it **once**. If it trips again immediately, stop and call a pro — never repeatedly reset.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the condenser. Gently rinse the exterior fins with a garden hose (low pressure, from the outside in) with the power off.
- **Condensate drain:** If you have a wet indoor coil, check that the drain line and pan aren't clogged and overflowing, which can trip a float switch and shut cooling off.
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the outdoor copper lines or indoor coil, turn the system to **OFF** and set the fan to **ON** to help it thaw, then call a technician.

If cooling doesn't return after a clean filter and confirmed power, it's time for a pro.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good tech follows a logical sequence — use this to sanity-check any quote:

1. **Verifies the complaint** and checks the thermostat call, airflow at the vents, and indoor/outdoor temperature split (typically 16–22°F across the coil).
2. **Inspects airflow** — filter, blower, and evaporator coil condition; confirms the coil isn't frozen.
3. **Checks electrical components** — measures the run capacitor with a meter, inspects the contactor, and confirms voltage at the condenser.
4. **Reads any codes** — on iComfort/SunSource systems, connects to the thermostat or uses the diagnostic app to pull fault and communication codes.
5. **Attaches refrigerant gauges** — measures suction and liquid pressures, subcooling, and superheat to determine if the charge is correct. This is the step that identifies a leak or a failing compressor.
6. **Leak search** if charge is low — with electronic detector or dye; a proper repair finds and fixes the leak before recharging.

Be cautious of anyone who "just adds a couple pounds of refrigerant" without searching for a leak — that's a temporary fix you'll pay for again.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak/warm air, dirty filter | Restricted airflow | Replace filter, clear vents | Clean coil, check blower speed |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan won't spin | Failed run capacitor | Turn unit off | Test and replace capacitor |
| Ice on copper lines or coil | Low refrigerant or low airflow | Turn to OFF, fan ON to thaw | Find/repair leak, recharge |
| Indoor blower runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker, contactor, or low-voltage fuse | Reset breaker once | Test contactor, fuse, wiring |
| Warm air, high bills, weak cooling | Low refrigerant leak | None (leave to pro) | Leak search + repair + recharge |
| Unit runs constantly, no cooling | Failed compressor | None | Diagnose, quote replacement |
| System won't respond at all | Thermostat or control board fault | Replace batteries, check settings | Test board/comm on iComfort |
| Outdoor unit dirty and hot | Blocked condenser coil | Rinse fins (power off) | Deep chemical coil cleaning |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges including parts and labor. Regional rates and system type (variable-capacity systems cost more) affect the total.

- **Air filter:** $10 – $40 (DIY)
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150 – $400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150 – $350
- **Low-voltage fuse / minor wiring:** $100 – $250
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $100 – $250
- **Condenser coil deep cleaning:** $150 – $400
- **Refrigerant leak diagnosis:** $150 – $350
- **Leak repair + recharge:** $600 – $1,600+ (varies with refrigerant type; R-410A pricing has risen sharply)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $900 – $2,500
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500 – $2,800+ (often worth comparing to a full system replacement)

If your Lennox system is under 10 years old, check for parts warranty coverage — the compressor and coil may be covered, though labor usually isn't.
