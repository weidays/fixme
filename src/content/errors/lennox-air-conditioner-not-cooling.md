## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a Lennox fault code. Your system runs, or tries to run, but the house does not get cool. Unlike a lockout code, this symptom does not reset on its own.

Some Lennox systems also show diagnostic information that can narrow down the cause:

- **Communicating systems (iComfort or S40 thermostat):** alert codes may appear on the thermostat, such as high-pressure or low-pressure trips, or a compressor that will not start.
- **Variable-capacity models (for example SL25XCV and EL18XCV):** the outdoor control board flashes LED codes.

Note the exact code if one appears. Any pressure or compressor lockout shown there is held by the control and needs a technician to diagnose it.

This page covers **split central air conditioners**. If your outdoor unit is a heat pump, see the related heat pump page instead.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat setting or power issue.** The thermostat is in the wrong mode, its batteries are dead, or the setpoint is above room temperature.
2. **Dirty air filter or blocked airflow.** This reduces cooling and can freeze the indoor coil.
3. **Tripped breaker or outdoor disconnect.** The indoor fan blows air, but the outdoor unit is dead.
4. **Failed run capacitor (dual capacitor).** This is the most common electrical failure in the outdoor unit. The compressor or fan hums but does not run.
5. **Failed or pitted contactor.** The outdoor unit does not engage even though the thermostat is calling for cooling.
6. **Frozen evaporator coil.** Caused by poor airflow or low refrigerant. Ice blocks heat transfer.
7. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** Indoor evaporator coil leaks are a well-documented issue across many brands, including some older Lennox coils.
8. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil or failed condenser fan motor.** Causes high head pressure and high-pressure switch trips.
9. **Clogged condensate drain tripping a float or safety switch.** This shuts cooling off on systems that have one.
10. **Compressor failure or a control board or inverter fault.** This is the least likely cause and the most expensive. Inverter faults apply to variable-capacity models.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:**
  - Set it to COOL with a setpoint at least 3°F below room temperature.
  - Replace the batteries if the display is weak or blank.
  - Wait 5 minutes. Many Lennox systems have a compressor anti-short-cycle delay.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it is dirty, and check that it faces the correct airflow direction.
- **Breakers:**
  - Check the AC breaker and the furnace or air handler breaker at the panel.
  - If one has tripped, reset it **once**.
  - If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
- **Outdoor disconnect:** Make sure it is fully seated and in the ON position.
- **Registers and returns:** Open the supply vents and clear any furniture or rugs blocking the returns.
- **Outdoor unit clearance:** Remove leaves, grass clippings and debris from around the outside of the unit. Do not open the unit.
- **Frozen coil:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines, turn the system OFF and set the fan to ON until the ice has fully melted.
- **Condensate:**
  - Check whether the drain pan or float switch area is holding water.
  - Clear the accessible condensate line opening, for example with a wet/dry vacuum at the outside drain end.
- **Panels:** Confirm the indoor unit's access panels are fully seated.

Stop there. Do not open the outdoor unit. Capacitors hold a dangerous charge even when the power is off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Confirm the cooling call.** Verify that 24 V is present at the outdoor unit's Y terminal and that the contactor pulls in.
- **Test the electrical components.** Measure the capacitor's microfarads against its rating, check voltage across the contactor, and measure compressor winding resistance and amp draw.
- **Check the condenser fan motor.** Confirm it runs and draws normal amps.
- **Measure temperature split.** A 15–22°F drop between return air and supply air is typical.
- **Check refrigerant charge with gauges.** On TXV-equipped Lennox units, charge is checked by subcooling. On fixed-orifice units, it is checked by superheat.
- **Find leaks if the charge is low.** Expect an electronic leak search, bubble test or nitrogen pressure test. Insist on finding the leak before any recharge.
- **Read stored fault history.** On communicating systems this comes from the iComfort or S40 thermostat. On variable-capacity models it comes from the outdoor board.
- **Inspect the coils.** Look for dirt on the evaporator and condenser coils, and confirm static pressure and blower operation.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing runs, thermostat blank | Dead batteries or no power | Replace batteries; reset the breaker once | Check transformer, fuse and wiring |
| Indoor fan runs, outdoor unit silent | Tripped breaker or disconnect, contactor, or no 24 V signal | Check the breaker and disconnect | Replace contactor; trace control wiring |
| Outdoor unit hums, fan not spinning | Failed run capacitor or fan motor | Turn power off at the breaker and call a technician | Replace capacitor or fan motor |
| Ice on lines or indoor coil | Dirty filter or low refrigerant | Replace filter; run fan only to thaw | Check charge, find leak, clean coil |
| Runs constantly, air only slightly cool | Low charge or dirty coils | Clear debris from around the outdoor unit | Leak repair and recharge; coil cleaning |
| Cooling stops, water near indoor unit | Clogged drain tripping float switch | Clear the condensate line | Clean pan; check float switch |
| Alert code on iComfort or S40 | Pressure or compressor lockout | Record the code | Diagnose the specific fault |

## Repair costs

| Repair | Typical US cost |
|---|---|
| Filter or thermostat batteries | $5–$40 (DIY) |
| Service call and diagnosis | $90–$200 |
| Run capacitor | $150–$400 |
| Contactor | $150–$350 |
| Condenser fan motor | $350–$800 |
| Drain line clearing or float switch | $100–$250 |
| Coil cleaning | $100–$400 |
| Leak search | $200–$600 |
| Refrigerant recharge, R-410A | $250–$800 |
| Refrigerant recharge, R-454B on 2025+ systems | Often higher than R-410A |
| Evaporator coil replacement | $1,200–$3,000 |
| Outdoor control board (variable-capacity models) | $500–$1,500 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800–$3,500+ |

On an older unit, compressor replacement often makes system replacement the better value. Warranty parts coverage can lower these costs considerably, but labor and refrigerant are usually not covered.
