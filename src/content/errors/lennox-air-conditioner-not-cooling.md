## What this code means

"Not cooling" is not a single Lennox fault code — it's a symptom. Your Lennox air conditioner may be powered on, the thermostat set below room temperature, and the system running, yet the air coming from your vents feels warm or only slightly cool. On systems paired with an **iComfort** thermostat, you may also see an equipment alert or a numbered code that points to a specific component; on older or non-communicating setups, you'll get no code at all — just warm air.

The root issue almost always falls into one of three buckets: **airflow restriction** (dirty filter or coil), **refrigerant loss** (a leak somewhere in the sealed system), or an **electrical/mechanical failure** in the compressor, capacitor, or contactor. This page helps you rule out the free DIY fixes before you pay for a service call, and understand what a fair diagnosis and quote look like.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Clogged air filter or blocked return airflow** — the single most common reason for weak cooling. Restricted airflow can also freeze the indoor coil, killing cooling entirely.
2. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, cottonwood, and dust insulate the coil so it can't reject heat. Very common in summer.
3. **Frozen evaporator (indoor) coil** — caused by low airflow or low refrigerant. You may see ice on the copper lines at the outdoor unit.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system slowly loses charge over months or years. Requires a licensed tech to find and repair.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — a common, inexpensive failure that stops the compressor or fan motor from starting.
6. **Bad contactor** — the relay that sends power to the compressor can pit, weld, or fail to close.
7. **Failing compressor** — the most expensive outcome; the unit hums or trips the breaker but won't cool.
8. **Thermostat or control-board fault** — a miscalibrated or dead thermostat, or on iComfort systems, a communication error between the board and thermostat.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are safe for any homeowner. Stop if anything requires opening the sealed refrigerant system or the electrical panel of the unit.

- **Filter:** Pull and inspect your air filter. If it's gray or you can't see light through it, replace it. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of weak or no cooling.
- **Thermostat:** Set it to **Cool** and at least 5°F below room temperature. If it's battery-powered and the screen is dim or blank, replace the batteries.
- **Breaker:** Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker for the AC (there are usually two — indoor air handler/furnace and outdoor condenser). Reset **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for the fan spinning and listen for the compressor hum. Gently clear leaves, grass, and debris from around the condenser and hose off visible dirt on the fins (power off first).
- **Ice check:** If you see ice on the coil or refrigerant lines, turn the system **off** and set the fan to **On** for a few hours to thaw before restarting. Persistent freezing points to low refrigerant or airflow problems.
- **Condensate drain:** A clogged drain line can trip a float switch and shut down cooling. Check for standing water near the indoor unit.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent Lennox tech should work methodically so you can sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — confirm thermostat call for cooling and measure supply/return air temperature split (a healthy split is roughly 14–20°F).
2. **Inspect airflow** — check filter, blower, and indoor coil for dirt or ice.
3. **Read the iComfort/control data** (if equipped) — pull any equipment codes or fault history from the thermostat or board.
4. **Electrical checks** — test the capacitor's microfarad rating, inspect the contactor, and confirm the compressor and fan motor draw proper amps.
5. **Refrigerant test** — connect gauges to read suction and liquid pressures and measure subcooling/superheat. This tells them if the charge is low.
6. **Leak search** — if the charge is low, they should locate the leak (electronic sniffer, dye, or bubble test) rather than just topping off.

Be wary of any tech who adds refrigerant without diagnosing why it's low — that's a temporary patch, not a repair.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak airflow, warm air | Clogged filter | Replace filter | Inspect blower and duct if it recurs |
| Outdoor unit dirty, warm air | Dirty condenser coil | Power off, hose off fins | Deep coil cleaning, fin straightening |
| Ice on lines or coil | Low airflow or low refrigerant | Turn off, run fan to thaw | Find airflow issue or refrigerant leak |
| Unit hums, fan/compressor won't start | Failed run capacitor | None — call a pro | Test and replace capacitor |
| No power to outdoor unit | Bad contactor or breaker | Reset breaker once | Test/replace contactor |
| Breaker trips repeatedly | Compressor or wiring fault | Stop resetting; call a pro | Diagnose compressor and electrical |
| Runs constantly, never cools | Low refrigerant (leak) | None — call a pro | Leak search, repair, recharge |
| Blank or dead thermostat | Dead batteries or control fault | Replace batteries | Diagnose thermostat/board comms |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor; varies by region and warranty status):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $75–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Refrigerant leak repair + recharge:** $200–$1,500 depending on leak location and refrigerant type (older R-22 systems cost more)
- **Evaporator coil replacement:** $800–$2,000
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,500–$2,500+ (often worth comparing against a full system replacement on older units)
- **iComfort thermostat replacement:** $250–$600

If your Lennox unit is under 10 years old, check your registered parts warranty — the compressor and coil may be covered, leaving you paying only labor and refrigerant.
