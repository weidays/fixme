## What this code means

"Not cooling" isn't a single fault code on a Lennox air conditioner — it's a symptom. Your system may be running, blowing air, and showing no error at the thermostat, yet the house never reaches the setpoint. On communicating Lennox systems (like the XC20, XC21 and XC25 paired with an iComfort thermostat), you may see a specific alert or error number stored in the thermostat's service menu. On older single-stage units (13ACX, XC16), there's usually no display at all, so diagnosis is entirely mechanical and electrical.

The underlying problem falls into one of a few buckets: **airflow** (filter, coil, blower), **heat transfer** (dirty condenser, frozen evaporator), **refrigerant charge** (a leak), or an **electrical component** (capacitor, contactor, compressor). This page walks through all of them in order of likelihood.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Dirty or clogged air filter** — restricts airflow, drops coil temperature, and can freeze the evaporator so no cool air moves.
2. **Frozen evaporator coil** — caused by low airflow or low refrigerant; ice blocks heat transfer, so warm air blows.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil** — grass clippings, dust and cottonwood block heat rejection, killing efficiency.
4. **Low refrigerant from a leak** — the system can't absorb heat; you may hear hissing or see ice on the copper lines.
5. **Failed run capacitor** — the compressor or fan motor won't start; the unit hums or clicks but doesn't cool.
6. **Failed contactor** — the outdoor unit gets no power to start the compressor and fan.
7. **Thermostat problem** — dead batteries, wrong mode, or a wiring/communication fault (more common on iComfort systems).
8. **Blower motor or capacitor fault** — indoor air handler isn't moving enough air across the coil.
9. **Compressor failure** — the most expensive cause; the outdoor unit runs but produces no cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are all homeowner-safe. Do **not** open the electrical panel of the outdoor unit, touch the capacitor, or handle refrigerant lines.

- **Air filter:** Pull it and hold it up to light. If you can't see through it, replace it. This is the single most common fix.
- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to **Cool**, the setpoint is below room temperature, and the fan is on **Auto**. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Breaker:** Check the panel for a tripped breaker. Reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro — repeated resets can damage the compressor.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs.
- **Outdoor unit:** Look for ice on the copper lines or coil. If you see ice, turn the system to **Off** at the thermostat but leave the **fan On** to help it thaw (this can take a few hours). Clear leaves and grass from around the unit.
- **Condensate drain:** A full drain pan or clogged line can trigger a safety float switch that shuts off cooling. Check for standing water near the indoor unit.
- **Evaporator coil area:** If you can safely see excessive dirt, a no-rinse coil cleaner may help — but leave sealed cabinets to a tech.

If cooling doesn't return after a filter change, a thaw, and a clean condenser, it's time for a professional.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good Lennox tech follows a logical sequence, and knowing it helps you sanity-check the quote:

1. **Verify the complaint** — confirms setpoint vs. actual temperature and checks thermostat mode/communication (pulls stored codes on iComfort systems).
2. **Check airflow** — inspects filter, blower operation, and static pressure.
3. **Inspect coils** — looks for ice, dirt, or restricted airflow at both the evaporator and condenser.
4. **Measure electrical** — tests the capacitor (microfarad reading), contactor, and compressor/fan amp draw.
5. **Gauge the refrigerant** — connects manifold gauges to read pressures and calculates superheat/subcooling to confirm charge. Low charge points to a **leak**, which requires a leak search (dye or electronic detector), not just a top-off.
6. **Isolate the failure** — determines whether it's a cheap part (capacitor/contactor), a refrigerant leak, or a failed compressor.

Be wary of any quote that adds refrigerant without mentioning a leak search — refrigerant doesn't get "used up."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air blows but isn't cold | Dirty filter or frozen coil | Replace filter; thaw coil (fan on, cooling off) | Confirm airflow, check charge |
| Ice on copper lines/coil | Low refrigerant or low airflow | Thaw unit, change filter | Leak search, recharge, repair |
| Outdoor unit hums, won't start | Failed run capacitor | None (do not open panel) | Test and replace capacitor |
| No power to outdoor unit | Failed contactor or breaker | Reset breaker once | Replace contactor |
| Warm air, dirty outdoor coil | Blocked condenser | Rinse debris off exterior fins gently | Deep coil cleaning |
| House won't reach setpoint | Undercharge or compressor | Check thermostat/filter | Gauge pressures, compressor test |
| No response at thermostat | Dead batteries or comms fault | Replace batteries | Check iComfort wiring/board |
| Water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain | Clear visible clog, add tablets | Clear line, test float switch |

## Repair costs

Honest US ballpark ranges (parts + labor; regional and model variation applies):

- **Air filter:** $10–$40 (DIY)
- **Thermostat batteries:** $5–$15 (DIY)
- **Condensate drain clearing:** $75–$250
- **Condenser coil cleaning:** $100–$250
- **Run capacitor replacement:** $150–$400
- **Contactor replacement:** $150–$350
- **Refrigerant leak search + repair + recharge:** $500–$1,600 (depends on leak location and R-410A pricing)
- **Blower motor:** $400–$900
- **Compressor replacement:** $1,200–$2,200+ (often worth weighing against full-system replacement on older units)
- **Diagnostic/service call:** $80–$180 (frequently credited toward the repair)

Note: Lennox variable-capacity units (XC20/XC25) use proprietary inverter boards; parts and labor on those run higher than single-stage 13ACX/XC16 models.
