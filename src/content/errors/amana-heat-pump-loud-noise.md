## What this code means

"Loud noise" is not a fault code on the board. It is a symptom, and the type of sound matters more than how loud it is. Amana heat pumps do not display a code just because they are noisy.

Some sounds are part of normal heat-pump operation and do not happen on a cooling-only AC:

- A whoosh or hiss when the unit enters or leaves defrost.
- A brief compressor rattle at shutdown.
- Steam rising from the outdoor coil in winter.

Other sounds point to mechanical or electrical failure and need a technician:

- Grinding
- Screeching
- Banging
- Constant buzzing
- Loud clanking

Noise does not "reset." If a sound is caused by a failing part, it continues or gets worse until the part is repaired.

Cooling-season noise issues overlap with the Amana AC Loud Noise page. This page focuses on heat-pump-specific sounds, especially those during heating and defrost.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost cycle.** The reversing valve shifts and you hear a loud whoosh or hiss. The outdoor fan stops and steam rises from the coil. This typically lasts 2–10 minutes, and how often it happens depends on the model and its defrost control.
2. **Ice striking the outdoor fan blade.** Ice builds up on the top grille, coil or fan guard and the blade hits it, causing clattering or scraping. This is common in freezing rain or when defrost is not working properly.
3. **Debris or a loose panel or fan guard.** Sticks or leaves in the unit, or a loose top grille or service panel, can cause rattling or vibration.
4. **Failing outdoor fan motor bearings.** These produce squealing, grinding or a rising hum. A loose or bent fan blade can cause rhythmic thumping.
5. **Weak capacitor or worn contactor.** These cause buzzing, humming or chattering, especially at startup.
6. **Reversing valve problems.** The whoosh is normal. A valve that is stuck mid-shift can cause a constant loud hiss or a repeated banging sound.
7. **Compressor issues.** Loud startup clunks, knocking or a rattle at shutdown can come from compressor issues. On units that have one, a missing or deteriorated sound blanket makes normal compressor noise louder.
8. **Indoor blower problems.** A failing blower wheel or motor, or a dirty or clogged filter, can cause whistling or roaring from the air handler.
9. **Refrigerant line vibration.** Lines touching the wall or the cabinet can cause buzzing that is transmitted through the house.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Identify the sound and timing.** A whoosh plus steam that stops within about 10 minutes in cold weather is most likely defrost. Write down when each noise happens. This helps the technician.
- **Look at the outdoor unit from outside the cabinet.** Check for leaves, sticks or ice buildup on the top grille or around the base. Clear loose debris from around the unit and keep 2 feet of clearance. Do not reach into the fan or chip ice off the coil.
- **Seat the exterior panels.** If the service or top panel is rattling, make sure it is fully seated in place. Do not remove it.
- **Check the air filter.** A clogged filter can cause indoor whistling or roaring. Replace it if it is dirty.
- **Check vents and registers.** Make sure supply and return vents are open and not blocked.
- **Thermostat.** If the noise is grinding, screeching or banging, set the system to OFF. In winter, use emergency heat (EM HEAT) if it is available, until a technician arrives.
- **Breaker.** If the unit trips the breaker, you may reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off and call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- They will run the system in heating and cooling, and may force a defrost cycle, to confirm whether the sound is normal.
- With the power off, they will spin the outdoor fan by hand to check for bearing roughness, blade wobble and loose set screws.
- They will measure capacitor microfarads and compressor and fan amp draw, and inspect the contactor for pitted contacts.
- They will check the reversing valve operation, coil voltage and system pressures to diagnose a valve that is stuck or leaking by.
- They will listen to the compressor for internal noise, check mounting grommets and the sound blanket, and review the age of the unit and its warranty registration.
- They will inspect the indoor blower wheel, motor mounts and line set strapping.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whoosh or hiss with steam in winter, fan stops briefly | Normal defrost | None; observe whether it ends within about 10 minutes | Only if defrost happens constantly or never ends |
| Scraping or clatter in icy weather | Ice contacting the fan blade | Turn the system off and switch to EM heat if available; do not chip ice | Check the defrost control, sensor and drainage |
| Rattling from the outdoor unit | Debris, or a loose panel or guard | Clear debris around the outside; seat the panels | Tighten fasteners and mounts |
| Squeal or grinding from the outdoor fan | Failing fan motor bearings | Turn off at the thermostat | Replace the fan motor and capacitor |
| Buzz or hum, slow or no start | Weak capacitor or worn contactor | Turn off; reset the breaker once if it tripped | Test and replace the capacitor or contactor |
| Constant loud hiss or banging | Reversing valve fault | Turn off | Diagnose and replace the valve (refrigerant work) |
| Knocking or loud clunk from the compressor | Compressor wear or a mounting issue | Turn off | Evaluate the compressor; replace if needed |
| Whistle or roar indoors | Dirty filter or blower issue | Replace the filter; open the vents | Repair the blower wheel or motor |
| Buzzing through the walls | Line set vibration | None | Re-strap or isolate the lines |

## Repair costs

These are typical US ranges and include labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Normal defrost noise, debris or panel fix | $0–$150 |
| Service call and diagnosis | $90–$200 |
| Run capacitor | $120–$350 |
| Contactor | $150–$350 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350–$800 |
| Fan blade | $150–$350 |
| Defrost control board or sensor | $200–$600 |
| Compressor sound blanket or mounts | $150–$400 |
| Indoor blower motor | $400–$1,000; ECM motors cost more |
| Reversing valve replacement | $900–$2,000 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,800–$3,500+ |

The compressor replacement cost may be lower if the part is covered under warranty, with labor still applying. If a major repair is needed on an older system, compare the quote against the cost of replacing the unit.
