## What this code means

Lennox Code **E206** means the integrated control is reporting a **second-stage gas valve relay failure** on a two-stage furnace. On two-stage and modulating furnaces, the control energizes separate first-stage (low fire) and second-stage (high fire) circuits at the gas valve; E206 is the control's report that the second-stage relay function has failed.

That is the extent of what the code itself tells you. It does not identify *why* the relay circuit failed — that is what the diagnosis below is for. Because the reported fault is inside the control and its low-voltage circuitry, E206 is **pro-level** work.

In practice the furnace may still light and run on first stage but will not deliver high fire. If the code clears on a power cycle and then returns on the next high-fire call, the underlying problem has not been fixed. Legend wording and reset behavior can vary by board revision — confirm against the label inside your unit.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

These are the possibilities a technician works through. The code names the failed function, not the root cause.

1. **Failed second-stage relay / control board** — the most common finding. Worn or damaged relay contacts, a failed driver circuit, or board damage from a voltage surge all end in the same repair: the integrated control board is replaced.
2. **Damaged or corroded gas-valve harness / connector** — a poor connection or damaged insulation on the second-stage lead is checked and ruled out before the board is condemned. Repairable if the board itself tests good.
3. **Second-stage gas valve problem** — the second-stage coil and the valve itself are tested to rule them out as a contributor. If the valve is at fault, it is replaced.
4. **Water or debris intrusion on the board** — condensate leaks or rodent damage near the control can corrode the board and its connections.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should attempt. Do not open the burner compartment, remove interior panels, or touch wiring.

- **Thermostat:** Confirm it's set to Heat and the setpoint is above room temperature. Replace the batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it's dirty. A clogged filter causes short-cycling and is worth ruling out regardless.
- **Breaker / service switch:** Cycle the furnace breaker and the switch on the unit **once**. This is your single allowed reset. If E206 returns, stop and call a pro.
- **Vents and registers:** Make sure supply and return registers are open and unblocked.
- **Condensate line:** On high-efficiency units, check the **external** drain line and trap outlet for a visible clog, and look for standing water on the floor around the unit. Do not open the cabinet or inspect anything near the control board yourself.

Do **not** repeatedly reset a furnace that keeps re-logging E206 — repeated resets will not repair the reported fault.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

1. **Read the fault history** on the control to see how often E206 has occurred and whether it's intermittent or persistent.
2. **Inspect the gas-valve harness and connectors** for corrosion, loose pins, or damaged insulation on the second-stage lead.
3. **Measure the second-stage output** with a multimeter during a high-fire call to see whether the control energizes the circuit.
4. **Test the second-stage gas valve coil** resistance to rule the valve in or out.
5. **Check incoming line and 24 VAC** to confirm the control is getting clean, stable power.
6. **Confirm the control itself** — if the harness and valve test good but the second stage will not switch, the integrated control board is condemned and replaced.

A good quote should identify *which* component failed — the board, the harness, or the valve — not simply swap parts blindly.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furnace runs on low fire only, weak heat on cold days | Second stage not being energized | Verify thermostat and filter; one reset | Test second-stage output, replace control board if confirmed |
| E206 returns immediately after reset | Persistent relay/board failure | Stop resetting; call pro | Replace integrated control board |
| Intermittent E206, works some days | Loose or corroded harness, or intermittent board fault | Note frequency and dates for the tech | Inspect/repair gas-valve harness, retest second stage |
| E206 with no high fire | Possible second-stage valve or coil problem | None | Test coil and valve, replace if faulty |
| E206 after a water leak near the unit | Corrosion on the control or connections | Clear the external condensate blockage | Inspect for water damage, replace board if needed |

## Repair costs

Honest US ranges, parts and labor combined:

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90 – $190
- **Gas-valve harness or connector repair:** $150 – $350
- **Integrated control board replacement:** $450 – $1,200 — communicating (iComfort-capable) controls sit toward the upper end
- **Two-stage gas valve replacement:** $400 – $750
- **DIY:** $0 — the only free option is a single reset; there is no homeowner-installable part for this fault.

Board replacement is the most common outcome for a genuine E206. If your furnace is under Lennox parts warranty, the board itself may be covered, leaving only labor.
