## What this code means

"Not cooling" is a symptom, not a stored fault code. On a Trane ductless mini-split, it means the indoor head is running but the room is not getting colder.

Mini-splits differ from central air in several ways:

- **Remote control:** the system is set by a wireless remote, not a wall thermostat.
- **Inverter compressor:** the compressor speeds up and slows down rather than switching fully on and off.
- **Restart delay:** the compressor waits a few minutes after power-up or a mode change before it starts.
- **Communication wire:** the indoor and outdoor units talk to each other over a dedicated wire.

Many faults on a Trane mini-split show up as a code or blinking LED on the indoor display. What the codes mean, and whether they clear on their own, varies by series. Some protective stops restart automatically once conditions return to normal. Others hold until power is cycled. Your model's service manual is the authority on which is which.

This page covers what is specific to ductless systems. For ducted central AC, see Trane AC Not Cooling.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Wrong remote setting.** The unit is in Dry, Fan, Auto or Heat mode, the set point is too high, or the remote batteries are weak and commands are not reaching the head.
2. **Clogged washable filters or a dirty indoor coil or blower wheel.** Wall units pull room air straight through fine mesh filters. Dust and mold build-up on the coil and blower wheel greatly cut airflow and cooling.
3. **Normal compressor restart delay or protective stop.** The inverter is waiting out its anti-short-cycle timer or has paused for a temperature or current protection.
4. **Outdoor unit airflow blocked.** Debris, plants, snow guards or a dirty condenser coil are restricting the outdoor unit, which triggers high-pressure or high-temperature protection.
5. **Low refrigerant from a leak.** Flare joints are the usual leak points. There may be ice on the indoor coil or small line, or the outdoor unit may run with little cooling.
6. **Communication fault between the indoor and outdoor units.** Causes include a damaged or miswired interconnect cable, loose terminals, or power problems. This usually shows an error code.
7. **Failed sensor.** A room, coil or outdoor temperature thermistor has failed, so the unit misreads conditions.
8. **Inverter board, outdoor fan motor or compressor failure.** These are less common and usually come with a fault code.
9. **Undersized unit or high heat load.** A single head may not be able to cool an open floor plan or a sunny room on a design-heat day.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Set the remote correctly.** Choose Cool mode (snowflake icon), with the set point at least 3–4°F below room temperature and the fan on Auto or High.
- **Replace the remote batteries.** Watch for the beep or the indoor display change that confirms the head received the command.
- **Wait 10 minutes.** This lets the compressor restart delay pass.
- **Clean the filters.** Turn the unit off, lift the front panel, and slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them with lukewarm water, let them dry completely, and reinstall them. Do not remove any other parts.
- **Check the louvers and the room.** Make sure the louvers are not stuck closed and that furniture or curtains are not blocking the indoor head. Close windows and doors to the cooled area.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove leaves and debris and trim plants back to about 2 ft of clearance. Do not open the outdoor unit.
- **Check the condensate line.** Confirm the drain line outlet is not blocked or kinked.
- **Check power.** Confirm the breaker and outdoor disconnect are on. If the indoor head shows a fault code, write it down. Then cycle power at the breaker or disconnect **once**: off for 5 minutes, then back on. If the code returns, stop and call a technician. Do not keep resetting it.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Read and interpret any indoor display code or outdoor board LED using the service manual for your series.
- Measure temperature split (return vs. supply air) at the indoor head and check indoor coil and blower wheel condition.
- Verify line voltage at the outdoor unit and the communication signal on the interconnect wiring.
- Check outdoor fan operation and condenser coil cleanliness.
- Connect gauges and measure pressures, superheat and subcooling against the charge chart.
- If the charge is low, leak-search (electronic detector, bubble solution, nitrogen pressure test), focusing on flare joints. Repair the leak, pull a vacuum, and weigh in the correct charge.
- Check thermistor resistance against the manufacturer's temperature/resistance table.
- Run board and compressor diagnostics before condemning an inverter board or compressor.

**Red flag:** a quote that only says "add refrigerant," with no leak search, deserves a second opinion.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan blows room-temperature air, no code | Wrong mode, high set point, weak remote | Set Cool, lower set point, replace batteries | Check remote receiver and controls |
| Weak airflow from the head | Clogged filters or dirty blower wheel | Rinse the mesh filters | Deep coil and blower wheel cleaning |
| Cooling starts after a few minutes | Normal restart delay | Wait 10 minutes | None needed |
| Outdoor unit stops on hot days | Blocked or dirty condenser | Clear debris around the unit | Clean coil, check fan motor and pressures |
| Ice on the indoor coil or small line | Low charge or poor airflow | Clean filters, turn off and let it thaw | Leak search, repair, vacuum, recharge |
| Code shown, outdoor unit silent | Communication or power fault | Cycle power once and record the code | Test interconnect wiring and boards |
| Unit runs nonstop, room stays warm | Undersized unit or high load | Close blinds and doors | Load calculation, add a head if needed |
| Code that points to a sensor or compressor | Failed thermistor, inverter board or compressor | Record the code | Component testing and replacement |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic visit | $90–$200 |
| Indoor coil and blower wheel deep clean | $150–$400 per head |
| Outdoor coil cleaning | $100–$250 |
| Flare leak repair, vacuum and recharge | $300–$900 |
| Evaporator or line set leak repair | $600–$1,800 |
| Thermistor replacement | $150–$350 |
| Communication wiring repair | $150–$500 |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350–$800 |
| Indoor or outdoor control/inverter board | $400–$1,200 |
| Inverter compressor replacement | $1,500–$3,500+ |
| Outdoor unit replacement | $2,000–$4,500+ |

Check your warranty registration before approving any large repair. Coverage of the compressor and parts can significantly change the cost.
