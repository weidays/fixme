## What this code means

"Ice on refrigerant line" isn't a displayed fault code. It's a visible symptom: frost or solid ice forms on the larger, insulated copper suction line running between the indoor coil and the outdoor Rheem condenser. You may also see frost on the service valve at the outdoor unit.

The suction line normally runs cool and sweaty, at roughly 40–50°F. Ice means the refrigerant inside is boiling below 32°F. That happens when too little heat reaches the indoor coil, when the refrigerant charge is low, or when refrigerant flow is restricted.

This page covers what's specific to the line icing. If the indoor coil itself is a block of ice, also see Rheem AC Frozen Evaporator Coil, which covers coil thawing and drain overflow in more depth.

**Does it reset?** There's nothing to reset on most models. Ice only clears once the unit stops cooling long enough to thaw. On communicating Rheem systems, any low-pressure lockout and how it resets depend on the board, so check your manual. Restart the system only once after thawing. If ice returns, stop and call a technician.

**What to do now:** Set the thermostat to **Off** and the fan to **On** so the ice thaws faster. Don't run cooling while the line is iced, because liquid refrigerant can flood back to the compressor.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Restricted airflow across the indoor coil.** This is the most common cause: a clogged filter, closed or blocked supply and return registers, or a collapsed return duct. With less warm air passing over the coil, its temperature drops below freezing. This is also the first item in Rheem's general cooling troubleshooting.
2. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Lower pressure means a colder coil, and frost often starts at the outdoor service valve and creeps along the line. AC refrigerant isn't "used up," so a low charge always means there's a leak.
3. **Dirty evaporator coil.** Dust buildup on the coil blocks airflow and heat transfer, with the same effect as a dirty filter.
4. **Blower problem.** A failing blower motor, bad capacitor (PSC motors), ECM motor or module fault, a dirty blower wheel, or the wrong fan speed tap or airflow setting for the installed tonnage.
5. **Metering device fault.** A stuck or underfeeding TXV, a wrong-size piston, or a restriction such as a clogged filter-drier or kinked line starves the coil.
6. **Running in low outdoor temperatures.** Cooling below about 60–65°F outdoors, for example on cool fall nights, without low-ambient controls drops suction pressure.
7. **Thermostat or control issue.** The blower doesn't run while the compressor does, for example from a miswired fan terminal or a control board fault.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thaw first.** Thermostat to Off and fan to On. Put towels near the indoor unit in case the melt overwhelms the drain pan.
- **Air filter.** Pull it and look. If it's gray, matted, or you can't see light through it, replace it with the same size. Seat it with the arrow pointing toward the blower.
- **Registers and returns.** Open every supply vent, and clear furniture, rugs and curtains from the return grilles. Closing vents in unused rooms raises the risk of icing.
- **Thermostat.** Confirm it's set to Cool at a reasonable setpoint (not 60°F) and the fan is on Auto or On, not stuck. Replace weak batteries.
- **Breaker and disconnect.** If the indoor blower seems dead while the outdoor unit hums, check the furnace or air handler breaker and the service switch. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, see Rheem AC Breaker Keeps Tripping.
- **Condensate line.** Once thawing starts, make sure water drains freely at the outlet. Clear a clogged line with a wet/dry vac from the outside end.
- **Panels.** Make sure the blower compartment door is fully seated. A loose door can stop the blower through its safety switch, and it also lets unfiltered air in.
- **One restart.** After the ice is fully gone and the coil is dry (allow 2–4 hours or more), run cooling once. Feel the line after 20 minutes. If frost reappears, shut the system off and call a technician.

Don't spray, scrape or heat the line, open panels on the outdoor unit, or add refrigerant yourself. Refrigerant handling requires EPA 608 certification.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- **Airflow first.** Expect checks of filter condition, total external static pressure, and temperature split across the coil (about 16–22°F is typical). The tech should also confirm the blower speed or airflow setting matches the condenser tonnage.
- **Visual check of the indoor coil and blower wheel.** This may require removing the coil access panel.
- **Blower electrical tests.** Capacitor test on PSC motors, plus motor and module checks on ECM units.
- **Refrigerant check with gauges or digital probes.** The tech measures superheat on piston systems or subcooling on TXV systems and compares against the Rheem charging chart on the unit's rating plate or service panel. A proper diagnosis follows these measurements. A tech who adds refrigerant without measuring isn't doing that.
- **Leak search if the charge is low.** Electronic detector, bubble solution, UV dye, or a nitrogen pressure test. Common leak points are the indoor coil, flare and service valve joints, and the Schrader cores.
- **Metering and restriction checks.** Temperature drop across the filter-drier, TXV bulb mounting and insulation, and kinks in the line set.

**Red flags on a quote:** "Just top it off" with no leak search on repeat visits, or a full system replacement recommended without any pressure, superheat or subcooling readings.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice on line plus weak airflow from vents | Clogged filter or blocked returns | Thaw; replace filter; open registers | Static pressure test if it recurs |
| Ice returns within a day despite a clean filter | Low refrigerant charge or leak | Turn off cooling; call | Charge check, leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Frost starts at the outdoor service valve | Low charge or metering restriction | Turn off cooling; call | Superheat/subcooling, filter-drier or TXV diagnosis |
| Outdoor unit runs, indoor blower silent | Blower motor, capacitor or board fault; blower door not seated | Check breaker; seat the blower door | Motor, capacitor or module tests and replacement |
| Ice only on cool nights or mornings | Low outdoor ambient operation | Raise setpoint; avoid cooling below about 65°F outside | Assess low-ambient kit or charge |
| Ice plus water on the floor | Coil frozen and thaw overflowing the drain | Thaw with towels down; clear condensate line | Coil cleaning, drain pan or float switch service |
| Ice with musty smell, reduced cooling over months | Dirty evaporator coil | Replace filter regularly | Coil cleaning |
