## What this code means

"Not responding to touch" is not a numbered error code. It means the Honeywell touchscreen is lit and showing information, but taps on the screen do nothing or register in the wrong place.

The key detail is that the screen still has power. If the display is fully blank, see the Blank Screen pages instead.

This page covers touchscreen models only. Button-operated models such as the FocusPRO TH6000 series and T6 Pro have no touch layer, so if their buttons stop working, the causes and fixes are different.

There are three common situations:

- **Intentional lock.** The screen is locked on purpose, and a lock icon or "LOCKED" message is usually shown.
- **Low power or frozen firmware.** The display still shows information but has stopped processing touches.
- **Hardware fault.** The touch layer itself has failed.

A lock holds until someone unlocks it. A frozen display usually clears when you cycle power. Exact menus and lock options vary by model series.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Screen lock is enabled.** VisionPRO and many other Honeywell touchscreen models support partial lock (only temperature changes allowed) or full lock. A lock can be set from the installer setup menu or remotely through the app. A lock is a common and easily missed cause of an unresponsive screen.
2. **Weak batteries.** Battery-powered or battery-backup models can keep the display lit while touch sensing becomes unreliable. A low-battery indicator may or may not appear. This does not apply to models powered only by a C wire, such as the T9/T10 and many Wi-Fi models.
3. **Frozen display or firmware glitch.** A software hang after a power blip or an update can stop the screen from processing touches until power is cycled.
4. **Dirty screen or a protective film.** Grease, moisture, or a shipping film left on new units can block or confuse the touch layer.
5. **Unstable power from the HVAC system.** A loose wallplate connection or an intermittent 24V supply can make the screen lag or ignore touches. This often shows up alongside resetting behavior.
6. **Failed touch panel.** Physical damage, age, or heat exposure can kill the touch layer while the display keeps working. In that case the thermostat needs replacing.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Look for a lock icon or "LOCKED" text.** If you see one, follow your model's unlock steps. This usually means entering a 4-digit code. 1234 is a common default, but confirm it in your manual, because on some models the code is user-set. On Wi-Fi models, open the Honeywell Home or Total Connect Comfort app and turn off the screen lock.
- **Replace the batteries (battery-powered models only).** Pull the faceplate straight off the wallplate, or open the battery door on models that have one. Install fresh, name-brand alkaline batteries of the correct size rather than rechargeables. The T9/T10 and many Wi-Fi models run on C-wire power and have no batteries, so skip this step for them.
- **Cycle power.** Remove the thermostat from its wallplate for about 30 seconds, then press it firmly back on until it seats. If that doesn't work, switch off the HVAC breaker or the furnace service switch for 30 seconds, then turn it back on. Afterward, allow a few minutes before expecting heating or cooling to restart, because built-in compressor time delays can hold the system off briefly.
- **Clean the screen.** Wipe it with a dry or barely damp microfiber cloth, and peel off any protective film. Never spray liquid directly on the thermostat.
- **Try a firmer or different touch.** Some older touchscreens need a firm fingertip press, not a light tap or a fingernail.
- **Check that the faceplate sits flush.** It should sit flat against the wallplate. Don't touch the wires. If the wallplate looks loose or wires are visible and disconnected, call a technician.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Confirm whether a lock or installer setting is active, and check the app or account for remote locks.
- Measure 24VAC between the R and C terminals to confirm stable power, and check for a missing or loose C wire.
- Inspect the wallplate terminals and wiring for loose or corroded connections.
- Check the HVAC control board's transformer and fuse if the power supply is unstable.
- Perform a factory reset if a firmware fault is suspected, then reconfigure the system settings.
- Recommend replacing the thermostat if power and settings are correct but the touch panel still fails.

A quote should not include furnace or AC repairs unless the technician finds a separate power-supply fault.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lock icon or "LOCKED" shown | Screen lock enabled | Enter the unlock code or turn off the lock in the app | Reset installer lock settings if the code is unknown |
| Display dim or slow, then ignores touches | Weak batteries (battery models) | Install fresh, name-brand alkaline batteries | Check whether the model needs a C wire |
| Screen frozen on one image | Firmware hang | Pull the faceplate for 30 seconds or cycle the breaker | Factory reset and reconfigure |
| Touches register in the wrong spot | Dirty screen or failing panel | Clean the screen and remove any film | Replace the thermostat |
| Lagging touches plus random restarts | Unstable 24V power | Reseat the faceplate firmly | Test R–C voltage and repair wiring or the transformer |
| Display works, touch completely dead | Failed touch layer | Replace the thermostat yourself if comfortable | Install and configure a replacement |
