## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a single Lennox fault code. It describes an air conditioner that starts, runs for only a few minutes, and shuts off before the house is cooled. It then repeats the pattern.

On conventional 24 V Lennox systems, there is usually no error display. You just notice the cycling. On communicating iComfort systems, the thermostat may log a related alarm, such as a high- or low-pressure trip or a compressor protection event. Read the alarm text on the thermostat screen, because it narrows the cause considerably.

How the system recovers depends on the model and board:
- **Standard pressure switch trips** usually reset automatically once pressure returns to normal, typically after the anti-short-cycle delay.
- **Repeated trips** on some Lennox outdoor control boards trigger a hard lockout. That lockout holds until power is cycled or the thermostat call is removed.

This page covers AC cooling short cycling only. Furnace and heat pump short cycling are covered separately.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat issues.** The thermostat may sit near a supply register, in sunlight, or next to a heat source. Low batteries, loose wiring, or incorrect cycle-rate settings can also cause short runs. On iComfort systems, check the configured settings.
2. **Restricted airflow from a dirty air filter or blocked returns.** Low airflow drops coil temperature and pressure, which can cause low-pressure trips or coil icing.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil or a failing outdoor fan.** Either one drives high head pressure, and the high-pressure switch opens. The switch resets once pressure falls, so the unit restarts and trips again.
4. **Incorrect refrigerant charge.** A leak or undercharge trips the low-pressure switch. An overcharge or non-condensables can trip the high-pressure switch.
5. **Electrical component problems.** Common culprits are a weak run capacitor, a pitted contactor, or a compressor overheating on its internal thermal overload.
6. **Oversized equipment.** The unit cools the air near the thermostat too fast. This causes short runs and poor humidity removal, but no actual fault.
7. **Control board or sensor faults.** These include a failing outdoor control, a pressure switch that trips intermittently, or communication problems on iComfort systems.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Confirm the thermostat is set to Cool with a reasonable setpoint and the fan on Auto. Replace the batteries if it uses them. Note whether a supply vent, lamp, or sunny window is affecting it.
- **Air filter:** Pull the filter. If it is gray or clogged, replace it with the correct size and rating.
- **Vents and returns:** Open all supply registers. Clear furniture and drapes away from return grilles.
- **Outdoor unit (from outside only):** Clear leaves, grass clippings, and debris from around the unit, keeping 2 feet of clearance. Do not open the panels.
- **Condensate:** Check the drain line and any float switch at the indoor unit. A full pan or tripped float switch can repeatedly cut power to the cooling call. Clear the drain line opening if it is accessible.
- **Breaker:** If the unit stopped completely, reset the AC breaker or disconnect **once**. Also check that the indoor unit's access panel is fully seated.
- **If it keeps happening:** Stop and call a technician. Repeated resets can damage the compressor.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will work through these steps. Use them to sanity-check a quote:

1. **Review fault history.** Read the iComfort thermostat alarms or the outdoor control board's diagnostic LED codes.
2. **Watch a full cycle.** Time the run and identify which device ends it: thermostat, pressure switch, or thermal overload.
3. **Check refrigerant pressures.** Attach gauges and measure superheat and subcooling against Lennox charging specifications. Any leak search is quoted separately.
4. **Test electrical components.** Check the capacitor's microfarad (µF) reading, the contactor contacts, and compressor amp draw and winding resistance.
5. **Inspect the outdoor unit.** Look at the condenser coil and check outdoor fan motor operation.
6. **Measure indoor airflow.** Check static pressure and blower settings, and inspect the evaporator coil for dirt or icing.
7. **Verify the controls.** Confirm thermostat wiring and placement, and check pressure switch continuity.
8. **Evaluate sizing.** If no fault is found, the technician may raise oversizing as the cause, ideally backed by a load calculation.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2–5 min, house never cools, thermostat near vent or sun | Thermostat placement or settings | Check settings and batteries; shade the thermostat | Relocate the thermostat or adjust the cycle rate |
| Short runs, weak airflow, ice on lines | Dirty filter or low refrigerant | Replace filter; turn system off to thaw | Check charge, find leak, clean coil |
| Outdoor unit stops, restarts minutes later, unit hot | High-pressure trip from dirty coil or fan | Clear debris around the outdoor unit | Clean coil, repair fan or capacitor |
| Compressor hums or clicks off quickly | Weak capacitor or thermal overload | None beyond one breaker reset | Test and replace the capacitor or contactor |
| iComfort pressure alarm, unit locked out | Repeated pressure switch trips | Note the alarm text; reset once | Diagnose charge and switch; repair the leak |
| Cools fast, shuts off, feels clammy | Oversized unit | Run fan on Auto; consider an Eco or dehumidify setting | Load calculation; blower or staging adjustment |
| Unit stops when the drain backs up | Condensate float switch tripping | Clear the drain line | Clean the pan and trap; fix the drain slope |
