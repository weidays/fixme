## What this code means

"Loud noise" isn't a Bryant fault code. It's a symptom, and on a heat pump many loud sounds are normal. Unlike a straight AC, a heat pump runs defrost cycles in winter.

**Normal heat pump sounds include:**
- A **whoosh or hiss** when the reversing valve shifts into or out of defrost
- The outdoor fan **stopping** during defrost
- **Steam** rising from the coil during defrost
- A brief **clunk or rattle** when the compressor starts or stops

**Sounds that are not normal include:**
- Grinding or squealing
- Repeated banging
- Constant buzzing or humming with no fan movement
- Metal-on-metal scraping
- A very loud compressor rattle that persists during operation

Noise does not set its own error code or lockout. Nothing needs to be "reset."

If you hear these sounds mainly in cooling mode, the separate article "Bryant AC Loud Noise" covers cooling-only causes. Ductless indoor units are covered in "Bryant Mini-Split Loud Noise." This page focuses on what's specific to heat pumps.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Normal defrost cycle.** This causes the reversing-valve whoosh, the fan stopping, and steam. Nothing is wrong.
2. **Ice hitting the fan blade or shroud.** Ice builds up on the top grille or blade during heavy frost or freezing rain. Overlap: see "Bryant Heat Pump Ice on Outdoor Unit."
3. **Outdoor fan motor bearing wear or a loose/bent fan blade.** This causes squealing, grinding, or a rhythmic ticking.
4. **Loose panels, fasteners, or a tubing rattle.** Vibration can also come from a unit that isn't level, or from loose refrigerant lines touching the cabinet.
5. **Failing capacitor or contactor.** This causes a buzz or hum, sometimes with the fan or compressor failing to start.
6. **A reversing valve that sticks or chatters.** You'll hear repeated loud clicks or bangs, or a constant hissing outside of defrost.
7. **Compressor problems.** These include loud knocking, a startup rattle that doesn't settle, or a hard-start growl. They are less common but the most expensive.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Watch one full cycle.** A whoosh, then fan off, then steam, then another whoosh after a few minutes is defrost. It's normal.
- **Look, don't touch.** Check for ice on the top grille, sticks, or debris visible in the fan area. Do not reach in. Do not chip ice with tools, because you can puncture the coil. Let defrost or mild weather clear it, or call a tech.
- **Check the air filter.** A clogged filter makes the indoor blower louder and can whistle at the registers. Replace it if it's dirty.
- **Open supply and return vents.** Whistling indoors often comes from closed or blocked registers.
- **Confirm exterior access panels are seated** and their screws are snug, if a panel is visibly rattling.
- **Grinding, banging, or a burning smell?** Turn the system off at the thermostat and call for service. If a breaker has tripped, you may reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off.

## How a technician will diagnose it

- Runs the system in heat, cool, and a forced defrost to separate normal valve shifts from faults.
- Locates the sound with power on, then inspects with power off. They check the fan blade, motor bearings, mounting, and tubing clearances.
- Tests the capacitor (microfarads) and inspects the contactor. They measure motor and compressor amp draw against the nameplate.
- Checks refrigerant pressures and temperatures to judge reversing-valve operation.
- Listens to the compressor at startup and while running. They may add a hard-start kit or sound blanket where appropriate.
- On Evolution systems, reviews the control's fault history.

A credible quote names the specific failed part. It should not just say "noise repair."

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whoosh, fan stops, steam outdoors (winter) | Normal defrost | None; observe one full cycle | None needed |
| Scraping or ticking in freezing weather | Ice contacting fan blade | Turn off; leave ice alone; call if it doesn't clear | Clear ice, check defrost controls and blade |
| Squeal or grinding from outdoor top | Fan motor bearings | Turn off at thermostat | Replace fan motor (and capacitor if weak) |
| Rhythmic thump or wobble | Bent or loose fan blade | Turn off | Replace or secure the blade |
| Buzz or hum, fan not spinning | Capacitor or contactor | Reset the breaker once only | Test and replace capacitor or contactor |
| Rattle that changes with panel pressure | Loose panel or fasteners | Seat exterior panels | Tighten fasteners, isolate tubing, level the pad |
| Repeated bangs or constant hiss outside defrost | Reversing valve or its solenoid | Turn off | Diagnose the valve and coil; repair the refrigerant circuit |
| Loud knock or growl from compressor | Compressor wear or hard start | Turn off | Hard-start kit or compressor replacement |
| Whistling at registers indoors | Dirty filter or closed vents | Replace filter; open vents | Duct or blower inspection |
