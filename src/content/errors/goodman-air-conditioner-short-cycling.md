## What this code means

"Short cycling" is not a stored error code on most Goodman air conditioners. It describes a symptom: the outdoor unit starts, runs for a few minutes or less, shuts off, and soon starts again. A healthy system usually runs about 10 to 20 minutes per cycle.

Short cycling can come from two places:

- **The thermostat ends the call early.** This happens with a bad location, low batteries, wiring faults or an oversized system.
- **A safety device inside the condenser stops the compressor.** Examples include a high- or low-pressure switch, the compressor's internal overload, or a failing capacitor.

**How resets work:**

- Pressure switches on Goodman units generally reset on their own once pressure returns to normal.
- Some models add a lockout after repeated trips. That lockout is cleared by removing thermostat power.
- Communicating models (ComfortBridge or ComfortNet) log the fault, and a technician can read it.
- Lockout behavior varies by model, so check the wiring diagram label inside your unit's panel or the installation manual.

This page covers short cycling in cooling mode on Goodman central AC. For the same symptom on other equipment, see the furnace and heat pump pages listed under Related codes.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Thermostat problems.** Low batteries, a thermostat mounted near a supply register, a sunny wall or a kitchen, a loose wire, or incorrect cycle-rate settings on a smart thermostat.
2. **Dirty air filter or restricted airflow.** Low airflow over the indoor coil drops suction pressure. That can trip the low-pressure switch or lead to icing. See the frozen coil page if you find ice.
3. **Dirty outdoor condenser coil or blocked airflow.** Head pressure rises until the high-pressure switch or the compressor overload opens. This is most common on hot afternoons.
4. **Failing run capacitor.** The compressor or outdoor fan struggles, overheats and trips its internal overload. This is very common on Goodman condensers that are several years old.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** Low suction pressure trips the low-pressure switch. Low charge also causes overheating.
6. **Faulty pressure switch, contactor or control wiring.** Pitted contactor points, a weak low-voltage connection, or a switch that opens at the wrong pressure.
7. **Oversized equipment.** The system satisfies the thermostat in a few minutes. This is a design problem, not a failed part.
8. **Compressor problems.** Internal overload trips caused by worn windings or mechanical wear.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Thermostat:** Replace the batteries. Confirm it is set to COOL with the fan on AUTO, at a setpoint a few degrees below room temperature. Check that no supply vent blows directly on it and that it is not in direct sun. On smart thermostats, look for the cycle-rate or minimum run-time setting and restore the default.
- **Air filter:** Replace it if it is dirty, and match the size printed on the frame. Do not switch to a very restrictive filter unless your system is rated for it.
- **Vents and registers:** Open all supply registers. Clear any furniture or rugs blocking return grilles.
- **Outdoor unit, visual check only:** Clear leaves, grass clippings and plants to at least 2 feet around the cabinet. Do not open the cabinet.
- **Breaker and disconnect:** If the unit has stopped completely, reset a tripped breaker **once**. If it trips again, leave it off and call for service.
- **Condensate:** Look for a full or blocked drain line. On some systems a float switch cuts cooling when the pan backs up, which can look like cycling. Clearing the line's outside end or the access port with a wet/dry vacuum is fine.
- **One reset:** Turn the thermostat to OFF for 5 minutes, then back to COOL. Do not reset repeatedly.
- **Watch for ice:** If you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil, turn cooling off and set the fan to ON so it can thaw.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A good technician will:

- Time the cycles and note what stops: the thermostat call (24V at Y drops) or the condenser while the call is still on.
- On communicating models, read stored faults through the thermostat or the CoolCloud app.
- Check thermostat wiring and voltage, and look for loose or chafed low-voltage wire.
- Measure static pressure and airflow, and inspect the blower and the indoor coil.
- Inspect and clean the condenser coil.
- Test the run capacitor with a meter (actual µF against the rating) and inspect the contactor.
- Connect gauges or probes to check suction and head pressure, superheat and subcooling. If the charge is low, they should look for the leak rather than simply topping it off.
- Test the pressure switches for continuity and confirm their trip points.
- Measure compressor amp draw and winding resistance to check for overload trips or a failing compressor.
- Evaluate equipment sizing against the home's load if every part tests good.

**Red flags in a quote:**

- Adding refrigerant with no leak search or explanation.
- Recommending a compressor or full system replacement without capacitor and amp-draw tests.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runs 2–5 minutes, house never cools evenly | Thermostat location or settings, oversized system | Replace batteries, check settings, move heat sources away from thermostat | Relocate thermostat, check wiring, evaluate sizing |
| Short cycles and airflow from vents is weak | Dirty filter, blocked returns | Replace filter, open registers | Check blower, clean indoor coil, measure static pressure |
| Outdoor unit stops on hot afternoons, restarts later | Dirty condenser coil, high head pressure | Clear debris around unit | Clean coil, test high-pressure switch and fan |
| Hum or click at startup, then shutoff | Weak capacitor, compressor overload | Turn system off and call for service | Replace capacitor or contactor, check amp draw |
| Ice on lines, short runs | Low refrigerant or low airflow | Turn off cooling, run fan to thaw, replace filter | Leak search and repair, recharge, test low-pressure switch |
| Cycling with water near indoor unit | Clogged condensate drain and float switch | Clear drain line | Clean pan, check float switch wiring |
| Indoor fan runs but outdoor unit cycles on and off | Contactor, wiring, pressure switch | None beyond one reset | Inspect contactor and low-voltage circuit, test switches |
