## What this code means

Lennox Code E204 is documented as a **gas valve mis-wired** fault. In plain terms, the integrated control board is sensing the gas valve circuit as energized or connected when it should not be — the valve is not landed on the terminals the control expects. When the board sees that condition, it flags E204 and prevents ignition.

This is a **control-circuit wiring fault, not a gas-supply or flame problem**. It is most common right after a repair, valve swap, or board replacement, when a wire ends up on the wrong terminal. It can also appear if a connector vibrates loose or a harness chafes over time and changes how the valve circuit presents to the board.

On most Lennox SureLight-style boards, **E204 holds until the wiring is corrected and power is cycled** — it does not auto-reset while the fault condition remains. Because every listed fix involves opening the cabinet and working on wiring, this is a **pro-level code**.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Gas valve wired to the wrong control-board terminals** — the most common trigger, usually following a recent service, valve replacement, or board swap.
2. **Loose or partially seated gas valve connector** at the valve or the board, often from vibration.
3. **Damaged, pinched, or chafed valve wiring harness** shorting or opening a conductor.
4. **Incorrect valve for the board** (wrong replacement part or a mismatched voltage valve).
5. **Gas valve fault** — a secondary possibility only, and only after the wiring and connector have been confirmed correct. Note that a valve that simply fails to open normally usually produces an ignition or flame-proving lockout rather than E204, so this is not the expected cause of a mis-wire code.
6. **Control board mis-reading the valve circuit** (least common; ruled out last, after wiring and valve are verified).

## Safe checks before you call anyone

These are the only steps a homeowner should perform. Everything else on this code is inside the cabinet and is technician work.

- **Confirm the thermostat** is set to Heat and the setpoint is above room temperature; replace thermostat batteries if it's battery-powered.
- **Check the furnace breaker** at the panel and the furnace service switch (looks like a light switch near the unit). If tripped, reset once.
- **Do one power cycle:** turn the furnace off at the service switch, wait 30 seconds, and turn it back on. Try this **only once** — repeated resets on a locked-out unit are unsafe.
- **Check the air filter** and replace it if dirty (unrelated to E204 but rules out airflow lockouts).
- **Make sure supply and return vents/registers are open** and unobstructed.
- **Inspect the condensate line/pan** for standing water on high-efficiency models.

If E204 returns after one power cycle, stop and call a licensed HVAC technician. Do not open the burner compartment or touch any wiring or the gas valve.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified tech will:

- Pull the diagnostic history from the control board and confirm E204 against the unit's fault legend.
- **De-energize the furnace** and compare the gas valve wiring against the Lennox wiring diagram on the unit, checking every terminal.
- Inspect the valve connector and harness for looseness, corrosion, or chafing.
- **Measure the control signal** to the valve with a multimeter to confirm the board is commanding the valve correctly.
- Verify the installed gas valve is the correct part number and voltage for that board.
- Correct the wiring, cycle power, and run a full heat cycle to confirm the code clears and ignition sequences normally.
- Only if wiring is confirmed correct and the code persists, evaluate the valve and finally the control board.

A trustworthy quote should start with **verifying and correcting wiring** — the cheapest and most likely fix — before jumping to valve or board replacement.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| E204 right after a recent repair or part swap | Valve wired to wrong terminals | Power cycle once | Rewire valve per Lennox diagram, retest |
| E204 comes and goes intermittently | Loose or vibrating connector | Power cycle once | Reseat/repair connector, secure harness |
| E204 with a burning smell or visible discoloration | Damaged harness | Shut the furnace off at the service switch and call a pro immediately; do not open the cabinet | Locate and replace/repair valve harness |
| E204 after installing a replacement valve | Wrong valve or voltage | Verify no DIY parts were installed | Confirm correct part, replace if needed |
| E204 persists after wiring verified good | Possible valve fault (secondary) | None | Test valve, replace only if confirmed faulty |
| E204 persists with valve and wiring good | Control board mis-reading the circuit | None | Replace integrated control board |

## Repair costs

Ranges are typical US pricing including parts and labor; your area and unit may vary. Because there is no homeowner-serviceable action for this code, every fix starts with a paid diagnostic — there is no $0 outcome.

- **Diagnostic / service call:** $90 – $180
- **Rewire or reseat gas valve connection (labor only):** $120 – $300
- **Repair or replace valve wiring harness:** $150 – $350
- **Gas valve replacement:** $350 – $900
- **Integrated control board replacement:** $450 – $850

Because E204 is most often a **wiring correction**, many repairs land at the low end — the diagnostic plus a wiring fix. Parts replacement only applies when wiring is confirmed correct and the valve or board itself is at fault. There is **no homeowner-installable part** for this code, so parts are listed as none above.

## Other codes on your unit

Lennox fault-code legends vary by board generation, and the same number can mean different things across board families. If your furnace is showing codes other than E204, read them off the diagnostic label inside the furnace or your specific unit manual rather than relying on a generic list, and give the technician the exact codes and the order they appeared.
