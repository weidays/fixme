## What this code means

"Not heating" is a symptom, not a single fault code. Your Lennox ductless heat pump runs, or tries to run, in heat mode but doesn't warm the room.

Lennox mini-splits heat by running the refrigeration cycle in reverse, using a reversing valve. Anything that stops that reversal, limits refrigerant flow, or keeps the inverter compressor from ramping up will cost you heat.

Not every pause is a fault. Two behaviors are normal and often get mistaken for a problem:

- **Warm-start delay:** the indoor fan waits until the coil is warm before blowing.
- **Defrost cycles:** in cold, damp weather the indoor fan stops for several minutes while the outdoor coil defrosts.

Some failures make the unit show an error code or a blinking LED on the indoor unit. Others, such as low refrigerant charge, often just reduce output with no code at all. Code meanings and blink patterns vary by Lennox series, so check your model's installation or service manual.

Most protective lockouts clear when you cycle power. A fault that is still present will return.

This page covers what is unique to heating on a mini-split. For cooling-side issues, see the related pages listed at the bottom.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Wrong mode or settings.** The remote is in Cool, Dry, Fan or Auto, or the set point is below room temperature. Dead remote batteries mean commands never reach the unit.
2. **Dirty indoor filter or coil.** Restricted airflow cuts heat delivery. It can also trigger high-pressure or coil over-temperature protection in heat mode, which throttles or stops the compressor.
3. **Normal defrost or cold-weather capacity limits.** The unit pauses heating to defrost. In deep cold, a standard (non-hyper-heat) model may simply be at the limit of its capacity.
4. **Outdoor unit blocked by snow, ice or debris.** A buried or iced coil can't absorb heat. Poor drainage of defrost meltwater can refreeze into a solid block.
5. **Low refrigerant charge from a leak.** This reduces heating output and is often silent. Look for frost patterns on the service valves, or long run times that still leave the room cool.
6. **Reversing valve or coil failure.** The unit runs but stays in cooling, or delivers lukewarm air, in heat mode.
7. **Temperature sensor (thermistor) faults.** Bad indoor-coil, outdoor-coil or ambient sensors cause wrong defrost decisions, false protection trips or no heat. These usually come with an error code.
8. **Inverter board, outdoor fan or compressor faults, or a communication error between the indoor and outdoor units.** These typically display a code and may stop operation entirely.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

- **Check the remote settings.** Confirm Heat mode (sun icon), not Auto or Cool. Set the temperature 3–5 °F above room temperature and make sure no timer or sleep mode is active.
- **Replace the remote batteries.** Watch for the indoor unit's acknowledgment beep when you press a button.
- **Clean or replace the indoor filters.** Lift the front panel and slide out the mesh filters. Rinse them in lukewarm water, let them dry fully, and reinstall. Do this every 2–4 weeks during heavy use.
- **Wait out defrost.** If the outdoor unit is steaming and the indoor fan has stopped, give it 15 minutes.
- **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Remove snow, leaves and debris from the coil and base, keeping 12+ inches of clearance. Make sure the unit sits above snow line. Do not chip ice off the coil; you can puncture the tubing.
- **Check the breaker and outdoor disconnect.** If the breaker has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
- **Reset once.** Cut power at the breaker for about 5 minutes, then restore it and try heat mode again. Write down any error code or LED blink pattern.
- **Check the indoor condensate drain.** Make sure the drain line isn't kinked or disconnected and that no water is dripping from the indoor unit.
- **Confirm the front panels and covers are fully seated.**

## How a technician will diagnose it

A qualified technician will typically work through these steps:

- Read the error code or LED blink history using the indoor display, the remote's check function, or the outdoor board's diagnostic LEDs.
- Measure supply and return air temperatures. A healthy heat-mode rise is often 25–40 °F or more, depending on conditions.
- Check line voltage and the indoor–outdoor communication wiring.
- Check whether the reversing valve coil is getting voltage in heat mode and whether the valve actually shifts.
- Measure thermistor resistance against the Lennox temperature/resistance chart.
- Connect gauges and check pressures, superheat and subcooling against the charging chart. If the charge is low, they should leak-search before recharging.
- Inspect the inverter board, compressor windings and outdoor fan motor operation.
- Verify the defrost cycle starts and terminates correctly.

A quote should name a specific failed component. Be wary of a "top-off" recharge offered without a leak search.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit blows room-temp or cool air, no code | Wrong mode, low set point, dead remote | Set Heat mode, raise set point, replace batteries | None if resolved |
| Weak heat, low airflow | Dirty filters or indoor coil | Wash filters | Deep-clean indoor coil and blower wheel |
| Indoor fan stops, outdoor unit steams | Normal defrost | Wait 10–15 min | Only if defrost never ends |
| Outdoor unit encased in ice | Blocked unit, drainage problem, defrost or sensor fault | Clear snow and debris, check clearance | Test defrost sensors and controls, fix drainage or mounting |
| Long run times, room never warms, no code | Low refrigerant from a leak | Note symptoms, call a pro | Leak search, repair, evacuate and recharge |
| Cold air in heat mode, no defrost | Reversing valve or its coil failed | Reset once | Test the coil, replace the valve or coil |
| Error code or blinking LED, unit stops | Sensor, board, communication or compressor fault | Record the code, reset once | Diagnose per Lennox service manual, replace the failed part |
| Breaker trips when heat starts | Compressor, board or wiring fault | Reset breaker once only | Electrical diagnosis |

## Repair costs

Typical US ranges, including parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Settings, batteries, filter cleaning | $0–$15 |
| Service call / diagnostic | $100–$250 |
| Professional indoor coil and blower cleaning | $150–$400 per head |
| Thermistor (temperature sensor) replacement | $150–$350 |
| Reversing valve coil | $150–$350 |
| Reversing valve replacement (brazing, evacuation, recharge) | $800–$1,800 |
| Leak search and repair plus recharge | $300–$1,500+, depending on leak location and refrigerant type (R-410A or R-32/R-454B on newer units) |
| Outdoor fan motor | $350–$800 |
| Inverter / outdoor control board | $500–$1,200 |
| Indoor control board | $300–$700 |
| Compressor replacement | $1,500–$2,500+ |

If a compressor fails out of warranty on an older system, replacing the whole system may make more sense. Lennox parts warranties often require registration, so check yours before paying for parts.
