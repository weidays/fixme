## What this code means

"Outdoor fan not spinning" is a symptom, not a numbered fault code. It means the condenser fan on top of your Lennox outdoor unit stays still while the system is calling for cooling. That fan pulls air across the condenser coil to reject the heat taken from your home. Without it, the system stops cooling well and the compressor overheats.

What you notice usually follows a pattern:

- **Warm air indoors:** the indoor blower keeps running, but the air coming out is warm or only slightly cool.
- **Noise from the outdoor unit:** you may hear the compressor humming or buzzing.
- **Cycling compressor:** the unit may shut off on its thermal overload and then restart after it cools down.

How the unit resets depends on the model:

- **Conventional units:** they have no lockout. Once the cause is fixed, they resume operation on the next cooling call.
- **Communicating iComfort units:** they may hold an alert, or lock out after repeated high-pressure or overload trips, until the condition clears or power is cycled. Behavior varies by board and firmware.

This page covers central air conditioners. If you have a heat pump, see the separate heat pump page listed under Related codes. It covers defrost-related fan behavior that does not apply here.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

1. **Failed run capacitor (or the fan side of a dual capacitor).** This is by far the most common cause on Lennox PSC-motor condensers.
   - Capacitors weaken with heat and age.
   - A classic sign is a fan that hums or spins slowly, or that starts if nudged.
   - A bulging or leaking capacitor top is another giveaway, though only a technician should look inside the panel.
2. **Failed condenser fan motor.**
   - Bearings seize, so the blade is stiff or won't turn freely.
   - Windings open up, so the motor overheats and trips its internal overload.
   - Motors in units older than about 10 years are frequent culprits.
3. **Worn or pitted contactor.** On single-pole contactors, a burned contact on the fan leg can leave the compressor powered but not the fan.
4. **Wiring fault.** Possibilities include a loose or burned wire at the capacitor, contactor or motor, or wires chewed by rodents or insects. Ant nests in contactors are common in Southern states.
5. **Obstruction or seized blade.** Debris, a bent fan guard, or a blade that has slipped on its shaft can stop the fan.
6. **Outdoor control board or ECM motor fault (variable-speed models only).** On XC25, SL28XCV, EL18XCV and similar units, the board commands the ECM fan motor. A failed motor module, board output, or communication fault can stop the fan. These units often log a code.
7. **No call reaching the outdoor unit.** In this case the fan and compressor are *both* off. That points to the thermostat, the disconnect, the breaker, or the low-voltage circuit rather than the fan itself. See Not cooling.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do **not** remove panels, touch the capacitor or contactor, or poke the fan blade. Capacitors hold a dangerous charge even with power off.

1. **Check the thermostat.**
   - Set it to Cool and set the temperature at least 3°F below room temperature.
   - Replace the batteries if the display is dim or blank.
   - Wait 5 minutes. Many Lennox thermostats and boards have a built-in compressor delay before the outdoor unit starts.
2. **Watch the outdoor unit while it runs.**
   - Is the compressor running, humming, or silent?
   - Is the fan completely still or turning slowly?
   - Is any code showing on an iComfort thermostat?

   Write it all down for your technician.
3. **Check the breaker and the disconnect.**
   - Look at the AC breaker in your main panel. If it tripped, reset it **once**. If it trips again, stop and call a pro.
   - Confirm the outdoor disconnect box next to the unit is fully on and seated.
4. **Replace a dirty air filter.** A clogged filter won't stop the outdoor fan, but it adds strain and can lead to a frozen coil while the system struggles.
5. **Clear around the unit from the outside only.** Remove leaves, branches, or covers sitting on top of the fan grille. Keep 2 feet of clearance around the unit.
6. **Turn it off if the fan won't run.** If the fan stays stopped while the compressor runs, set the thermostat to Off. Running the system this way risks compressor damage.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech should follow roughly this order.

1. **Confirm the cooling call.** They check for 24 VAC at the contactor coil and confirm the contactor is pulling in.
2. **Kill power and discharge the capacitor safely.** Then they inspect it for bulging or leaks and measure capacitance with a meter. A reading more than about 6% below the rated µF means the capacitor should be replaced.
3. **Spin the blade by hand with power off.**
   - A stiff or gritty blade points to failed bearings, which means a motor replacement.
   - A free blade calls for checking the motor windings for continuity and shorts to ground.
4. **Check voltage at the motor leads with power on.**
   - Voltage present but the motor dead means a bad motor or capacitor.
   - No voltage means a contactor or wiring problem.
5. **Inspect the contactor.** They look for pitted or burned contacts and check for a voltage drop across them.
6. **On ECM and communicating units:**
   - Read the board's alert codes.
   - Verify the board's fan output and communication signals.
   - Test the motor according to the Lennox service procedure for that model.
7. **Check system health afterward.** After the repair, they verify that fan amps are within the nameplate rating and that the compressor wasn't damaged. That means checking amps and pressures if the unit overheated repeatedly.

Be wary of a quote that jumps straight to "replace the compressor" or "replace the whole unit" without capacitor and motor tests.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compressor hums or runs, fan still, fan hums | Weak or failed run capacitor | Turn system off; don't nudge the blade | Test and replace capacitor |
| Fan still, blade stiff or grinding | Seized fan motor bearings | Turn system off | Replace condenser fan motor (and usually the capacitor) |
| Fan starts, then stops after a few minutes | Motor overheating on internal overload; weak capacitor | Clear debris around the unit; turn off | Test windings and capacitor; replace motor |
| Compressor runs, fan silent, no hum | Contactor leg, wiring break, or open motor | Check disconnect is fully on | Inspect contactor and wiring; test motor |
| Fan and compressor both off | No call, tripped breaker, or disconnect off | Check thermostat, batteries, breaker (reset once) | Trace low-voltage and high-voltage circuits |
| Fan off, alert code on iComfort thermostat | ECM motor or outdoor board fault | Record the code; power-cycle once | Diagnose per Lennox code chart; replace ECM motor or board |
| Fan blocked by debris or guard | Obstruction | Clear the top grille from outside only | Straighten guard; reset blade on shaft |

## Repair costs

Typical US prices, parts and labor:

| Repair | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic or service call | $75 – $200 |
| Run or dual capacitor replacement | $120 – $350 |
| Contactor replacement | $150 – $400 |
| Wiring repair | $100 – $300 |
| PSC condenser fan motor (with capacitor) | $350 – $750 |
| ECM variable-speed fan motor (XC25, SL28XCV class) | $700 – $1,500+ |
| Outdoor control board (variable-speed units) | $600 – $1,500 |

Some things change these numbers:

- **Warranty:** Lennox parts warranties (often 5–10 years with registration) can cover the motor or board, leaving you to pay labor.
- **After-hours visits:** emergency calls during heat waves add $100 – $250.
