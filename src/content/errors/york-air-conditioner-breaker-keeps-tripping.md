## What this code means

"Breaker keeps tripping" is not a York fault code. It means the circuit breaker feeding your outdoor condensing unit has detected one of two problems:

- **Overcurrent:** too many amps for too long.
- **Short circuit or ground fault:** a sudden, very high current.

The breaker cuts power to protect the wiring. It does **not** reset by itself. It stays off until someone resets it by hand. The York unit cannot clear this condition on its own.

**When it trips tells you a lot:**

- **Instantly when you reset it:** this points to a dead short or ground fault, such as a grounded compressor, shorted fan motor, or damaged wiring.
- **A few seconds after the compressor tries to start:** this points to a hard-starting or locked compressor, or a failed capacitor.
- **After running for a while:** this points to sustained high amp draw, such as a dirty coil, a struggling motor, a loose connection heating up, or a weak breaker.

If your system is a **heat pump** rather than a cooling-only AC, see the York Heat Pump Breaker Keeps Tripping page. Heat pumps have extra causes, such as the reversing valve and defrost operation.

**Severity is "pro."** Every likely cause is inside the electrical cabinet, which carries 240 V and capacitor charge. Your role is limited to:

1. Confirming the airflow basics.
2. Resetting the breaker **once**.
3. Calling a licensed technician if it trips again.

## Common causes, ranked by probability

York does not publish a dedicated troubleshooting sequence for breaker trips. The ranking below follows standard field frequency for York and other split-system ACs, from most likely to least likely.

1. **Failed or weak run capacitor.** A bulged, leaking or out-of-spec dual run capacitor keeps the compressor from starting properly. The compressor then draws locked-rotor amps until the breaker trips.

2. **Compressor hard-starting or locked rotor.** An aging compressor with worn bearings, or one trying to start against unequalized pressure, pulls very high current at startup.

3. **Loose, burnt or corroded electrical connections.** This includes connections at the contactor, capacitor, compressor terminals or disconnect. Poor connections cause heat and arcing, and can lead to nuisance trips or a short.

4. **Shorted or grounded compressor winding.** A winding that has failed to ground trips the breaker instantly. This is the most expensive cause.

5. **Failing outdoor fan motor.** A seized or shorted condenser fan motor draws excess current. Overheated head pressure from no fan also raises compressor amps. (If the fan simply doesn't spin, see York AC Outdoor Fan Not Spinning.)

6. **Pitted or welded contactor.** Burnt contacts cause voltage drop and high current. A welded contactor keeps the unit running continuously.

7. **Dirty outdoor coil or restricted airflow.** Heavy debris, cottonwood, or dryer lint on the condenser coil raises head pressure and amp draw. This usually trips the breaker only on hot afternoons.

8. **Damaged wiring.** Examples include lawn-equipment damage, rodent chewing, or a chafed whip between the disconnect and the unit, any of which can cause a short to ground.

9. **Undersized or weakened breaker.** The breaker may be smaller than the unit's data plate allows, or it may have been weakened by repeated trips. This is diagnosed only after the unit itself checks out.

10. **Refrigerant overcharge or system restriction.** Both raise compressor load and amps. These are less common causes of breaker trips and are often found alongside performance complaints.

## Safe checks before you call anyone

Do only these steps. Do not remove the outdoor unit's service panel or open the electrical cover.

1. **Turn the thermostat to OFF.** Do this before touching the breaker so the unit doesn't try to restart under load.

2. **Check the air filter.** Replace it if it's dirty. A badly clogged filter can lower system pressures and contribute to icing and strain (see York AC Frozen Evaporator Coil). Use the size printed on the existing filter's frame.

3. **Look at the outdoor unit from outside.**
   - Clear leaves, grass clippings and debris from around the cabinet.
   - Keep 2 feet of clearance.
   - Rinse the fins gently with a garden hose from the outside, with the power off at the breaker.
   - Do not use a pressure washer.
   - Do not reach inside the fan grille.

4. **Check for obvious damage, but don't touch it.** Look for chewed or cut wiring, scorch marks, or a burning smell at the outdoor disconnect or the unit. If you see any of these, **leave the breaker off** and call a pro.

5. **Make sure the indoor return and supply registers are open** and not blocked by furniture.

6. **Reset the breaker once.**
   - Push the handle fully to OFF, then back to ON.
   - Wait at least 5 minutes so system pressures can equalize.
   - Set the thermostat back to COOL.

7. **Watch what happens.**
   - **If it trips again, stop.** Leave the breaker off and call a licensed HVAC technician.
   - Write down **when** it tripped: instantly, at compressor start, or after running. This helps the technician diagnose faster.

Do not reset it repeatedly. Do not replace the breaker with a larger one. Do not tape the breaker on.

## How a technician will diagnose it

A competent tech will typically work in this order. Use it to sanity-check what you're told.

1. **Verify supply.**
   - Measure voltage at the disconnect and contactor.
   - Inspect the breaker and disconnect for heat damage.
   - Confirm that breaker size matches the MOCP (max overcurrent protection) on the York data plate.

2. **Visual inspection of the cabinet.** Look for burnt wires, a pitted contactor, a swollen or leaking capacitor, and a damaged compressor terminal cover.

3. **Test the capacitor** with a capacitance meter, compared against the µF rating on its label (usually within ±6%).

4. **Measure compressor windings.**
   - Ohm common-to-run, common-to-start and run-to-start.
   - Check each terminal to ground with a megohmmeter or meter.
   - A grounded winding means compressor replacement or a system decision.

5. **Check the fan motor windings** to ground, and check that the motor spins freely.

6. **Measure amp draw.**
   - At startup: locked-rotor amps vs. the data plate.
   - While running: running amps vs. RLA and FLA.
   - A clamp meter shows hard-starting or overloading.

7. **Check the system** with gauges or probes, only after the electrical checks pass. This includes pressures, superheat and subcooling, and coil condition, to rule out overcharge or restriction.

8. **On variable-speed York models,** read the inverter and board fault history. Inverter drives have their own fault reporting, which varies by model.

**Red flags in a quote:**

- Recommending a compressor or full system replacement **without** documented winding and ground readings.
- Replacing a breaker with a larger one than the data plate allows.

## Symptom, cause and what to do

| Symptom | Likely cause | DIY action | Technician job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breaker trips the instant it's reset | Grounded compressor, shorted fan motor or wiring | Leave breaker off; call a pro | Megohm/ground test on windings and wiring; repair or replace |
| Trips a few seconds after compressor tries to start, humming heard | Failed run capacitor or locked compressor | Reset once only; if repeat, leave off | Test and replace capacitor; consider hard-start kit; check compressor amps |
| Trips only on hot afternoons after running | Dirty condenser coil, weak capacitor, high head pressure | Clear debris; hose-rinse fins with power off | Coil cleaning, capacitor test, amp draw and charge check |
| Trips with burning smell or scorch marks at unit | Loose or burnt connection, failing contactor | Leave breaker off; do not open panel | Repair terminals and wiring; replace contactor |
| Outdoor fan not turning, then breaker trips | Seized or shorted fan motor | Leave breaker off | Test and replace fan motor (and its capacitor if needed) |
| Trips intermittently, unit tests normal | Weak or undersized breaker, loose panel connection | Note frequency and timing | Verify breaker size vs. MOCP; replace breaker (electrician) |
| Trips after recent refrigerant work, poor cooling | Overcharge or restriction | None beyond reporting history | Verify charge by superheat/subcooling; correct charge or restriction |

**Check your warranty before paying out of pocket.** York compressor warranties are often 10 years with registration, so the compressor part may be covered, leaving you to pay labor and refrigerant. Have your model and serial number ready.
